# GARY HALBERT

## 1. The Coat of Arms Letter (1971)

- **Product:** $2 one-page "family name report" (coat of arms + surname history). Extra copies $1.
- **Medium:** personalized one-page letter, first-class, plain hand-addressed envelope.
- **Results:** [Author's claim] "the most-widely mailed (140 million, all sent first-class) letter in history." 7.3M cash-with-order customers, 700+ employees. [Folklore] "600 million" / "1 billion" — ignore.
- **Read it:**
  - Original letter PDF: http://www.markjoyner.name/temp/halbert/coa.pdf
  - A real received copy, transcribed: https://www.zygmuntfrankel.com/zf502.html
  - Line-by-line breakdown: https://gameofconversions.com/gary-halbert-a-breakdown-of-the-famous-coat-of-arms-sales-letter/
- **Opening:** *"Dear Mr. Macdonald, Did you know that your family name was recorded with a coat-of-arms in ancient heraldic archives more than seven centuries ago?"*

**Breakdown**
- **Hook:** a question about the reader's *own surname*. As personal as a headline gets.
- **Credibility:** signed by a person ("Nancy L. Halbert"), not a company. The research is framed as an accident ("for some friends of ours who have the same last name as you").
- **Objection — "why so cheap?":** "All we are asking… is enough to cover the added expenses."
- **Scarcity:** "our supply is pretty slim" — soft, believable.
- **Price + upsell in the P.S.:** extra copies $1 each, "great gifts for relatives."

**Steal:**
1. Personalize to identity, not just a first name — niche, dog breed, city, hobby. ("Golden retriever owners: …")
2. Give a *reason why* the price is low.
3. Put the bundle upsell in the P.S. / below the CTA.
4. Cheap front end, planned back end (they later sold frames).

> ⚠️ Heraldry experts call surname coats of arms misleading. Lesson: the technique is powerful — don't build on a half-truth. Platforms and chargebacks will punish you faster than the 1970s post office did.

## 2. "The Amazing Diet Secret of a Desperate Housewife" (1979)

- **Product:** $10 diet book. Written as "Nancy Pryor," 35, mother of three.
- **Results:** no figures found. (The "Nancy Reagan diet" ad you may have heard of — no evidence it exists. *This* is Halbert's diet ad.)
- **Read it:** full scan — https://admoneymachine-downloads.b-cdn.net/images/Amazing_Diet_Secret.jpg
- **Opening:** *"My name is Nancy Pryor. I am 35 years old. I am a housewife and mother of three children. I would like to tell you something personal about myself. One day last September I just broke down and started to cry."*

**Breakdown**
- **Hook:** curiosity ("secret") + a relatable persona ("desperate housewife").
- **Pain with numbers:** "the scales said I weighed 188 pounds," list of every diet she'd failed.
- **Proof:** she's a peer, not an expert. Specific before/after (75 lbs, size 18 → 6).
- **Objection-killing subheads:** NO HUNGER · NO EXERCISE · HEALTHY AND SAFE · SIMPLE AND EASY.
- **Mechanism teased, never revealed:** "four reasons this diet works so well."
- **Risk reversal:** "postdate your check by 30 days… I will not deposit it."

**Steal:**
1. First-person story in the first 3 lines — this IS the UGC ad format.
2. One subhead per objection. A skimmer gets the whole case from the subheads.
3. Specific numbers everywhere.
4. A risk reversal bigger than "30-day refund."

## 3. The Dollar Bill Letter

- Real $1 bill attached to the top. Opening: *"As you can see I have attached a nice, crisp dollar bill to the top of this letter. Why have I done this? Actually there are two reasons:"*
- **Read it:** http://www.copywriting1.com/2007/10/gary-halbert-dollar-bill-letter.html
- **Steal:** a pattern interrupt ("grabber") — *always* paired with an explicit reason why it's there. In ads: the weird first frame. In fulfilment: a surprising insert in the box.

## 4. Tova Borgnine Perfume

- Headline: *"Mrs. Ernest Borgnine Agrees To Give Away 100,000 Free Samples Of Her New Perfume Just To Prove It Is Safe To Wear In Public!"* — she "swears under oath" it contains no illegal sexual stimulant.
- **Steal: deny the benefit.** It never claims to be an aphrodisiac — it plants the idea by denying it. Safer claim, way more curiosity.
- Breakdown: https://gameofconversions.com/gary-halbert-tova-perfume-ad/

## 5. The Boron Letters (1984) — free

Letters from federal prison to his son. https://www.thegaryhalbertletter.com/Boron/TChapter1.htm

- **Ch. 6 — The Starving Crowd.** *"The only advantage I want… is A STARVING CROWD!"* Market beats copy beats product. Judge hunger by **buying behaviour**, not demographics.
  → For you: target proven buyers (purchaser lookalikes, communities already spending in the niche, your own customers). Not "people who might like this."
- **Ch. 11 — A-pile vs B-pile.** People sort mail into personal (A) and obvious ads (B). Look like the A-pile.
  → For you: UGC that looks native to the feed. Emails that look like a friend wrote them.
- **His 7-step order** (from his newsletter):
  1. Get attention
  2. Show why they should be interested
  3. Show why they should believe it
  4. Prove it
  5. Itemize the benefits
  6. Tell them how to order
  7. Tell them to order NOW

# EUGENE SCHWARTZ

## 1. "How modern Chinese Medicine helps BURN DISEASE OUT OF YOUR BODY… lying flat on your back, using nothing more than the palm of your hand!" (1979/81)

- **Product:** *The Book of Internal Exercises*, $12.98.
- **Results:** [Folklore] "20-year control." [Author's claim] "We paid the author well over a million dollars" in royalties.
- **Read it:** full scan — https://swiped.co/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/schwartz-burn-disease-1981.png

**Breakdown**
- **Hook:** violent verb ("BURN") + absurdly easy mechanism ("lying flat on your back… palm of your hand"). Old promise (health), new mechanism.
- **Credibility:** author photo + credentials box. "New (although it is 4,000 years old)."
- **Proof by demonstration:** gives away one full exercise in the ad so you can try it right now.
- **Fascination bullets with page numbers:** "as shown… on page 59."
- **Guarantee:** "return the book at any time, for every cent of your money back."

**Steal:**
1. Put the **mechanism** in the headline — the "how."
2. Give a free taste of the result inside the ad.
3. Bullets that tease specific results ("How to ___ — page 59").
4. Schwartz said he wrote only 7 paragraphs — the rest was the author's own words. Mine your reviews and supplier info; don't invent.

## 2. "DO YOU HAVE THE COURAGE TO EARN HALF A MILLION DOLLARS A YEAR?" (1967)

- **Read it:** https://swiped.co/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Schwartz-Courage-Ad-0001.png
- **Opening:** *"This is a private advertisement. It is meant for the tremendously ambitious man only."*
- **Steal:** a headline that **dares** the reader. Copy that *repels* the wrong buyer and flatters the right one. Numbered benefit modules. A "you do NOT need X" objection killer.

## 3. "FOOD IS YOUR BEST MEDICINE" (1968)

- **Read it:** https://swiped.co/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/schwartz-food-medicine-1968_.png
- **Steal:** a pre-head that runs into the headline. Celebrity testimonial box. "How to use this" section that makes the reader imagine owning it already.

## 4. Boardroom Reports: "NOW! READ 300 BUSINESS MAGAZINES IN 30 MINUTES!"

- **Read it:** https://swiped.co/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/schwartz-boardroom-direct-mail-book-summaries.png
- **Steal:** number-contrast headline (300 vs 30). Curiosity bullets that ask a question the reader can't answer.

## 5. *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966) — the theory you need most

**Mass desire.** *"Copy cannot create desire for a product. It can only take the hopes, dreams, fears and desires that already exist in the hearts of millions of people, and focus those already-existing desires onto a particular product."*
→ You don't make people want things. You find the desire already there and point it at your product.

**5 Levels of Awareness** — decides how you open:

| Level | They know… | Your opening |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Most aware | your product, want it | Name + deal. "50% off ends tonight." |
| 2. Product aware | your product, not convinced | Proof, new angle, new mechanism |
| 3. Solution aware | the result they want, not your product | Lead with the result, then reveal the product |
| 4. Problem aware | they have a problem | Name the problem, twist the knife, product as the inevitable answer |
| 5. Unaware | nothing | Lead with an emotion, story or identity. Sell nothing up top. |

Cold traffic on Meta/TikTok = mostly levels 3–5. Most beginner ads are written as if the viewer is at level 1.

**5 Stages of Market Sophistication** — decides what you claim:

1. First to market → simple direct claim.
2. Competitors copy → bigger claim.
3. Claims stop working → **new mechanism** (a new way the old promise works).
4. Mechanisms copied → better mechanism (faster, easier, surer).
5. Prospect believes nothing → **identification** (who they are, not what it does).

**Viral dropship gadgets are almost always stage 3–5.** "Fixes your posture" is dead. "The 2-strap X-brace that trains your muscles to hold the position on their own" (mechanism) or "for people who sit 9 hours a day and refuse to look old at 30" (identity) is alive.

**"Copy is not written. Copy is assembled."** (attributed) — Read every review, every competitor ad, every supplier spec. Highlight every claim. Assemble the strongest ones.

# JOSEPH SUGARMAN — the dropshipper's copywriter

Sugarman sold gadgets via full-page magazine ads (JS&A). Same game as you: physical product, cold audience, one ad has to do the whole sale.

## The axioms (from *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook*)

- **#2:** All the elements of an ad exist to get you to read the first sentence.
- **#3:** *"The sole purpose of the first sentence in an advertisement is to get you to read the second sentence."* Keep it short, almost incomplete: "It's easy." "It had to happen."
- **#6 Slippery slide:** the reader should be unable to stop until the end.
- **Seeds of curiosity:** end paragraphs with a pull: "But there's more." "Let me explain." "Now here comes the good part."
- **#9:** *Never sell a product. Sell a concept.*
- **#11:** copy should be as long as it needs to be to cause the action.
- **#15:** a cure sells easier than a prevention — unless the prevention is framed as a cure.

**31 psychological triggers** (Ch. 19): involvement/ownership, honesty, integrity, credibility, value & proof of value, justify the purchase, greed, authority, satisfaction conviction, nature of product, prospect nature, current fads, timing, linking, consistency, harmonize, desire to belong, desire to collect, curiosity, urgency, fear, instant gratification, exclusivity, simplicity, human relationships, storytelling, mental engagement, guilt, specificity, familiarity, hope.

## 1. BluBlocker — "Vision Breakthrough" (1986–87)

- **Results:** [Author's claim] 20M+ pairs sold overall (mostly via later TV infomercials). Sugarman: the ad "started an entire business."
- **Read it:** https://swiped.co/file/vision-break-through-blublocker-ad-by-joe-sugarman/
- **Headline:** *Vision Breakthrough — When I put on the pair of glasses what I saw I could not believe. Nor will you.*
- **Opening:** *"I am about to tell you a true story. If you believe me, you will be well rewarded. If you don't believe me, I will make it worth your while to change your mind. Let me explain."*

**Breakdown**
- Headline never names the product. Pure curiosity + challenge ("Nor will you").
- Skeptical narrator discovery story: "I kept taking them off and putting them on."
- Educates about a hidden danger in what they already own (regular sunglasses let in blue/UV light).
- "DON'T BE CONFUSED" box handles "do they really work?"
- Guarantee framed as a test: compare against your old sunglasses, 30-day refund.

**Steal:** this is a UGC script. Skeptic tries it → can't believe it → explains why → dares you to test it.

## 2. Midex 55 — "Burglar Alarm Breakthrough" (1977–78)

- **Results:** [Author's claim] ran 3+ years; JS&A became one of the biggest burglar-alarm sellers in the US.
- **Headline:** *A new computerized burglar alarm requires no installation and protects your home or business like a thousand dollar professional system.*

**Breakdown**
- **No fear tactics.** Sugarman: *"Never did I try to scare the prospective customer with crime statistics."* He figured buyers wanted two things: it works, and it's easy. He wrote only for those.
- Proof: testing specs (military standard, run at 150°F for a week), astronaut testimonial (Wally Schirra), founder story ("after we were burglarized").
- Admits imperfection: *"No product is perfect, but judge for yourself."*
- Price anchor: "like a thousand dollar professional system" for $199.95.
- Order bump in the coupon: extra horns $39.95, "we recommend the purchase of two."

**Steal:** figure out the *nature of the product* — what does the buyer actually need reassured about? Anchor against the expensive alternative. Order bump.

## 3. "Pocket CB" (1975–77)

- **Results:** [Author's claim] 250,000+ units at $39.95.
- **Scan:** https://hackaday.com/2023/01/30/retro-gadgets-the-cb-cell-phone/
- **The move:** it was a walkie-talkie. CB radio was the craze. He called it "Pocket CB" and rode the fad. *Sell the concept.*
- Linking: "Remember the first time you saw a pocket calculator?"
- Multi-unit built in: "Order only two units on a trial basis" — a walkie-talkie needs a pair.

**Steal:** what's your product called in *this year's* trend language? Can you build a 2-pack into the logic?

## 4. The Price-Increase Ad

- Sugarman sold a calculator at $49.95; the manufacturer forced him to $69.95. He ran a *small* ad announcing his "error" and giving a few days at the old price. It out-pulled the full-page ad.
- **Steal:** a real price increase is the best urgency there is. **Must be genuine** — fake countdowns get you in trouble with the FTC and ad platforms.

# CLAUDE HOPKINS (full texts in `02-full-texts.md`)

*Scientific Advertising* (1923), free: https://archive.org/details/scientific-advertising

| Principle | His line |
|---|---|
| Ads exist to sell | "The only purpose of advertising is to make sales." |
| Nobody cares about you | "They care nothing about your interest or your profit." |
| Long copy | "The more you tell the more you sell." |
| Headlines | "It is not uncommon for a change in headlines to multiply returns from five to ten times over." |
| Specificity | "Platitudes and generalities roll off the human understanding like water from a duck." |
| Not entertainment | "Ads are not written to entertain." |
| Sampling | "The product itself should be its own best salesman." |
| Testing | "Go to the court of last resort – the buyers of your product." |
| Cure > prevention | "People will do anything to cure a trouble, but little to prevent it." |

The pieces:
- **Schlitz** — told the process every brewer used but nobody described. "I gave purity a meaning." ("Steam-cleaned bottles" is folklore — the real ads say washed four times / sterilized.)
- **Puffed Wheat** — "Foods shot from guns." Failures until he found the curiosity angle. [Documented in the ad] "a million packages monthly."
- **Pepsodent** — invented "the film" and had you feel it with your tongue. [Author's claim] made him $1M personally.
- **Palmolive** — $700 test in one small town, scaled only once repeat sales paid the ad bill.

# THE OTHERS

## John Caples — "They Laughed When I Sat Down at the Piano" (1925–27)
Full text in `02-full-texts.md`. Cliffhanger story headline; the subheads tell the story; ad embedded inside the story. Template for you: *"My wife laughed when I ordered a $29 ___ — until…"*

## Max Sackheim — "Do You Make These Mistakes in English?" (~1921, ran 40+ years)
Full text in `02-full-texts.md`. [Documented] ~3x the response of the alternative headline. Template: *"Do you make these 5 mistakes when ___?"*

## Martin Conroy — Wall Street Journal "Two Young Men" (1975)
- [Documented] in use 28 years (1975–2003). [Claimed] ~$2B in subscriptions.
- Opening: *"On a beautiful late spring afternoon, twenty-five years ago, two young men graduated from the same college. They were very much alike, these two young men."*
- Read: https://copychief.com/a-2-billion-story-in-11-sentences/
- **Steal:** two identical people, different outcomes — the product is the hidden difference. Let the reader reach the conclusion before you name the product. Use the P.S. as a second CTA.

## David Ogilvy — Rolls-Royce (1958)
- Headline: *"At 60 miles an hour the loudest noise in this new Rolls-Royce comes from the electric clock."*
- Image: https://swiped.co/rolls-royce-ad-by-david-ogilvy
- The headline is a quote from a car magazine's technical editor — borrowed authority. Body = a numbered list of hard facts, zero adjectives. Price stated confidently at the end.
- **Steal:** one hyper-specific sensory fact beats "quiet." Numbered facts = your product page bullets.

## Gary Bencivenga
- **The lesson worth the price of admission:** *"Why the price of silver **may** rise steeply"* beat *"…**will** rise steeply"* — reportedly by ~200%. **Believability beats hype.**
- Fascination bullets — his Rodale "Vest-Pocket Sampler" was built on 174 of them: https://www.verygoodcopy.com/bencivenga-bullets
- ("Is the Bible Right About Disease?" — couldn't be traced to him. Skip.)

## Robert Collier (1930s)
- Attributed: *"Always enter the conversation already taking place in the customer's mind."*
- → Open with what they're already thinking about right now (the heat wave, back pain after a long shift, a trend), not with your product.

## Victor Schwab
- 5 steps: attention → show an advantage → prove it → persuade them to grab it → ask for action.
- From his "100 Good Headlines": *"Hands That Look Lovelier in 24 Hours — Or Your Money Back"* (promise + time frame + guarantee in one headline). List: https://lindfieldlife.co.uk/lindfield-blog/100-good-advertising-headlines-victor-schwab
- He key-coded every coupon to test. Today = UTMs + a discount code per ad.
