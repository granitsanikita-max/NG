# The Fundamentals + Your Scorecard

Everything from the masters, boiled down. Then the scorecard your copy gets graded on.

---

## The 12 fundamentals

### 1. Market first, copy second (Halbert, Schwartz)
You can't write desire into people. Find a hungry crowd with a desire that already exists, then point it at your product. If the product/market is wrong, no copy fixes it.

### 2. Know their awareness level (Schwartz)
Cold TikTok/Meta traffic hasn't heard of you. Open with their problem, their desire or their identity — not your product name and a discount.

### 3. Know how jaded the market is (Schwartz)
If they've seen 50 ads for this product type, a plain claim is dead. Lead with a **new mechanism** (how it works differently) or **identity** (who it's for).

### 4. The headline/hook does 80% of the work (Hopkins, Caples)
Changing the headline can multiply results 5–10x. Write 20 hooks before you choose one. Hook types that won for the masters:
- **Story / cliffhanger** — "They laughed when I sat down at the piano…"
- **Self-diagnosis** — "Do you make these mistakes…?"
- **Curiosity + persona** — "The amazing diet secret of a desperate housewife"
- **Mechanism** — "…lying flat on your back, using nothing more than the palm of your hand"
- **Specific sensory fact** — "the loudest noise… comes from the electric clock"
- **Dare / identity** — "Do you have the courage to…?"
- **Personal** — "Did you know your family name…?"
- **Numbers contrast** — "Read 300 magazines in 30 minutes"

### 5. Every line's job is to get the next line read (Sugarman)
Short first sentence. Seeds of curiosity at the end of paragraphs. If a line doesn't pull them forward, cut it.

### 6. Specific beats general, every time (Hopkins, Ogilvy)
"Great quality" = invisible. "Run at 150°F for a week before it ships" = believed. Use numbers, times, names, places.

### 7. Show the mechanism (Hopkins, Schwartz)
"Foods shot from guns." "The film on your teeth." Give the reader a picture of *how* it works so the result feels inevitable.

### 8. Proof > promises (everyone)
In order of strength: demonstration they can try themselves (Pepsodent tongue test) → specific results with numbers → named customers → expert/authority → numbers of buyers. Believability beats hype ("may" beat "will").

### 9. Kill every objection out loud (Halbert, Caples)
List every reason they'd *not* buy. Kill each one in one line. Subheads that each kill one objection are gold.

### 10. Sell the outcome / concept, not the object (Sugarman, Hopkins)
Not soap — beauty. Not a walkie-talkie — a "Pocket CB." Sell the cure, not the prevention.

### 11. Reverse the risk harder than competitors (Halbert, Sugarman)
"30-day money back" is the minimum. "Compare it to what you own now — if it's not dramatically better, send it back." Make the guarantee a challenge.

### 12. Make the action easy and urgent — honestly (Sackheim, Sugarman)
One clear CTA. A real reason to act now (genuine price rise, real stock limit, a bundle that ends). Fake urgency costs trust and gets ads flagged.

## Formulas to write from

**Halbert's 7 steps (long copy / product page / advertorial)**
Attention → why care → why believe → proof → benefits → how to order → order now.

**Schwab's 5 steps (shorter ads)**
Attention → advantage → proof → persuade → action.

**UGC video script (Sugarman BluBlocker structure)**
1. Hook (skeptic, curiosity, or problem — 0–3 sec)
2. "I didn't believe it…" / problem story
3. The moment it worked (demo on camera)
4. Why it works (mechanism in one line)
5. Objection kill ("and no, it's not ___")
6. Offer + guarantee as a dare
7. CTA

## SCORECARD — how your copy gets graded

When you paste copy, include: **product · price · who it's for · platform (Meta / TikTok / product page / email) · cold or warm traffic.**

Each category gets a score from 0 to 10, a one-line verdict, and the exact fix (with a rewritten line).

| # | Category | What a 10 looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | Stops the scroll. Specific. Creates a curiosity gap or names a real pain or identity. Could not be about any other product. |
| 2 | **Awareness match** | The opening fits how much a cold viewer actually knows. |
| 3 | **Sophistication match** | Has a mechanism or identity angle, not a claim they've heard 50 times. |
| 4 | **Slippery slide** | Every line pulls you to the next. No dead sentences, no throat-clearing. |
| 5 | **Specificity** | Numbers, details, sensory facts. Zero filler adjectives ("amazing," "high-quality," "game-changer"). |
| 6 | **Mechanism** | The reader can picture *why* it works. |
| 7 | **Proof** | Believable evidence, ideally something they can verify themselves. |
| 8 | **Objections** | The top 3 reasons not to buy are named and killed. |
| 9 | **Offer + risk reversal** | Clear price/value, a guarantee strong enough to feel like a dare. |
| 10 | **CTA + urgency** | One clear action, an honest reason to do it now. |

**Total /100.** Under 50 = rewrite from the hook. 50–70 = fix the 2–3 weakest categories. 70+ = ready to test. 85+ = test it against your current control.

### Beginner red flags (instant point losses)
- Opening with the brand or product name to cold traffic
- "Introducing…" / "Are you tired of…" / "Game-changer"
- Adjectives instead of facts
- Features listed with no "so that…" benefit
- No mechanism — just "it works"
- Claims the reader has heard 50 times
- 3 CTAs, or no CTA
- Fake scarcity ("only 3 left!" on an unlimited product)
- Writing *about* the product instead of *to* one person
