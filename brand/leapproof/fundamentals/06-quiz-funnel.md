## The job of this page
**Where it sits.** Ad → quiz → reveal → checkout (→ ① Universal 4.2). The quiz replaces the presell and the PDP's decision work in one flow.
**When to use it.**
- High-variant catalogs where choice paralysis kills the sale (shade, scent, size, recipe): Jones Road, Native, Dr. Squatch, Caraway.
- Commodities you want to reframe as a personalized plan, with the price held back until effort has been invested: The Farmer's Dog, Ollie.
- Regulated or sensitive categories where "eligibility" is the frame: Hims, Hers.
- Education-heavy or newer categories (AG1, Seed, Javy run quiz or advertorial gates).
**The one point.** Make the reader believe the recommendation was built for them, so buying feels like accepting their own answer. Pick the secondary jobs on purpose: capture the email, segment by belief and pain for follow-up, raise AOV with a routine.
**What a quiz is not.** It is not "take our quiz." The quiz is the vehicle, never the pitch. Every CTA promises the outcome.
**Evidence base.** Seven brands were walked live on a phone-sized browser on Oct 6, 2026: The Farmer's Dog, Ollie (all the way to checkout), Hims (two intakes), Caraway (two quizzes), Jones Road, Dr. Squatch and Native. The Farmer's Dog reveal and the Hims reveal were not seen, because both sit behind a hard gate. Their price evidence comes from their landing pages.
## Fundamentals
### 1. The CTA promises the result or the price
- **Rule:** Name the output on every button that starts the quiz. Never "take a quiz."
- **Why it works:** "See plans & pricing" feels like comparison shopping. "Take a quiz" feels like homework.
- **Weak:** "Take Our Skin Quiz" *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "Find My Routine + Price in 2 Minutes" *(illustrative)*
- **Evidence:** "See plans & pricing" (The Farmer's Dog). "Get 70% Off Your First Box" (Ollie). "See if I'm eligible" (Hims). "FIND MY SHADE" (Jones Road). "MEET YOUR MATCH." (Native).
### 2. State the time cost up front
- **Rule:** Tell them how long it takes before the first question. Three minutes is the standard.
- **Why it works:** An unknown length is a reason not to start. A small, known cost makes starting easy, and finishing feels like keeping a promise.
- **Weak:** A "Start" button with no hint of length. *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "TAKES 3 MINUTES" on the intro badge (Ollie)
- **Evidence:** "This will only take \~3 minutes per dog." (The Farmer's Dog). "Estimated time: 3 minutes." (Caraway coffee quiz). "120s Average quiz time" (Jones Road).
### 3. One question per screen, big tap targets, auto-advance
- **Rule:** One question fills the screen. Single-select answers advance on tap. On multi-select, keep Next disabled until an answer exists.
- **Why it works:** Each tap is a tiny yes with no decision about scrolling. Momentum builds, and stopping halfway feels like wasting the taps already made.
- **Weak:** Ten fields on one long form. *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "What sex is Biscuit?" Female / Male, and the tap moves straight on (Ollie)
- **Evidence:** Ollie, Hims, Dr. Squatch, Native and Jones Road auto-advance. Caraway and Jones Road disable Next until an answer is picked.
### 4. Visible, honest progress
- **Rule:** Show progress on every screen, ideally as named chapters. If branching adds steps, let the count grow honestly.
- **Why it works:** Chapters make a 19-step quiz feel like three easy parts. An honest count keeps trust when the quiz runs long.
- **Weak:** No progress indicator at all (Hims shows none). **Strong:** SKIN PROFILE / SHADE DISCOVERY / YOUR PREFERENCES chapters, with a counter that grew from 17 to 19 as branches added steps (Jones Road)
- **Evidence:** "Me (1/4)" chapters (The Farmer's Dog). "STEP X OF 6" plus a fill bar (Ollie). A percent counter (Caraway coffee quiz).
### 5. Q1 is the easiest and most personal
- **Rule:** Open with a question anyone can answer in one second, about themselves or the one they're buying for.
- **Why it works:** Zero friction gets the first tap. Talking about yourself feels good and starts the investment.
- **Weak:** "What's your budget?" as question 1. *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "What's your dog's name?" (Ollie)
- **Evidence:** "Do you leave the toilet seat up?" (Dr. Squatch). "How many cups of coffee do you drink a day?" (Caraway). "What's Your Skin Type?" (Jones Road).
### 6. Answer options are copy
- **Rule:** Write options in the customer's own words. Include at least one confession option. Plant your product traits or mechanism inside the options.
- **Why it works:** The options are where the selling happens, and most frameworks ignore them. A confession creates a small guilt the product fixes. A planted trait makes the result feel like it came from the user.
- **Weak:** "Sometimes / Often / Never" *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "Honestly, when I remember. Which isn't that often." / "I didn't know I was supposed to clean it." (Caraway, on cleaning the coffee maker)
- **Evidence:** "COMBO: My skin is a mix - never just one thing" (Jones Road). Dr. Squatch plants scent notes inside a playlist question: "Slow jams… warm vanilla, amber."
### 7. The reveal plays the inputs back
- **Rule:** The result names their specifics and puts the buy button right next to them. Route answers to products properly, and never let an input default pass silently.
- **Why it works:** Hearing your own details is the proof the plan is yours. Playback accuracy carries more weight than routing precision. Dr. Squatch mapped pine, bourbon and cabin answers to a citrus and marine result, and the flattering label still carried it.
- **Weak:** Caraway's home quiz ignored the answers "Myself and a partner." and "I balance quality with value. It's worth it if it lasts." and showed a 10-product grid. **Strong:** "We created a meal plan just for Biscuit—a 30-pound active pup—so he gets the perfect portions to stay healthy, happy, and thriving." (Ollie)
- **Evidence:** "ABOUT YOUR MATCH. Selected just for you based on your quiz answers:" with "✓ SKIN TYPE: Combo" (Jones Road). The risk: after a Neutral answer, Jones Road matched a pencil "for light-medium skin with cool undertones," and its shade bar defaulted to VERY PALE and accepted it on NEXT. Mismatches turn into returns.
### 8. Proof is timed to the drop-off points
- **Rule:** Put proof inside the quiz where people quit, not only on the reveal. Choose each card for one job.
- **Why it works:** People quit mid-to-late, when effort is spent and nothing has come back yet. Proof at that moment renews the reason to finish.
- **Weak:** No proof until the reveal (Dr. Squatch, Native). **Strong:** Nothing on Q1 to Q3, then "2.5 more years together" on Q4 and "90% see positive changes" on Q6, right before the reveal (Ollie)
- **Evidence:** Hims alternates question, stat card, question, stat card: "1 in 7 adults have taken a GLP-1 for weight loss", then "79% of Hims customers say they feel more confident…"
### 9. A reward for finishing
- **Rule:** Attach a discount, gift or perk to completing the quiz, and frame it as already given.
- **Why it works:** A gift already "given" is something to lose by leaving. It also turns the effort into a fair trade.
- **Weak:** A discount that only appears at checkout. *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "You've been gifted 50% off your first purchase!" in the top bar before the quiz starts (The Farmer's Dog)
- **Evidence:** Ollie 70% off plus a free gift. Dr. Squatch 20% on the bundle. Caraway \$50 off the second purchase. Jones Road free shipping over \$55.
### 10. The quiz itself must be credible
- **Rule:** In categories where people distrust online matching, sell the quiz before it starts. Treat the quiz as a product with its own objections.
- **Why it works:** If the buyer doubts the matching, every result is suspect. Answering that doubt first makes the reveal believable.
- **Weak:** A quiz page that only says "Start the Quiz." *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "16M+ Quizzes Taken / 90% Shade match accuracy / 4.5 Average star rating / 120s Average quiz time" (Jones Road)
- **Evidence:** Jones Road quiz reviews: "I was nervous about trusting the website's quiz to pick the right one, but I really am happy with it." and "Usually these quizzes don't work for me." Plus Bobbi Brown's credentials on the quiz landing page.
### 11. Price waits for the plan, then shows up small and honest
- **Rule:** Show no price until the reveal. Confirm any discount before the number appears. Show the price in the smallest honest unit with the original anchored (→ ① Universal 3.1). State the renewal price as a dollar figure near the button (→ ① Universal 3.3). That last part is the honesty floor.
- **Why it works:** The price meets a plan that already feels like theirs. A buried renewal shows up on the bank statement as a surprise, and surprises become chargebacks.
- **Weak:** "From \$149/mo†" with "\$39 for the first month, auto-renews at \$149/month thereafter" only in a footnote (Hims). Ollie's checkout says only "This order has a recurring charge." **Strong:** The loader says "Success! 70% off and free gift applied" before any number, then the reveal shows "~~\$48.71~~ \$14.61/Week" (Ollie). Add the renewal line in dollars that Ollie leaves out.
- **Evidence:** The Farmer's Dog promises a "daily price" on its intro. Caraway shows "4 payments of \$124".
## Techniques
### Zone 1: Entry
- **Need-state picker that rewrites the quiz intro.** 4 or 5 problems on the homepage. Each choice sets the intro headline over the same quiz (→ ① Universal 3.5: modular hero).
	- Swipe: "Build a plan for your dog's needs. Tell us what matters most right now." Weight loss / Picky eating / Senior longevity / Puppy growth / I have multiple dogs → "Better food for picky eaters starts here" / "Better food for more good years starts here" (The Farmer's Dog)
	- **Good for →** message match inside the funnel.
	- **When to use →** whenever ads run on different pains.
- **Personalization preview.** Show the personal result on the homepage before the quiz starts.
	- Swipe: "Bella\| isn't average. Their food shouldn't be either." (Ollie, the name types out with a blinking cursor)
	- **Good for →** pet, kid, or any product bought for a named someone.
	- **When to use →** homepage hero of a quiz-led store.
- **Many doors, one intake.** Every product card's button opens the same quiz, with an optional pre-quiz question.
	- Swipe: "See if I'm eligible", then a bottom sheet: "Do you have a treatment in mind already?" GLP-1 pills / GLP-1 injections / "I'd like a provider recommendation" (Hims)
	- **Good for →** catalogs where the right product depends on the buyer.
	- **When to use →** once the quiz converts better than the PDP. Asking the pre-quiz question again inside the quiz, as Hims does, reinforces the choice.
- **Quiz in the hero, with forward-only answers.** Two or three questions on the landing page itself, where every answer moves on.
	- Swipe: "Do you want to boost your confidence in the bedroom? Yes / I'm not sure." then "Good news: You've got options." (Hims)
	- **Good for →** sensitive categories where a full quiz feels like too much at first.
	- **When to use →** as the hero of a lander, before the full intake.
- **Quiz landing page that sells the quiz.** Answer distrust of online matching before question 1.
	- Swipe: "You're in the Right Place If… You're tired of guessing your shade online…" + "DRAG TO REVEAL" before/after sliders (Jones Road)
	- **Good for →** shade, fit, dose and any match people have been burned on.
	- **When to use →** when distrust is high. Include an accuracy stat, the number of quizzes taken, 2 or 3 reviews about the quiz, founder or expert credentials, a 3-step "how it works", and the "you're in the right place if" list.
- **Two-step popup as a mini-quiz.** → ① Universal 3.6 owns it. On a quiz store it doubles as the on-ramp.
	- Swipe: "You've Got \$50 Off Your Second Purchase\*. To claim your offer, tell us what you're shopping for:" (Caraway). "UNLOCK FREE SHIPPING… WHAT IS YOUR SKIN TYPE?" (Jones Road).
	- **Good for →** sites without a full quiz, or as a quiz on-ramp. A second-purchase discount buys the email without cutting first-order margin (→ ① Universal 3.1).
	- **When to use →** fire it on quiz entry pages, and pass the answer into Q1.
- **Quiz named for its output, in brand voice.**
	- Swipe: "Perfect Portion Quiz" (Ollie). "Take our scent personality quiz (it's like therapy, but cheaper)" (Dr. Squatch). "How Clean is Your Coffee Routine?" (Caraway).
	- **Good for →** making the quiz sound like a result, not a task.
	- **When to use →** every quiz name, badge and nav link.
### Zone 2: Intro screen
- **Value preview.** List exactly what they will get.
	- Swipe: "Keep Going to Find Your Ideal: 1 COMPLEXION PRODUCTS, 2 SHADE MATCHES, 3 EVERYDAY ROUTINE." (Jones Road). "Let's determine your meal plan, recommended recipes, and daily price!" (The Farmer's Dog).
	- **Good for →** making the effort feel worth it.
	- **When to use →** always, on the first screen, with the headline matched to the entry angle.
- **Effort cue plus trust row.** Size the effort, then show why to trust the result.
	- Swipe: "This will only take \~3 minutes per dog." + "Real food / Ready-to-serve / Convenient deliveries" + TODAY, Fast Company and Vogue logos (The Farmer's Dog)
	- **Good for →** cold traffic meeting the brand for the first time.
	- **When to use →** the intro screen, under the CTA. Three icons or logos, no more.
### Zone 3: Question design
- **"Why we ask" line per field.** One line that turns a personal question into an input for a calculation.
	- Swipe: "Sex affects metabolism and calorie needs" / "Spayed and neutered pups need less calories." (Ollie). "This helps us fine-tune tips for what your skin needs right now." (Jones Road). "Responses prior to account creation will not be used as part of your medical assessment." (Hims).
	- **Good for →** making personal fields feel like inputs, not data harvesting.
	- **When to use →** every question about age, weight, sex, health or money.
- **Mad-libs sentence form.** The visitor fills in a sentence about themselves.
	- Swipe: "I have \[ 0 \] dogs… dog named \[ enter pet's name \]" / "My first name is \[ ___ \] and I \[ select ▾ \] that fresh food is the healthiest for Biscuit." (The Farmer's Dog)
	- **Good for →** a conversational feel, and hiding a belief question in small talk.
	- **When to use →** the warm-up section.
- **Belief question as data.** → ① Universal 3.6 owns it. In a quiz, ask it in the warm-up and route the reveal copy and the emails by the answer.
	- Swipe: "already believe / am not yet sure / don't believe" (The Farmer's Dog). "Have you tried treatment before?" (Hims). "Have you cooked with us before?" (Caraway, new vs existing customer).
	- **Good for →** education-heavy products and stores with returning buyers.
	- **When to use →** one question in the warm-up. Store the answer.
- **Name used everywhere, pronoun matched.**
	- Swipe: "What sex is Biscuit?" → "Tell us his ideal weight—we'll help him get there." (Ollie)
	- **Good for →** any quiz that collects a name.
	- **When to use →** every question after the name is given.
- **Fun answer labels.**
	- Swipe: "Couch Potato" / "Walk Enthusiast" / "Zoomie Machine" (Ollie). "Yes, it's called efficiency" / "No, I was raised with fear and respect" (Dr. Squatch).
	- **Good for →** low-stakes categories where entertainment drives completion and sharing.
	- **When to use →** lifestyle and identity questions. Keep health or money questions plain.
- **Decision rule inside the question.** Tell them which answer to pick when they're unsure (→ ① Universal 3.3: decision rules).
	- Swipe: "If your pup is between levels, choose higher level." (Ollie)
	- **Good for →** any question with borderline answers.
	- **When to use →** under the options, as microcopy.
- **Price-sensitivity question that doesn't feel cheap.**
	- Swipe: "When choosing new items for your kitchen, how do you factor in your budget?" … "I balance quality with value. It's worth it if it lasts." (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** wide catalogs with several price tiers.
	- **When to use →** late in the diagnosis, phrased so no answer is embarrassing.
- **Slider with a live verdict.** The feedback line rewrites as the slider moves, and every position ends at the same pitch.
	- Swipe: "Almost every day: Coffee shop loyalty noted. But if your go-to order is \$5–7 a cup, that's potentially \$1,500–2,500 a year. A great home setup starts looking pretty compelling." (Caraway. Every position, even "Never", ends at "your machine matters.")
	- **Good for →** cost-of-inaction math, delivered as feedback.
	- **When to use →** usage-frequency questions.
- **Practical method inside a question.** Give the user a real check to do.
	- Swipe: "TIP: Pull up a photo of yourself on your phone and hold it near these swatches." (Jones Road)
	- **Good for →** making the result believable because the user did the check.
	- **When to use →** shade, size or fit questions people get wrong.
- **Reference faces that match the inputs.** Example photos that change to look like the user.
	- Swipe: Jones Road's shade scale showed one young woman, then three women aged about 40 to 60 once the user picked 35-44 and Light Medium.
	- **Good for →** visual matching across ages, skin tones or body types.
	- **When to use →** any question answered by picking from photos.
- **Branching that creates the cross-sell.** Branch on problem answers so each added step earns a specific product later.
	- Swipe: a Dark Circles answer added "What Color Shows Up Under Your Eyes?", which later justified the Neutralizer Pencil recommendation (Jones Road).
	- **Good for →** routines and multi-product catalogs.
	- **When to use →** on every concern that maps to a product you sell.
- **Diagnostic arc that ends in an implied cost.** Fear fact, then confession, then cost math, then the pain named, then a scored verdict with one fix.
	- Swipe: "Think you know what goes into your daily brew?" → "¼ cup of plastic" fact → "Honestly, when I remember" → "\$1,500–2,500 a year" → "What frustrates you most…" → "You've Got Room to Brew" (Caraway coffee quiz)
	- **Good for →** unaware or barely problem-aware buyers.
	- **When to use →** when the product fixes a problem the buyer hasn't noticed yet.
- **Last question names the pain.** The final act before the reveal is stating their problem.
	- Swipe: "What frustrates you most about your current coffee routine?" then the CTA "See My Results" (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** every quiz.
	- **When to use →** as the last diagnostic question.
### Zone 4: Interstitials

	- **Good for →** keeping people moving through the middle of the quiz.
	- **When to use →** every 2 or 3 screens. The strongest card goes right before the gate or the reveal. Every stat is cited.

- **Answer-reactive screen.** The next screen responds to the specific answer.
	- Swipe: "You've got it. With our wide range of options, we'll help you find a match for your needs." (Hims)
	- **Good for →** proving the quiz is listening.
	- **When to use →** after any choice between product types, followed by a short comparison.

	- **When to use →** before the gate, and again before the reveal with their name.

- **Gate framed as results delivery.** Promise the thing they just worked for, never marketing.
	- Swipe: "Where Should We Send Your Results?" (Jones Road). The weakest framing seen: "Want to stay in touch?… latest product drops, sales, and more." (Native)
	- **Good for →** every gate except the legal one.
	- **When to use →** always. The gate's promise sets the open rate of email 1.
- **Gate inside the sentence.** The email field is the next blank in the mad-libs sentence, with a utility line under it.
	- Swipe: "My email address is \[ enter email \]" + "You can use your email to access your recommendations anytime." (The Farmer's Dog)
	- **Good for →** early, hard gates.
	- **When to use →** when you gate before the reveal and the quiz already uses sentence form.

	- Swipe: "What's Your Name? This makes it easier to save and revisit your recommendations later." NEXT / SKIP (Jones Road)
	- **Good for →** late, soft gates and personalized summaries.
	- **When to use →** the step right before the email ask.
- **Email 1 matches the gate.** Write email 1 before the gate copy, then make the gate promise exactly what email 1 delivers.
	- Swipe: "access your recommendations anytime" (The Farmer's Dog). "shade matches along with application tips" (Jones Road).
	- **Good for →** every quiz that captures an email.
	- **When to use →** before launch. Route later emails by the belief answer and the pain answer.
### Zone 6: Loader and anticipation
- **Anticipation screen.** A deliberate pause before the reveal, stacked with proof.
	- Swipe: "Are You Ready to See Your Results? Your custom shade match is ready. Let's take a look." + "Over 75,000 5-Star Reviews" + press logos (Jones Road)
	- **Good for →** building suspense so the reveal lands as an event.
	- **When to use →** the screen right before the loader or the reveal.
- **Labor-illusion loader that sells during the wait.** Status lines that name real work, plus the discount, the guarantee and the expert.
	- Swipe: "Success! 70% off and free gift applied" / "Curating your pup's recipes and portions, building your Welcome Kit" / "Adding our health check-ins and vet support" (Ollie). "Analyzing your profile across 5 products to find the ones for you." + founder card + "SHADE MATCH GUARANTEE" (Jones Road).
	- **Good for →** making the result feel computed, confirming the discount before any price, and removing risk with the guarantee and the expert.
	- **When to use →** 4 to 8 seconds, right before the reveal.
### Zone 7: Reveal and offer
- **Identity label or scored verdict.** A flattering name for who they are, or a gentle score.
	- Swipe: "The Kitchen Upgrader" (Caraway). "YOU ARE: THE LOW-KEY LEGEND" (Dr. Squatch). "The Earthy Escapist" (Native, with SHARE). "You've Got Room to Brew" with a 1-of-3 score (Caraway coffee quiz).
	- **Good for →** shareability and self-concept. People buy into an identity more easily than a product.
	- **When to use →** the top of every reveal. Use gentle, no-shame verdicts for diagnostics.
- **"Built for you because" playback.** A checklist of their answers, then a "why this works" paragraph that cites them.
	- Swipe: "ABOUT YOUR MATCH. Selected just for you based on your quiz answers:" then "WHY THIS WORKS: Your light-medium coverage preference pairs perfectly with our What The Foundation…" (Jones Road)
	- **Good for →** making the recommendation feel computed from their inputs.
	- **When to use →** directly under the identity label, above the price.
- **One hero product, pre-selected, then the routine.** A prescription reads confident. A grid reads like a catalog.
	- Swipe: "Your Match: WHAT THE FOUNDATION, Beige…" then "Other Matches To Round Out Your Routine… designed to help address your dark circles and redness." (Jones Road). Ollie pre-checks "Full Fresh Plan" with a "RECOMMENDED" badge and three recipes.
	- **Good for →** every reveal. The defaults are the decision, since most "choice" is accepting what was preset.
	- **When to use →** one hero per outcome. Each cross-sell is justified by a concern they chose. If you run a free-shipping threshold, price the hero just under it, as Jones Road does ("ADD TO CART \$46. Free shipping on US orders over \$55.") (→ ① Universal 3.1: spend-threshold meter).
- **Discount tied to accepting the recommendation.**
	- Swipe: "Add to cart for 20% off!" (Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** stores that give a quiz reward.
	- **When to use →** on the hero product's button, so the reward exists only if they take the match.
- **Trust row at the button plus quiz-accuracy reviews.**
	- Swipe: "Obsession guaranteed / Flexible delivery / Pause or cancel anytime" (Ollie). "TRUST THE COLOR MATCH QUIZ!" review header (Jones Road).
	- **Good for →** the last doubts: "will it work" and "will the match be right."
	- **When to use →** beside the button. Quiz-accuracy reviews first, then outcome reviews.
- **Real scarcity on the reveal.** → ① Universal 3.4 owns true urgency. On a reveal, show real stock and real ship dates as they are.
	- Swipe: "Lamb w/ Cranberries" marked "OUT OF STOCK" with "Notify Me" (Ollie). "Estimated Ship Date: 12/18" (Caraway).
	- **Good for →** signalling demand without a timer.
	- **When to use →** only when the stock or date is real.
- **Human fallback.** → ① Universal 3.3 owns it. On a reveal it catches people who still don't trust the match.
	- Swipe: "Still not sure? Send a no make-up selfie to get matched by the experts." (Jones Road)
	- **Good for →** shade, fit, dose decisions.
	- **When to use →** under the guarantee on the reveal.
- **Save and share.** Optional, ungated capture plus a share button (save-your-results capture → ① Universal 3.6).
	- Swipe: "Save Your Results. Enter your email to receive a copy of your quiz results…" (Caraway). SHARE button on the persona page (Native).
	- **Good for →** ungated quizzes and gift buyers.
	- **When to use →** below the offer, never in place of the buy button.
- **Result pages as permanent links.** Give each persona or result its own URL.
	- Swipe: Caraway's persona slugs `/pages/90s-baby` and `/pages/room-to-brew`. Jones Road's result carries `?variant=`. Native's persona pages work as shoppable category pages.
	- **Good for →** sharing, retargeting and email links straight back to "your" result.
	- **When to use →** once the result types are stable.
### Zone 8: Post-reveal steps before checkout
① Universal 3.1 owns the down-sell inside the decision, choose-your-gift and the order bump at the same discount. In a quiz, place them like this.
- **Down-sell on the reveal.** A cheaper yes, shown below the recommended plan.
	- Swipe: "Half Fresh Plan. From ~~\$30.87~~ \$9.26/week. Not ready to go all in? Try a half-serving of fresh instead!" (Ollie)
	- **Good for →** catching price objectors before they exit.
	- **When to use →** on the reveal itself, below the hero plan.
- **Free gift chosen in their name.**
	- Swipe: "Choose Biscuit's free gift" with the Puzzle Toy pre-selected (Ollie)
	- **Good for →** ownership. Leaving now means losing something already picked.
	- **When to use →** the step after the reveal, before any bump.
- **Bump page at the same discount.**
	- Swipe: "Would Biscuit like a treat? Choose up to 3", each ~~\$22.00~~ \$6.60 with "+ Add to your plan" and Skip / Continue (Ollie)
	- **Good for →** AOV on the first order.
	- **When to use →** after the gift step. Max 3 items, each tied to a problem from the quiz, with a clear Skip.
- **Checkout recap of the plan.** First shipment, cadence, and how to pause or cancel.
	- Swipe: "Your pup's plan starts here. Based on your pup's profile, we'll deliver fresh meals every 4 weeks." then "Adjust, pause, or cancel anytime. Ships every 4 weeks." (Ollie)
	- **Good for →** subscriptions sold through a quiz.
	- **When to use →** at checkout, near the pay button, with the guarantee and the renewal price in dollars. Checkout itself → ⑦ Cart, Checkout & Upsell.
## Blueprint
1. **Decide before you build.** The gate position, one hero recommendation per outcome, the reward for finishing, what email 1 delivers, and the renewal price you will show in dollars.
2. **Entry.**
	- Homepage need-state picker with 4 or 5 problems. Each one sets the intro headline.
	- Every CTA promises the result or the price.
	- If trust in online matching is low, a quiz landing page: accuracy stat, quizzes taken, 2 or 3 reviews about the quiz, founder or expert credentials, a 3-step "how it works", a "you're in the right place if" list.
	- Optional: a two-step popup that asks one easy question, then the email.
3. **Intro screen (1 screen).**
	- Headline matched to the entry angle.
	- A list of exactly what they will get.
	- The time cost ("\~3 minutes").
	- Three trust icons or press logos.
4. **Warm-up (2 or 3 questions).**
	- Q1 is the easiest, most personal question: a name, or a fun identity question.
	- Use the name from then on, with matched pronouns.
	- One belief or awareness question styled as conversation. Store the answer.
5. **Diagnosis (4 to 8 questions, in 3 named chapters).**
	- Honest step count that grows if branches add steps.
	- A "why we ask" line on each sensitive question.
	- Options in the customer's words, with at least one confession option and product traits planted inside.
	- Branch on problem answers so each added step earns a specific product.
	- An instant verdict or mini-reveal after the key answer, with a fact the user can check.
	- Proof cards every 2 or 3 screens, chosen by job (normalize, fear or cost, outcome). Strongest card right before the gate or reveal.
	- The last question names the main pain.
	- No input default that passes silently.
6. **Confirm and gate.**
	- Summary screen: "Here's what you told us. Look right?" with YES and an edit option.
	- The gate, at the position chosen in step 1, framed as delivering the results, with Skip if conversion-first.
	- A personalized second summary: "\[Name\], here's what you've told us."
7. **Build-up.**
	- Anticipation screen with the review count and press logos.
	- Loader for 4 to 8 seconds, naming the work, showing the guarantee and the expert or founder, confirming any discount is applied.
8. **Reveal.**
	- Identity label or score at the top.
	- "Built for you because" checklist of their answers.
	- One hero product, pre-selected, with a "RECOMMENDED" badge and a "why this works" paragraph citing their answers.
	- Price per day or week, original anchored, discount applied, renewal stated in dollars.
	- A down-sell below the hero.
	- "Round out your routine" cross-sells, each tied to a concern they chose.
	- Quiz-accuracy reviews, then outcome reviews, beside the button.
	- Guarantee, cancel or exchange terms, and a human fallback.
	- Optional save-your-results and share.
9. **Pre-checkout.**
	- A free-gift picker named for the person or pet.
	- A bump page: up to 3 add-ons at the same first-order discount, with a clear Skip.
	- Checkout recap: first shipment timing, cadence, pause or cancel, the guarantee and the renewal price near the pay button.
10. **Follow-up.**
	- Email 1 delivers exactly what the gate promised.
	- Route later emails by the belief answer and the pain answer.

### 14. Quiz-specific killers
- **What it is:** a quiz that ignores its own answers, accepts silent defaults, or gates results behind a marketing promise.
- **Evidence:** Dr. Squatch's result came back citrus and marine after pine, moss and cabin answers. Jones Road matched a Face Pencil described as "cool" undertone after a "Neutral" answer, and its shade bar defaulted to VERY PALE, which NEXT accepted. Native's gate asked to "stay in touch" for drops and sales instead of sending results. Ollie and Hims buried the renewal price (warning 5).
- **Fix:** → ⑥ Quiz Funnel. Map every answer to a real difference in the result. No pre-highlighted answer on a question that changes the match. Frame the gate as delivering the result.
- **Check:** take the quiz three times with opposite answers. Do the results change in ways that match the answers?

### 5. Hidden money terms (referenced by warning 14 as "warning 5")
- **What it is:** renewal price, cadence, return fees or "first order only" kept out of sight until the bank statement.
- **Evidence:** The audit never saw a dollar renewal figure in Ollie's reveal or checkout, only "This order has a recurring charge" with auto-renew legal text. Hims shows "From \$149/mo†" and puts "\$39 for the first month, auto-renews at \$149/month thereafter… Membership is billed separately" in a footnote. Trustpilot gaps for Hiya, MUD\\WTR and Dr. Squatch are dominated by "hard to cancel / unauthorized recurring charges / forced subscription." Nikita: policies are trust signals, so don't bury them in the footer. Put them right under the add-to-cart button.
- **Fix:** → Universal F8 and 3.3 Renewal stated in the hero. One plain line under every button: today's price, the renewal price, the cadence, how to cancel. Hiya does it: "\$15 each today, \$30 each on a monthly basis thereafter." Brooklinen states its "\$9.95 return fee" outright.
- **Check:** can a buyer read the renewal price and cadence without opening a footnote, an accordion or the terms page?

### 10. Popups that block the read (gate/popup warning)
- **What it is:** an overlay that interrupts before the page has earned it, asks for an email with nothing in return, guilts the decline, or fails on mobile.
- **Evidence:** Javvy drops a "Try your luck / Match 3 cards to see what you win" overlay onto a reading page. Brooklinen's decline reads "No thanks, I'll pay full price," which works on some audiences and grates on others. Native's gate promises marketing ("stay in touch… drops, sales, and more") instead of results. The audits did not time popup load or close behavior, so test that yourself on a real phone.
- **Fix:** → Universal 3.6. One tap-to-answer question first, email second, a deliverable promised. On presells, never cover the headline or the offer button. Decline copy in brand voice.
- **Check:** on a phone with a slow connection, does the popup close in one tap, stay closed on scroll, and never cover the button or fire twice?

### 4. Fake urgency (applies to quiz reveal scarcity)
- **What it is:** timers that restart, scarcity no one can verify, and urgency boilerplate copied from other brands.
- **Evidence:** Miracle's countdown read 14:55, then 14:36, then 14:34 across visits. Ryze's "OFFER RESERVED 00:06:34" is session-based. "Sell-Out Risk: High" is a made-up meter styled like a stock indicator. The same sentence, "This limited-time deal is in high demand and stock keeps selling out," sits on the offer cards of five different brands. Nikita's CRO notes: fake urgency (countdown timers, "only 3 left") "looks desperate, kills credibility." The reader who refreshes or comes back from an email sees the timer restart, and from then on every claim is suspect, the guarantee included.
- **Fix:** → Universal 3.4. Use only urgency you will honor: a real end date, a real launch window, real stock, a first-order-only offer, a real ship-by date. No true deadline means no timer.
- **Check:** open the page, close it, open it in a private window and on a second device. Does any timer restart? Can you point to the real date or stock number behind every scarcity line?

### Pre-launch kill check (full list, verbatim)
Run before any page gets traffic, and monthly on every live page.
- [ ] Ad and first screen match: noun phrase, number, offer (1)
- [ ] Every ad set's temperature maps to the right page type (2)
- [ ] Every number and product fact matches the number sheet on ad, lander, PDP, cart and email (3)
- [ ] No timer restarts on reload or a second device, and every scarcity line has a real date or stock number (4)
- [ ] Renewal price, cadence and fees readable under the button without a footnote (5)
- [ ] Every name, credential and masthead is real (6)
- [ ] Dates, seasons and "Last Updated" are current, with one sale reason per page (7)
- [ ] First screen says what it is, why it's different, and what happens if it fails (8)
- [ ] No nav or side exits on landers and advertorials (9)
- [ ] Popup closes in one tap on mobile and never covers the headline or button (10)
- [ ] Every live ad link loads a matching page with all widgets filled (11)
- [ ] Title tag, slug, H1 and reason count agree, and each reason body proves its title (12)
- [ ] No proof in sliders, no stock photos, sizing and shipping answered on the page (13)
- [ ] Quiz results change with the answers, no silent defaults, gate promises results (14)

<!-- Universal excerpt: Meta-secret 1.11 -->
### 1.11 Popups segment first and discount second
**What it is.** The arrival popup asks one easy question before (or instead of) the email. The answer routes the next page, the email flow or both.
**Why it works.** One tap is easier than typing an email. Once someone has answered, the email feels like the natural next step, and the answer becomes data that personalizes follow-up.
**Evidence.** MUD\\WTR: "You've Got Up To 43% Off / What benefit matters most to you? MORE ENERGY / BETTER FOCUS / HAPPIER GUT / HEALTHIER BODY." Graza: "What do you use olive oil for?" Nécessaire: "What are you shopping for? Body / Hair." Jones Road: "WHAT IS YOUR SKIN TYPE?" Caraway asks what you're shopping for, then the email, and the offer is "\$50 Off Your Second Purchase," which protects first-order margin.
**Do this:** make the arrival popup one tap-to-answer question, then the email, then route the email flow by the answer (Zone 3.6).

<!-- Universal excerpt: Fundamental F17 -->
### F17. Capture everyone, and promise something useful for the email
- **Rule:** every page that can bounce has a capture point. The capture promise is a deliverable ("your plan," "your results," "your match"), not "stay in touch."
- **Why it works:** most visitors don't buy on the first visit. What the gate promises sets the open rate of email 1.
- **Weak:** "Sign up for our newsletter" *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "Where Should We Send Your Results? We'll send your shade matches along with application tips" (Jones Road).
- **Evidence:** Nikita's Chad funnel: "collect everyone's email even non-buyers → convert later via email/SMS." The Farmer's Dog ("You can use your email to access your recommendations anytime"), Gymshark OOS "Stock alerts." Native's weak version: "drops, sales, and more." → Zone 3.6.

## 3. Techniques
Pick per goal. This page owns these, so other pages point here by zone number and only add their placement.
### Zone 3.1: Offer and pricing
- **Subscribe as the default.** Subscription pre-selected and badged "Most Popular" or "Best Value."
	- Swipe: "Subscribe and Save (Most Popular): ~~\$60~~ \$34 (43% OFF) \| \$1.14/serving" (MUD\\WTR)
	- **Good for →** any consumable or refill product.
	- **When to use →** whenever the product gets used up. Pair it with the renewal and cancel terms (Zone 3.3).
- **The one-time defined by what it lacks.** List what the one-time buyer gives up, next to the price.
	- Swipe: "One Time Purchase \$66.65 \$2.38/day · No Free Shipping · No First Order Discount" (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** pushing the default without pressure copy.
	- **When to use →** any two-option buy box. List only losses that are true.
- **Per-day or per-unit price.** Divide the price to the smallest honest unit.
	- Swipe: "As low as \$0.87/Serving" (ARMRA); "\$21.66/shirt" (True Classic); "\$14.61/Week" (Ollie); "Treatments start as low as \$2/dose" (Hims)
	- **Good for →** premium prices, subscriptions, bundles.
	- **When to use →** every buy box, offer card and quiz reveal.
- **Dual-scale number.** The same math stretched big for the saving and small for the cost.
	- Swipe: "Replaces \$4,000+/year in supplements for \$6 a day" (im8)
	- **Good for →** products that replace several things or a recurring spend.
	- **When to use →** hero subheads, offer cards, ad hooks.
- **Replacement math table.** Itemize what the buyer pays now, total it, put your price beside it, and let them subtract.
	- Swipe: "Your traditional routine \$463.84" vs "Whole-body benefits with ARMRA® \$119.99" (ARMRA); "if your go-to order is \$5–7 a cup, that's potentially \$1,500–2,500 a year" (Caraway coffee quiz)
	- **Good for →** high sticker prices.
	- **When to use →** lander, PDP, quiz reveal. Use real competitor prices.
- **Gift stacking with dollar values.** Free items itemized, each with a struck-through value.
	- Swipe: "Pumpkin Spice Creamer ~~\$14~~ FREE, Mush Magnet ~~\$5~~ FREE, Spoon & Scoop ~~\$10~~ FREE, Acacia Jar ~~\$25~~ FREE, US Shipping ~~\$4~~ FREE" (Ryze); "Welcome Kit free ~~€28~~… Total €87 ~~€156~~" (AG1)
	- **Good for →** first-order conversion, making the subscription feel generous as well as cheap.
	- **When to use →** offer cards and buy boxes. Digital gifts work too and cost nothing to ship.
- **Choose-your-gift.** Turn the bonus into a decision the buyer makes.
	- Swipe: "SELECT ONE FREE GIFT WITH PURCHASE: 4.5QT Deep Sauté \$189 Value / Griddle \$159 Value" (HexClad); "Choose Biscuit's free gift" (Ollie)
	- **Good for →** creating ownership before payment.
	- **When to use →** buy box, post-quiz, pre-checkout.
- **Quantity ladder with per-unit drop.** Packs with escalating savings, one tier tagged.
	- Swipe: "1 bottle \$17.47/ea → 2 @ \$16.77 → 3 @ \$16.07 → 4+ @ \$15.37/ea ("BEST DEAL")" (Javy); "10-Pack Save \$213 / 6-Pack Save \$105 / 3-Pack Save \$40" (True Classic)
	- **Good for →** AOV on products people use in multiples or give as gifts.
	- **When to use →** any buy box where more than one unit makes sense. Dr. Squatch only unlocks its 15% at 3 to 4 units, which pushes bigger first orders. Bombas ties giving to pack size: "4 Purchased = 4 Donated."
- **Tagged bundle tiers.** Two or three bundle cards, each with its dollar value and a tag, so one tier reads as the smart buy.
	- Swipe: "Best Seller — Cookware Duo (7 Piece) \$239.95 (\$284 Value)", "Most Savings — Cookware Set (13 Piece) \$359.95 (\$518 Value)" (Our Place)
	- **Good for →** AOV on sets and systems. The top tier mostly exists to sell the middle one.
	- **When to use →** when you have real sets of different sizes. Show a thumbnail of what's in each one.
- **Presupposition quantity question.** Ask how many, not whether.
	- Swipe: "How many beds in your home would you like to have GroundingWell Bedsheets on?" (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** household and multi-user products.
	- **When to use →** as the label on the quantity selector.
- **Kit builder inside the buy box.** Add-ons chosen without leaving the button.
	- Swipe: "Make It A Kit: Wallet / +KeyCase / +Power Bank / +Power Bank +KeyCase" (Ridge)
	- **Good for →** ecosystems and routines.
	- **When to use →** when you have 2 to 4 natural companions. Caraway runs a lighter "Don't Forget These."
- **Spend-threshold meter.** Visible progress toward free shipping or a gift.
	- Swipe: "FREE SHIPPING ON ORDERS \$15+ / FREE GIFT ON ORDERS \$35+ / FREE GIFT ON ORDERS \$55+" (Native); "Spend \$90 to unlock free shipping!" (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** carts with many low-price SKUs.
	- **When to use →** top bar and cart drawer, with thresholds set just above your median order.
- **Reason-why discount.** Give a harmless reason for the price cut so quality isn't questioned.
	- Swipe: "It's our third birthday, and we're lowering prices. Not just for a week but indefinitely" (Grüns); "We cut the markup so you get three premium quality t-shirts for less than the price of one designer tee!" (True Classic)
	- **Good for →** permanent price drops, low prices in a premium category.
	- **When to use →** any time the price looks too good to be true.
- **Guarantee scaled to commitment.** The longer plan gets the longer guarantee.
	- Swipe: "Three full months to experience the difference. If you don't feel it — complete refund, no questions." (im8, quarterly plan)
	- **Good for →** pushing the longer plan.
	- **When to use →** only if both guarantee lengths are real and stated identically everywhere.
- **Second-purchase discount for the email.** Buy the email without cutting first-order margin.
	- Swipe: "You've Got \$50 Off Your Second Purchase\*" (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** margin-sensitive stores.
	- **When to use →** the sitewide popup, when the first order already carries an offer.
- **Instant reward on the first order.** Loyalty points or perks credited at purchase, so the first order already pays something back.
	- Swipe: "Instantly receive 125 points on us!" (Chomps); "Earn 64 Lume Points." (Lume)
	- **Good for →** consumables with a loyalty program.
	- **When to use →** in the buy box, next to the subscription option.
- **Installments, HSA/FSA and verified-audience discounts.** Payment framing that shrinks or legitimizes the price.
	- Swipe: "Starting at \$44/mo or 0% APR with Affirm" (HexClad); "4 payments of \$32 with Shop Pay" (Caraway); "HSA/FSA eligible with Truemed" (MUD\\WTR); GovX discounts for military and first responders (Create, Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** premium tickets, health-adjacent products.
	- **When to use →** next to the price, only where you actually qualify.
- **Low-AOV entry item and prepaid multi-month.** A cheap first yes for cold traffic, or cash and retention taken up front.
	- Swipe: Dr. Squatch's longest-running ad (211 days) is a "\$16 lip-balm 4-pack"; MUD\\WTR sells prepaid 3-month SKUs (\~\$150)
	- **Good for →** cold traffic, cash flow, churn.
	- **When to use →** once the main offer is proven.
**After the first yes.** Order bumps, OTOs, down-sells and post-purchase upsells → ⑦ Cart, Checkout & Upsell. The rule that governs all of them is in the Chad funnel (section 4.3).
### Zone 3.2: Proof system
**Proof policy (stated once for the whole doc).** Use real, seeded, verifiable proof: real customers, real UGC, real lab numbers, real named experts with real credentials. Never fabricate specific reviews, lab results or named doctors. That is what freezes ad accounts and payment processors. Hiya is the model: real experts with titles ("Board-Certified Pediatrician," "Board-Certified Pediatric Gastroenterologist") plus a disclosure under testimonials ("Testimonials… may include individuals who have received compensation"). Our Place tags some reviews "Review from a paid tester." The pattern to avoid is Mellow's advertorial: a "By Dr. Ralph" byline over a fictional narrator named Sarah. Reviews, testimonials, before/afters, transformations and AI-generated creative are all fine to use.
- **Scale number as the headline.** The aggregate number stated as a headline, separate from the per-product star rating.
	- Swipe: "✨High Protein, 0-2g Sugar, and 80,000+ 5-Star Reviews✨" (Magic Spoon); "4,000,000+ Homes Have Made the Switch" (Blueland); "Find us in 65,000+ stores nationwide." (Chomps); "OVER 240 MILLION CUPS SERVED" (MUD\\WTR); "99% would recommend these products" (Graza)
	- **Good for →** any brand with real volume, retail reach or a high recommendation rate.
	- **When to use →** top bar, hero eyebrow, proof section headline.
- **Dated, linkable lab badge.** A certification with a date and a link to the result.
	- Swipe: "Tested by Light Labs in SEP 2026 / 35 substances tested" (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** anything ingested, applied or sold as "clean."
	- **When to use →** beside the buy box and in the proof section.
- **Clinician platform with the incentive pre-answered.** Third-party clinicians who say up front they weren't paid.
	- Swipe: "Clinicians' Choice — 757 clinicians share this on FrontrowMD without compensation" (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** health claims a skeptic can't verify alone.
	- **When to use →** near the buy box, and above lander headlines (Grüns).
- **Expert placed at the claim.** Each expert quote sits beside the one claim it backs.
	- Swipe: Hiya's pediatric dentist quote inside reason #2, the sugar and cavities reason (Hiya)
	- **Good for →** health, safety and technical claims.
	- **When to use →** every page with expert quotes. Don't pool them in one "experts" block only.
- **Credibility gate on endorsers.** Answer "they're paid" inside the endorsement itself.
	- Swipe: "I was a customer of IM8 for months before I became a partner." (Lily Collins, im8); "AG1 DRINKER SINCE 2021" (Hugh Jackman's title, AG1)
	- **Good for →** celebrity, creator and partner proof.
	- **When to use →** whenever the endorser has a commercial tie.
- **Credibility ladder.** Stack proof from institution to expert to elite user to the crowd, in that order.
	- Swipe: in-house research ("over 12 PhDs … 50 clinical studies") → named PhD advisory board → pro athletes → million-nights stat → review wall (Eight Sleep)
	- **Good for →** technical or expensive products where one proof type isn't enough.
	- **When to use →** homepage and long PDPs, as the proof section order.
- **Clinical-to-felt bridge.** Pair the study number with what it feels like.
	- Swipe: "The Clinical Trial said 95% felt more energy. Here's what that actually feels like." (im8)
	- **Good for →** studies and stats.
	- **When to use →** proof and review section headlines.
- **Hero stat with method footnote.** One big number, with its sample and date underneath.
	- Swipe: "86% of Oura Members see their health improve" / "Based on a 2026 survey with 3,501 Oura members after using Oura Ring for 30 days" (Oura); "90% of Hims users are satisfied with their treatment" / "Based on survey responses of 869 Hims ED users… Customers may have been compensated." (Hims)
	- **Good for →** outcome claims.
	- **When to use →** anywhere you cite a survey. Run your own customer survey to get one.
- **Testimonial casting.** Pick each quote for one job: an objection, a claim or a segment (meta-secret 1.9).
	- Swipe: "My friends keep asking if I've lost weight or been hitting the gym." (Brian G., True Classic, the social-reward job); "I was skeptical. A mattress cover that controls temperature sounded like a luxury gimmick. I was wrong." (Eight Sleep, the skeptic job)
	- **Good for →** every proof block.
	- **When to use →** map your top 5 objections, then find one real review for each.
- **Observer proof.** Someone else notices the result, or feels it without knowing.
	- Swipe: "My wife was like 'Wait, when did you start working out?!'" (True Classic); "Her husband also told her that he slept deeper, without even knowing he was sleeping on the grounding bedsheet." (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** results that could be called placebo or vanity.
	- **When to use →** lander reasons, review selection, ad hooks.
- **Review as argument.** Let the customer say the bold or blunt thing the brand can't.
	- Swipe: "a sweaty dude that does HVAC… no more swamp butt." (Mando); "I used this to stop drinking beer." (Liquid Death); "Works just as well as my previous life of toxic chemicals!!!" (Blueland)
	- **Good for →** taboo, bold or legally sensitive benefits.
	- **When to use →** pull lines straight from real reviews into headlines and section copy.
- **Tenure proof.** Durability or loyalty shown by years of use.
	- Swipe: "4 years and going strong… it has survived many many falls, a little fire, and a lot of water!" (Ridge); "I've been using AG1 since 2012" (Huberman, AG1)
	- **Good for →** durable goods and long-term habits.
	- **When to use →** review casting and endorsement titles.
- **Reviews filtered by the buyer's situation.** Filters or tabs so each visitor finds the reviewer like them.
	- Swipe: "Hot sleepers / Couples / Hot flashes / Insomnia" (Eight Sleep); Hiya filters by kid's age, wellness goal and "We try to limit sugar" (Hiya)
	- **Good for →** products with distinct segments.
	- **When to use →** PDP and lander review sections.
- **Review-theme chips.** Objections and praise themes turned into review navigation.
	- Swipe: "Free of PFAs," "Oven-Safe Pan," "Replaces my Cookware," "Literally Does it All" (Our Place)
	- **Good for →** big review sets.
	- **When to use →** above the review list.
- **AI review summary that keeps the cons.** A short summary of all reviews that names the weak spot too.
	- Swipe: "praised for its non-stick surface, ease of cleaning, and beautiful aesthetics... feedback on the heat-resistant handle is mixed" (Our Place); "Some note difficulty accessing cards… screws loosening over time" (Ridge)
	- **Good for →** high review counts.
	- **When to use →** above the review list.
- **Brand replies to reviews.** A named reply under reviews, critical ones first.
	- Swipe: HexClad replies to nearly every review; Bearaby replies to a 4-star "needs more colors" (HexClad, Bearaby)
	- **Good for →** signalling an accountable company.
	- **When to use →** always, and fastest on the critical ones.
- **Press quotes over press logos.** Quote the outlet's sentence instead of showing its logo.
	- Swipe: The Guardian: "a stunning generational leap for smart rings" (Oura); "If 'no-make-up' make-up is more your vibe, then Jones Road should be on your radar." (Jones Road); "Awarded Oprah's Favorite Things 2018" (Cozy Earth)
	- **Good for →** brands with real coverage or awards.
	- **When to use →** instead of an "as seen on" strip. The premium-PDP video: "'as seen on' logos are cheap now — use a QUOTE from the publication instead."
- **UGC video wall and wall-of-love grid.** Real customer video and photos in a grid, not a slider.
	- Swipe: "The buzz is real" with real creator handles (Manscaped); member photos, verified quotes and real X posts mixed in one wall (Eight Sleep)
	- **Good for →** anything visual or demonstrable.
	- **When to use →** PDP mid-page, lander after the mechanism.
### Zone 3.3: Trust and risk reversal
- **Named guarantee.** Give the guarantee a name and an emotion, not a policy title.
	- Swipe: "Perfect Fit, 100-Day Made to Last Guarantee" (True Classic); "100% Happiness Guarantee" (Bombas); "Feel the Difference or It's On Us." (AG1); "30 Day Sudisfaction Guarantee" (Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** every store.
	- **When to use →** under the button, as its own section, in the FAQ.
- **Guarantee as a dare.** Phrase the guarantee as the founder's confidence.
	- Swipe: "Taste it. Feel the difference. Decide." (AG1); "You Will Love It. Guaranteed." (Ryze)
	- **Good for →** products where trying is the proof.
	- **When to use →** guarantee section headline.
- **Us-vs-them guarantee.** Explain why your guarantee differs from the category's.
	- Swipe: "most other grounding companies offer 'guarantees' that only work if you never open the package… We do the opposite. We WANT you to try it." (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** categories with known return traps.
	- **When to use →** guarantee section on landers and PDPs.
- **Refund rate as proof.** Turn the guarantee into evidence by saying how few people use it.
	- Swipe: "Less than 1% of customers use our Money-Back Guarantee." (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** brands with a real low refund rate.
	- **When to use →** under the button. Only with a real number.
- **Guarantee as a numbered reason.** On listicles, the guarantee is its own reason near the end.
	- Swipe: "The 'perfect fit' guarantee" as reason 5 (True Classic); the "90 DAYS RISK-FREE" seal as #9 (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** listicles and advertorials.
	- **When to use →** second-to-last reason, after desire is built.
- **Trust bar in site chrome.** Guarantee, shipping and warranty pinned on every page.
	- Swipe: "99-DAY RISK-FREE TRIAL / LIFETIME GUARANTEE" (Ridge top bar); "Money-Back Guarantee / Free Shipping Over \$49 / 2-Year Warranty" (Manscaped)
	- **Good for →** stores with strong terms.
	- **When to use →** top bar or under the hero, sitewide.
- **Renewal and cancel terms stated up front.** Price after the first order, cadence and how to pause or cancel, in the hero and under the button (meta-secret 1.6).
	- Swipe: "Renews monthly at full price." (Hiya); "We'll remind you 3 days before it ships" (Lume); "Free shipping / Cancel anytime / Pause or skip anytime" (Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** any subscription.
	- **When to use →** hero offer card, under the button, at checkout.
- **Human fallback.** A real person behind the algorithm or the policy.
	- Swipe: "Send a no-makeup selfie to get matched by the experts." (Jones Road); "Or give us a call." (The Farmer's Dog)
	- **Good for →** fit, shade, size and dosage decisions.
	- **When to use →** quiz reveals, PDP buy box, FAQ.
- **Trusted second buy path.** Offer a marketplace the buyer already trusts, next to your own button.
	- Swipe: "Buy on Amazon — Free 2-Day Shipping \$34.99" (Bloom)
	- **Good for →** risk-averse buyers, brands already selling on a marketplace.
	- **When to use →** on the PDP, below your main option. Skip it if marketplace margin hurts.
- **Decision rules that remove the hard choice.** A one-line rule that picks the size, dose or model for them.
	- Swipe: "Your Napper should be roughly 10% of your body weight (150lb = 15lb Napper)" (Bearaby); "Under 5'7 Tall = Regular / Above 5'7 Tall = High" (Mellow); "Between sizes? Size up for relaxed, down for fitted." (True Classic); "Choose the 30L if… Choose the 40L if…" (Nomatic)
	- **Good for →** sizing, dosing, model choice.
	- **When to use →** in the buy box, at the selector.
- **FAQ that teaches correct use.** Prevent the bad review before the first use.
	- Swipe: "Always cook on a low to medium heat setting." (Caraway); "medium with HexClad equals medium-high with others." (HexClad)
	- **Good for →** products that fail when misused.
	- **When to use →** PDP FAQ and post-purchase email 1.
- **Flaw-to-feature reframe.** Name the known complaint as proof of quality.
	- Swipe: "the coveted ARMRA® clump, it's ARMRA Colostrum's purity on full display." (ARMRA); "get even softer with every wash" (Miracle, Cozy Earth)
	- **Good for →** products with a known quirk.
	- **When to use →** FAQ and mechanism sections. Only when the reframe is true.
- **Disclaimer as trust.** Visible compliance copy right under bold claims.
	- Swipe: "Disclaimer: this is an advertisement, using a fictional customer… not an actual news article." (Miracle); "on-page quiz questions are not a medical assessment." (Hims); "does not guarantee cut protection" (Manscaped)
	- **Good for →** health, claims-heavy and advertorial pages.
	- **When to use →** directly under the claim, or in the footer of editorial-style pages.
### Zone 3.4: Urgency and scarcity (true only)
Nikita's stance: fake urgency "looks desperate, kills credibility." His PDP notes allow scarcity and urgency only when "ethical." The forensics show why fakes backfire: Miracle's countdown read 14:55, then 14:36, then 14:34 across visits because it resets on load. Ryze's fine-print deadline was a month stale. "Sell-Out Risk: High" is a made-up metric styled like a stock indicator. A skeptical buyer reloads once and the trust is gone. Use only urgency you will honor.
- **Real stock.** Sold-out states shown honestly, with a waitlist.
	- Swipe: "You Just Missed Out. Don't worry, we'll be back soon and you're going to want in on this." (ARMRA); "Lamb w/ Cranberries… OUT OF STOCK… Notify Me" (Ollie)
	- **Good for →** genuine sell-outs.
	- **When to use →** when inventory is actually limited. Pair with stock-alert capture (Gymshark).
- **Real dates.** Ship-by dates and true offer end dates.
	- Swipe: "Ships by Thu, Oct 8th" (Miracle); "Arrives 10/08 - 10/13" (True Classic); "Estimated Ship Date: 12/18" (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** every store.
	- **When to use →** under the button. A delivery date is urgency that is also service.
- **Real limited editions and drops.** Seasonal or collab SKUs that genuinely end.
	- Swipe: "NEW! Shrek Berry Far Far Away. Grab it before it's far, far gone." (Grüns); seasonal Fall soaps and the IShowSpeed collab page (Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** consumables with flavor or scent variants.
	- **When to use →** as ad angles and top bars. Retire them when they end.
- **First-order-only offers.** The deadline is the first purchase, not a clock.
	- Swipe: "Special sale discount is valid only on the first delivery of a new RYZE subscription started from this page" (Ryze); "New customers only" (Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** cold traffic.
	- **When to use →** offer cards and ads, stated plainly.
- **Live demand signals, if real.** Counters pulled from real data.
	- Swipe: "23 people have this in their cart right now." (Brooklinen); "SOLD OUT 4X" (Miracle)
	- **Good for →** high-traffic stores.
	- **When to use →** only when the counter is live and true.
- **Season hygiene.** Stale seasonal copy is urgency's mirror: it tells the buyer nobody is minding the store.
	- Swipe (the failure): "THIS SUMMER- SLEEP COOLER" still live in October (Cozy Earth)
	- **Good for →** every store running dated or seasonal lines.
	- **When to use →** audit every dated line monthly and when a season ends.
### Zone 3.5: Message match and funnel continuity
- **Modular hero, universal body.** Swap the top screen per angle, keep the body (meta-secret 1.5).
	- Swipe: "Better food for picky eaters starts here" / "Better food for strong puppies starts here" over the same quiz (The Farmer's Dog)
	- **Good for →** every brand running more than one ad angle.
	- **When to use →** from day one. One hero per ad angle.
- **Ad noun phrase in the H1 or reason 1.** Move the ad's exact words or number into the first thing they read.
	- Swipe: "FIVE WAYS TO CHARGE. ONE DEVICE." (Ridge, matching its ad's "five ways to power")
	- **Good for →** every ad-to-page handoff.
	- **When to use →** always. Write the hero after the ad.
- **Offer-first hero.** The headline is the deal, for ads that sold the deal.
	- Swipe: "UP TO 38% OFF YOUR FIRST ORDER + FREE SHIPPING" (Javy); "Get Started for 43% off" (MUD\\WTR CTA)
	- **Good for →** offer-led ads and warm or most-aware traffic.
	- **When to use →** when the ad's hook was the offer. Lead with the benefit when the ad's hook was the problem.
- **Segment filter in the headline.** The reader self-identifies before reading on.
	- Swipe: "10 Reasons Why Women Over 50 Are Switching to this 'Special Sheet'…" (GroundingWell); "💊 FOR GLP-1 USERS" (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** segmented ads.
	- **When to use →** whenever the ad targets one segment.
- **Per-creator and per-founder landers.** A separate slug built around each face that runs ads (meta-secret 1.12).
	- Swipe: `/pages/tech-MKBHD-6RLP` with "Over my 15 years of reviewing tech, the Ridge power bank quickly became my favorite" (Ridge)
	- **Good for →** whitelisted and creator ads.
	- **When to use →** any creator, founder or publisher page with its own ad set.
- **Number sheet.** One sheet holding every number for the offer, which every ad and page pulls from (F3).
	- Swipe (the failure it prevents): ad "UP TO 67% OFF," lander "50% Off" (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** every funnel.
	- **When to use →** before the first ad goes live, and every time the offer changes.
- **Redirect retired slugs to a live offer.** Old ad links land on a current page, never a 404.
	- Swipe: `/pages/5-reasons-why-page` now redirects to the Pastries PDP with the subscription preselected (Magic Spoon)
	- **Good for →** stopping old ads, posts and emails from hitting dead pages.
	- **When to use →** every time a lander is retired.
- **Lander URL in the ad copy.** The slug itself becomes part of the promise.
	- Swipe: "Try now ➡️ [shop.ryzesuperfoods.com/p/11-reasons…](http://shop.ryzesuperfoods.com/p/11-reasons…)" (Ryze)
	- **Good for →** making the destination part of the hook.
	- **When to use →** when the slug sells ("11-reasons").
### Zone 3.6: Popups, capture and segmentation
- **One-question popup.** Tap one answer first, give the email second, route the flow by the answer (meta-secret 1.11).
	- Swipe: "What benefit matters most to you? MORE ENERGY / BETTER FOCUS / HAPPIER GUT / HEALTHIER BODY" (MUD\\WTR); "tell us what you're shopping for: Bakeware / Cookware / Food Storage / Coffee Maker" then the email field (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** stores with more than one benefit or audience. Raises opt-in without a full quiz.
	- **When to use →** the arrival popup, sitewide.
- **Belief question as data.** Ask where the visitor stands on your core belief and route later messages by it.
	- Swipe: "I \[already believe / am not yet sure / don't believe\] that fresh food is the healthiest for Biscuit." (The Farmer's Dog)
	- **Good for →** education-heavy products.
	- **When to use →** quiz warm-up or popup step 1.
- **Gamified capture.** A game or prize mechanic in place of a plain discount form.
	- Swipe: "PICK A BOX TO REVEAL YOUR DEAL" (Ridge); "Earn 139 Sweepstakes Entries" (Miracle); match-3 cards (Grüns, Javvy, Mellow); spin wheel (Native)
	- **Good for →** deal-driven audiences.
	- **When to use →** volume brands. Premium-restraint brands skip it.
- **Capture on the sales page and order page.** An email or SMS field before the payment step.
	- Swipe: "EMAIL + SMS REMARKETING: add capture on the SP and order page" (Nikita's Chad funnel notes)
	- **Good for →** long-form funnels with expensive cold traffic.
	- **When to use →** every advertorial, sales page and order form, before the payment step.
- **Capture at the dead end.** Stock alerts and saved results catch intent where the page can't sell.
	- Swipe: "Stock alerts" (Gymshark OOS); "Save Your Results. Enter your email to receive a copy of your quiz results." (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** catching intent that would otherwise leave.
	- **When to use →** OOS states, quiz results, size guides.
- **Gate placement by goal.** Where you ask for the email decides what you trade off.
	- Swipe: "You can use your email to access your recommendations anytime" (The Farmer's Dog, early hard gate)
	- **Good for →** quizzes and multi-step funnels.
	- **When to use →** early and hard to maximize leads (The Farmer's Dog). At checkout to maximize completion (Ollie). Late and soft with a Skip to protect completion and still capture (Jones Road). Always frame it as delivering results.
- **Decline copy in brand voice.** The "no thanks" link does a last bit of persuading.
	- Swipe: "No thanks, I'll pay full price." (Brooklinen)
	- **Good for →** deal-driven audiences.
	- **When to use →** match it to the brand voice. It grates on premium-restraint audiences.

### 4.1 Nikita's traffic temperature rule
From his notes: "if ads are bottom-of-funnel (ready to buy), don't send to the homepage — send straight to the PDP." The colder and less aware the traffic, the more pre-selling the page does before it shows a buy box.
