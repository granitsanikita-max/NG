# 📚 Reference — Copywriting Fundamentals

## ① Skills for this doc
- **`stop-slop`** — run every draft through it (kills AI tells).
- **`product-pulse`**** / ****`agent-reach`** — pull the customer's real words / voice.
- **Amazon review-mining** — 1–2★ → objection list; 5★ → the exact benefit language they use.
## ② Fundamentals vs Techniques — read this first
**The law (prime directive):** every element's only job is to **get them to the next word.** Copy is a chain — one line earns the next.
- **Fundamentals** = non-negotiable. Active in **every** copy, whatever the goal. Nail these first.
- **Techniques** = the toolbox. You **pick** these based on the copy's **goal + awareness + sophistication** (Section ③). The right technique for a cold advertorial is the wrong one for a warm product page.
**Order of work:** get the goal (③) → make every **fundamental** (④) active → layer the **techniques** (⑤) the goal calls for → chief it (⑥).

## ④ Fundamentals — active in EVERY copy
1. ⭐ **Argument \> words (the foundation).** Copy convinces through an airtight **argument**, not fancy words. Build a logical AND emotional case that leads to ONE conclusion — they can't help but arrive at your belief. Power words on no argument = nothing; craft the argument first, layer stronger verbs on the edit pass. Before writing, know the ONE belief they must hold to buy (**North Star**) and the step-by-step case that gets them there. Every line moves them one step down that chain — and all roads lead to your **unique mechanism** (a proprietary fix only you have, never a generic "it's amazing").
	- *Weak (words-first):* "Our AMAZING pillowcase gives you INCREDIBLE sleep!" → hype, no reason to believe, they scroll.
	- *Strong (argument-first):* "Cotton soaks up the oil + bacteria off your face all night → you press it back into your skin for 8 hours → that's why you break out no matter what serum you use. Silk doesn't absorb it. Switch the case, remove the cause." → barely any hype words; they walk themselves to the conclusion.
2. **The Slippery Slide.** Every element's only job is to get them to the next. First line = a **3–6 word vacuum** that pulls them in.
3. **Clarity Mechanics.** 5th–8th grade, active voice. Repeaters (say key points twice: the term, then dead-simple + metaphor). Compression (one atomic message). **Make them feel smart** — dumb the jargon down ("kills the fungus," not "kills Malassezia"); never lecture.
	- *Match the grade level to the buyer (Gunning Fog Index).* Lower grade = wider audience; for most ads more clarity = broader appeal = more response. Cold / mass market → short sentences, simple words (\~5th–8th grade); upscale / niche → you can go bigger. Quick check on the first \~100 words: **(avg sentence length + % of 3+-syllable words) × 0.4 = grade level** (skip proper names, easy compounds like "yellowtail," and verbs that only hit 3 syllables from -ed/-es). Don't over-optimise — vary sentence length and use the bigger word when it's the right one.
4. **Speed to Value + Density.** Signal value fast; maximise value ÷ non-value; cut anything they don't need.
5. **Rhythm & Cadence.** Vary sentence length, mostly staccato.
6. **Emotional Resonance.** Name the target emotion, screen every word for it. Fear / shock make them share; hope / relief make them buy. Buying is emotional — every element is a tuning fork; keep every "vibration" positive, cut anything that triggers a negative reaction.
7. **Human Voice.** Write how the avatar talks; run stop-slop; read it aloud.
8. **Anticipate & Resolve Objections (Sugarman #8–9).** Every objection you don't raise, the buyer raises for you — then leaves. Always paired: **Anticipate** (say the worry out loud) → **Resolve** (kill it with proof or an honest alternative). Power move: name your biggest flaw before they spot it, then flip it. Clusters: price · "will it work" · "for someone like me" · trust · ease/install · upkeep · time to results · returns/guarantee · looks. Mine 1–2★ reviews to build the list fast. Place lines at the price and right before the CTA.
9. **Honesty (Sugarman's #1 rule).** Truth outsells hype — the customer is smarter than you think and senses a lie. Raise your flaws up front, explain why to buy anyway → trust → they buy more. "You can't fake the truth." (Pairs with #8: name the flaw, then flip it.)
10. **The Consciousness Method (iceberg).** 5 depths: conscious thought · emotion · belief · identity · consciousness (worldview). Reach the deepest depth the surface allows.
## ⑤ Techniques — pick per goal (each: good for →)
### Persuasion plays (pick by awareness / sophistication)
- **Belief Change** — find the one belief blocking the buy; change it with mechanism, proof, or reframe. Depth = skepticism × price. **Good for →** cold / skeptical, high-price, saturated markets (heavy lift); dial down when most-aware.
- **Curiosity & Contrast (open loops)** — expectations vs reality: pain → their belief → your contrarian truth → why yours wins → one fast proof. Two builds: **(a) Juxtaposition** — pair two things that don't belong ("all-natural deodorant that performs like a toxic one"). **(b) Curiosity through fear** — a warning outpulls a tip. Guardrail: low valence / intensity — tap the emotion, don't fear-monger; leave space between the two ideas (the gap is what they read on to fill); **never open a loop you won't pay off.** **Good for →** hooks, cold ads & advertorials.
- **Involvement / Ownership (Sugarman)** — make them own it in their head: (a) put it in their hands with words; (b) sensory journey ("feel the breeze, punch the accelerator"); (c) ownership language ("when your \[product\] arrives…"); (d) involvement device (make them DO something). **Good for →** product pages, demos.
- **Linking (Sugarman)** — bridge the new to the known: a live fad / **trend-hijack**, a body part ("The Nose" = smoke detector), a familiar concept ("vitamins for your car"), quality (gold = premium), the old version, or an emotional/memory anchor. Pick POSITIVE links — a bad association backfires. **Good for →** novel / unfamiliar products, cold traffic.
- **Fear / loss aversion (Sugarman)** — make the cost of NOT acting concrete and present, not a someday risk; frame even a preventive as a real present threat. Genuine danger only; pair fear with relief/hope. **Good for →** problem-aware TOF, urgency.
- **Human element (Sugarman — Human Relationships)** — first-person conversational byline · a real story · light inoffensive humor · a **human hand holding a small product** (scale + warmth) · relatable/attractive people they'd want to belong to. **Good for →** native trust / warmth.
- **"That's-why" evidence** 🐱 — after a claim (esp. about the CAUSE of their problem), anchor it to something they already see: **\[claim\] → "…that's why \[the thing they already experience\]."** They prove it to themselves — nothing to argue with. **Good for →** mechanism / cause claims.
- **The silent problem** 🐱 — "most \[group\] already have this and don't know it — it happens silently, no obvious symptoms." Removes the need to see a symptom + expands who qualifies. True only; pair with hope. **Good for →** problem-unaware, expanding the market.
- **Let them reach the conclusion** — imply the point, don't spell it out. Give the facts, then stop before the obvious conclusion and let them connect it themselves. A conclusion they reach on their own feels like their idea and sticks harder; over-explaining makes them feel talked down to. **Good for →** skeptical / savvy readers, premium & comparison copy.
- **Facts over opinions (no objection surface)** — state a verifiable fact instead of a claim or an opinion, and there is nothing to argue with, so the objection never even forms. An opinion ("ours is the cleanest," "the best on the market") invites the reflex "sure it is"; a plain, specific fact can't be argued, so the reader draws the flattering conclusion themselves ("that's genuinely clean") and believes it, because it was their own idea. State the fact, don't sell it, then stop. Medium-agnostic — it works in an ad, on a page, in an email, on the packaging, in a video, anywhere you catch yourself asserting instead of showing. **Good for →** any surface, any market; strongest where buyers are skeptical or the category is saturated. Pairs with **Let them reach the conclusion** and **That's-why evidence**.
- **Communicates \> says (read between the lines)** — a line's power is not what it states, it's what it makes the reader conclude and feel. One compressed line can carry a whole story or identity without ever spelling it out: "the dating app designed to be deleted" (you'll find love and won't need us), "you miss 100% of the shots you don't take" on a spirits brand. Say less, mean more — decide the meaning you want them to land on, then cut back to the fewest words that still trigger it. **Good for →** headlines, taglines, hero lines, end cards, any short high-impact line; and the edit pass on long copy. Pairs with **One Format = One Line** and **Let them reach the conclusion**.
- **Sell the cure, not the prevention (Sugarman, Axiom 15)** — people barely pay to *prevent* a problem they don't feel yet ("that'll never happen to me"), but pay far more, far faster, to *cure* one they already have. Frame the promise as the fix for a pain they feel RIGHT NOW, not protection against a someday risk. If the product is really a preventive, reposition it as a cure — lead with the acute problem it solves today, underplay the "avoid this later" angle (e.g. a fuel additive: "stops engine knock, saves 10% on gas," not "prevents engine problems"). **Good for →** problem / solution-aware buyers who already feel the pain; picking the promise & headline. **When to use →** any time you're deciding what promise the copy makes — especially if the product leans preventive. If the buyer feels no present problem, don't use it raw — first make the problem feel real (pair with **the silent problem** play).
### Named plays (library) — *ACH = Adweek Copywriting Handbook · OGSL = OG Sales Letters*
- **Openings & flow:** Pre-headline (pull eyes to the headline) · Don't ask a "no" question (only when the answer's a guaranteed yes) · Logical flow (answer questions in order, face-to-face). *(Vacuum first line lives in Fundamental #2.)* **Good for →** the top of any piece.
- **Keeping them reading:** Harmony / "Yes" ladder (nodding every line) · Comfortable buying environment (layout + first paragraphs) · Long copy that works (long beats short IF it drives action, never padding). **Good for →** advertorials, long PDPs.
- **The angle & the sell:** Sell the concept, not the product · **Unique mechanism** (the different "how it works" everything hangs off — vital in saturated markets) · Fascinations (outcome-painting bullets; diversify them). **Good for →** saturated markets, the core pitch.
- **Proven frameworks:** "I was just like you — but worse" · Halbert "If I ***, then I ***" open loop · Make it feel obtainable ("desperate nerd from Ohio"). **Good for →** story / founder / personal-story copy.
- **Psychology & state:** Humiliation open (start at the bottom; the climb hits hard) · The call-out (call out the exact person) · Make them believe they can obtain it · Know the emotional job first · Post-purchase (kill anxiety, then upsell) · **Desire to belong** (sell membership in the group that owns it) · **Sense of urgency** (a TRUE reason to act now — real deadline / price rise / limited quantity, never faked, placed at the end). **Good for →** cold TOF, closes, upsells.

## ⑦ New plays (from swipe sources) 🗂️
*New material only — anything already in ④/⑤ (argument-first, unique mechanism, anticipate-&-resolve objections) is deliberately not repeated here.*
- **Necessary Beliefs (pre-writing technique).** Before you write a word, list the **MAX 6 beliefs** the prospect must accept to buy — each phrased as an **"I believe that…"** statement. The copy's only job is to march them to all of them, in order. If it doesn't move them toward one of the six, cut it. (This is the concrete build for the **North Star** in Fundamental #1.) **Good for →** planning any long-form piece before drafting.
- **Specificity Rule (concretize everything).** Specific always beats vague — it reads as proof, not puffery. Specific numbers beat ranges: *"22 days"* \> *"a few weeks."* Specific dollars beat rounded ones: *"\$2,700"* \> *"a couple thousand."* *"I made \$97,000 in 13 days"* \>\> *"I made thousands."* On the edit pass, hunt every vague word and swap in the exact number, date, or figure. **Good for →** proof, headlines, claims — every surface.
- **Power Words (definition + bank).** Power words are **1–3 word phrases** that act as *heat-seeking missiles for attention.* When you frame a pain or an outcome, swap flat phrasing for extreme / vivid language (the *argument* stays first — this is the layer you add on the edit pass, per Fundamental #1). Keep a **running, performance-ranked power-word bank** you can hot-swap into hooks. *(The method for mining and building that bank lives in the Hook-Mining Method doc.)* **Good for →** hooks, the edit/polish pass.
- **One Format = One Line.** Each ad format exists to deliver a **single line of copy.** If your idea needs three lines to land, it's the **wrong format** — pick the format the line fits, don't cram the line into the format. **Good for →** picking ad formats, static / short-form ads.
- **"The Number Is the Hook" (lists).** For a listicle, the **counted / ranked promise is itself the hook** — *"3 reasons…," "the 30 worst…"* — the number does the pulling. But **never inflate the count**: three real reasons beat seven weak ones. Deliver every item at full strength. **Good for →** listicle ads & advertorials, ranked-content hooks.
