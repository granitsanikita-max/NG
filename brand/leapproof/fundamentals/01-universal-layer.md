## The job of this page
This page holds what is true on every funnel page: ad lander, advertorial, listicle, sales page, PDP, home, collection, quiz, cart, checkout, upsell. Each item here showed up on at least three page types. Anything that only matters on one page type lives on that page.
**What this page owns.** Offer architecture, the proof system, trust and risk reversal, true urgency, message match, popups and capture, visual and mobile rules, the tech stack and the funnel map. Other pages give a one-line pointer here (for example "→ Universal 3.1") plus their page-specific placement.
**What it does not own.** Copy craft (argument, slippery slide, specificity, objections, belief change, curiosity, mechanism, call-outs) lives in the **Copywriting Fundamentals** doc. Order bumps, OTOs, down-sells and post-purchase live on **⑦ Cart, Checkout & Upsell**.

## 1. Why nine-figure funnels win: the meta-secrets
No single teardown names these. They show up when you line 50 brands up side by side. Each one ends with the move to make.
### 1.1 The belief chain is relayed in the ad's own words
**What it is.** The big brands install one chain of beliefs across the whole funnel. The ad installs belief one. The lander picks up that belief in its hero, in the ad's exact noun phrase, number or offer, and installs the next. The PDP handles what is left (price, fit, "will it work for me"). Cart and checkout handle the last fear (risk, renewal). Post-purchase reassures, then sells the next problem. No page restarts the argument.
**Why it works.** The reader checks "is this what I clicked?" in under a second, and checks literal words and numbers first. A page that continues the thought keeps the momentum. A page that restarts the pitch feels like a different conversation, so they bounce.
**Evidence.**
- **Hiya.** Ad: "Vitamins in the morning, wind-down time at night… buy the Multivitamin and get Bedtime free for month one. Pediatrician-approved." Lander hero: "Day & Night Duo / First Month of Bedtime on Us! / Buy Multivitamin, get Bedtime FREE." The PDP runs the villain ("Many kids vitamins are sugar-coated gummies stored in plastic bottles. Hiya is different. See how we compare."), the FAQ pays it off ("as much sugar as in an Oreo cookie"), and the reviews end up reciting the brand's words: "Zero sugar. Zero gummy additives. Zero cavities."
- **MUD\\WTR.** Ad: "Most 'mushroom coffees' = regular coffee with a fairy-dust sprinkle. MUD\\WTR's OG blend = zero coffee." Lander H1: "6 Ways MUD\\WTR Original Is Different From Mushroom Coffee." The ad's enemy is the page's headline.
- **Sassy Saints.** Ad: "your nails aren't damaged, they're depleted… 3 lipids." Lander reason #1: "Your nails aren't damaged, they're dried out… three lipids."
- **Ridge.** Ad lists "five ways to power." Lander section: "FIVE WAYS TO CHARGE. ONE DEVICE." **AG1**'s ad and lander both say "Save 40% in your first month."
- **True Classic advertorial.** Each of the 5 reasons answers the doubt the last one raised: tighter fit (1), "Say goodbye to itchy and tight" (2), price (3), proof (4), guarantee (5).
- **Weak matches in the same set:** Ryze's generic ad into a generic listicle, HexClad's durability angle buried at reason #1 instead of the hero, Cozy Earth's "THIS SUMMER- SLEEP COOLER" still live in October.
**Do this:** write the ad first, lift its exact noun phrase, number and offer into the H1 or reason 1, and write down which belief each page in the funnel owns.
### 1.2 Offer architecture does more lifting than prose
**What it is.** On consumables, the sale is engineered in the buy box before any copy is read. The pattern repeats: subscription is the default and the cheaper, more generous path. The one-time option is defined by what it lacks. Gifts carry dollar values. Price is shown in the smallest honest unit. The food and bev teardown put it plainly: MUD\\WTR and Javy "win on offer design, not prose." Nikita's CRO notes put it first on the list: "FIX THE OFFER FIRST (offer = price, bundles, upsells, guarantees)."
**Why it works.** People compare options, not absolute prices. When the alternative is visibly worse (no free shipping, no discount, no gift), the default stops feeling like a push and starts feeling like the smart choice. A per-day price drags the number below the pain threshold.
**Evidence.**
- **Grüns:** "Subscribe & Save \$29.99 \$66.65 \$1.07/day · Free Shipping Today · Pause Or Cancel Any Time · 30-Day Money-Back Guarantee · Free gifts included" against "One Time Purchase \$66.65 \$2.38/day · No Free Shipping · No First Order Discount."
- **MUD\\WTR:** "Subscribe and Save (Most Popular): ~~\$60~~ \$34 (43% OFF) \| \$1.14/serving" vs "One-Time Purchase: \$60 \| \$2/serving," with gifts itemized: "Free frother w/ 1st order (\$20 value)," "Free shipping (\$5 value)."
- **im8** stretches one number both ways: "Replaces \$4,000+/year in supplements for \$6 a day."
- **AG1** (EU): "WITHOUT AG1: UP TO 15 SEPARATE SUPPLEMENTS 5,03 € per day / WITH AG1: 2,90 € per day / Save 64 €/mo."
- Free shipping on subscriptions only is the norm (Hiya, AG1, Olipop, Dr. Squatch, Bloom). The one-time pays shipping.
**Do this:** design the buy box (default, alternative, unit price, gifts, guarantee) before you write a word of page copy.
### 1.3 Claim drift is the silent killer
**What it is.** The same fact stated differently on different surfaces: a discount, a guarantee length, a review count, a price per serving. Measured on live funnels:
- GroundingWell: ad "UP TO 67% OFF," lander "50% Off."
- Dr. Squatch: ad "59% OFF," page 56%.
- Javvy: ad "less than 30 cents per serving," lander "under 50¢."
- Miracle: ad "Try them for 30 days," page "first 100 days."
- Mellow: the advertorial says the cover "zips off and goes in the washing machine," the PDP says "The pillow itself is not machine washable." Three different review counts (2,400 / 34,672 / 50,868).
- Grüns: "100M+ Gummies Sold" on one page, "2B+" on another.
- Hiya: ad "6,000+ parents," page "11,000+ Reviews \| Millions of Happy Parents."
**Why it kills.** A mismatched number reads as a lie even when both numbers are true. At nine figures, brand equity absorbs it. For a new store, every drift is a refund request, a chargeback or a ticket that starts "your ad said…".
**Do this:** make one number sheet per offer before the first ad goes live, and check every ad and page against it each time the offer changes (F3).
### 1.4 Proof is shown as artifacts, not adjectives
**What it is.** The winners rarely say "trusted" or "proven." They show the thing that proves it: a dated lab badge, a certificate number, a study ID, a screenshot, an un-rounded count.
**Why it works.** An adjective asks for belief. An artifact lets the reader verify it, or feel that they could. Un-rounded numbers read as pulled from a system.
**Evidence.**
- Grüns: "Tested by Light Labs in SEP 2026 / 35 substances tested." Our Place: "Lab-verified by Light Labs · May 2026 · 692 substances screened · See the report."
- Caraway: "Third-Party Tested for Safety • See Lab Results / This product complies with CA AB1200." HexClad: a PFAS table with every row "Non-Detect."
- Brooklinen prints its OEKO-TEX certificate number. im8 cites a [clinicaltrials.gov](http://clinicaltrials.gov) NCT number. MUD\\WTR's lander footer lists real PMC and NCT IDs by benefit.
- GroundingWell's listicle embeds a screenshot of Facebook comments, a collage of research abstracts and a "90 DAYS RISK-FREE" seal.
- Miracle's review cards show the exact variant bought: "ITEM TYPE: Miracle Sheets (Stone) - Extra Luxe."
- Un-rounded counts: "24,078+ FIVE-STAR REVIEWS" (Miracle), "757 clinicians" (GroundingWell), "Based on a 2026 survey with 3,501 Oura members" (Oura).
**Do this:** replace every "trusted," "proven" or "clinically backed" with the artifact itself, and link it where you can.
### 1.5 One universal body, many swappable heroes
**What it is.** The big brands build one body (mechanism, proof, offer, guarantee, FAQ) and swap only the top of the page per angle, segment, creator or season.
**Why it works.** Message match lives in the first screen. The conversion logic lives in the body, and you only want to build and test that once. Heroes are cheap, so every ad can get a matching page.
**Evidence.**
- **The Farmer's Dog** need-state picker rewrites the quiz intro headline for each of 5 choices ("Better food for picky eaters starts here," "Better food for more good years starts here") while the rest of the screen stays put.
- **ARMRA** rotates personalized hooks above the fold: "You built the stack. This goes underneath it." / "The routine underneath your routine." / "One step that earns its place in the morning."
- **Grüns** runs a GLP-1 version ("7 Benefits GLP-1 Users Love About Grüns (Besides the Poops)"), a men's version (/pages/nutrition-support-men) and a slug per celebrity (`benefits-listicle-db`).
- Five brands (Miracle, Ryze, GroundingWell, Javvy, Sassy Saints) share an almost identical listicle skeleton, down to "Reaching #N means one thing." The skeleton is a commodity. The edge is the specificity inside the hero and the reasons.
- Pages die fast. Lume's `/fb-pro-2` was a 404 within about 3 weeks of last-seen. Magic Spoon turned a retired listicle slug into a redirect to the new product page with the subscription preselected (`?selling_plan=`).
**Do this:** build the body once and make the hero a module you can clone per ad angle, segment or creator (Zone 3.5).
### 1.6 Subscription anxiety is the category wound
**What it is.** Trustpilot gap analysis for Hiya, MUD\\WTR and Dr. Squatch is dominated by "hard to cancel / unauthorized recurring charges / forced subscription." The best pages state the renewal terms in plain words in the hero or right under the button.
**Why it works.** Fear of a cancel trap blocks the subscription click more than price does. Naming the terms early turns a hidden risk into a visible promise.
**Evidence.** Hiya hero card: "Free shipping · Limited time offer / Renews monthly at full price." Hiya under the button: "\$15 each today, \$30 each on a monthly basis thereafter." Lume's "Why subscribe?" list: "We'll remind you 3 days before it ships." Dr. Squatch under the button: "Free shipping / Cancel anytime / Pause or skip anytime." Hims: "\$39 for the first month, auto-renews at \$149/month." The anti-pattern: Ollie's checkout never showed a dollar renewal figure, and Dr. Squatch's thank-you page offers (Rokt) show up in complaints as "third-party post-purchase offers causing unexpected charges."
**Do this:** put the renewal price, cadence and how to pause or cancel in the hero offer card and under the button. If your cancel is easy, sell it.
### 1.7 Volunteered negatives are a conversion device
**What it is.** The brand raises a limitation before the customer finds it: a mixed rating, a fee, a cosmetic flaw, a weak study design.
**Why it works.** A visible admission makes every other claim more believable. It removes the "too good to be true" reflex and pre-empts the one-star review.
**Evidence.**
- AG1 shows "Rated 4,3 stars on Trustpilot" and "Reviews prior to \[Jan 2025\] reflect an earlier formulation."
- Hiya's review widget shows the full distribution, including 82 one-star reviews. Nomatic: "we never hide low ratings."
- Nécessaire FAQ: "No. This product does not hold The Seal of Approval by the National Eczema Association."
- HexClad FAQ: "Will my HexClad pans scratch? Yes, even a diamond can scratch!… only cosmetic." Brooklinen: "365 days… \$9.95 return fee."
- ARMRA footnotes its own study as "an open-label clinical research survey of 60 participants." GroundingWell: "larger-scale research to support their product claims is still developing."
- LMNT publishes the DIY recipe for its own product in the FAQ.
**Do this:** put one true limitation on every claims-heavy page, in your words, before the customer can find it (F15).
### 1.8 Authority is borrowed and placed at the exact claim
**What it is.** The brand rarely makes its hardest claim in its own voice. A named expert, a lab, a customer or an institution says it, and that quote sits next to the claim it backs.
**Why it works.** Readers discount the brand's voice and trust the third party. With the expert at the claim, the doubt and the answer show up together.
**Evidence.** Hiya's experts say "junk" so the brand doesn't have to ("So many vitamins are loaded with excess sugar and junk. Hiya is really clean."). Hiya's Multivitamin + Probiotic lander puts a pediatric dentist quote inside reason #2 (sugar and cavities). AG1 and GroundingWell use FrontrowMD with the incentive objection pre-answered ("650 clinicians share this without compensation"). im8's Lily Collins gates her own endorsement: "I was a customer of IM8 for months before I became a partner."
**Do this:** find the claim the reader doubts most and put a real, named third party saying it right beside that claim (Zone 3.2).
### 1.9 Testimonials are cast, not collected
**What it is.** Each review on the page is picked to do one job: answer a specific objection, echo a specific claim or match a specific buyer segment.
**Why it works.** A wall of praise proves volume. A cast set proves the argument, in the customer's mouth, saying what the brand can't say itself.
**Evidence.** True Classic's three advertorial reviews map to the social reward, the fit mechanism and the odor and softness objections. Ryze picks "As a Momma of 3" and "still sleep soundly at night since it only has half the caffeine." Eight Sleep tabs its reviews "Hot sleepers / Couples / Hot flashes / Insomnia." Dr. Squatch's offer page uses a review that defuses the ad itself: "It's the first Instagram advertised product I have purchased."
**Do this:** list your top five objections and your main segments, then place one real review for each job (Zone 3.2, Testimonial casting).
### 1.10 Retention copy is written into acquisition pages
**What it is.** The page that sells the first order already defends the subscription by telling the buyer when results show up.
**Why it works.** Most cancels happen when nothing visible happens in week one. A timeline set before purchase turns "it's not working" into "right on schedule."
**Evidence.** ARMRA: "Results build with consistency. Some people notice changes quickly, while others see and feel benefits over 4+ weeks." Grüns: "Great health doesn't happen overnight, but it does happen daily" plus a 3 / 14 / 30 day timeline. Seed: "7 Days: Reduces Bloating + Gas… 2 Weeks… 4 Weeks… 6 Weeks+." Create's testimonial: "The first week I wasn't sure anything was happening. By week two I wasn't as sore." Caraway and HexClad use the FAQ to teach correct use before the first cook, which prevents the bad review.
**Do this:** put a results timeline on every page that sells a subscription, and teach correct use before the first use (F14).
### 1.11 Popups segment first and discount second
**What it is.** The arrival popup asks one easy question before (or instead of) the email. The answer routes the next page, the email flow or both.
**Why it works.** One tap is easier than typing an email. Once someone has answered, the email feels like the natural next step, and the answer becomes data that personalizes follow-up.
**Evidence.** MUD\\WTR: "You've Got Up To 43% Off / What benefit matters most to you? MORE ENERGY / BETTER FOCUS / HAPPIER GUT / HEALTHIER BODY." Graza: "What do you use olive oil for?" Nécessaire: "What are you shopping for? Body / Hair." Jones Road: "WHAT IS YOUR SKIN TYPE?" Caraway asks what you're shopping for, then the email, and the offer is "\$50 Off Your Second Purchase," which protects first-order margin.
**Do this:** make the arrival popup one tap-to-answer question, then the email, then route the email flow by the answer (Zone 3.6).
### 1.12 The face in the ad is the proof on the page
**What it is.** Creator and whitelisted ads (ads run from a person's or publisher's page) land on pages built around that same person.
**Why it works.** The click was a decision to trust that face. If the page drops them, the trust has nowhere to go.
**Evidence.** Ridge's top ad "pages" are individuals' profiles, and the MKBHD lander uses his quote as the proof block ("Over my 15 years of reviewing tech, the Ridge power bank quickly became my favorite"). MUD\\WTR's founder page "Shane Heath Art" runs 15 ads into a page with an "Our Founding Story" video. Mellow ran 130 days from the Scary Mommy page into a "SPECIAL REPORT" story advertorial.
**Do this:** give every creator, founder or publisher ad set its own lander, with that person's face and quote in the hero.
### 1.13 Trust has two winning schools. Pick by audience.
**What it is.** Premium-restraint brands (Seed, ARMRA, LMNT, AG1, Nécessaire, Vuori, Tecovas) win through mechanism, third-party verification and calm design, with little or no urgency. Volume brands (Ryze, Grüns, Miracle, Javvy, Nomatic) win through huge review counts, gift stacks, countdowns and personality. Liquid Death converts with almost no reviews and no guarantee, on identity and comedy. The premium-PDP video's definition holds for both: "Premium = something people BEG to buy = TRUST."
**Why it matters.** Both work at nine figures. But the brand-film homepages (Vuori, Tecovas, Oura, Hatch) lean on a brand that already exists and convert on the PDP. A new brand has no equity to lean on.
**Do this:** copy the proof-forward structure either way, then choose restraint or volume by how skeptical and how deal-driven your buyer is.
### 1.14 The big brands test everything and guess nothing
**What it is.** Intelligems (on-site A/B and price testing) runs on Hiya, Ridge, Olipop, MUD\\WTR, True Classic and HexClad, and on the GroundingWell and Sassy Saints listicles. Seed uses Optimizely, AG1 uses VWO. Fairing post-purchase surveys ("how'd you hear about us") show up at scale because operators no longer trust pixel attribution.
**What it implies.**
- Price, offer and layout are test variables, not one-time decisions.
- Pages show the traces. Sassy Saints' title tag says 11 reasons, the URL says 9, the H1 says 7.
- The live winners you copy are a snapshot of someone's current test. Copy the structure, test the specifics.
**Do this:** install a testing tool before launch, and make price, offer card and hero your first three tests.

## 2. Fundamentals
Always on, on every page type. Ordered by impact. Each has the rule, why it works, a weak and a strong example, and who does it.
### F1. One page, one job, one next step
- **Rule:** before building, write the single job of the page and the single action it ends on. Cut or move anything that doesn't serve that job.
- **Why it works:** every extra choice is an exit. A page with one destination makes the next step feel obvious.
- **Weak:** a lander with a nav bar, three product tiles, a blog link and a "Shop all" button *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** the listicle offer card that is the only boxed element on the page, so it reads as the destination (Miracle, Ryze).
- **Evidence:** Nikita's notes: "Know the POINT of your advertorial before you make it," "HOMEPAGE HAS 1 JOB → clarity, value, direction," "PDP has 3 jobs → INFORM, PERSUADE, DRIVE ACTION," "ORDER PAGE: as little friction as possible, as clear as possible." Hims routes every CTA to one intake. Liquid Death's water PDP exists to send you to "SHOP IN STORE."
### F2. The top of the page continues the ad
- **Rule:** the hero headline, subhead and visual repeat the ad's exact angle, noun phrase, number and offer. Same discount, same guarantee length, same segment. Write the hero after the ad.
- **Why it works:** the first-second check is "is this what I clicked?" A mismatch is the biggest single bounce leak (meta-secret 1.1).
- **Weak:** ad "Most cast iron rusts in a month," lander H1 "Cookware for every kitchen" *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** ad "Most 'mushroom coffees' = regular coffee with a fairy-dust sprinkle," lander H1 "6 Ways MUD\\WTR Original Is Different From Mushroom Coffee" (MUD\\WTR).
- **Evidence:** Hiya Day & Night, Sassy Saints ("depleted… 3 lipids" into reason #1), Ridge ("FIVE WAYS TO CHARGE. ONE DEVICE."), AG1 ("Save 40% in your first month"), Grüns GLP-1 ("💊 FOR GLP-1 USERS"). → Copywriting doc: Slippery slide.
### F3. Every claim is literally true and identical everywhere
- **Rule:** keep one number sheet per offer: price, discount %, per-day price, guarantee length, shipping terms, review count, customer count, study numbers. Ads, landers, PDP, cart and emails all pull from it. Update the sheet first, then every surface.
- **Why it works:** drift reads as lying even when both numbers are true, and it triggers refunds and chargebacks (meta-secret 1.3).
- **Weak:** ad "30-day guarantee," page "100-night trial," FAQ "60 days" *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "60-day money-back guarantee" word for word in the ad, the hero, under the button, in the FAQ and at checkout *(illustrative)*.
- **Evidence:** drift measured live on GroundingWell ("UP TO 67% OFF" vs "50% Off"), Dr. Squatch (59% vs 56%), Javvy ("less than 30 cents" vs "under 50¢"), Miracle (30 vs 100 days), Mellow (three review counts), Grüns ("100M+" vs "2B+"), Hiya ("6,000+ parents" vs "11,000+ Reviews").
### F4. Above the fold answers three questions
- **Rule:** without scrolling, the visitor knows what they get, what makes it different, and why they can feel safe buying. Hero = a headline that says exactly what you offer, a subhead on why it matters to them, the product front and center, one obvious action.
- **Why it works:** the first screen is the only one everyone sees. If it doesn't answer the three questions, most visitors never reach the part that does.
- **Weak:** a lifestyle photo, the brand name, "Shop Now" *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** rating and count, a one-line promise, 3 to 6 claim chips, price with savings, one button, a guarantee line under it (GroundingWell PDP: "Reduces Insomnia · Anti-Inflammation · Eases Anxiety…" chips).
- **Evidence:** premium-PDP video: above the fold is "the only section 100% see; \~60% never scroll past an un-optimized one." Nikita: "Hero = first (sometimes only) chance to answer the customer's questions." Miracle KSP, Bloom ("Bloat Relief · Energy · Gut Health"), Seed's subhead ("clinically validated to relieve bloating, gas, and irregularity").
### F5. Proof before claim
- **Rule:** put the review count, rating or authority line above or right beside the headline or product title. On mobile, above the title.
- **Why it works:** people read top to bottom. Trust installed first colors every line read after it.
- **Weak:** reviews start in a carousel nine screens down *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "40,000+ Reviews" as the eyebrow above the advertorial H1 (True Classic).
- **Evidence:** Ridge shows "51,824" above the product name. Miracle puts "24,078+ FIVE-STAR REVIEWS" above the H1. Grüns runs "1,600+ clinicians shared this with their patients without compensation" above its listicle H1. Magic Spoon's sticky "80,000+ 5-Star Reviews" banner. Premium-PDP video: "MOBILE ORDER: social proof FIRST (above the title)." The weak pages in the set (Vuori, Bombas, Oura, Hatch) bury proof mid-page or leave it off.
### F6. Build the offer before the page
- **Rule:** decide the default option, the alternative, the unit price, the gifts and their values, and the named guarantee before writing copy. Subscription or the multi-pack is pre-selected and badged. The alternative is defined by what it lacks.
- **Why it works:** people judge a price against the option next to it. A visibly worse alternative makes the default feel like the smart choice without any pressure in the copy (meta-secret 1.2).
- **Weak:** one price, one "Add to cart," a guarantee in the footer *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "Subscribe & Save \$29.99 \$66.65 \$1.07/day · Free Shipping Today · Pause Or Cancel Any Time · 30-Day Money-Back Guarantee · Free gifts included" against "One Time Purchase \$66.65 \$2.38/day · No Free Shipping · No First Order Discount" (Grüns).
- **Evidence:** Nikita's CRO notes, item 1: "FIX THE OFFER FIRST (offer = price, bundles, upsells, guarantees)." MUD\\WTR and Javy "win on offer design, not prose." Every consumable brand in the set leads with subscription (Hiya, AG1, Olipop, MUD\\WTR, Dr. Squatch, Bloom, Javy, Seed). → Zone 3.1.
### F7. Price is never shown alone
- **Rule:** every price gets an anchor (compare-at or the one-time price), the saving spelled out in \$ and %, and the smallest honest unit (per day, per serving, per shirt, per week, per installment).
- **Why it works:** a price with no reference point gets judged against the buyer's gut, which is always cheaper. The unit price shrinks the number below the pain threshold.
- **Weak:** "\$89" *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "\$214 / \$139 / 35% OFF - YOU SAVE \$75" (Miracle).
- **Evidence:** premium-PDP video: "Price + savings: spell the saving out in \$/%. Don't make them do math." True Classic "\$21.66/shirt," Ollie "~~\$48.71~~ \$14.61/Week," Chomps "\$2.56/Stick," HexClad "Set Value: \$999.00 / Set Savings: -\$300.00 / You Pay: \$699.00 / Total Savings: \$300.00."
### F8. Risk reversal and money terms sit under the button
- **Rule:** directly under every primary button, state the guarantee, the cancel terms, the renewal price and cadence, and the shipping terms in specific words. Then repeat the guarantee as its own section. Say which discounts are first-order only. State fees and exclusions honestly.
- **Why it works:** the button is where fear peaks. Hidden terms are the #1 complaint across subscription brands, so plain terms work as a trust device and a refund shield at once (meta-secret 1.6).
- **Weak:** "Satisfaction guaranteed" and "*Terms apply" in the footer *(illustrative)*. Strong:*\* "Try Miracle for 100 nights. Donʼt like it? Get a 100% refund. No questions." under the button and again as a section (Miracle). "\$15 each today, \$30 each on a monthly basis thereafter" under the button (Hiya).
- **Evidence:** premium-PDP video: take the biggest fear-reducer and "EXPOSE + EXPLAIN it." Nikita's CRO notes: "POLICIES AS TRUST SIGNALS — don't bury in footer; put them right under the add-to-cart button." LMNT ("No Questions Asked Refund Policy" under ATC), Our Place ("100-Day Trial · Free Shipping & Returns · 3-Year Warranty"), Dr. Squatch ("Free shipping / Cancel anytime / Pause or skip anytime"), Hims ("\$39 for the first month, auto-renews at \$149/month"), Brooklinen ("\$9.95 return fee"), Ridge (trial and warranty in the sitewide top bar).
### F9. Social proof has to be believable
- **Rule:** show the source, names, faces, video and specifics (date, variant bought, verified tag). Show it up front, not in a slider. Give the proof section a specific headline. Every proof image shows your buyer.
- **Why it works:** generic praise reads as manufactured. Specifics read as pulled from a real system, and a grid shows volume at a glance.
- **Weak:** "What our customers say" over three anonymous 5-star quotes in a carousel *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "Join 1 million+ enjoying hotel-quality sleep with cooler, fresher sheets" over a photo grid with first name, verified tag, date and variant (premium-PDP video).
- **Evidence:** premium-PDP video: sliders get under 1 to 2% interaction, and "Real people, real results" is "garbage." Nikita: "Every image targets the ICP." Miracle shows the variant on each review, im8 shows its own widget and Trustpilot side by side, HexClad replies to nearly every review.
### F10. Objections and practical questions are answered where they come up
- **Rule:** handle the biggest objection inside the section that creates it, not only in the FAQ. Put the practical answers (sizing, shipping timeline, how to use) on the page. Keep the FAQ, but treat it as selling: first accordion open, biggest selling point inside it.
- **Why it works:** an unanswered question at the moment it forms is a bounce. Nikita's CRO notes: "bounce = unanswered questions."
- **Weak:** "Is it too spicy?" answered only in a closed FAQ at the bottom *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "Intensely flavorful yet not off-the-charts spicy" inside the product description (Fly By Jing).
- **Evidence:** Nikita: "handle the biggest/most important ones EARLIER via embedded social proof + embedded FAQs," and weak product info means missing "sizing charts, shipping timelines, clear benefit explanations." Premium-PDP video: dropdowns get 1 to 2% engagement, so keep the first one open. Hiya FAQ ("If there's no added sugar, how does your vitamin taste so sweet?"), Ryze FAQ ("Will they make me trip?"), Jones Road ("Will this clog my pores? No"). → Copywriting doc: Anticipate and resolve objections.
### F11. Benefit in the headline, spec in the body
- **Rule:** every section headline is the outcome or a felt benefit. The number, material or mechanism goes underneath as the proof. Cover benefits, features and outcomes on the page.
- **Why it works:** most people read only the headlines. A spec headline makes them do the translation. A benefit headline does it for them.
- **Weak:** "Triple-layer construction" or "Premium quality, luxurious feeling" *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "A Pocket For Everything" over "12 internal pockets…" (Nomatic). "Wake up refreshed, not sweaty" (Miracle).
- **Evidence:** premium-PDP video: "Benefit in the HEADLINE (most read only headlines)… Body explains HOW." Nikita: "Have BENEFITS + FEATURES + OUTCOMES all three on the page." Our Place ("Note the Nonstick"), HexClad ("Unbeatable Searing Power"), Eight Sleep ("Quit fighting over the thermostat"). → Copywriting doc: Sell the cure.
### F12. Images carry the argument
- **Rule:** treat every gallery, hero and section image as an argument with a caption. Plan the sequence: product, pain or old way, value props, how it works, proof, comparison, guarantee, lifestyle sprinkled in.
- **Why it works:** product imagery is the most-clicked element on the page, and many visitors decide from images alone. Copy and media do most of the selling.
- **Weak:** eight pretty lifestyle photos with no text *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "WIDE OPEN ACCESS \| Granting access to 22 purposeful pockets" burned onto the open-bag shot (Nomatic).
- **Evidence:** premium-PDP video: "Operate as if they ONLY see the images" and "Infographic imagery (image + text) beats pure lifestyle." Nikita: "COPY + MEDIA are the two that matter most," and from Caples, "Don't run pictures without captions." Ridge (7 "Infographic" slides), HexClad, Blueland, Lume, Olipop. → Zone 3.7.
### F13. One enemy, named the same way on every page
- **Rule:** pick the villain (an ingredient, a method, a category default, a habit) and use the same words for it from ad to FAQ. Attack the method, not a competitor by name, unless you have hard numbers.
- **Why it works:** repetition installs the belief. By the review section, customers use your enemy words for you.
- **Weak:** "other brands" on the lander, "cheap alternatives" on the PDP, "generic products" in the FAQ *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "Many kids vitamins are sugar-coated gummies stored in plastic bottles," carried from PDP to FAQ ("as much sugar as in an Oreo cookie") until reviews repeat "Zero sugar. Zero gummy additives. Zero cavities." (Hiya).
- **Evidence:** Caraway, Our Place and HexClad ("forever chemicals"), The Farmer's Dog ("Brown Pellets," "3% beef"), MUD\\WTR ("without the crash"), Mando and Lume (odor masked after it forms vs stopped before it starts). → Copywriting doc: Us vs them, Silent problem.
### F14. Expectations are set on a timeline
- **Rule:** tell the buyer when they will notice what: day, week, month, or night 1, week 1, week 4. Teach correct use before the first use.
- **Why it works:** it answers "will it work?" and protects the subscription from the week-one cancel (meta-secret 1.10).
- **Weak:** "Results may vary" *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "Some people notice changes quickly, while others see and feel benefits over 4+ weeks" (ARMRA).
- **Evidence:** Seed ("7 Days: Reduces Bloating + Gas... 2 Weeks: Supports Healthy Regularity... 4 Weeks: Smooths Skin Aging... 6 Weeks+: Whole Body Health."), Grüns (3 / 14 / 30 days), Create, Mellow's "Night 1, Morning 1, Week 1 … Week 4" pills, Sassy Saints (week-by-week as reason 5), GroundingWell ("Instantly / 15 Minutes / 1 Hour / 8 Hours / 1 Week / 1 Month+").
### F15. Volunteer one honest negative
- **Rule:** every page with heavy claims carries at least one admitted limit: a mixed review, a "not for you if," a fee, a "yes, it can scratch."
- **Why it works:** the admission buys belief for every other claim (meta-secret 1.7).
- **Weak:** a page of 100% five-star praise and absolute claims *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "Use it when you need carry-on capacity rather than an under-seat personal item" (Nomatic).
- **Evidence:** AG1 ("Rated 4,3 stars on Trustpilot"), Hiya (82 one-star reviews on show), Nécessaire (no eczema seal), HexClad ("Yes, even a diamond can scratch!"), Caraway ("Do I need to use oil or butter? Yes—just a little!"), Bearaby ("A handful of our customers actually found the Cotton Napper too cool"). → Copywriting doc: Honesty.
### F16. Mobile first, thumb first
- **Rule:** design at phone width first. Single column, big tap targets, options as chips not dropdowns, a sticky buy bar or sticky CTA.
- **Why it works:** paid social traffic is mobile. A dropdown or a button that scrolls away costs a tap the buyer may not make.
- **Weak:** a size dropdown and a buy button that scrolls off screen *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** a sticky bar reading "YES, GET 50% OFF ✓ SPECIAL DISCOUNT ACTIVE" on the shared listicle template (Miracle, Ryze, GroundingWell, Javvy, Sassy Saints).
- **Evidence:** every forensics render was done at 430px for this reason. Olipop's sticky mini buy-bar, Miracle size chips, Cozy Earth, Brooklinen, all quizzes. → Zone 3.7.
### F17. Capture everyone, and promise something useful for the email
- **Rule:** every page that can bounce has a capture point. The capture promise is a deliverable ("your plan," "your results," "your match"), not "stay in touch."
- **Why it works:** most visitors don't buy on the first visit. What the gate promises sets the open rate of email 1.
- **Weak:** "Sign up for our newsletter" *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "Where Should We Send Your Results? We'll send your shade matches along with application tips" (Jones Road).
- **Evidence:** Nikita's Chad funnel: "collect everyone's email even non-buyers → convert later via email/SMS." The Farmer's Dog ("You can use your email to access your recommendations anytime"), Gymshark OOS "Stock alerts." Native's weak version: "drops, sales, and more." → Zone 3.6.
### F18. Low clutter, two-click navigation
- **Rule:** on landers and advertorials, no nav. On store pages, anything is findable in two clicks. Copy is neither a wall nor a single line under a blurry photo. Long pages are fine when every section gives a reason to keep scrolling.
- **Why it works:** clutter and dead ends make people leave. Length does not, if each section earns the next.
- **Weak:** a mega-menu with 40 links over a product page *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** a single-column lander with a sticky bottom button and no exits but the offer (Mellow's advertorial, the shared listicle template).
- **Evidence:** Nikita's CRO mistakes: "Cluttered navigation → if they can't find it in two clicks, they leave," and "Too much OR too little text." Premium-PDP video: "Long pages are fine — people read and scroll IF given a reason." Hims quiz lander.
### F19. Same brand, same voice, to the edges
- **Rule:** colors, fonts, tone and naming stay consistent from the ad to the 404 page. Microcopy (empty cart, error page, popup decline, email consent) is written, not left as default.
- **Why it works:** inconsistency reads as a cheap or fake store. Voice at the edges tells the buyer a real team is behind it.
- **Weak:** a default "Your cart is empty" and a generic 404 *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "Empty cart, full potential." (David). "Oops! Unlike This Page, Lume Is ALWAYS Working... EVERYWHERE!" (Lume 404).
- **Evidence:** Nikita's CRO notes list "Inconsistent branding" as a trust killer. Liquid Death email consent ("agree to be brainwashed by Liquid Death marketing"), Ridge 404 ("engineered properly, not rushed"), Native empty cart ("Smells like... nothing. Time to shop!").

## 3. Techniques
Pick per goal. This page owns these, so other pages point here by zone number and only add their placement.
### Zone 3.1: Offer and pricing
- **Subscribe as the default.** Subscription pre-selected and badged "Most Popular" or "Best Value."
	- Swipe: "Subscribe and Save (Most Popular): ~~\$60~~ \$34 (43% OFF) \| \$1.14/serving" (MUD\\WTR)
	- **Good for →** any consumable or refill product.
	- **When to use →** whenever the product gets used up. Pair it with the renewal and cancel terms (Zone 3.3).
- **The one-time defined by what it lacks.** List what the one-time buyer gives up, next to the price.
	- Swipe: "One Time Purchase \$66.65 \$2.38/day · No Free Shipping · No First Order Discount" (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** pushing the default without pressure copy.
	- **When to use →** any two-option buy box. List only losses that are true.
- **Per-day or per-unit price.** Divide the price to the smallest honest unit.
	- Swipe: "As low as \$0.87/Serving" (ARMRA); "\$21.66/shirt" (True Classic); "\$14.61/Week" (Ollie); "Treatments start as low as \$2/dose" (Hims)
	- **Good for →** premium prices, subscriptions, bundles.
	- **When to use →** every buy box, offer card and quiz reveal.
- **Dual-scale number.** The same math stretched big for the saving and small for the cost.
	- Swipe: "Replaces \$4,000+/year in supplements for \$6 a day" (im8)
	- **Good for →** products that replace several things or a recurring spend.
	- **When to use →** hero subheads, offer cards, ad hooks.
- **Replacement math table.** Itemize what the buyer pays now, total it, put your price beside it, and let them subtract.
	- Swipe: "Your traditional routine \$463.84" vs "Whole-body benefits with ARMRA® \$119.99" (ARMRA); "if your go-to order is \$5–7 a cup, that's potentially \$1,500–2,500 a year" (Caraway coffee quiz)
	- **Good for →** high sticker prices.
	- **When to use →** lander, PDP, quiz reveal. Use real competitor prices.
- **Gift stacking with dollar values.** Free items itemized, each with a struck-through value.
	- Swipe: "Pumpkin Spice Creamer ~~\$14~~ FREE, Mush Magnet ~~\$5~~ FREE, Spoon & Scoop ~~\$10~~ FREE, Acacia Jar ~~\$25~~ FREE, US Shipping ~~\$4~~ FREE" (Ryze); "Welcome Kit free ~~€28~~… Total €87 ~~€156~~" (AG1)
	- **Good for →** first-order conversion, making the subscription feel generous as well as cheap.
	- **When to use →** offer cards and buy boxes. Digital gifts work too and cost nothing to ship.
- **Choose-your-gift.** Turn the bonus into a decision the buyer makes.
	- Swipe: "SELECT ONE FREE GIFT WITH PURCHASE: 4.5QT Deep Sauté \$189 Value / Griddle \$159 Value" (HexClad); "Choose Biscuit's free gift" (Ollie)
	- **Good for →** creating ownership before payment.
	- **When to use →** buy box, post-quiz, pre-checkout.
- **Quantity ladder with per-unit drop.** Packs with escalating savings, one tier tagged.
	- Swipe: "1 bottle \$17.47/ea → 2 @ \$16.77 → 3 @ \$16.07 → 4+ @ \$15.37/ea ("BEST DEAL")" (Javy); "10-Pack Save \$213 / 6-Pack Save \$105 / 3-Pack Save \$40" (True Classic)
	- **Good for →** AOV on products people use in multiples or give as gifts.
	- **When to use →** any buy box where more than one unit makes sense. Dr. Squatch only unlocks its 15% at 3 to 4 units, which pushes bigger first orders. Bombas ties giving to pack size: "4 Purchased = 4 Donated."
- **Tagged bundle tiers.** Two or three bundle cards, each with its dollar value and a tag, so one tier reads as the smart buy.
	- Swipe: "Best Seller — Cookware Duo (7 Piece) \$239.95 (\$284 Value)", "Most Savings — Cookware Set (13 Piece) \$359.95 (\$518 Value)" (Our Place)
	- **Good for →** AOV on sets and systems. The top tier mostly exists to sell the middle one.
	- **When to use →** when you have real sets of different sizes. Show a thumbnail of what's in each one.
- **Presupposition quantity question.** Ask how many, not whether.
	- Swipe: "How many beds in your home would you like to have GroundingWell Bedsheets on?" (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** household and multi-user products.
	- **When to use →** as the label on the quantity selector.
- **Kit builder inside the buy box.** Add-ons chosen without leaving the button.
	- Swipe: "Make It A Kit: Wallet / +KeyCase / +Power Bank / +Power Bank +KeyCase" (Ridge)
	- **Good for →** ecosystems and routines.
	- **When to use →** when you have 2 to 4 natural companions. Caraway runs a lighter "Don't Forget These."
- **Spend-threshold meter.** Visible progress toward free shipping or a gift.
	- Swipe: "FREE SHIPPING ON ORDERS \$15+ / FREE GIFT ON ORDERS \$35+ / FREE GIFT ON ORDERS \$55+" (Native); "Spend \$90 to unlock free shipping!" (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** carts with many low-price SKUs.
	- **When to use →** top bar and cart drawer, with thresholds set just above your median order.
- **Reason-why discount.** Give a harmless reason for the price cut so quality isn't questioned.
	- Swipe: "It's our third birthday, and we're lowering prices. Not just for a week but indefinitely" (Grüns); "We cut the markup so you get three premium quality t-shirts for less than the price of one designer tee!" (True Classic)
	- **Good for →** permanent price drops, low prices in a premium category.
	- **When to use →** any time the price looks too good to be true.
- **Guarantee scaled to commitment.** The longer plan gets the longer guarantee.
	- Swipe: "Three full months to experience the difference. If you don't feel it — complete refund, no questions." (im8, quarterly plan)
	- **Good for →** pushing the longer plan.
	- **When to use →** only if both guarantee lengths are real and stated identically everywhere.
- **Second-purchase discount for the email.** Buy the email without cutting first-order margin.
	- Swipe: "You've Got \$50 Off Your Second Purchase\*" (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** margin-sensitive stores.
	- **When to use →** the sitewide popup, when the first order already carries an offer.
- **Instant reward on the first order.** Loyalty points or perks credited at purchase, so the first order already pays something back.
	- Swipe: "Instantly receive 125 points on us!" (Chomps); "Earn 64 Lume Points." (Lume)
	- **Good for →** consumables with a loyalty program.
	- **When to use →** in the buy box, next to the subscription option.
- **Installments, HSA/FSA and verified-audience discounts.** Payment framing that shrinks or legitimizes the price.
	- Swipe: "Starting at \$44/mo or 0% APR with Affirm" (HexClad); "4 payments of \$32 with Shop Pay" (Caraway); "HSA/FSA eligible with Truemed" (MUD\\WTR); GovX discounts for military and first responders (Create, Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** premium tickets, health-adjacent products.
	- **When to use →** next to the price, only where you actually qualify.
- **Low-AOV entry item and prepaid multi-month.** A cheap first yes for cold traffic, or cash and retention taken up front.
	- Swipe: Dr. Squatch's longest-running ad (211 days) is a "\$16 lip-balm 4-pack"; MUD\\WTR sells prepaid 3-month SKUs (\~\$150)
	- **Good for →** cold traffic, cash flow, churn.
	- **When to use →** once the main offer is proven.
**After the first yes.** Order bumps, OTOs, down-sells and post-purchase upsells → ⑦ Cart, Checkout & Upsell. The rule that governs all of them is in the Chad funnel (section 4.3).
### Zone 3.2: Proof system
**Proof policy (stated once for the whole doc).** Use real, seeded, verifiable proof: real customers, real UGC, real lab numbers, real named experts with real credentials. Never fabricate specific reviews, lab results or named doctors. That is what freezes ad accounts and payment processors. Hiya is the model: real experts with titles ("Board-Certified Pediatrician," "Board-Certified Pediatric Gastroenterologist") plus a disclosure under testimonials ("Testimonials… may include individuals who have received compensation"). Our Place tags some reviews "Review from a paid tester." The pattern to avoid is Mellow's advertorial: a "By Dr. Ralph" byline over a fictional narrator named Sarah. Reviews, testimonials, before/afters, transformations and AI-generated creative are all fine to use.
- **Scale number as the headline.** The aggregate number stated as a headline, separate from the per-product star rating.
	- Swipe: "✨High Protein, 0-2g Sugar, and 80,000+ 5-Star Reviews✨" (Magic Spoon); "4,000,000+ Homes Have Made the Switch" (Blueland); "Find us in 65,000+ stores nationwide." (Chomps); "OVER 240 MILLION CUPS SERVED" (MUD\\WTR); "99% would recommend these products" (Graza)
	- **Good for →** any brand with real volume, retail reach or a high recommendation rate.
	- **When to use →** top bar, hero eyebrow, proof section headline.
- **Dated, linkable lab badge.** A certification with a date and a link to the result.
	- Swipe: "Tested by Light Labs in SEP 2026 / 35 substances tested" (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** anything ingested, applied or sold as "clean."
	- **When to use →** beside the buy box and in the proof section.
- **Clinician platform with the incentive pre-answered.** Third-party clinicians who say up front they weren't paid.
	- Swipe: "Clinicians' Choice — 757 clinicians share this on FrontrowMD without compensation" (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** health claims a skeptic can't verify alone.
	- **When to use →** near the buy box, and above lander headlines (Grüns).
- **Expert placed at the claim.** Each expert quote sits beside the one claim it backs.
	- Swipe: Hiya's pediatric dentist quote inside reason #2, the sugar and cavities reason (Hiya)
	- **Good for →** health, safety and technical claims.
	- **When to use →** every page with expert quotes. Don't pool them in one "experts" block only.
- **Credibility gate on endorsers.** Answer "they're paid" inside the endorsement itself.
	- Swipe: "I was a customer of IM8 for months before I became a partner." (Lily Collins, im8); "AG1 DRINKER SINCE 2021" (Hugh Jackman's title, AG1)
	- **Good for →** celebrity, creator and partner proof.
	- **When to use →** whenever the endorser has a commercial tie.
- **Credibility ladder.** Stack proof from institution to expert to elite user to the crowd, in that order.
	- Swipe: in-house research ("over 12 PhDs … 50 clinical studies") → named PhD advisory board → pro athletes → million-nights stat → review wall (Eight Sleep)
	- **Good for →** technical or expensive products where one proof type isn't enough.
	- **When to use →** homepage and long PDPs, as the proof section order.
- **Clinical-to-felt bridge.** Pair the study number with what it feels like.
	- Swipe: "The Clinical Trial said 95% felt more energy. Here's what that actually feels like." (im8)
	- **Good for →** studies and stats.
	- **When to use →** proof and review section headlines.
- **Hero stat with method footnote.** One big number, with its sample and date underneath.
	- Swipe: "86% of Oura Members see their health improve" / "Based on a 2026 survey with 3,501 Oura members after using Oura Ring for 30 days" (Oura); "90% of Hims users are satisfied with their treatment" / "Based on survey responses of 869 Hims ED users… Customers may have been compensated." (Hims)
	- **Good for →** outcome claims.
	- **When to use →** anywhere you cite a survey. Run your own customer survey to get one.
- **Testimonial casting.** Pick each quote for one job: an objection, a claim or a segment (meta-secret 1.9).
	- Swipe: "My friends keep asking if I've lost weight or been hitting the gym." (Brian G., True Classic, the social-reward job); "I was skeptical. A mattress cover that controls temperature sounded like a luxury gimmick. I was wrong." (Eight Sleep, the skeptic job)
	- **Good for →** every proof block.
	- **When to use →** map your top 5 objections, then find one real review for each.
- **Observer proof.** Someone else notices the result, or feels it without knowing.
	- Swipe: "My wife was like 'Wait, when did you start working out?!'" (True Classic); "Her husband also told her that he slept deeper, without even knowing he was sleeping on the grounding bedsheet." (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** results that could be called placebo or vanity.
	- **When to use →** lander reasons, review selection, ad hooks.
- **Review as argument.** Let the customer say the bold or blunt thing the brand can't.
	- Swipe: "a sweaty dude that does HVAC… no more swamp butt." (Mando); "I used this to stop drinking beer." (Liquid Death); "Works just as well as my previous life of toxic chemicals!!!" (Blueland)
	- **Good for →** taboo, bold or legally sensitive benefits.
	- **When to use →** pull lines straight from real reviews into headlines and section copy.
- **Tenure proof.** Durability or loyalty shown by years of use.
	- Swipe: "4 years and going strong… it has survived many many falls, a little fire, and a lot of water!" (Ridge); "I've been using AG1 since 2012" (Huberman, AG1)
	- **Good for →** durable goods and long-term habits.
	- **When to use →** review casting and endorsement titles.
- **Reviews filtered by the buyer's situation.** Filters or tabs so each visitor finds the reviewer like them.
	- Swipe: "Hot sleepers / Couples / Hot flashes / Insomnia" (Eight Sleep); Hiya filters by kid's age, wellness goal and "We try to limit sugar" (Hiya)
	- **Good for →** products with distinct segments.
	- **When to use →** PDP and lander review sections.
- **Review-theme chips.** Objections and praise themes turned into review navigation.
	- Swipe: "Free of PFAs," "Oven-Safe Pan," "Replaces my Cookware," "Literally Does it All" (Our Place)
	- **Good for →** big review sets.
	- **When to use →** above the review list.
- **AI review summary that keeps the cons.** A short summary of all reviews that names the weak spot too.
	- Swipe: "praised for its non-stick surface, ease of cleaning, and beautiful aesthetics... feedback on the heat-resistant handle is mixed" (Our Place); "Some note difficulty accessing cards… screws loosening over time" (Ridge)
	- **Good for →** high review counts.
	- **When to use →** above the review list.
- **Brand replies to reviews.** A named reply under reviews, critical ones first.
	- Swipe: HexClad replies to nearly every review; Bearaby replies to a 4-star "needs more colors" (HexClad, Bearaby)
	- **Good for →** signalling an accountable company.
	- **When to use →** always, and fastest on the critical ones.
- **Press quotes over press logos.** Quote the outlet's sentence instead of showing its logo.
	- Swipe: The Guardian: "a stunning generational leap for smart rings" (Oura); "If 'no-make-up' make-up is more your vibe, then Jones Road should be on your radar." (Jones Road); "Awarded Oprah's Favorite Things 2018" (Cozy Earth)
	- **Good for →** brands with real coverage or awards.
	- **When to use →** instead of an "as seen on" strip. The premium-PDP video: "'as seen on' logos are cheap now — use a QUOTE from the publication instead."
- **UGC video wall and wall-of-love grid.** Real customer video and photos in a grid, not a slider.
	- Swipe: "The buzz is real" with real creator handles (Manscaped); member photos, verified quotes and real X posts mixed in one wall (Eight Sleep)
	- **Good for →** anything visual or demonstrable.
	- **When to use →** PDP mid-page, lander after the mechanism.
### Zone 3.3: Trust and risk reversal
- **Named guarantee.** Give the guarantee a name and an emotion, not a policy title.
	- Swipe: "Perfect Fit, 100-Day Made to Last Guarantee" (True Classic); "100% Happiness Guarantee" (Bombas); "Feel the Difference or It's On Us." (AG1); "30 Day Sudisfaction Guarantee" (Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** every store.
	- **When to use →** under the button, as its own section, in the FAQ.
- **Guarantee as a dare.** Phrase the guarantee as the founder's confidence.
	- Swipe: "Taste it. Feel the difference. Decide." (AG1); "You Will Love It. Guaranteed." (Ryze)
	- **Good for →** products where trying is the proof.
	- **When to use →** guarantee section headline.
- **Us-vs-them guarantee.** Explain why your guarantee differs from the category's.
	- Swipe: "most other grounding companies offer 'guarantees' that only work if you never open the package… We do the opposite. We WANT you to try it." (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** categories with known return traps.
	- **When to use →** guarantee section on landers and PDPs.
- **Refund rate as proof.** Turn the guarantee into evidence by saying how few people use it.
	- Swipe: "Less than 1% of customers use our Money-Back Guarantee." (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** brands with a real low refund rate.
	- **When to use →** under the button. Only with a real number.
- **Guarantee as a numbered reason.** On listicles, the guarantee is its own reason near the end.
	- Swipe: "The 'perfect fit' guarantee" as reason 5 (True Classic); the "90 DAYS RISK-FREE" seal as #9 (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** listicles and advertorials.
	- **When to use →** second-to-last reason, after desire is built.
- **Trust bar in site chrome.** Guarantee, shipping and warranty pinned on every page.
	- Swipe: "99-DAY RISK-FREE TRIAL / LIFETIME GUARANTEE" (Ridge top bar); "Money-Back Guarantee / Free Shipping Over \$49 / 2-Year Warranty" (Manscaped)
	- **Good for →** stores with strong terms.
	- **When to use →** top bar or under the hero, sitewide.
- **Renewal and cancel terms stated up front.** Price after the first order, cadence and how to pause or cancel, in the hero and under the button (meta-secret 1.6).
	- Swipe: "Renews monthly at full price." (Hiya); "We'll remind you 3 days before it ships" (Lume); "Free shipping / Cancel anytime / Pause or skip anytime" (Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** any subscription.
	- **When to use →** hero offer card, under the button, at checkout.
- **Human fallback.** A real person behind the algorithm or the policy.
	- Swipe: "Send a no-makeup selfie to get matched by the experts." (Jones Road); "Or give us a call." (The Farmer's Dog)
	- **Good for →** fit, shade, size and dosage decisions.
	- **When to use →** quiz reveals, PDP buy box, FAQ.
- **Trusted second buy path.** Offer a marketplace the buyer already trusts, next to your own button.
	- Swipe: "Buy on Amazon — Free 2-Day Shipping \$34.99" (Bloom)
	- **Good for →** risk-averse buyers, brands already selling on a marketplace.
	- **When to use →** on the PDP, below your main option. Skip it if marketplace margin hurts.
- **Decision rules that remove the hard choice.** A one-line rule that picks the size, dose or model for them.
	- Swipe: "Your Napper should be roughly 10% of your body weight (150lb = 15lb Napper)" (Bearaby); "Under 5'7 Tall = Regular / Above 5'7 Tall = High" (Mellow); "Between sizes? Size up for relaxed, down for fitted." (True Classic); "Choose the 30L if… Choose the 40L if…" (Nomatic)
	- **Good for →** sizing, dosing, model choice.
	- **When to use →** in the buy box, at the selector.
- **FAQ that teaches correct use.** Prevent the bad review before the first use.
	- Swipe: "Always cook on a low to medium heat setting." (Caraway); "medium with HexClad equals medium-high with others." (HexClad)
	- **Good for →** products that fail when misused.
	- **When to use →** PDP FAQ and post-purchase email 1.
- **Flaw-to-feature reframe.** Name the known complaint as proof of quality.
	- Swipe: "the coveted ARMRA® clump, it's ARMRA Colostrum's purity on full display." (ARMRA); "get even softer with every wash" (Miracle, Cozy Earth)
	- **Good for →** products with a known quirk.
	- **When to use →** FAQ and mechanism sections. Only when the reframe is true.
- **Disclaimer as trust.** Visible compliance copy right under bold claims.
	- Swipe: "Disclaimer: this is an advertisement, using a fictional customer… not an actual news article." (Miracle); "on-page quiz questions are not a medical assessment." (Hims); "does not guarantee cut protection" (Manscaped)
	- **Good for →** health, claims-heavy and advertorial pages.
	- **When to use →** directly under the claim, or in the footer of editorial-style pages.
### Zone 3.4: Urgency and scarcity (true only)
Nikita's stance: fake urgency "looks desperate, kills credibility." His PDP notes allow scarcity and urgency only when "ethical." The forensics show why fakes backfire: Miracle's countdown read 14:55, then 14:36, then 14:34 across visits because it resets on load. Ryze's fine-print deadline was a month stale. "Sell-Out Risk: High" is a made-up metric styled like a stock indicator. A skeptical buyer reloads once and the trust is gone. Use only urgency you will honor.
- **Real stock.** Sold-out states shown honestly, with a waitlist.
	- Swipe: "You Just Missed Out. Don't worry, we'll be back soon and you're going to want in on this." (ARMRA); "Lamb w/ Cranberries… OUT OF STOCK… Notify Me" (Ollie)
	- **Good for →** genuine sell-outs.
	- **When to use →** when inventory is actually limited. Pair with stock-alert capture (Gymshark).
- **Real dates.** Ship-by dates and true offer end dates.
	- Swipe: "Ships by Thu, Oct 8th" (Miracle); "Arrives 10/08 - 10/13" (True Classic); "Estimated Ship Date: 12/18" (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** every store.
	- **When to use →** under the button. A delivery date is urgency that is also service.
- **Real limited editions and drops.** Seasonal or collab SKUs that genuinely end.
	- Swipe: "NEW! Shrek Berry Far Far Away. Grab it before it's far, far gone." (Grüns); seasonal Fall soaps and the IShowSpeed collab page (Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** consumables with flavor or scent variants.
	- **When to use →** as ad angles and top bars. Retire them when they end.
- **First-order-only offers.** The deadline is the first purchase, not a clock.
	- Swipe: "Special sale discount is valid only on the first delivery of a new RYZE subscription started from this page" (Ryze); "New customers only" (Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** cold traffic.
	- **When to use →** offer cards and ads, stated plainly.
- **Live demand signals, if real.** Counters pulled from real data.
	- Swipe: "23 people have this in their cart right now." (Brooklinen); "SOLD OUT 4X" (Miracle)
	- **Good for →** high-traffic stores.
	- **When to use →** only when the counter is live and true.
- **Season hygiene.** Stale seasonal copy is urgency's mirror: it tells the buyer nobody is minding the store.
	- Swipe (the failure): "THIS SUMMER- SLEEP COOLER" still live in October (Cozy Earth)
	- **Good for →** every store running dated or seasonal lines.
	- **When to use →** audit every dated line monthly and when a season ends.
### Zone 3.5: Message match and funnel continuity
- **Modular hero, universal body.** Swap the top screen per angle, keep the body (meta-secret 1.5).
	- Swipe: "Better food for picky eaters starts here" / "Better food for strong puppies starts here" over the same quiz (The Farmer's Dog)
	- **Good for →** every brand running more than one ad angle.
	- **When to use →** from day one. One hero per ad angle.
- **Ad noun phrase in the H1 or reason 1.** Move the ad's exact words or number into the first thing they read.
	- Swipe: "FIVE WAYS TO CHARGE. ONE DEVICE." (Ridge, matching its ad's "five ways to power")
	- **Good for →** every ad-to-page handoff.
	- **When to use →** always. Write the hero after the ad.
- **Offer-first hero.** The headline is the deal, for ads that sold the deal.
	- Swipe: "UP TO 38% OFF YOUR FIRST ORDER + FREE SHIPPING" (Javy); "Get Started for 43% off" (MUD\\WTR CTA)
	- **Good for →** offer-led ads and warm or most-aware traffic.
	- **When to use →** when the ad's hook was the offer. Lead with the benefit when the ad's hook was the problem.
- **Segment filter in the headline.** The reader self-identifies before reading on.
	- Swipe: "10 Reasons Why Women Over 50 Are Switching to this 'Special Sheet'…" (GroundingWell); "💊 FOR GLP-1 USERS" (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** segmented ads.
	- **When to use →** whenever the ad targets one segment.
- **Per-creator and per-founder landers.** A separate slug built around each face that runs ads (meta-secret 1.12).
	- Swipe: `/pages/tech-MKBHD-6RLP` with "Over my 15 years of reviewing tech, the Ridge power bank quickly became my favorite" (Ridge)
	- **Good for →** whitelisted and creator ads.
	- **When to use →** any creator, founder or publisher page with its own ad set.
- **Number sheet.** One sheet holding every number for the offer, which every ad and page pulls from (F3).
	- Swipe (the failure it prevents): ad "UP TO 67% OFF," lander "50% Off" (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** every funnel.
	- **When to use →** before the first ad goes live, and every time the offer changes.
- **Redirect retired slugs to a live offer.** Old ad links land on a current page, never a 404.
	- Swipe: `/pages/5-reasons-why-page` now redirects to the Pastries PDP with the subscription preselected (Magic Spoon)
	- **Good for →** stopping old ads, posts and emails from hitting dead pages.
	- **When to use →** every time a lander is retired.
- **Lander URL in the ad copy.** The slug itself becomes part of the promise.
	- Swipe: "Try now ➡️ [shop.ryzesuperfoods.com/p/11-reasons…](http://shop.ryzesuperfoods.com/p/11-reasons…)" (Ryze)
	- **Good for →** making the destination part of the hook.
	- **When to use →** when the slug sells ("11-reasons").
### Zone 3.6: Popups, capture and segmentation
- **One-question popup.** Tap one answer first, give the email second, route the flow by the answer (meta-secret 1.11).
	- Swipe: "What benefit matters most to you? MORE ENERGY / BETTER FOCUS / HAPPIER GUT / HEALTHIER BODY" (MUD\\WTR); "tell us what you're shopping for: Bakeware / Cookware / Food Storage / Coffee Maker" then the email field (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** stores with more than one benefit or audience. Raises opt-in without a full quiz.
	- **When to use →** the arrival popup, sitewide.
- **Belief question as data.** Ask where the visitor stands on your core belief and route later messages by it.
	- Swipe: "I \[already believe / am not yet sure / don't believe\] that fresh food is the healthiest for Biscuit." (The Farmer's Dog)
	- **Good for →** education-heavy products.
	- **When to use →** quiz warm-up or popup step 1.
- **Gamified capture.** A game or prize mechanic in place of a plain discount form.
	- Swipe: "PICK A BOX TO REVEAL YOUR DEAL" (Ridge); "Earn 139 Sweepstakes Entries" (Miracle); match-3 cards (Grüns, Javvy, Mellow); spin wheel (Native)
	- **Good for →** deal-driven audiences.
	- **When to use →** volume brands. Premium-restraint brands skip it.
- **Capture on the sales page and order page.** An email or SMS field before the payment step.
	- Swipe: "EMAIL + SMS REMARKETING: add capture on the SP and order page" (Nikita's Chad funnel notes)
	- **Good for →** long-form funnels with expensive cold traffic.
	- **When to use →** every advertorial, sales page and order form, before the payment step.
- **Capture at the dead end.** Stock alerts and saved results catch intent where the page can't sell.
	- Swipe: "Stock alerts" (Gymshark OOS); "Save Your Results. Enter your email to receive a copy of your quiz results." (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** catching intent that would otherwise leave.
	- **When to use →** OOS states, quiz results, size guides.
- **Gate placement by goal.** Where you ask for the email decides what you trade off.
	- Swipe: "You can use your email to access your recommendations anytime" (The Farmer's Dog, early hard gate)
	- **Good for →** quizzes and multi-step funnels.
	- **When to use →** early and hard to maximize leads (The Farmer's Dog). At checkout to maximize completion (Ollie). Late and soft with a Skip to protect completion and still capture (Jones Road). Always frame it as delivering results.
- **Decline copy in brand voice.** The "no thanks" link does a last bit of persuading.
	- Swipe: "No thanks, I'll pay full price." (Brooklinen)
	- **Good for →** deal-driven audiences.
	- **When to use →** match it to the brand voice. It grates on premium-restraint audiences.

	- **When to use →** the design review of every screen.
- **Numbered buy-box steps.** "Step 1 Select…, Step 2 Select…" like driving directions.

	- **Good for →** PDPs and FAQs.

## 4. Funnel map (the blueprint)

### 4.1 Nikita's traffic temperature rule
From his notes: "if ads are bottom-of-funnel (ready to buy), don't send to the homepage — send straight to the PDP." ### 4.3 The Chad funnel step by step (Nikita's notes, p13)
1. **Ad.** Stops the scroll and sets the angle.
2. **Advertorial.** Know its point before writing it: "are you pre-handling objections? Getting them into the right state?"
3. **Sales page.** Must-haves from his notes: a benefit-driven headline using at least 3 to 4 of curiosity, call out pain, promise solution, specificity, simplicity, credibility, time frame. Then true urgency, true scarcity, a guarantee or creative risk reversal, the mechanism (UMP + UMS), embedded social proof, FAQs, and credibility from the right authority for this buyer.
4. **Order page.** "As little friction as possible, as clear as possible." Email and SMS capture here too.
5. **OTO (upsell).** VSL or TSL. It must "solve the same problem the main product solves, but better/faster/easier" or "solve a NEW problem created after the first was solved." "Upsells can be MORE expensive than the main product."
6. **Downsell.** A cheaper yes for those who declined the OTO.
7. **Post-purchase.** "make them feel good about the purchase, kill their anxiety, THEN work toward selling the next product."
Build details for steps 4 to 7 → ⑦ Cart, Checkout & Upsell.

## 5. Chiefing checklist
Run this on every page before it gets traffic. Each line maps to a fundamental (F) or a zone on this page.
**Job and match**
