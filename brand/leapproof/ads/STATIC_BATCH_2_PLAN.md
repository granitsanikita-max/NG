# Leapproof — Static Batch 2 (ABO #2) — cold TOF plan

**Structure:** 4 ad sets (one per FORMAT) · 2 variants each = 8 statics · $20/day/ad set = **$80/day total**.
Broad US, women 28–50. Zero offer, zero price, zero product-hero. CTA → quiz. Judge: cost-per-quiz-start + CTR (format at ad-set level, variant at ad level).

**Spine (from the 155-snippet bank):** lead with **anger + defiance + identity**, never shame. Emotion tally: anger 20 > shame 11 > desire/pride 10 > grief 8 > fear 5 — and the anger is at *being lied to* ("it's normal, wear a pad"). The deepest line in the whole dataset: *"My PT told me to quit running. I told her, with tears in my eyes, that I couldn't."* — the brand is the answer to that sentence.

**Compliance locked (CJ product):** no absolute leak claims; no "you leak/do you suffer"; open on the MOMENT; no pee-vs-blood / mechanism / attacking period underwear; no banned words (incontinence, protection, discreet, dignity, accident); enemy = category PATTERN only, never a named competitor; never shame.

## Ad set 1 — SIDE-BY-SIDE · job: the enemy (anger at the lie + daily indignity)
**1a "The 40-year errand"**
- Visual: LEFT = a tall pile of disposable pads/liners (the forever pile). RIGHT = one folded black pair, alone.
- In-image: **"An industry would rather sell a pad every month for 40 years than fix the leak once."**
- Sub: "The first adult pad shipped in 1978. Their big idea is still: here's another pad."
- Built on: anger at the lie ("I'm so tired of false ads") + P&G Attends 1978 / disposable lock-in.

**1b "A pad's week vs one pair"**
- Visual: LEFT = seven liners in a row, each more bunched/greyed, ending in a bin. RIGHT = one black pair + a small wash→hang loop.
- In-image: **"A pad reminds you every single day. Underwear just lets you forget."**
- Sub: "Bunch. Shift. Bin. Repeat — or wash, wear, done."
- Built on: daily indignity ("walking around in a wet diaper" / "I forget I am wearing special underwear").

## Ad set 2 — DOODLE · job: identity + defiance
**2a "I told her no"** (the devastating line)
- Visual: rough doodle — woman running, a PT with a clipboard ("maybe stop running?"), she shakes her head, one tear, keeps running.
- In-image: **"My PT told me to stop running. I cried. Then I said no."**
- Sub: "You don't quit the sport. You fix the gear."
- Built on: C128.

**2b "The skip nobody sees"**
- Visual: doodle — a jump rope on the floor, she looks at it, thought bubble "…maybe not today."
- In-image: **"You didn't stop doing double-unders because they got hard."**
- Sub: "Common. Not normal. And definitely not old."
- Built on: athlete silent-shrinking + "not old" identity.

## Ad set 3 — THEN / NOW DOODLE · job: belief-shift
**3a "Taught to rebuild vs handed a pad"** (France)
- Visual: split doodle. LEFT "FRANCE": mom + a calendar of rebuild sessions, ✓. RIGHT "USA": mom + one pad + "that's normal after kids."
- In-image: **"France rebuilds new moms. America hands them a pad."**
- Sub: "Since 1985, every French mother gets taught to rebuild — paid for. You got a 6-week checkup."
- Bottom: "You weren't broken. You were dropped."
- Built on: France 1985 decree (confirmed defensible).

**3b "Common vs normal"** (her flip)
- Visual: THEN/NOW doodle. THEN: woman in class, thought "half of us leak too — guess it's just normal." NOW: same woman mid-jump, clear-faced.
- In-image: **"Half your class leaks too. That makes it common — not normal."**
- Sub: "We do not have to pee ourselves."
- Built on: C134 + C21.

## Ad set 4 — HANDWRITTEN NOTE · job: raw moment + postpartum confession
**4a "The trampoline"** (moment, dark-funny, most shareable)
- Visual: real handwritten note on a kitchen counter / gym bench. No product, no brand.
- Note: *"Jumped on the trampoline with the kids today. Peed a little — like a sneeze snuck out. Children destroy women's bladders 🙃 (8 years since my last baby btw)"*
- Built on: C124 + C123 (verbatim).

**4b "Cleared"** (postpartum, cleared-but-broken)
- Note: *"6-week check: 'You're all cleared!' Me, still crossing my legs before every sneeze. Nobody warned me this was part of 'getting my body back.'"*
- Built on: the cleared-but-broken gap + the bounce-back lie.
