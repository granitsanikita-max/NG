## 0. Goal analysis (do this before writing — Fundamentals ③)

| | 1a + 1b (same spine) |
|---|---|
| **Surface** | Native ad = image + long first-person caption that pre-sells into a quiz. The caption is a mini-advertorial (AD_RESEARCH S1 long-form native story — the format that is UltraDries' #1 and #2 statics by impressions). |
| **Reader arrives** | Scrolling, not shopping. Guard up. ~1 in 3 is **burned/skeptical** (fell for Everdries / period undies / pads). |
| **Awareness** | **Problem-aware, solution-skeptical.** She knows she leaks on a sneeze/jump. She does NOT believe leak-proof underwear is real. → Agitate the pain she feels now, then shift the belief by making her *current* product (the pad/liner) the problem. |
| **Sophistication** | **Stage 4–5.** Market tired of leak-proof-underwear claims. Can't win on a plain claim. → Enter on a **new enemy frame** (the forever-pad racket) + **identity** (active, not old) + **honesty/proof** (the wear-test). |
| **North Star belief** | "The pad was never a fix — it's a subscription to the problem. There's a different category — underwear made for the leak — and a free 60-second quiz tells me which set is mine." |
| **Target emotion** | **Anger at being lied to** (20 in the bank, the #1 emotion) → **relief**. Never shame. The enemy is the category/industry, never her, never a named brand. |

**Necessary beliefs the caption installs, in order (Fundamentals ⑦):**
1. Leaking on a sneeze/jump is **common but NOT normal** — not my fault, not a life sentence after kids.
2. The pad/liner is the **wrong tool** — built to be reordered forever, not to fix anything.
3. There's a **real alternative**: underwear made for the leak, worn and washed like normal underwear.
4. **This one's different / verifiable** — the keep-the-pairs wear-test, not another scam.
5. It's **for ME** (active 30s–40s, not "old").
6. The next step is a **quick, free, 60-second quiz** that matches me — and I know exactly what it is before I tap.

**Density rule (F#4 — standing rule for every script in this batch):** write only what's needed to (a) stop + identify, (b) shift the belief, (c) earn the committed tap. Every line moves a belief down the chain or moves her toward the quiz. If a line does neither, it's fluff — cut it. No throat-clearing, no restating, no second callback.

**Techniques pulled (⑤/⑦):** Curiosity & contrast (juxtaposition, *paid off* not left open) · Sell the concept not the product · Make their current product the problem (Sugarman "Sunglass Danger") · Fear / loss aversion (present + concrete, paired with relief) · Facts over opinions + Specificity (1978; one a day; the bin every night) · Let them reach the conclusion · Human element (first-person confession byline, dry-funny) · Honesty (raise the scam flaw first, then flip it) · Anticipate & resolve objections (scam → wear-test; for-me → active) · Communicates > says (the in-image line) · Yes-ladder / logical flow · Desire to belong · **No fake urgency** (the only "act now" is the free quiz).

## Compliance lock (CJ column — checked on every line)
- Enemy = **category pattern / "an industry" / "whoever makes them"** — NEVER a named competitor.
- No absolute leak claims ("100% leak-proof / never leaks"). "Made for the leak," "holds," "made for this" only.
- No "do you leak / you suffer" — condition never **attributed to the viewer as a personal attribute** (Meta). Lead first-person + communal ("leaking on a sneeze is common"), avoid "your leak / you leak."
- No pee-vs-blood / Leak-Lock mechanism / attacking period underwear. Set 1 is about **disposables (pads/liners)** only.
- No banned words: incontinence, SUI, bladder weakness, protection, dignity, discreet, accident.
- "feels like a diaper" is allowed **in the caption** (her verbatim, a feeling) — but **never as on-image text** (plan rule).
- 1978 adult-pad fact is defensible (P&G Attends 1978 — doc4 [H3b]). "40 years / rest of your life" is rhetorical loss-aversion, not a stat.
- Guarantee referenced = **30-Night Leak-Test** (from delivery, full refund, keep the pairs, one claim) — this is risk-reversal, not an "offer/price," so it stays inside the zero-offer rule. Quiz reward wording = **"unlocks your free gifts."**
- Never shame. Anger points at the product/industry; relief and identity point at her.

## 2. Chief pass (read back as Mara, 36 — Fundamentals ⑥)

**Does it talk to HER, deeply?**
- Opens on her **moment/ritual** (packing a liner; the lunchtime bunch-fix), not the condition. ✓ (§7 rule: open on the moment.)
- Her exact world: double-unders, trampoline, "laughed too hard at my kid," "I'm 36 not 86." Active, not old. ✓ (§5 ICP.)
- Her verbatim, lightly: "feels like a diaper," "fell for the marketing," the math of re-buying. ✓ (§12 best lines.)
- Leads with **validated anger + relief, never shame** — the enemy is the racket, the reframe is "not your fault, not old." ✓ (§6.)

**Is it moving her to buy (walking the belief chain)?**
- B1 common-not-normal ✓ · B2 pad = wrong tool/subscription ✓ · B3 real alternative ✓ · B4 verifiable, not a scam (wear-test) ✓ · B5 for me (active) ✓ · B6 the quiz is the next step and she knows what it is ✓. All six, in order.

**Are the fundamentals active?**
- Argument > words: the case is airtight — one a day × years → they profit from the leak → that's a subscription → stop renting it → here's how you find your set. Power words sit on top of the argument, not instead of it. ✓
- 3–6 word vacuum first line: "I did the math." / "Nobody warns you about the bunching." ✓
- Every block earns the next (slippery slide); nothing deletable. ✓
- Clarity 5th–8th grade, staccato + varied, read-aloud voice, stop-slop. ✓
- Facts over opinions + specificity (1978, one-a-day, the bin). ✓
- Objections raised + resolved: scam/does-it-work → the 30-night keep-the-pairs test; for-me → active/36; "still buying forever" → the subscription reframe. ✓
- Honesty: the scam flaw is raised *first* ("I got burned too"), then flipped. ✓ (Sugarman #1.)
- Let them reach the conclusion: the facts are laid out; she concludes "the pad is the problem" herself. ✓

**Sugarman moves present (from the teardown):**
- **Curiosity headline that can't be filed away** — "A pad every month. For the rest of your life." (you can't mentally file it as "sale," you have to read on). ✓
- **"I" / letter, not a company** — first-person confession byline throughout. ✓
- **Make their current product the problem** (Sunglass Danger) — the pad isn't just inferior, it's *designed* to keep her leaking. Her existing habit becomes the reason to act. ✓
- **Name the flaw, then flip it** (the scam rep). ✓
- **The dare / involvement** — "wear them, wash them, give them a real sneeze for 30 nights." She runs the experiment, like "compare them to your old sunglasses." ✓
- **Specificity for credibility** (1978, 30 nights, 60 seconds, six questions). ✓
- **Identity list / desire to belong** — the women who stopped renting the problem. ✓
- **One clear action with direction** — the arrow CTA chip + the caption's single ask. ✓

**No-curiosity-click check (the new bar):** the image names the quiz, the caption spells out exactly what's behind the tap (60s, 6 questions, matches you, unlocks gifts, know before you spend). Only someone who believes the premise taps — and she lands on a quiz she was promised, zero surprise. ✓
