A1 · The moment

N1 · The parking-lot sneeze

Sub-format: Long-copy native static · First-person moment story ("The parking-lot sneeze") · happy-customer friend-to-friend post

THE AD (paste this)

Primary text (caption):

I sneezed, and I froze.

Right there in the grocery store parking lot. Cart half-loaded, keys in my hand.

You know the freeze. That half-second where you check. Did I? Yeah. A little.

So I did the thing I'd done for about four years. Reached into my bag, past the keys and the old receipts, for the liner I keep in there. Always.

Four years of that. A liner for every errand. A spare pair that lived in my gym bag.

It started after my second was born. I figured it would pass. It didn't.

A sneeze could get me. A real belly laugh. Jumping on the trampoline with the kids. Standing up too fast, even.

And I always figured this was just something you dealt with later in life. Not someone still chasing her kids around the yard.

I'd even tried the underwear off the ads. The ones that promise the world. Bought a pair once. First real sneeze, straight through. Felt stupid.

So I'd pretty much decided this was just me now.

Then a woman in my mom group mentioned Leapproof.

Plain black underwear, she said. Made for the sneeze, the laugh, the jump. Not a pad. Not a diaper. Just underwear.

I rolled my eyes. Heard it all before.

But she said there's a 30-night thing. You wear them, and if they don't hold, you get your money back and you keep the pairs. That part got me. I wasn't really risking anything.

I did a quick quiz on their site. It asked about my moments, figured out my size, and said how many pairs I'd actually need. No guessing.

A little while later, a plain package showed up. And honestly? They just look like my regular black underwear. No bulk. Nothing that reads as a pad.

First real test was a sneeze at the kitchen sink. I did my usual freeze.

Then I just carried on. Rinsed the plate. Didn't have to go change.

I actually laughed.

Here's the part I didn't see coming.

Couple weekends ago my daughter had her party at the trampoline park. Normally I'm the mom on the bench holding everyone's shoes.

This time I kicked mine off and climbed in.

We did the dumb bouncing. She laughed so hard she couldn't breathe, which set me off, and I laughed the real kind. The one I'd been holding in for years.

And the whole time, I wasn't thinking about it. Not once.

That's the thing nobody tells you. It was never really about the underwear. It's about getting your whole self back in the game.

Turns out a lot of us deal with this. Doesn't mean we just have to live with it.

So if you're the one on the bench right now, with a liner in your bag. If you got burned by the ads before, me too.

I just wanted you to know these exist.

Go take a look. Do the little quiz, it finds your size and how many pairs you need, and you get the 30 nights to try them on your own sneeze.

No pressure from me. I just wish someone had shown me sooner. 🖤

Find your match.

Headline: Black underwear made for the sneeze, the laugh and the jump.

CTA: Soft in-copy close that qualifies the click: "Go take a look. Goal + target

  • surface: Ad copy (native long-form). Reads like a real woman's post a friend wrote, not an ad. Earns the click with story first; it is NOT a product page. Native editorial voice, big white space, one line per thought.
  • awareness entry: Problem-aware -> enters on the MOMENT (post-sneeze freeze in a store parking lot), never on the condition or 'do you leak?'. Agitates the moment, reveals the solution as a CATEGORY ('just black underwear, made for the sneeze'), not a mechanism.
  • sophistication positioning: Stage 4-5, ~1/3 burned. Cannot win on plain claim or mechanism (both banned on CJ). Plays Stage 5: identity + NEW CATEGORY ('not a pad, not a diaper, just underwear') + the honesty / anti-fake-ad stance. The only proof offered is the 30-Night test on her own sneeze, never an asserted result.
  • avatar line: Mara, 36. Two kids, back chasing them around the yard. Keeps a liner in her bag, a spare pair in the gym bag, pees 'just in case'. Burned once by a 'leak-proof' ad that went straight through. Not old, not broken, recoils at 'diaper'. The mom on the bench holding everyone's shoes at the trampoline park.
  • north star belief: Leaking on a sneeze/laugh/jump is common but not normal, it was never my fault, and there is finally just black underwear made for the moment that I can test risk-free for 30 nights — not a pad, not a diaper.
  • emotion: Validated relief + dry, matter-of-fact humor. Anger pointed at being sold a sticker ('felt stupid'), never shame at herself. Every vibration ends positive: the freeze resolves into a belly laugh on the trampoline.
  • what this copy must do: Stop the scroll on a true moment, make her think 'she gets me', walk her 1->6 to the North Star, raise and resolve her real objection (scam / fit / is-this-for-me) with honesty and the 30-Night test, paint the movie of her life after (trampoline party), and hand her to the live hero + quiz with one clear, qualified next step.

I brace before I laugh.

Not a polite little hold. A full stop-the-whole-table, clench, cross-my-legs brace.

Big dinner, everyone's being funny, and I'm the one who goes quiet so I don't have to pay for a proper laugh.

So I did the workarounds.

A liner "just in case." A spare pair of underwear in the gym bag. Pee before the class, pee again before the run. And pads. Pads and liners bunch, feel like a diaper, and go in the trash every day.

I told myself this was just my life now. After kids. At my age.

I'd bought "leak-proof" stuff off ads before. Soaked through. I fell for the marketing and felt like an idiot. So when a friend swore by these plain black briefs, I rolled my eyes so hard.

Then came the part I didn't expect.

They're just underwear. Black, soft, nothing to them. Not a pad, not a diaper. They're leak-resistant, but I honestly couldn't feel it. I put them on and forgot they were any different.

What got me wasn't a big promise. It was that I didn't have to take their word for it. You wear them through your own moments for 30 nights, and if they don't hold, you get your money back and you keep the pairs.

So I wore them to the one thing I'd been quietly dreading. My daughter's trampoline party.

I climbed in. I jumped. I did the loud, ugly laugh.

I didn't sneak off to check. I didn't pack a spare. I didn't sit on the bench holding everyone's shoes and calling it "watching."

I just jumped with my kid.

Nothing about me got repaired. It's underwear, not a treatment. I'm still me. Strong, not old, not broken. I just stopped building my whole day around where the nearest bathroom is.

So, to the woman who crosses her legs before she laughs.

Picture the next big laugh. The whole table loud. And you're just laughing. Not bracing, not doing the math on where the bathroom is.

It's a black brief, it comes in your size, and there's a quick quiz that sorts out which size and how many pairs you'd actually need. Then 30 nights to see for yourself.

I wish someone had shown me this years ago.

Find your match. 👇
