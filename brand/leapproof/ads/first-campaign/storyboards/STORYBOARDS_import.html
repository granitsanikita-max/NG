What the research says (why the boards are cut this way)

I ran scene-cut detection on the actual reference videos from our research:

  • UltraDries VO b-roll (#6 on its page) — a new shot every 2.6s, 8 cuts between 3s and 10s, and every cut is a new room, person or object. Hook = pads thrown at the lens, held 3s with a boxed caption. V1 copies this rhythm.
  • UltraDries Pixar talking pad (#4 on its page) — the talking pad moves to a new room on every cut (living room → vanity → bedroom → kitchen), with the woman reacting behind it. V2 copies this: the characters change angle or location on every line.
  • Everdries talking objects — each object in its own room, but each holds ~7s. That is the weak part; we cap every character shot at ~2.5–3s.
  • Everdries hand demo (not in its top 30) — one 41-second take. The anti-pattern.
  • All six refs hold the hook shot ~3s with no hard cut — but something moves inside it every second (throw, grab, flinch, text pop).
The pacing law every board follows
① Hook = one shot, 1.5–3s, 2–3 motion events inside it, text on frame 0, never a dark or still first frame.
② 3–10s = the fastest part: a new picture every 1–2s, an SFX on every cut.
③ 10s to CTA = 2–3s shots; a product hold can run 3–4s only if it moves.
④ No frame sits still for more than 3s. Talking characters change angle or room on every line; edit punch-ins (110–125%) count as an angle change and cost nothing.
⑤ End card 3–5s, button on screen the whole time, something moving.

Word rule applied everywhere (your call from the natives): 'brief/briefs' → 'underwear'; 'liner' → 'panty liner' on first mention. 'Not a pad, not a diaper.' stays.

Blocked until you sort them: (1) the 5 real CJ product photos — every product frame is built from them, or filmed on your phone when the samples land (better); (2) which video generator you're using — the tool on each shot is my recommendation only; no prompts get written and nothing gets generated until you confirm and say go.
