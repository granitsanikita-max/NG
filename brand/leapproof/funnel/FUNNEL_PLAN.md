Funnel type check (Universal 4.2): cold-to-warm traffic, a sensitive category, a "which one is right for me" fear (absorbency, size, how many) → **Ad → quiz → reveal → checkout** is the right funnel. The landing page is the "quiz landing page that sells the quiz" (Quiz Zone 1), because distrust here is high, but it's distrust of the *category*, not of online matching. So the landing page sells "a real fix exists and this finds yours," not "our quiz is accurate."

## 2. Who is arriving and what the funnel must do

- **Temperature:** cold Meta/TikTok traffic. **Awareness:** 40% problem-aware, 28% solution-aware, 26% product-aware. **Sophistication:** Stage 4–5. About 1 in 3 have been burned (46–49 of 155 snippets).
- **What that demands** (Copywriting ③B/③C): problem-aware → agitate the moment, then reveal the fix and the mechanism. Solution-aware → prove our way beats pads and period undies. Stage 4–5 → don't claim harder. Lead with **identity + experience** (the active woman, not the patient) and **proof as artifacts**.
- **Dominant emotion:** anger at being lied to (20) > shame (11). Copy moves her anger/shame → relief → pride. **Never lead with shame or fear.** (Also Meta's appearance/personal-attribute rules.)
- **Trust school** (Universal 1.13): **premium restraint**, not volume. We have no review volume, no fake urgency allowed, and a skeptical buyer. We win on honesty, mechanism, and a real guarantee.
- **The weapon:** "We can't outspend them, so we out-honest them." Every page carries one volunteered negative (F15).

## 9. PAGE 2 — The quiz: "Find Your Leak Match"

**Job (Quiz):** make her believe the match was built for her. The quiz does most of the selling: every option is in her words, at least one confession per chapter, mechanism planted inside the options, and her answers decide what the product page says.
**Gate:** late and soft, with Skip (Quiz Zone 5): we want completion *and* emails.
**Reward:** $15 off the 5- or 10-pair set, auto-applied, framed as already given (Quiz F9).
**Length:** 11 questions + 1 optional name, 3 named chapters, about 2 minutes. One question per screen, single-select auto-advances, multi-select Next disabled until picked (Quiz F3). Progress shows the chapter name + step count (Quiz F4).
**Data stored for routing:** `moment`, `onset` (persona), `current`, `amount`, `triggers`, `knewmech`, `blocker` (objection), `sitout`, `wear`, `size`, `cut`, `howmany`, `want`, `name`.

### 9.1 Intro screen
- Headline: swaps by angle (§5). Default: "Let's find what holds through your moment."
- Value preview (Quiz Zone 2): "You'll get: **1** your match for how much you leak · **2** your size · **3** how many pairs you actually need · plus your price with $15 off."
- "About 2 minutes. No email needed to see your match."
- Trust row (3 items, no more): "[30/60]-Night Leak-Test" · "Free shipping" · "Fit Promise"
- Button: "Start my match"
- Small line: "This isn't a medical assessment. It matches you to underwear." (Universal 3.3 disclaimer as trust, Hims pattern)

### 9.2 Chapter 1 — "Your moment" (warm-up)

**Q1 · moment** (easiest, personal; answered on the landing page if she tapped a tile)
"Which moment catches you out the most?"
- The sneeze I don't see coming
- Laughing till I cry
- Jumping — trampoline, jump rope, box jumps
- Running
- Lifting heavy
- Honestly? All of the above
→ Stored for the reveal headline and emails. No pre-highlighted answer.

**Q2 · onset → persona** (sensitive; "why we ask")
"When did this start?"
*Why we ask: what's behind it changes how much and how often, which changes your match.*
- After having my baby (or babies)
- In the last few years, kind of out of nowhere
- When I started training harder
- It's been around for as long as I can remember
- I'd rather not say
→ Persona: baby → **The Comeback** · out of nowhere → **The Reset** · training → **The Unbothered Athlete** · long time / rather not say → **The Unbothered**.

**Card 1 — Normalise (by Q2 answer)** (Quiz Zone 4; every stat footnoted)
- Comeback: "You're not alone. About 1 in 3 women leak after having a baby.*"
- Reset: "You're not alone. Leaking gets more common every decade: from about 1 in 6 women in their 20s to about half by their 50s.*"
- Athlete: "You're not alone. Studies put it at about 1 in 5 up to nearly half of women who do CrossFit.*"
- Unbothered: "Leaking when you sneeze is common. It's never considered normal. — Ridgeview Medical Center"
- All four end with: "Common. Not normal. And something you can do something about."

**Q3 · current fix** (the confession + belief/failed-solution data; Quiz Zone 3 "belief question as data")
"Be honest. What are you doing about it right now?"
- A pad or liner, most days
- Period underwear, just in case
- Nothing. I cross my legs and hope.
- Avoiding it — I pee first, I skip the trampoline
- I've tried leak-proof underwear. It still leaked.
→ `current` routes the product page education block and the email flow.

**Answer-reactive screen (after Q3)** (Quiz Zone 4)
- Pad: "Pads are built for a slow trickle you can see coming. A sneeze isn't a trickle."
- Period underwear: "Period underwear is built for blood, which is slow and thick. Pee comes out fast and thin. That's usually why it goes out the sides."
- Cross legs: "Crossing your legs is a full-time job. Let's give you the day off."
- Avoiding: "You shouldn't have to sit out the trampoline. Let's fix that."
- Tried leak-proof, still leaked: "You're not the problem. A lot of leak-proof underwear is period underwear with a new label. We'll show you the difference."
  *(Layer B line — on CJ, end at "We'll find something that suits your leak.")*

### 9.3 Chapter 2 — "What's really happening" (diagnosis)

**Q4 · amount** (sensitive; decision rule under options)
"On a normal day, how much are we talking?"
*Why we ask: this sets how much your underwear needs to hold, so you're never wearing more than you need.*
- A few drops
- A real splash on a big sneeze or jump
- Enough that a liner isn't enough
*Between two? Pick the bigger one. It's better to have room to spare.* (Quiz Zone 3 decision rule)
→ `amount` routes the absorbency level (only if more than one exists — D9) and the set size.

**Q5 · triggers** (multi-select, Next disabled until one is picked)
"What sets it off? Pick all that apply."
- Sneezing or coughing · Laughing · Jumping, running, HIIT · Lifting · Standing up fast or getting out of bed · Not making it to the bathroom in time

**Mini-reveal (after Q5)** (Quiz Zone 4: a result she can check against her own life)
- If no "bathroom in time": "**Your leak type: the sudden-pressure kind.** A sneeze or a jump pushes on your bladder and a little escapes in a fast burst. That's why how fast your underwear absorbs matters more than how thick it is."
- If "bathroom in time" is picked: "**Your leak type: sudden pressure, plus the 'gotta go' kind.** Both come fast, so how fast your underwear absorbs matters more than how thick it is."
- Buttons: "That's me" / "Change my answers"
- Small line: "This isn't a diagnosis. If leaking is new or getting worse, a GP or pelvic-floor physio can help."

**Q6 · mechanism planted** *(Layer B — switch off on CJ, D1)*
"Did you know most leak-proof underwear started life as period underwear?"
- No. Is there a difference?
- Yes. That's why mine goes out the sides.

## 12. Claims layers and compliance

### Layer A — safe on any product (CJ or Lynmiss)
The moment and how it feels · looks and feels like normal underwear (only if true of the sample) · washable and reusable · the guarantee and Fit Promise · free shipping · the gifts · "common, not normal" with sources · cited stats · the category fact that period products are built for a different flow (with the urology quote) · honest delivery time.

### Layer B — Lynmiss only, and each one only with its proof on file
"Built for pee, not blood" as *our* product's design · any absorbency number ("holds up to X ml") → needs a test report · sealed / flatlock edges → must be true of the actual garment · "lab-tested" → needs the report · "PFAS-free" → needs a total-fluorine report (CA/NY <100 ppm, 50 ppm from 2027) · durability "wash after wash" numbers → needs data.

### Never, anywhere
"Do you suffer from…" / "Struggling with bladder leaks?" · "incontinence," "discreet," "dignity," "protection," "accident," "bladder weakness" in ads and on the landing page (clinical terms only in product-page FAQ, for accuracy) · "100% leak-proof," "zero leaks," "never leaks" · "medical-grade," "clinically proven," "cures," "prevents UTIs," bare "non-toxic" · fake or unnamed doctors · fake reviews or uncredited forum quotes presented as reviews · timers, fake stock, rolling deadlines · shame or smell hooks.
