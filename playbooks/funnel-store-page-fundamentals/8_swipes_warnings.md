## What kills pages

Ordered by damage, most expensive first. Each one: what it is, what the audits found, the fix, and a check you can run in five minutes.

### 1. The top of the page doesn't continue the ad
- **What it is:** the hero headline, subhead or visual starts a different idea, number or offer from the ad that earned the click.
- **Evidence:** HexClad's ad says "Stop buying cheap cookware you have to replace every year!" and the lander opens on "HexClad does what your other pans can't." The durability angle only appears at reason #1. Ryze runs a generic mushroom-coffee ad into a generic listicle, a loose match. Cozy Earth's ad "Wake up refreshed—not sweaty" lands on "THIS SUMMER- SLEEP COOLER" in October. The best handoffs are word for word: Ridge "five ways to power" → "FIVE WAYS TO CHARGE. ONE DEVICE."
- **Fix:** → Universal F2 and 3.5. One swappable hero per ad angle over one shared body. Write the hero after the ad.
- **Check:** put the ad and the first screen side by side on a phone. Does the H1 or reason #1 repeat the ad's noun phrase, number and offer?

### 2. The wrong page for the traffic's temperature
- **What it is:** ready-to-buy traffic sent through a long presell or the homepage, or cold traffic dropped on a bare product page that can't change the belief it needs.
- **Evidence:** Nikita: "if ads are bottom-of-funnel (ready to buy), don't send to the homepage — send straight to the PDP." The big brands split by maturity. Brand-aware traffic lands on a PDP or bundle page that matches the ad (Hiya `day-night-bundle`, Dr. Squatch `-nco`). Education-heavy categories gate with an advertorial or quiz (AG1, Seed, Javy, The Farmer's Dog).
- **Fix:** → Universal 4.1 and 4.2. Tag every ad set by temperature and map each tag to one page type before launch.
- **Check:** for each live ad, name its temperature and its destination. Does a retargeting or offer ad ever land on a 2,000-word listicle, or a cold mechanism ad on a bare PDP?

### 3. Claim drift between pages
- **What it is:** the same fact stated differently on the ad, lander, PDP or cart: a discount, a guarantee length, a review count, a product fact.
- **Evidence:** every audited multi-page funnel drifted somewhere. Full measured list → Universal 1.3. The worst kind is a product fact, not a number: Mellow's advertorial says the cover "zips off and goes in the washing machine" and its PDP says "The pillow itself is not machine washable…" Mellow also shows three review counts (2,400, 34,672 and 50,868). GroundingWell's ad says "UP TO 67% OFF" and its lander "50% Off." At nine-figure scale these survive. For a new store each one is a refund, a chargeback or a ticket that starts "your ad said…".
- **Fix:** → Universal F3. One number sheet per offer. Every ad and page copies from it. Free shipping is only claimed on the plans that actually get it.
- **Check:** list every number and product fact on the ad, lander, PDP, cart and confirmation email. Do they all match the sheet?

### 4. Fake urgency
- **What it is:** timers that restart, scarcity no one can verify, and urgency boilerplate copied from other brands.
- **Evidence:** Miracle's countdown read 14:55, then 14:36, then 14:34 across visits. Ryze's "OFFER RESERVED 00:06:34" is session-based. "Sell-Out Risk: High" is a made-up meter styled like a stock indicator. The same sentence, "This limited-time deal is in high demand and stock keeps selling out," sits on the offer cards of five different brands. Nikita's CRO notes: fake urgency (countdown timers, "only 3 left") "looks desperate, kills credibility." The reader who refreshes or comes back from an email sees the timer restart, and from then on every claim is suspect, the guarantee included.
- **Fix:** → Universal 3.4. Use only urgency you will honor: a real end date, a real launch window, real stock, a first-order-only offer, a real ship-by date. No true deadline means no timer.
- **Check:** open the page, close it, open it in a private window and on a second device. Does any timer restart? Can you point to the real date or stock number behind every scarcity line?

### 5. Hidden money terms
- **What it is:** renewal price, cadence, return fees or "first order only" kept out of sight until the bank statement.
- **Evidence:** The audit never saw a dollar renewal figure in Ollie's reveal or checkout, only "This order has a recurring charge" with auto-renew legal text. Hims shows "From $149/mo†" and puts "$39 for the first month, auto-renews at $149/month thereafter… Membership is billed separately" in a footnote. Trustpilot gaps for Hiya, MUD\\WTR and Dr. Squatch are dominated by "hard to cancel / unauthorized recurring charges / forced subscription." Nikita: policies are trust signals, so don't bury them in the footer. Put them right under the add-to-cart button.
- **Fix:** → Universal F8 and 3.3 Renewal stated in the hero. One plain line under every button: today's price, the renewal price, the cadence, how to cancel. Hiya does it: "$15 each today, $30 each on a monthly basis thereafter." Brooklinen states its "$9.95 return fee" outright.
- **Check:** can a buyer read the renewal price and cadence without opening a footnote, an accordion or the terms page?

### 6. Fake credentials and invented publications
- **What it is:** a made-up credentialed byline, a fake expert, or a fake publication masthead on an editorial-style page.
- **Evidence:** Mellow's story is narrated by "Sarah" and signed "By Dr. Ralph." Sassy Saints runs a "DERMATOL TIMES" masthead. Ryze and Javvy use the same byline name, "Jade M.," so anyone who compares sees a template, not an author. The format itself is fine: Miracle discloses "using a fictional customer" in its footer. The fake credential is what breaks it, and in health categories it is what gets ad accounts and payment processors frozen.
- **Fix:** byline from a real staffer, the founder or the brand voice. Your brand's own editorial label instead of a fake publication. Experts only with real names, real credentials and real published quotes. Proof policy → Universal 3.2.
- **Check:** for every name, title and masthead on the page, could you show a stranger the real person or publication behind it?

### 7. Stale dates and seasons
- **What it is:** dates, deadlines and season words that no longer match the calendar, or sale reasons that contradict each other.
- **Evidence:** Cozy Earth ran "THIS SUMMER- SLEEP COOLER" in October. Ryze's fine print showed the deadline "by 9/8/2026" on Oct 6. Mellow's byline read "Last Updated: Dec 3, 2025" on an advertorial still live in October 2026. Grüns stacked "End of Summer Sale," "It's Our Birthday! We Lowered Our Prices" and "Order by October 6th" on one page. Done right: Javvy's fine-print date matched the day it was viewed.
- **Fix:** keep season words in one swappable field. Generate dated deadlines and "Last Updated" dates automatically. One sale reason per page. A monthly calendar reminder to review every live page.
- **Check:** search each live page for month names, seasons, years and "Last Updated." Is every one current, and is there only one reason for the discount?

### 8. A first screen that answers nothing
- **What it is:** above the fold, the visitor can't tell what she gets, why it's different, or why it's safe to buy.
- **Evidence:** the premium-PDP video: above the fold is "the only section 100% see; ~60% never scroll past an un-optimized one." Oura and Hatch withhold price, rating and guarantee above the fold on their homepages. That works for brands with equity. A new brand has none to spend.
- **Fix:** → Universal F4 and F5. Proof above the headline, a one-line promise, the offer, one button and a risk line under it.
- **Check:** show the first screen on a phone to someone for five seconds. Can they say what it is, why it's different and what happens if it doesn't work?

### 9. Clutter, nav and exits
- **What it is:** a lander or advertorial with a menu, footer links and side doors, or a store where the shopper can't find things fast. Text that is either a wall or one line under a blurry photo.
- **Evidence:** Nikita's CRO mistakes: "Cluttered navigation → if they can't find it in two clicks, they leave," and "Too much OR too little text (drowning in copy vs 1 sentence + blurry photo)." The winning listicles show the inverse: single column, no nav, and an offer card that is the only boxed element on the page (Miracle, Ryze).
- **Fix:** → Universal F18. No nav on landers and advertorials. Two clicks to anything in the store. One idea per screen.
- **Check:** count the tappable exits on the lander. Is anything there besides the offer button, the sticky CTA and the disclaimer link?

### 10. Popups that block the read
- **What it is:** an overlay that interrupts before the page has earned it, asks for an email with nothing in return, guilts the decline, or fails on mobile.
- **Evidence:** Javvy drops a "Try your luck / Match 3 cards to see what you win" overlay onto a reading page. Brooklinen's decline reads "No thanks, I'll pay full price," which works on some audiences and grates on others. Native's gate promises marketing ("stay in touch… drops, sales, and more") instead of results. The audits did not time popup load or close behavior, so test that yourself on a real phone.
- **Fix:** → Universal 3.6. One tap-to-answer question first, email second, a deliverable promised. On presells, never cover the headline or the offer button. Decline copy in brand voice.
- **Check:** on a phone with a slow connection, does the popup close in one tap, stay closed on scroll, and never cover the button or fire twice?

### 11. Dead links and broken widgets
- **What it is:** ads still pointing at retired or recycled pages, and on-page elements that fail to load and show something worse than nothing.
- **Evidence:** Lume's `/fb-pro-2` was a 404 within about 3 weeks of last-seen. Magic Spoon's `/pages/5-reasons-why-page` now redirects to a different product. Grüns' `benefits-listicle-db` 301s to the homepage. Native's review widget rendered "(0)," likely a load failure, which reads as zero reviews.
- **Fix:** → Universal 3.5 Redirect retired slugs. Redirect every retired slug to a live page that matches the old offer, never to a 404 or an unrelated product. Hide any widget that can render empty.
- **Check:** once a week, tap every live ad's link on a phone. Does each one load the matching page with reviews, prices and images showing?

### 12. Sloppy seams
- **What it is:** the page contradicts itself: counts that don't agree, a title its body doesn't prove, a narrator who changes person.
- **Evidence:** Sassy Saints: the title tag says 11, the URL says 9, the H1 says 7. MUD\\WTR's reason #1 is titled "It Isn't Coffee" but its body is about prebiotic fiber. Mellow slips into third person once ("tension headaches creeping up from her shoulders"). Ryze promises 11 and lists 10 on purpose, because the offer is #11. A deliberate gap works. An accidental one reads as sloppy.
- **Fix:** when a reason is cut in testing, update the title tag, the slug (or redirect it), the H1 and any ad that names the count. Reread every reason title against its body.
- **Check:** do the title tag, slug, H1 and the actual number of reasons agree? Does every reason's body prove its own title?

### 13. Generic proof and cheap visuals
- **What it is:** proof that can't be believed or isn't seen, stock photos, and branding that changes from page to page.
- **Evidence:** the premium-PDP video: a heading like "Real people, real results" is "garbage," sliders get under 1 to 2% interaction, and "as seen on" logos are "cheap now." Nikita's CRO mistakes: "Low-quality images / stock photos → product looks cheap," "Inconsistent branding," and "Weak product info (need sizing charts, shipping timelines, clear benefit explanations — bounce = unanswered questions)."
- **Fix:** → Universal F9, F12 and F19. Named, specific reviews shown up front under a specific headline. Real product and customer images with captions. One set of colours, fonts and voice from ad to 404 page. Sizing and shipping answered on the page.
- **Check:** is any proof hidden in a slider? Is any image stock? Would a shopper still have an unanswered question about size, shipping or how to use it?

### 14. Quiz-specific killers
- **What it is:** a quiz that ignores its own answers, accepts silent defaults, or gates results behind a marketing promise.
- **Evidence:** Dr. Squatch's result came back citrus and marine after pine, moss and cabin answers. Jones Road matched a Face Pencil described as "cool" undertone after a "Neutral" answer, and its shade bar defaulted to VERY PALE, which NEXT accepted. Native's gate asked to "stay in touch" for drops and sales instead of sending results. Ollie and Hims buried the renewal price (warning 5).
- **Fix:** → ⑥ Quiz Funnel. Map every answer to a real difference in the result. No pre-highlighted answer on a question that changes the match. Frame the gate as delivering the result.
- **Check:** take the quiz three times with opposite answers. Do the results change in ways that match the answers?

## Pre-launch kill check

Run before any page gets traffic, and monthly on every live page.

- [ ] Ad and first screen match: noun phrase, number, offer (1)
- [ ] Every ad set's temperature maps to the right page type (2)
- [ ] Every number and product fact matches the number sheet on ad, lander, PDP, cart and email (3)
- [ ] No timer restarts on reload or a second device, and every scarcity line has a real date or stock number (4)
- [ ] Renewal price, cadence and fees readable under the button without a footnote (5)
- [ ] Every name, credential and masthead is real (6)
- [ ] Dates, seasons and "Last Updated" are current, with one sale reason per page (7)
- [ ] First screen says what it is, why it's different, and what happens if it fails (8)
- [ ] No nav or side exits on landers and advertorials (9)
- [ ] Popup closes in one tap on mobile and never covers the headline or button (10)
- [ ] Every live ad link loads a matching page with all widgets filled (11)
- [ ] Title tag, slug, H1 and reason count agree, and each reason body proves its title (12)
- [ ] No proof in sliders, no stock photos, sizing and shipping answered on the page (13)
- [ ] Quiz results change with the answers, no silent defaults, gate promises results (14)
