## Home Page

### Job

**One line:** clarity, value, direction. In one screen, tell a visitor what you sell, why it matters to them and where to click. Nikita's notes: "HOMEPAGE HAS 1 JOB → clarity, value, direction." The hero is the "first (sometimes only) chance to answer the customer's questions."

**Who actually lands here.** Not cold paid traffic. Nikita's traffic rule: "if ads are bottom-of-funnel (ready to buy), don't send to the homepage — send straight to the PDP." Colder traffic goes to a lander or advertorial first (Universal 4.2). The home gets warm, brand-aware, browsing visitors: returning buyers, people who look you up by name, and anyone a retired lander redirects home (Grüns and MUD\\WTR 301 their old slugs to the home page).

**The key move for a new single-product or small-catalog brand.** You have no brand equity, so the home can't run on mood. Make it a near-PDP: the product in the hero, the live offer, proof and the guarantee in the first screen, and the buy box on the page. Grüns runs its full buy box inline on its home. See Fundamental 2.

Questions the home must answer:
- What exactly do you sell?
- Why should I care, and is it for someone like me?
- Can I trust you?
- Where do I start?

### Fundamentals

#### 1. One job: clarity, value, direction
- **Rule:** The hero states exactly what you sell in the headline and why it matters to the visitor in the subhead. The product sits front and center in the image, with one obvious action.
- **Why it works:** A visitor who can't tell what you sell in a few seconds leaves. Nikita: the hero is the first and sometimes only chance to answer their questions.
- **Weak:** "Subtle. Power." over a lifestyle shot with an "Explore" button (Oura, flagged in the sleep teardown as the wrong model for a brand with no equity). **Strong:** "High Protein Bars - 28g Protein, 150 Calories, 0g Sugar" / "Protein has a new bar." (David)
- **Evidence:** Grüns: "60+ Ingredients in one pack. Starting at just $29.99." with CTA "Save 55% + Free Shipping." Material Kitchen: "Try Our #1 Bestseller: The MK Free Board" with CTA "Shop MK Free Boards." Hatch and Oura withhold price, rating and guarantee above the fold and scored weaker for it.

#### 2. Send the home only traffic it can convert
- **Rule:** Point cold and ready-to-buy paid traffic at a PDP, an offer page or a lander, never the home. Build the home for warm, brand-aware visitors. If you sell one product, make the home a near-PDP with the product, the offer and the buy box on it.
- **Why it works:** The home has the least context of any page. It serves everyone, so it can't continue any one ad's angle (message match → Universal F2). Ready-to-buy traffic dropped here has to hunt for the product and the offer, and many won't.
- **Weak:** A cold prospecting ad pointed at a home page with a mood video, three category tiles and "Shop Now" *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** Grüns' home: proof and price in the hero ("⭐ 4.8 stars • 100K+ from 100,000 reviews • 1M+ members" / "60+ Ingredients in one pack. Starting at just $29.99."), "Find Your Flavor" tiles, then the full buy box embedded on the page.
- **Evidence:** Nikita: "if ads are bottom-of-funnel (ready to buy), don't send to the homepage — send straight to the PDP." Universal 4.2 rates "Ad → collection / home" as "Only for brands with equity or wide catalogs. Not for a new single-product store." Ads in the set land on product pages (True Classic pack PDPs, Mando's everywhere-pack, Bearaby, Our Place) or on campaign pages per push (Dr. Squatch × IShowSpeed, HexClad × Gordon Ramsay), "not the homepage." Even the brand-film homes (Vuori, Tecovas) "convert on the PDP, not the home."

#### 3. Pick the hero school that fits your stage
- **Rule:** Use an offer-led or proof-led hero (discount, gift, proof number, entry price) while the brand is new. Use a brand-film hero (mood, no price, no proof) only once the name already carries the trust.
- **Why it works:** Vuori and Tecovas can withhold proof and price because their name does the trusting. A new brand has nothing to lean on, so the first screen has to supply the proof.
- **Weak:** For a new brand, "One West" / "The edge of everywhere." over a desert landscape (Tecovas). **Strong:** For a new brand, "UP TO 38% OFF YOUR FIRST ORDER + FREE SHIPPING" with "100+ Million Happy Coffees Made" and "30-Day Money-Back Guarantee" (Javy).
- **Evidence:** The apparel set split in two. Nomatic, True Classic, Gymshark and Bombas run offer-led homes. Vuori and Tecovas run brand-film homes. Liquid Death converts with no reviews or guarantee on its home, but its voice is the moat. A new functional brand can't copy that.

#### 4. Every tile says what it does
- **Rule:** Caption every category tile, product tile and product line with the job it does for the buyer. Never a bare noun or a product name alone.
- **Why it works:** New visitors don't know your product names. A benefit caption lets them pick a path without opening anything.
- **Weak:** Tiles labeled "Cleansers / Serums / Sets" with no caption *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "DEODORANT — Smell good all day with aluminum-free stick, spray, and whole body formulas." (Native, one of 6 benefit-captioned "Shop by Category" tiles)
- **Evidence:** Hatch swaps names for jobs: "The sleep tracker that doesn't believe in scores," "The phone-free smart sleep clock for adults," "24/7 baby sleep support from night one." Graza names each oil for its use: "'Drizzle' — Made for eating, never heating." / "'Sizzle' — Use it every day, in every way." / "'Frizzle' — Your new high heat kitchen hero." Mando: "For Creases + Crevices." The kitchen teardown's rule: "Name + one benefit line, immediately… Never name-only."

**Always on, owned by Universal (placement only):**
- **Proof before claim.** → Universal F5. On the home: a proof number in or directly under the hero.
- **Risk reversal at the point of friction.** → Universal F8 and 3.3 Trust bar in site chrome. On the home: the named guarantee in the top bar or the trust strip, with shipping and returns one tap away. Nikita's CRO notes: policies are trust signals, so don't bury them in the footer.
- **Low clutter, two-click navigation.** → Universal F18. Nikita's CRO mistakes: cluttered nav ("if they can't find it in two clicks, they leave"), stock photos, a wall of text or a single line. On the home: a short menu, category tiles, and every product two taps from the hero.
- **Same brand to the edges.** → Universal F19. On the home: the same colors, fonts and voice as the PDP and cart, down to the 404 and the empty cart.
- **Every claim identical everywhere.** → Universal F3. The top bar, hero offer and trust strip pull from the same number sheet as the PDP.
- **Season hygiene.** → Universal 3.4. Every dated hero line gets a take-down date.
- **Capture with a real promise.** → Universal F17.

### Techniques by zone

#### Zone 1: Top bar

- **Trust bar in site chrome.** → Universal 3.3. On the home: pin the named guarantee in the top bar, e.g. "Love It or Leave It: 60-Day Return Window (U.S.)" (Lume announcement bar).
- **Spend-threshold ladder.** → Universal 3.1 Spend-threshold meter. On the home: the tiers in the top bar from the first pixel, "FREE SHIPPING ON ORDERS $15+ / FREE GIFT ON ORDERS $35+ / FREE GIFT ON ORDERS $55+" (Native).
- **First-order hook in the banner.** One line telling a first-time visitor what buying or joining gets them.
	- Swipe: "Extra 10% OFF when you become an Insider." (True Classic) / "20% Off First Order with Code COMFORT20" (Bombas) / "GET FREE Kids Daily Greens with orders $45+!" (Hiya)
	- **Good for →** every store with a first-order offer.
	- **When to use →** always, worded exactly like the popup and the PDP (→ Universal 3.6).
- **Offer framed as a gift.** Tell visitors the discount is already theirs.
	- Swipe: "You've been gifted 50% off your first purchase!" (The Farmer's Dog, persistent top bar)
	- **Good for →** first-order discounts. The gift wording creates ownership before any commitment.
	- **When to use →** only when the offer is true for every new customer.
- **Objection or reach line in a second bar.** A bar that answers the category's top doubt, above or beside the offer bar.
	- Swipe: "Picky eaters love us (and the feeling's mutual)" (Ollie, above its offer bar) / "Get Jinx shipped fast from Walmart, Chewy, Amazon or pick up in store!" (Jinx)
	- **Good for →** categories with one dominant doubt: fussy users, delivery speed, availability.
	- **When to use →** as a second bar, so the offer bar stays.
- **Mission proof stack.** The cause and its scale pinned at the top of the site.
	- Swipe: "ONE PURCHASED = ONE DONATED", "200 MILLION+ DONATIONS", "100% Happiness Guarantee" (Bombas)
	- **Good for →** cause brands.
	- **When to use →** when the mission is a buying reason and the number is real.

#### Zone 2: Hero

Pick the school first (Fundamental 3). For a new brand, start with the first three.

- **Single-product hero.** Your best seller is the headline and the CTA.
	- Swipe: "Try Our #1 Bestseller: The MK Free Board" / "Shop MK Free Boards." (Material Kitchen) / "The Lawn Mower® Ultra Gen 6 / Our newest groin and body hair trimmer" (Manscaped)
	- **Good for →** new single-product brands and one-hero-SKU stores.
	- **When to use →** when one product drives most revenue. This is the default for a new brand (Fundamental 2).
- **Offer-first hero.** The headline or CTA is the deal.
	- Swipe: "Get Started for 43% off" (MUD\\WTR) / "Get 50% Off Your First Box" (Ollie) / "UP TO 38% OFF YOUR FIRST ORDER + FREE SHIPPING" (Javy)
	- **Good for →** comparison-shopped categories.
	- **When to use →** when the offer is your strongest hook and true for every visitor.
- **Proof-plus-price hero.** Rating, customer count and an entry price in one screen.
	- Swipe: "⭐ 4.8 stars • 100K+ from 100,000 reviews • 1M+ members" then "60+ Ingredients in one pack. Starting at just $29.99." (Grüns) / "Start from $2.61/day" (im8) / "Now starting at $1,999" (Eight Sleep)
	- **Good for →** consumables and premium tickets.
	- **When to use →** when you have real proof and a low entry number. Per-day framing → Universal 3.1.
- **Spec-stack hero.** The numbers are the headline.
	- Swipe: "High Protein Bars - 28g Protein, 150 Calories, 0g Sugar" (David)
	- **Good for →** numbers-led products.
	- **When to use →** when the spec is the differentiator.
- **Delivery-promise hero.** Say what arrives and how often.
	- Swipe: "Get your childhood favorites delivered to your door monthly." (Magic Spoon)
	- **Good for →** subscription consumables.
	- **When to use →** when the routine is part of the appeal. State the renewal near the CTA (→ Universal 3.3 Renewal stated in the hero).
- **Category-ownership line.** Claim you're redefining the category, so what they own now feels out of date.
	- Swipe: "Reimagining the daily children's vitamin" (Hiya, first block under the hero) / "Clean cooking starts here." (Caraway, product overview)
	- **Good for →** challengers to a default product.
	- **When to use →** when a named enemy backs it up further down the page (→ Universal F13).
- **Emotional outcome hero, price deferred.** Lead with the life outcome and let a quiz reveal the price.
	- Swipe: "More good years start here." / "Fresh food plans developed by vets and clinically shown to support healthy aging." / CTA "See plans & pricing", secondary "Or give us a call." (The Farmer's Dog)
	- **Good for →** commodities reframed as personalized plans.
	- **When to use →** when a quiz sets the price. The CTA promises the price, so clicking feels like shopping, not homework.
- **Identity question hero.** A question the right buyer answers yes to, paired with a face.
	- Swipe: "Have high standards?" / "Ciara Miller & Jasper do, too." (Jinx)
	- **Good for →** low-differentiation categories where identity sells harder than spec.
	- **When to use →** only when the answer is a guaranteed yes (Nikita: "Don't use a question unless the answer is a guaranteed yes").
- **Customer line as the hero.** → Universal 3.2 Review as argument. On the home: a real customer's words as hero copy, "If you're struggling with stress, more coffee isn't the answer." (MUD\\WTR)
- **Founder or celebrity hero.** → Universal 3.2 Credibility gate on endorsers. On the home: the person is the headline, "Decades of Beauty Expertise in a Bag." (Jones Road). im8 leads with its celebrity partners the same way.
- **Personalized hero.** → ⑥ Quiz, Zone 1 (Personalization preview on the homepage). On the home: a name slot in the headline, "Ma isn't average. Their food shouldn't be either." (Ollie)
- **Drop or season takeover hero.** The newest product or the season owns the hero for a set window.
	- Swipe: "NEW IN: SHAPE" / "Soft. Stretchy. Shaping." (Gymshark) / "2026 Advent Calendars Are Here" / "Pre-Order Now." (Fly By Jing) / "Fall plans? We're in. Hit the trail, take the long way home, or cancel everything and get cozy. Just make sure you have some sticks within reach." (Chomps)
	- **Good for →** repeat buyers, product drops, gifting seasons.
	- **When to use →** only with a take-down date (→ Universal 3.4 Season hygiene). Keep the evergreen line on the page, as Fly By Jing kept "The addictive chili oil you won't be able to live without."
- **Attitude hero.** The joke or the stance is the headline.
	- Swipe: "EASY-DRINKING ENERGY FOR PEOPLE-DRINKING VAMPIRES" / "MURDER YOUR THIRST." (Liquid Death)
	- **Good for →** identity and entertainment brands.
	- **When to use →** only when the voice is the moat (Fundamental 3).

#### Zone 3: Trust strip and belief row

- **Proof strip under the hero.** → Universal F5 and 3.2 Count-in-headline. On the home: one row of 3 to 4 proofs directly under the hero, e.g. "1M+ Dogs Served / 135K+ Health Check-Ins / 20K+ 5-Star Reviews" (Ollie stats strip).
- **Scrolling trust marquee.** Positioning, proof and live offers in one moving ribbon.
	- Swipe: "CLEAN. SIMPLE. EFFECTIVE. • 350,000+ FIVE STAR REVIEWS • CRUELTY FREE & VEGAN • SUBSCRIBE & SAVE 25% • BUNDLE & SAVE 20%" (Native)
	- **Good for →** brands with several short proofs and live offers.
	- **When to use →** directly under the hero. Every item must be true today.
- **Belief icons.** Three or four icon blocks, each installing one belief the purchase depends on, before any price.
	- Swipe: "Designed for Lifelong Health" / "Simplified to the Essentials" / "Teaches Healthy Habits," each with a one-line reason (Hiya) / "Powerfully Effective / Plastic-Free Formulas / Clean Ingredients / Better Value" (Blueland, under "4,000,000+ Homes Have Made the Switch")
	- **Good for →** products that need a belief in place before the price makes sense.
	- **When to use →** the first content block after the hero. One belief per icon. Icon basics → Universal 3.7.

#### Zone 4: Navigation and product entry

- **Category bento under one promise.** One emotional headline over tiles for each need.
	- Swipe: "The care you've always deserved" over weight loss, sex, hair, testosterone and labs tiles (Hims)
	- **Good for →** multi-problem brands.
	- **When to use →** when visitors arrive with different needs.
- **Use-case panels so every visitor sees themselves.** One panel per buyer type, each with its own benefit headline.
	- Swipe: "Quit fighting over the thermostat" (couples) / "Your body changes. Your bed should, too." (women's health) / "Your snoring problem, finally solved." (Eight Sleep)
	- **Good for →** one product bought for different reasons.
	- **When to use →** when segments buy for different reasons.
- **Mission or quiz link in the nav.** Put the cause or the quiz in the main menu.
	- Swipe: "JOIN OUR FIGHT 🥊" and "Take the Quiz" (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** mission brands and high-variant catalogs.
	- **When to use →** when the quiz or the mission is a real entry point.
- **Embedded buy box on the home.** The full product buy box, inline on the home.
	- Swipe: Grüns runs its guided buy box on the home after its "Find Your Flavor" tiles and value props.
	- **Good for →** one-hero-product brands.
	- **When to use →** always for a single-product brand (Fundamental 2), and whenever the home gets buying traffic.
- **Product showcase, then Shop All.** Feature the few products that sell, then one link to the rest.
	- Swipe: hero → Drizzle / Sizzle / Frizzle showcase → "Shop All" (Graza) / a shop-all grid mid-page after the product bands (David)
	- **Good for →** small catalogs.
	- **When to use →** when a handful of products make most of the sales.
- **Tiles with savings.** → Collection, Zone 4 (Savings on the card). On the home: Grüns' "Find Your Flavor" tiles show the dollar saving.
- **Build-a-bundle band.** → Collection, Zone 6. On the home: its own band right after the category tiles (Native "BUILD A BUNDLE," Lume "Save up to 30%").

#### Zone 5: Proof

Real, named experts and customers only → Universal 3.2 Proof policy.

- **Proof headline that frames scale.** → Universal 3.2 Count-in-headline and Distribution and scale. On the home: the proof section's headline, "Join the 2.5M+ Home Cooks Who Made the Swap" (Caraway) / "Over 1 billion meals delivered" (The Farmer's Dog).
- **Playful stats block.** Proof numbers written in brand voice.
	- Swipe: "A lot of happy, nice-smelling people over here / 87,563 fresh pits / 365 good hair days / 300,000+ IG FOLLOWERS / 20+ beloved scents" (Native)
	- **Good for →** voice-led brands.
	- **When to use →** when you want proof that also entertains. Every number stays real.
- **Credibility ladder.** Stack proof from institution to expert to elite user to mass.
	- Swipe: in-house research ("over 12 PhDs … 50 clinical studies") → named advisory board (Andrew Huberman PhD, Matthew Walker PhD) → pro athletes → "Over one million nights of sleep improved" (Eight Sleep)
	- **Good for →** premium tech and health.
	- **When to use →** when you have real proof at several levels.
- **Expert row that repeats one villain word.** Several named experts, each saying the enemy word in their own voice.
	- Swipe: "So many vitamins are loaded with excess sugar and junk. Hiya is really clean." (Dr. Mark Hyman, M.D., Hiya) / "Hiya gives my patients the nutrients they need without the sugary junk." (Dr. Monica Madan, Hiya)
	- **Good for →** health, kids and safety categories.
	- **When to use →** with real published quotes and full titles. Place experts at the claim on the PDP too (→ Universal 3.2).
- **Authority wall.** Portraits of real partners or experts, each with a credential and a first-person line.
	- Swipe: "Trusted by the world's best" wall with Beckham, Giannis, Sabalenka and a Mayo Clinic doctor (im8) / "Built on science. Backed by experts." (David) / "Approved By Leaders" (MUD\\WTR)
	- **Good for →** brands with real partners.
	- **When to use →** when authority persuades your buyer more than volume. LMNT runs the same wall on a collection page.
- **Press quotes.** → Universal 3.2 Press quotes over press logos. On the home: a quote wall, "Material always wows us. The quality of each tool is a step above…" (Material Kitchen) / "World Changing Ideas" (Fast Company, Blueland).
- **Retail logos.** → Universal 3.2 Distribution and scale. On the home: Walmart, Instacart, Whole Foods and Target logos under "Available in stores" (Olipop).
- **Wall of love.** → Universal 3.2 UGC wall and F9. On the home: a long wall, not a slider, e.g. "No stink! WOOHOO!!!!" (Diana L., Lume), Chomps' "WHAT THEY'RE SAYING" Instagram wall, Caraway's "903k+ Followers @caraway_home" with named handles.

#### Zone 6: Story, mission and education

- **Founder story block.** Who made it and why, in their voice.
	- Swipe: "HEY, JING HERE" (Fly By Jing) / "Created by Dads, Backed by Science. As new dads, we were surprised when we learned more details about gummy vitamins." (Hiya)
	- **Good for →** founder-led brands.
	- **When to use →** mid-page, after proof.
- **Manifesto with a named enemy.** A plant-the-flag band that makes buying a side to take.
	- Swipe: "PLASTIC RECYCLING IS A MYTH. MOST PLASTIC IS SENT TO LANDFILLS. ALUMINUM IS INFINITELY RECYCLABLE." (Liquid Death)
	- **Good for →** mission brands with a villain.
	- **When to use →** when the enemy is named the same way on every page (→ Universal F13).
- **Us-vs-them table on the home.** → Copywriting doc: Us vs them. On the home: a short table mid-page, "Mando: Scientifically proven to last 72 hours / Stops odor before it starts" vs "Traditional Deodorants: Quits working midday / Masks odor after it forms" (Mando). Hiya runs a competitive comparison chart on its home.
- **Lab results on the home.** → Universal 3.2 Dated, linkable lab badge. On the home: "600+ types of PFAS and Total Organic Fluorine tested — zero detected" and "Third-party laboratory results — publicly available" (Caraway).
- **Fun-facts ticker.** A rotating strip of surprising true facts about the product or category.
	- Swipe: "It takes about 5,000 (!) olives just to make one bottle of 'Drizzle'." / "Vegetable oil doesn't come from vegetables. Sneaky!" (Graza)
	- **Good for →** commodity categories where quality is invisible.
	- **When to use →** when a fact can teach quality without a sales tone.
- **Live provenance line.** Where this batch comes from, right now.
	- Swipe: "Now Serving Olives from: Andalusia, Spain / Harvesting Season: October – January." (Graza)
	- **Good for →** food, drink and anything with a harvest, batch or origin.
	- **When to use →** when freshness or origin is a reason to buy. Update it with every batch.
- **Learn-more link to a science or difference page.** Send the curious to an education page instead of loading the home.
	- Swipe: LMNT links its science hub and "sodium deficiency: symptoms, causes, and solutions" content from its home.
	- **Good for →** categories that need a new belief before the product.
	- **When to use →** when the buyer needs teaching. Build the destination as a difference page (→ ④ Presell).

#### Zone 7: Quiz entry

Build details live on ⑥ Quiz. Here is placement only.

- **Need-state picker.** → ⑥ Quiz, Zone 1. On the home: directly under the hero, "Build a plan for your dog's needs. Every plan is built for your whole dog. Tell us what matters most right now." with "Weight loss / Picky eating / Senior longevity / Puppy growth" (The Farmer's Dog).
- **Quiz with a funny promise and a reward.** A home band that sells the quiz with a joke and a discount.
	- Swipe: "FIND YOUR SCENT & SCORE 20% OFF. Take our scent personality quiz (it's like therapy, but cheaper) and get 20% off your personalized bundle." (Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** high-variant catalogs.
	- **When to use →** when choice paralysis is common.
- **Quiz as the way out of browsing.** Offer the quiz inside the browse section, for people who can't pick.
	- Swipe: "WE GET IT — YOU LIKE SMELLING GOOD" scent-family section, closed with "Or take our Scent Quiz" (Native)
	- **Good for →** large variant catalogs.
	- **When to use →** next to any section with many similar options.
- **Quiz to kill the wrong-pick fear.**
	- Swipe: "Take the Quiz" on the home and "Find my Shade" repeated through the site (Jones Road)
	- **Good for →** shade, size and fit products.
	- **When to use →** when the wrong pick is the top return reason.

#### Zone 8: Lower-page selling

- **Bestseller rail named in voice.** A product rail of proven sellers with a headline that sounds like the brand.
	- Swipe: "Shop the all-time faves" (Native). Hiya runs a best-sellers carousel right after its value section, Lume a "new & notable" rail.
	- **Good for →** multi-SKU stores.
	- **When to use →** lower half, after the visitor has a reason to trust you.
- **Membership framed as a club.** The subscription sold as belonging. Offer mechanics → Universal 3.1.
	- Swipe: "Introducing the Tastemaker Club... Become a Tastemaker and step into a new world of flavor forever." with "Join Membership & Save 20%" (Fly By Jing)
	- **Good for →** consumables with a fan base.
	- **When to use →** as a lower home band, once the product has repeat buyers.
- **Final CTA band.** One last band that restates the promise and the action before the footer.
	- Swipe: "Daily Habits for Lifelong Wellness" CTA band near the end of the home (Hiya). The Farmer's Dog also ends its home on a final CTA.
	- **Good for →** every home.
	- **When to use →** last band before email capture and the footer. Repeat the hero's offer and CTA word for word.

### Order (blueprint)

**A. Multi-product store**
1. Top bar: live offer and the named guarantee.
2. Hero: what you sell (headline), why it matters (subhead), product in the image, one CTA. Offer-led or proof-led for a new brand.
3. Proof strip: one number plus 3 to 4 icons.
4. Belief icons: the 3 or 4 beliefs the purchase depends on.
5. Shop by category or product tiles, benefit-captioned. Quiz entry if choice is hard.
6. Build-a-bundle band.
7. Why us: the named enemy, a short us-vs-them table.
8. Proof: scale headline, wall of love, expert row or authority wall, press quotes.
9. How it works, short.
10. Founder or mission block.
11. Bestseller rail or membership band.
12. Guarantee block.
13. Final CTA band, email capture, footer with policies linked.

**B. New single-product brand (modeled on Grüns and Material Kitchen)**
1. Top bar: live offer and the named guarantee.
2. Single-product hero with proof and entry price.
3. Proof strip.
4. Belief icons or "how it works."
5. Embedded buy box (variant tiles with savings if you have flavors or sizes).
6. Us-vs-them table.
7. Proof: wall of love, expert or lab proof.
8. Founder story.
9. Guarantee block.
10. Final CTA band, email capture, footer with policies linked.

## Collection Page

### Job

**One line:** help the shopper pick the right product fast, then hand them to a PDP that's already half-sold.

**Who lands here.** Shoppers browsing a category from the menu or the home tiles, and visitors from category-level ads that sell a need instead of one SKU (Cozy Earth). Universal 4.2 rules this route out for a new single-product store, so build a collection once shoppers have a real choice to make.

Questions it must answer:
- Which of these is for me?
- What's the difference between them?
- Which one do most people pick?
- Can I bundle or build a set?

**Evidence note.** Few collection pages were torn down in full (Gymshark, Cozy Earth, LMNT, Native's catalog). Several grid moves below come from product-page pickers and quiz-result grids, which work the same way on a collection. Each entry says where it ran. Where nothing in the set was worth copying (default sort), it says so.

### Fundamentals

#### 1. Every card carries proof, price and a benefit line
- **Rule:** Each product card shows a star rating with a review count, the price with the real saving if there is one, and one line on what the product does.
- **Why it works:** Review counts are a merchandising cue. A card with only a photo and a name gives the shopper no reason to pick it over the next one.
- **Weak:** A product photo, a name and a price *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** Every tile carries its own star count, "★★★★★ 17955" on the Deluxe Bundle and "15268" on the fitted sheet (Cozy Earth, "review counts used as a merchandising cue site-wide").
- **Evidence:** ARMRA repeats "4.7 ... 15,944 reviews" on every product card. Gymshark puts its ratings on the cards ("Card-level ratings across collection") instead of the hero. Grüns' flavor tiles show the dollar saving. Hatch's product carousel uses benefit lines in place of names (Home Fundamental 4).

#### 2. Recommend one pick, don't just list
- **Rule:** Tell newcomers which product to start with and mark it. Label the one or two products most people should buy.
- **Why it works:** A grid with no recommendation hands the shopper all the work. The quiz teardown found that "A single verdict reads as a prescription. A grid (Caraway home quiz, Native) reads as a catalog and comes across as less confident."
- **Weak:** 40 identical tiles sorted by newest *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "If you're new to Gymshark and need a recommendation for the best gym leggings, we'd recommend our Everyday Seamless leggings. This best-selling style…" (Gymshark)
- **Evidence:** Native labels its scent tiles "MOST LOVED ❤️", "ON FIRE 🔥", "HIDDEN GEM 💎." Caraway tags "Best Seller" and "Best Value" in its set selector. Decision rules for close choices → Universal 3.3.

#### 3. Group by how the buyer thinks
- **Rule:** Build sections and filters around the buyer's need, use or taste family. Never around your SKU list or launch names.
- **Why it works:** Shoppers arrive with a problem or a preference, not your product names. Grouping by need lets them find their three options instead of scanning forty.
- **Weak:** Filters for "Core / Signature / New Arrivals" *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** Scent families with sensory copy, "Creamy vanillas and alluring spices. A treat for your senses." inside the "We get it — you like smelling good" explorer (Native).
- **Evidence:** Hims groups by problem (weight loss, sex, hair, testosterone, labs). Jones Road's shade picker uses outcome tabs "ALL / TINT / BLUSH / HIGHLIGHT / COLORLESS" (on its PDP). Eight Sleep sorts reviews by buyer, "Hot sleepers / Couples / Hot flashes / Insomnia." Graza's names encode the job (Home Fundamental 4).

#### 4. Put the selling words where researchers land
- **Rule:** Give the collection its own category argument in the buyer's words. The collection can carry the persuasion so PDPs stay lean.
- **Why it works:** Shoppers comparing a category land on the collection first. Words placed there reach them before they've picked a product.
- **Weak:** A collection page with a heading and a grid. **Strong:** "Because nothing kills focus faster than leggings that won't stay put. From waistbands that dig in, to leggings that shift mid-workout, we know the details matter." (Gymshark)
- **Evidence:** Gymshark keeps its PDPs near prose-free. Its women's leggings collection body was its single most copy-dense surface. The apparel teardown calls where the copy lives a lever of its own. Keep that copy below the grid on mobile so it doesn't push products down (→ Universal F16).

#### 5. A collection can be a lander when the ad sold a category
- **Rule:** When an ad promises a category or a need, not one product, land it on a collection whose hero continues the ad (→ Universal F2), then the grid.
- **Why it works:** The shopper keeps the ad's thread and still gets to choose.
- **Weak:** A "cooler sleep" ad sent to a generic all-products grid *(illustrative)*. **Strong:** "THIS SUMMER- SLEEP COOLER" hero, a "Cozy Earth vs Other Bamboo / Silk / Cotton" grid, "OPRAH'S FAVORITE THINGS 5 YEARS IN A ROW," then the product grid (Cozy Earth).
- **Evidence:** The lander teardown lists Cozy Earth among brands that send ads "straight to product or collection pages." It got the angle right and the timing wrong: the summer hero was still live in October (→ Universal 3.4 Season hygiene).

**Always on, owned by Universal (placement only):**
- **Two-click navigation.** → Universal F18. Every collection is one tap from the menu or a home tile.
- **Every claim identical everywhere.** → Universal F3. Card prices and savings match the PDP.
- **Mobile first.** → Universal F16. Filters as chips, not dropdowns. Category copy never pushes the grid below the first screen.

### Techniques by zone

#### Zone 1: Header and intro

- **Named collection with a promise.** Name the family like a product and give it one promise.
	- Swipe: "Meet the Sunday Collection: our best-selling joggers… built to keep pace with every move… while keeping the softness that makes them your everyday go-to." (Vuori) / "The MK Free Collection: no stains, no smells, and no microplastics left behind." (Material Kitchen)
	- **Good for →** product families that share one feature.
	- **When to use →** as the header of any collection that has one shared promise.
- **Inner-monologue intro.** → Copywriting doc: The call-out. On the collection: a short intro that names the personas and their exact frustrations before the grid.
	- Swipe: "Whether you're a hybrid girlie who needs her workout leggings to stand up to every burpee, sled push and wall ball, a zen queen who never goes a week without her Pilates class..." / "adjusting mid-squat is never the goal." (Gymshark)
	- **Good for →** segments with their own slang, fit and function categories.
	- **When to use →** when 2 to 3 personas shop the same collection.
- **Confident editorial line.** One tagline that tells the shopper how this ends.
	- Swipe: "You're gonna choose Vital. Every time." (Gymshark)
	- **Good for →** voice-led brands.
	- **When to use →** as the collection's tagline.
- **Comparison strip above the grid.** Settle "why this material or method" before the shopper scans products.
	- Swipe: "Cozy Earth vs Other Bamboo / Silk / Cotton" (Cozy Earth)
	- **Good for →** categories where the material or method is the difference.
	- **When to use →** on collections used as landers, right under the hero.
- **Authority above the grid.** → Home, Zone 5 (Authority wall). On the collection: LMNT's `/collections/salt` runs an athlete and health-leader slider (Lance Armstrong, Tia-Clair Toomey-Orr, Bradley Beal, Brett Favre). Cozy Earth leads with "AS FEATURED IN" and "OPRAH'S FAVORITE THINGS 5 YEARS IN A ROW."

#### Zone 2: Filters and routing

- **Objection router tile.** → ② PDP, Zone 5 (Inline objection router). On the grid: a tile at the top of the family it applies to, "Do you have sensitive skin? Try our Sensitive Deodorant." (Native).
- **Quiz exit from the grid.** → Home, Zone 7 (Quiz as the way out of browsing). On the collection: close the family section with "Or take our Scent Quiz," as Native does.
- **Card CTA that routes to the consult.** Every card's button opens the same quiz or intake instead of a cart.
	- Swipe: every product card's "Get started" points to the same intake. "Many doors lead to one funnel." (Hims)
	- **Good for →** products that need a fit, dose or eligibility check first.
	- **When to use →** when the right product depends on the buyer's answers.

#### Zone 3: Badges and labels

- **Labels that pick for the shopper.** Short labels that point at a few products for different reasons.
	- Swipe: "MOST LOVED ❤️", "ON FIRE 🔥", "HIDDEN GEM 💎", "LIMITED EDITION" (Native scent tiles) / "⚡ TRENDING", "⭐ TOP RATED", "🔥 SELLING FAST", "🎁 TOP GIFT", "LIMITED STOCK", "PERFECT ADD-ON" (Caraway quiz-results grid)
	- **Good for →** grids with many similar products.
	- **When to use →** on a few products, not all. Each label reflects real sales or stock data. "SELLING FAST" and "LIMITED STOCK" only from live numbers (→ Universal 3.4). Variant-level labels → ② PDP.
- **Bestseller and value tags.** Mark the one or two products you want most people to buy.
	- Swipe: "Best Seller," "Best Value" (Caraway set selector) / "Our #1 best seller!" (Fly By Jing) / "BEST SELLER" flag on the title (AG1) / "bestseller" flag on the first image (Our Place)
	- **Good for →** every collection.
	- **When to use →** always, matched to real sales, on the same product as the newcomer pick (Fundamental 2).
- **New, limited and last-chance tags.** Flag freshness and true scarcity on the tile.
	- Swipe: "New" / "Limited" / "Last Chance" tags on colorways (Cozy Earth)
	- **Good for →** drops and colorways.
	- **When to use →** only when literally true (→ Universal 3.4 Real limited editions and drops).

#### Zone 4: Card anatomy (beyond Fundamental 1)

- **Savings on the card.** → Universal F7. On the card: the real saving for the card's default option, e.g. Grüns' "Find Your Flavor" tiles with dollar savings, or "~~$675~~ $445 (34% off) SAVE $230" (Caraway quiz-results grid).
- **Hover or second image.** A clean product shot by default, the product in use on hover.
	- Swipe: clean white product shot with a lifestyle image on hover (Native)
	- **Good for →** desktop browsing.
	- **When to use →** to show use without cluttering the grid.
- **Sensory descriptor on flavor and scent cards.** → ② PDP, Zone 5 (Descriptor on every swatch). On the card: one line the shopper can taste or smell, "A vacay in every sniff." (Native).
- **Names in brand voice, with a descriptor.** Product names as brand copy.
	- Swipe: "Severed Lime, Cherry Obituary, Sweet Reaper, Rootbeer Wrath, Mango Chainsaw" (Liquid Death)
	- **Good for →** identity and entertainment brands.
	- **When to use →** only with a plain descriptor under the name, or the card fails Fundamental 1.
- **Sold-out card with a stock alert.** → Universal 3.6 and ② PDP (Back-in-stock capture). On the grid: keep the card visible with an alert button instead of hiding it, as Gymshark's "Stock alerts" capture does.

#### Zone 5: Sort

- **Default sort: best-selling.** No brand in the set ran a sort trick worth copying. Set the default to best-selling so the order agrees with the bestseller tags, and put the newcomer pick (Fundamental 2) first.
	- **Good for →** every collection.
	- **When to use →** as the default. Test it against your own data.

#### Zone 6: Bundles on the grid

- **Build-a-bundle tile or band.** Mix-and-match with the saving stated.
	- Swipe: "BUILD A BUNDLE" mix and match, "save 20%" (Native) / BYO bundle "Save up to 30%" (Lume) / "Build your bundle." with "Buy 4 cartons and get your 5th free" (David) / "Custom Mixed Bundle" (Magic Spoon)
	- **Good for →** multi-SKU consumables.
	- **When to use →** at the top or middle of the grid, and as its own band on the home. A kit builder inside the buy box → Universal 3.1.
- **Variety pack for the undecided.** A pack of several flavors or scents, offered to people who can't choose.
	- Swipe: "Can't pick just one? Shop Variety Packs" (Olipop, on its PDP) / "4-Flavor Variety Pack" and "3-Flavor Variety Pack" tiles (Liquid Death)
	- **Good for →** flavor and scent catalogs.
	- **When to use →** when indecision is common. Put it first in the grid for new visitors.
- **Set sizes on one card.** → Universal 3.1 Quantity ladder. On the grid: one card per set, with the sizes and tags inside it, e.g. "6-pc / 12-pc BEST SELLER / 16 / 24 / 31-pc BEST VALUE" (Caraway, on its set page).
- **Gifting logistics.** Remove the hassle of buying several gifts.
	- Swipe: "Buying multiple gifts? Send to multiple addresses here." (Graza, on its product page)
	- **Good for →** giftable products.
	- **When to use →** in gifting seasons.

### Order (blueprint)

1. Header matched to the traffic: the ad angle or the named collection and its promise.
2. Optional short intro in the buyer's words: personas and their frustrations.
3. On a collection used as a lander: comparison strip or authority above the grid.
4. Groups and filters by need, use or family. A "new here? start with this" pick. A quiz exit.
5. Bundle or variety-pack tile.
6. Product grid: cards with rating and count, price and real saving, benefit line, one honest label, hover image. Sold-out cards keep a stock alert.
7. Chooser or comparison block if products are close.
8. Proof strip: review highlights or authority wall.
9. Longer category copy for researchers, below the grid.
10. Guarantee and shipping line, email capture.
