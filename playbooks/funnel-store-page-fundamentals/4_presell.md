## The job of this page

**Where it sits.** Chad funnel: AD → ADVERTORIAL → SALES PAGE or PDP → ORDER PAGE. The ad earns the click. The presell changes one belief. The next page closes.

**When to use it.** Cold traffic that does not know you, in a category where a belief has to change before a product page can sell. Problem-aware or solution-aware readers who have tried other things. New mechanisms, invisible benefits (antibacterial sheets, grounding, mushroom coffee, gut health), or anything the reader would file as "another one of those."

**When to skip it.** Mature, brand-aware or bottom-of-funnel traffic goes straight to the PDP or a bundle page. Hiya sends it to `day-night-bundle`, Dr. Squatch to a `-nco` new-customer page. Nikita's rule: "if ads are bottom-of-funnel (ready to buy), don't send to the homepage — send straight to the PDP."

**The one job.** Move the reader from the ad's one idea to "I'm ready to try it," then hand her one offer. Which belief you change is decided in Fundamental 1.

## Fundamentals

Universal fundamentals F2 (top continues the ad), F3 (one number sheet), F8 (risk reversal under the button) and F18 (no nav on landers) all apply here. They are not repeated.

### 1. Write the point sentence before any copy
- **Rule:** finish this sentence before you write a word: "After reading, \[reader\] believes \_\_\_ and is ready to \_\_\_." Then pick the page's one job:
	- **Pre-handle objections** (listicle, one objection per reason).
	- **Change state** (story, from "I've tried everything" to "maybe it was never me").
	- **Install the mechanism** (Difference page, why the old way fails and the new way works).
	- **Segment** (one page per audience: "Women Over 50", "GLP-1 Users", "Dad Bods").
- **Why it works:** the job decides the format, the reason order and the proof. A presell built without one turns into a brochure that sells nothing in particular. If you cannot finish the sentence, you are not ready to build.
- **Weak:** "A page about our new sheets that covers all the benefits." **Strong:** "After reading, a hot sleeper believes her sheets cause the 3 AM sweats, not her body, and is ready to start a 100-night trial." *(illustrative)*
- **Evidence:** Nikita: "Know the POINT of your advertorial before you make it: are you pre-handling objections? Getting them into the right state? Don't build it not knowing the aim." Each live page picks one job. True Classic pre-handles objections (fit, comfort, price, proof, guarantee). Mellow changes state ("'Maybe It's Not The Pillow. Maybe It's Just Me.'"). Lume installs a mechanism ("Instead of masking odor with fragrance, Lume blocks it at the source with Mandelic Acid…"). Grüns segments ("7 Benefits GLP-1 Users Love About Grüns (Besides the Poops).").

### 2. The H1 or reason #1 picks up the ad's one idea
- **Rule:** move the ad's exact noun phrase, enemy, segment or mechanism into the H1 or reason #1. Write the page after the ad, never before. (General rule → Universal F2 and 1.1.)
- **Why it works:** the reader clicked for one idea. On a listicle, reason #1 is the first proof of the ad's claim, so the click pays off inside one scroll. If the page opens on a different idea, she thinks she landed in the wrong place.
- **Weak:** HexClad's ad says "Stop buying cheap cookware you have to replace every year!" and the lander opens on "HexClad does what your other pans can't." The durability angle only shows up at reason #1. **Strong:** MUD\\WTR's ad says "Most 'mushroom coffees' = regular coffee with a fairy-dust sprinkle" and the H1 is "6 Ways MUD\\WTR Original Is Different From Mushroom Coffee."
- **Evidence:** Sassy Saints ad "your nails aren't damaged, they're depleted… 3 lipids" lands on reason #1 "Your nails aren't damaged, they're dried out… three lipids." Ridge ad "five ways to power" lands on "FIVE WAYS TO CHARGE. ONE DEVICE." Grüns ad "especially on a GLP-1" lands on a "💊 FOR GLP-1 USERS" pill over the GLP-1 headline.

### 3. Call out the reader in the headline
- **Rule:** name who the page is for in the H1, with the label she would use for herself. (→ Copywriting doc: the call-out. Segment pages → Universal 3.5.)
- **Why it works:** she confirms "this is me" before reading any argument. Everyone else leaves cheaply, and whoever stays reads every reason as written for her.
- **Weak:** "5 Reasons People Love Our Pans." **Strong:** "5 Reasons Home Cooks Who Hate Scrubbing Are Switching to Hybrid Pans." *(illustrative)*
- **Evidence:** "Women Over 50" (GroundingWell), "GLP-1 Users" (Grüns), "Dad Bods" (True Classic), "Americans" (Miracle). Sassy Saints calls out by problem instead of person: "7 REASONS YOUR NAILS KEEP SPLITTING…"

### 4. One job per reason, in the 10-slot order
- **Rule:** each reason installs one belief or clears one objection. Order them the way her doubts surface: outcome first, guarantee second-to-last, crowd number last. Each reason should answer the doubt the previous one raised.
- **Why it works:** a listicle is an argument in disguise (→ Copywriting doc: argument, necessary beliefs). Fragments that each clear one doubt add up to "I can't lose." The guarantee lands right before the ask, when "what if it doesn't work for me" is loudest (→ Universal 3.3 Guarantee as a numbered reason).
- **Weak:** seven reasons that are all features ("soft," "cool," "stylish," "durable"), with the guarantee in the footer. *(illustrative)* **Strong:** True Classic's chain, where each reason answers the doubt the last one planted: "Look jacked WITHOUT going to the gym" → "Buttery soft & breathable" ("Say goodbye to itchy and tight") → "Premium quality, for less" → "A shirt sold every 5 seconds" → "The 'perfect fit' guarantee."
- **Evidence:** the same role order ran across all five "Reaching #N" listicles (Miracle, Ryze, GroundingWell, Javvy, Sassy Saints). GroundingWell's guarantee is reason #9 of 10, a "90 DAYS RISK-FREE" seal.

Slot notes:
- Short lists compress the order. True Classic's 5 reasons run identity outcome, comfort, price, proof, guarantee.
- Foreign or "sells out everywhere" proof can fill the crowd slot: "It Sells Out Everywhere (Even in Korea)." (Sassy Saints)
- The observer slot is the most underused. If a pet, partner or child notices the change, the reader can't call it placebo (→ Universal 3.2 Observer proof).

### 5. Every reason is a complete mini-ad
- **Rule:** build every reason from five parts. If a reason has no pain scene, cut it or merge it. Keep every reason open on the page, never behind an accordion.
	1. **Benefit title** (the reason headline).
	2. **Pain scene** in the reader's own words.
	3. **Mechanism** in one plain line.
	4. **Concrete outcome** with a number.
	5. **Proof artifact** plus a benefit-phrased link.
- **Why it works:** many readers skim to one reason and decide there, so each reason has to sell alone. The skeleton is a commodity. Five brands run near-identical listicle copy, and Ryze and Javvy even share the byline "Jade M." The reader has seen the frame. She has not seen her own 3 AM. (→ Copywriting doc: specificity.)
- **Weak:** "Sleep better every night." *(illustrative)* Or HexClad's "6 Reasons Why You'll Love HexClad" set as a collapsible accordion, so most reasons stay closed. **Strong:** the shared listicle pattern: pain scene "Wide awake at 3 AM. Mind racing. Body aching." (GroundingWell), then the mechanism "normalizes cortisol rhythms," then the outcome "6, 7, even 8 solid hours."
- **Evidence:** Ryze closes each reason with its own benefit link ("👉 Energy That Lasts Past Lunch.") and a captioned image band ("⚡ Steady Energy"). True Classic's comfort reason names the exact negative and flips it: "Say goodbye to itchy and tight with a tee that fits just right."

### 6. Show proof as a thing on screen
- **Rule:** across the reasons, show at least three artifacts: a screenshot, a seal, a chart, a photo pair, a dated lab badge, a citation list. Put each one inside the reason it proves. (Why artifacts beat adjectives → Universal 1.4.)
- **Why it works:** an artifact reads as evidence the reader could check. A sentence reads as a claim she has to take on trust.
- **Weak:** "Backed by science." *(illustrative)* **Strong:** GroundingWell #7, a collage of research abstracts with the link "Explore The Research Database."
- **Evidence:** GroundingWell #5 embeds a screenshot of Facebook comments and #9 a "90 DAYS RISK-FREE" seal. Ryze #3 shows "Spiked Cortisol" vs "Balanced Cortisol" photos. Grüns: "Tested by Light Labs in SEP 2026 / 35 substances tested." MUD\\WTR's footer lists citations by benefit with real PMC and NCT IDs.

### 7. One offer object at the end, reached by a pivot
- **Rule:** close on one bordered card that stacks price, what's included, shipping, guarantee and a true deadline, with one button and the renewal terms under it. Make it the only boxed white element on the page. Bridge into it with a pivot line.
- **Why it works:** the eye reads the only box as the destination. One button leaves nothing to stall on. The pivot turns the time she spent reading into commitment.
- **Weak:** the article ends on three product tiles and a "Shop all" link. *(illustrative)* **Strong:** Ryze's pivot "Reaching #11 Means One Thing: You're Serious About a Better Morning." leads into one card: "UP TO 40% OFF FOR A LIMITED TIME ONLY!… \[GET 40% OFF\]… Try it today with a 30-Day Money-Back Guarantee!"
- **Evidence:** in Miracle and Ryze the offer card is the only boxed white element on the page. Hiya states the money terms under the button: "$15 each today, $30 each on a monthly basis thereafter."

### 8. Editorial costume, honest footer
- **Rule:** dress the page as editorial (kicker, byline, "Last Updated" date, summary) and close with an advertisement disclaimer. The byline is a real staffer, the founder, or the brand voice. The date is current. (Proof policy → Universal 3.2.)
- **Why it works:** an editorial format lowers ad resistance, and a fresh date reads as maintained. The disclaimer keeps the costume honest. A fake credential on top of it turns a format into a fabrication.
- **Weak:** Mellow's "By Dr. Ralph / Last Updated: Dec 3, 2025" over a story narrated by "Sarah," still running in October 2026. **Strong:** "By Linda L. / Last Updated Oct 4, 2026" (GroundingWell), with a footer like Miracle's: "Disclaimer: this is an advertisement, using a fictional customer… not an actual news article."
- **Evidence:** "By Jade M. / Last Updated Sep 6, 2026" (Ryze). GroundingWell adds an FDA statement to its footer. Miracle discloses its fictional customer in the footer.

## Techniques

Pick per goal. Moves the Universal page owns appear as one-line pointers with the presell placement only.

### Zone 1: Top of page

- **Sticky deal bar.** A bar pinned at the top with the offer and a true deadline.
	- Swipe: "EARLY HALLOWEEN SALE / UP TO 40% OFF WITH FREE GIFTS / OFFER RESERVED 00:06:34" (Ryze). "SOLD OUT 5× / CLAIM 50% OFF" (Mellow).
	- **Good for →** keeping the offer in view through 2,000 words of reading.
	- **When to use →** every listicle. The deadline must be a real date or real stock. Ryze's "reserved" timer is session-based and restarts on reload, so copy the wording, not the reset (→ Universal 3.4).
- **Arrival segmenter popup.** → Universal 3.6 One-question segmenter. On a presell: optional. Use the answer to pick the email flow, and never let it cover the first screen of the article.
- **Masthead kicker.** A label above the headline that signals editorial.
	- Swipe: "SPECIAL REPORT" (Mellow).
	- **Good for →** story and mechanism formats.
	- **When to use →** with your brand's own editorial label. Don't invent a publication. Sassy Saints' "DERMATOL TIMES" masthead is the pattern to skip.
- **Proof line above the H1.** → Universal F5 and 3.2 (clinician platform). On a presell: one real number or third-party line as the eyebrow, e.g. "1,600+ clinicians shared this with their patients without compensation." (Grüns), "40,000+ Reviews" (True Classic).
- **Ogilvy editorial layout.** → Universal 3.7. On a story advertorial: big photo, headline under it, a drop cap to open the body, a caption under every image.

### Zone 2: Headline, pull quote, summary

- **Switching headline.** The default listicle H1: "\[N\] Reasons Why \[Self-ID group\] Are Switching to \[Category or 'Nickname'\] \[to Outcome\] \[in Season/Year\]."
	- Swipe: "10 Reasons Why Women Over 50 Are Switching to this 'Special Sheet' to Wake Up Refreshed And Pain-Free in 2026." (GroundingWell)
	- Anatomy: bandwagon ("Switching"), self-ID ("Women Over 50"), curiosity (product unnamed, "Special Sheet" in quotes), outcome ("Refreshed And Pain-Free"), recency ("2026"). That is 4 of Nikita's 7 headline ingredients. "Switching" implies a crowd has already moved without naming a competitor.
	- **Good for →** cold, broad audiences.
	- **When to use →** as the default listicle H1. Swap the self-ID group per ad set and keep the year current.
- **Ditching headline.** The H1 names the old category the reader is leaving.
	- Swipe: "5 Reasons Why Millions Are Ditching Green Powders" (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** a challenger in a category the reader already uses and dislikes.
	- **When to use →** when the ad attacks the old solution. Back it with a category-degrade line in the hero.
- **Problem-titled headline.** The headline names the problem and the product appears later.
	- Swipe: "7 REASONS YOUR NAILS KEEP SPLITTING…" (Sassy Saints, product first appears at reason 3)
	- **Good for →** problem-aware readers who don't know a solution exists.
	- **When to use →** when the product name would trigger "ad" too early.
- **Segment headline with a joke.** A segment-built H1 that lowers a taboo with humor.
	- Swipe: "7 Benefits GLP-1 Users Love About Grüns (Besides the Poops)."
	- **Good for →** embarrassing or bodily categories.
	- **When to use →** one page per segment, each matched to a segment ad.
- **Identity headline with an overheard subhead.** An insecurity in the H1, then a line someone else said as the subhead.
	- Swipe: "The Best T-Shirts for Dad Bods: 5 Reasons Why Men Are Switching to True Classic" / "My wife was like 'Wait, when did you start working out?!'"
	- **Good for →** appearance and identity products. The subhead sells the social reward without making a claim (→ Universal 3.2 Observer proof).
	- **When to use →** when the real payoff is how other people see the buyer.
- **Ordinal "different from" headline.** The H1 attacks the category and each reason is a numbered difference.
	- Swipe: "6 Ways MUD\\WTR Original Is Different From Mushroom Coffee" / "1. First — It Isn't Coffee." / "2. Second — Way More Mushrooms."
	- **Good for →** crowded categories where you must separate from look-alikes.
	- **When to use →** when the ad's hook is an enemy. Make sure each reason's body proves its title.
- **Accusation headline.** The H1 tells the reader she has the problem, then names the fix.
	- Swipe: "You Have Nutrition Gaps. Grüns Fills Them." (Grüns first-order page)
	- **Good for →** common, invisible problems the reader assumes she doesn't have.
	- **When to use →** offer-led or first-order pages. Follow it with a sourced problem stat, as Grüns does: "90% of U.S. adults don't meet recommended daily nutrient intake."
- **Pull quote that names the rival solution.** An interrupt quote above the summary, aimed at what the reader uses now.
	- Swipe: "'Read this BEFORE you take one more sleep/pain pill!'" (GroundingWell)
	- **Good for →** stopping her from filing you next to her current fix.
	- **When to use →** when the reader is actively using an alternative.
- **Summary line.** A TL;DR that delivers the whole pitch before reason 1.
	- Swipe: "SUMMARY: Upgrade your mornings… without the jitters, acidity, or 3pm slump." (Ryze)
	- **Good for →** skimmers. Many never read reason 2.
	- **When to use →** every listicle. Shape it as the outcome plus the pains it removes, or as pain, reframe, why now.
- **Category degrade.** Lower the category before you position against it.
	- Swipe: "Most mushroom coffees are watery, brothy, or sometimes rancid… only have a sprinkle of mushroom powder." (MUD\\WTR). "Greens powders taste awful… require daily cleanup." (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** challenger brands in a known category.
	- **When to use →** only with a true, specific flaw. Vague trash talk reads as bitterness.

### Zone 3: Inside each reason

- **Captioned image band.** → Universal 3.7 Caption burned on the image. On a listicle: a colour band under every reason photo carrying the benefit, not the product name ("⚡ Steady Energy", Ryze).
- **Benefit-phrased link.** Every reason ends in its own link, written as the payoff.
	- Swipe: "👉 Energy That Lasts Past Lunch." (Ryze)
	- **Good for →** readers who are sold halfway down the list.
	- **When to use →** every reason. Every link goes to the same offer card or product page.
- **Identity escalation line.** A two-word before and after, then the honest mechanism behind it.
	- Swipe: "Go from DAD to DADDY. Our premium tees are designed to be tighter around your arms, chest and shoulders, with a little room in the belly to hide you 'dad bod'." (True Classic)
	- **Good for →** appearance products where the buyer is embarrassed about the starting point.
	- **When to use →** reason 1 of identity products. Name the flaw out loud and show how the product works around it.
- **Checklist inside a reason.** 3 to 4 parallel ✔️ lines that each kill one sensory objection.
	- Swipe: "✔️ Ultra-soft signature fabric / ✔️ Free movement in shoulders / ✔️ Stretchy and breathable / ✔️ Stylish, classic fit for all sizes" (True Classic)
	- **Good for →** feel, fit and texture objections online shoppers can't test.
	- **When to use →** the comfort or quality reason.
- **Ingredient-to-benefit arrows.** Ingredient, then plain benefit, then the felt outcome.
	- Swipe: "Lion's Mane → focus and clarity, so your brain feels switched on instead of scrambled." (MUD\\WTR)
	- **Good for →** formulas, materials, tech specs.
	- **When to use →** the "how it works" reason (slot 4).
- **Segment problem grid.** A tile grid of the problems one segment has, each tied to a symptom she feels.
	- Swipe: "6 Common Gaps when on GLP-1" with tiles like Vitamin D: muscle aches, Fiber: poor digestion (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** segment pages where the reader doesn't know what she's missing.
	- **When to use →** the hidden-cause reason (slot 3) on a segment listicle.
- **Reviews that name the rival.** A customer review inside a reason that names the old solution.
	- Swipe: "I was taking AG1 but… $94 a month and the drink was definitely gritty/chalky… half the price." (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** the comparison reason. The customer says what the brand can't.
	- **When to use →** when your real reviews contain switcher stories. Mine them.
- **Reason-why price line.** → Universal 3.1 Reason-why discount. On a listicle: the value reason (slot 8). "We cut the markup so you get three premium quality t-shirts for less than the price of one designer tee!" (True Classic)
- **Timeline reason.** → Universal F14. On a listicle: its own reason at slot 5, e.g. Sassy Saints reason 5 "Week 1, Week 2-4, Month 1-2 … Month 5-6".
- **Expert at the claim.** → Universal 3.2. On a listicle: the quote sits inside the reason it backs. Hiya puts a pediatric dentist inside reason #2, the sugar and cavities reason.
- **Review trio cast by job.** → Universal 3.2 Testimonial casting. On a listicle: three reviews in the proof reason, one job each. True Classic picks the payoff (Brian G.), the fit detail (Andrew B.) and the secondary objections (Gabe L.).
- **Survey stat with an asterisk.** → Universal 3.2 Hero stat with method footnote. On a listicle: inside the proof reason, e.g. "87% of guys agree they look more fit in a True Classic®️ tee.*"

### Zone 4: The pivot

- **Finish-line reward.** Reaching the bottom is framed as proof of intent, and the discount as earned.
	- Swipe: "Reaching #11 Means One Thing: You're Serious About a Better Morning. We don't run this offer every day — consider it your welcome gift for reading this far." (Ryze)
	- **Good for →** turning reading time into commitment.
	- **When to use →** every listicle, between the last reason and the offer card.
- **Hidden extra reason.** The title promises N reasons, N minus 1 are listed, and the offer is reason N.
	- Swipe: Ryze's "11 Reasons" lists 10. The offer is #11.
	- **Good for →** sliding from argument into offer without a gear change.
	- **When to use →** when the offer is a genuine reason (a big first-order discount, a free gift, a long trial). Say so in the pivot, or the count reads as a mistake.
- **Anti-influencer close.** The narrator explains why she's telling you, and that she isn't selling.
	- Swipe: "Why I'm Telling You All This." / "I'm not a sleep influencer" (Mellow)
	- **Good for →** defusing "this is an ad" right before the CTA.
	- **When to use →** story advertorials (beat 10).

### Zone 5: Offer card and fine print

- **Stacked offer card.** Discount, deadline, shipping and guarantee in one bordered card with one button.
	- Swipe: "UP TO 40% OFF FOR A LIMITED TIME ONLY! This limited-time deal is in high demand and stock keeps selling out. \[GET 40% OFF\] DEAL ENDING IN 00:06:33 / Sell-Out Risk: High | FREE shipping / Try it today with a 30-Day Money-Back Guarantee!" (Ryze)
	- **Good for →** the single decision point.
	- **When to use →** every presell. Keep the structure. Drop the stock sentence and the "Sell-Out Risk" meter unless stock is truly limited. The same stock sentence runs on five brands' cards, so it proves nothing. State shipping and guarantee terms that are true for the plan the button buys.
- **Sticky button or risk triplet.** → Universal 3.7 Sticky CTA. On a listicle: a sticky bottom button ("YES, GET 50% OFF ✓ SPECIAL DISCOUNT ACTIVE"), or MUD\\WTR's "Try it and save 43% / 30-Day Money Back Guarantee / Free USA Shipping" after every reason.
- **Renewal under the button.** → Universal 3.3 Renewal stated in the hero. On the offer card: one plain line under the button, e.g. "$15 each today, $30 each on a monthly basis thereafter." (Hiya)
- **One-time defined by what it lacks.** → Universal 3.1. On a presell card with two options: "One Time Purchase $66.65 $2.38/day · No Free Shipping · No First Order Discount." (Grüns)
- **Page-scoped, dated fine print.** → Universal 3.4 First-order-only offers and Real dates. On a presell: limit the discount to the first delivery, this page, a real date, and generate the date automatically. Javvy's read "…created on try.javvycoffee.com/as by 10/06/2026…" on the day it was viewed. Ryze's still showed "by 9/8/2026" on Oct 6.
- **Advertisement disclaimer.** → Universal 3.3 Disclaimer as trust. On a presell: the last line of the footer, every time (Fundamental 8).
- **Reviews after the card.** → Universal 3.2 Testimonial casting. On a presell: 3 to 7 short reviews that mirror the reasons, under a specific header like "Over 2,400 People Are Already Sleeping Better." (Mellow). The count must match the PDP.

Craft notes:
- Beat 3 is the engine. Itemize real alternatives with real prices. Each line neutralizes one competitor.
- Beat 4 uses Nikita's humiliation logic: the lower the starting point, the bigger the climb (→ Copywriting doc: "I was just like you, but worse").
- Beat 5: pick the authority your reader already trusts. Nikita: "don't assume it's 'just a doctor'; find the right authority through market/ICP research."
- Beat 11 is the most skipped and the most valuable. One honest limit makes the timeline believable (→ Universal F15).
- One narrator, one voice, first person throughout. Mellow slips into third person once ("tension headaches creeping up from her shoulders") and signs the page "Dr. Ralph" while the narrator is "Sarah."

### Zone 7: The "Difference" page

A shorter mechanism page. Use it as a warm or retargeting lander, or as the home page's "learn more" destination.

- **Difference-page arc.** Hook, mechanism, founder origin, safety line, testimonials, product chooser.
	- Swipe: hook "Outrageously Effective Odor Control." Mechanism: "Instead of masking odor with fragrance, Lume blocks it at the source with Mandelic Acid… This breakthrough approach is tested, patented, and proven." Founder: "When big brands said, 'no one needs this,' she built Lume from the ground up to prove them wrong. And with over 300,000 five star reviews, it's clear she did." Safety: "Strong enough for underarms. Gentle enough for everywhere." (Lume)
	- **Good for →** a product whose method is the differentiator.
	- **When to use →** when the ad sells the mechanism and the reader needs to understand it before she shops.
- **Comparison table on a presell.** Your product against "some" or "most" alternatives, row by row.
	- Swipe: MUD\\WTR "Not All Blends Are Created Equal" (Taste / Organic / >2,000mg / USA grown / L-theanine against "Some"). Mando: "Scientifically proven to last 72 hours / Stops odor before it starts" vs "Quits working midday / Masks odor after it forms."
	- **Good for →** markets where every brand claims the same benefit (sophistication stage 3 and 4).
	- **When to use →** before the offer. Write each row in parallel grammar so the contrast reads as fact.
- **Replacement math.** → Universal 3.1 Replacement math table. On a presell: the value reason (slot 8), or its own block right before the offer card.
- **Creator and founder slugs.** → Universal 3.5 Per-creator and per-founder landers. On a presell: one slug per whitelisted page, with that person's quote as the proof block (Ridge `/pages/tech-MKBHD-6RLP`, MUD\\WTR rise-2 for the founder's page).

## Blueprint

### Blueprint A: Listicle (build order)

1. **Write the point sentence.** "After reading, \[reader\] believes \_\_\_ and is ready to \_\_\_." Pick the job (Fundamental 1).
2. **Pull from the ad:** its noun phrase, enemy, segment and every number. Take the numbers from the number sheet (→ Universal F3).
3. **Sticky bar:** offer plus a true deadline.
4. **Byline and "Last Updated" date:** a real person or the brand voice, dated this month.
5. **Proof line above the H1** (optional) and a **pull quote** naming the rival solution (optional).
6. **H1:** "\[N\] Reasons Why \[Self-ID group\] Are Switching to \[Category or 'Nickname'\] \[to Outcome\] in \[Season/Year\]", in the ad's words.
7. **Summary line:** the outcome plus the pains it removes.
8. **Reasons (6 to 10):** each built from the five parts in Fundamental 5, ordered by the 10-slot table, each with a captioned image and a benefit link.
9. **Pivot:** "Reaching #\[N\] Means One Thing: You're Serious About \[Outcome\]."
10. **Offer card:** one price, what's included, shipping that is true for this plan, guarantee, real deadline, one button, renewal terms under it.
11. **3 to 6 reviews** that mirror the reasons, with a count that matches the PDP.
12. **Fine print and advertisement disclaimer:** first delivery, this page, a real auto-generated date.
13. **Sticky bottom button.**
14. **Seam audit:** title tag, URL slug and H1 state the same reason count. Every figure matches the ad and the PDP. Season words fit the current month.

### Blueprint B: Story advertorial (build order)

1. **Write the point sentence and choose one narrator:** a real customer with permission, the founder, or a fictional customer disclosed in the footer.
2. **Kicker** ("Special Report" or your own editorial label) and a third-person headline with the attempt count and the price.
3. **Byline** that matches the narrator.
4. **Pain ritual** with a sunk-cost number.
5. **Priced graveyard:** 3 to 6 failed alternatives, each with what went wrong and what it cost.
6. **Self-blame line.**
7. **Authority diagnosis** from the type of expert your reader already trusts.
8. **Skeptical research beat.**
9. **Spec checklist with the price last**, reframed against the sunk cost.
10. **Timeline:** 4 to 6 stages, each one small, concrete win.
11. **Plain-voice mechanism.**
12. **"Why I'm telling you"** plus 3 "If you've…" qualifiers.
13. **Honest under-claim.**
14. **Recap bullets, risk line and one button** that names the risk ("Try \[Product\] Risk-Free").
15. **Reviews** with a count that matches the PDP.
16. **Disclaimer footer and seam audit:** one narrator throughout, byline matches, every number matches the PDP.

## Chiefing checklist

**Point and match**
- [ ] Point sentence written and one job picked before any copy (F1)
- [ ] H1 or reason #1 uses the ad's exact noun phrase, enemy, segment or mechanism (F2)
- [ ] Headline names who the page is for (F3)
- [ ] Headline uses at least 3 to 4 of the 7 ingredients (Zone 2, Switching headline)
- [ ] Summary line delivers the pitch before reason 1 (Zone 2)

**Reasons**
- [ ] Reasons follow the 10-slot order. Guarantee second-to-last, crowd number last (F4)
- [ ] Each reason answers the doubt the previous one raised (F4)
- [ ] One "not your fault" reason, and one observer-proof reason if the category allows it (F4)
- [ ] Every reason has a pain scene in customer language, a one-line mechanism, an outcome with a number, and a benefit link (F5)
- [ ] All reasons are open on the page, none hidden in an accordion (F5)
- [ ] Every reason image carries a benefit caption (Zone 3)
- [ ] At least 3 proof artifacts, each inside the reason it proves (F6)
- [ ] Each reason's body proves its own title (Zone 2, Ordinal headline)

**Offer**
- [ ] Pivot line between the last reason and the offer (F7, Zone 4)
- [ ] One offer card, one button, the only boxed element on the page (F7)
- [ ] Renewal terms under the button, in plain words (Zone 5)
- [ ] Shipping claim is true for the plan the button buys (Zone 5)
- [ ] Deadline is real and auto-dated. No timer that resets on reload, no stock meter without real stock (Zone 1, Zone 5)
- [ ] Fine print limits the discount to first delivery, this page and a real date (Zone 5)

**Costume and seams**
- [ ] Byline is a real person or the brand voice. No invented credential or publication (F8, Zone 1)
- [ ] "Last Updated" date and season words are current (F8)
- [ ] Advertisement disclaimer in the footer (F8)
- [ ] Title tag, URL slug and H1 state the same reason count (Blueprint A, step 14)
- [ ] Every number matches the ad and the PDP: discount, price per unit, guarantee days, review count (→ Universal F3)
- [ ] Story version: one narrator in first person throughout, byline matches the narrator, honest under-claim present (Zone 6)
