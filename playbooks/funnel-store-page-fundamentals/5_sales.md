## The job of this page

**Where it sits.** Nikita's Chad funnel runs AD → ADVERTORIAL → SALES PAGE → ORDER PAGE → ONE-TIME OFFERS → DOWNSELL (→ ① Universal 4.3). The advertorial changes the reader's state. The sales page closes. In DTC ecom the long sales page is often the product page built long: HexClad, Our Place and Caraway run editorialized PDPs where "the product page IS the sales letter." Use this page whenever a standard PDP can't hold the full argument.

**When to use it.**
- Warm traffic from a presell (→ ④ Pre-Sales Page) or retargeting.
- Cold traffic where the reader must be sold a new mechanism, a premium price, a subscription, or a stigmatized problem. Hims runs a long-form ED condition guide. Hers runs a weight-loss advertorial.
- Not for bottom-of-funnel buyers who already want the product. Send them straight to the PDP or an offer page (→ ① Universal 4.1).

**The one point.** Get the order. Nikita's Holy Grail ranks the inputs: copy first, media second, design third. "Mess up the headline, nothing else matters, they bounce."

**The arc (Adweek, from Nikita's notes).** Create a comfortable buying environment. Hook the reader, get them nodding, force the new belief, then a CTA that closes. Sell the outcome, not the product. (→ Copywriting doc: slippery slide, yes ladder, belief change.)

**VSL vs TSL.** Same skeleton. A VSL puts the lead, the story and the mechanism in a video at the top, with the offer stack and proof below it. A TSL puts the same beats in text. Write the TSL first. The VSL script is the TSL read aloud over b-roll.

**Evidence note.** No full DTC VSL or classic long-form TSL was captured end to end in the research. The live examples come from long PDPs, advertorials and condition guides (HexClad, Hims, Hers, Mellow, Lume, Grüns, AG1, Hiya). Nikita's notes are the source of truth for the structure. The brands show each move working in the wild.

## Fundamentals

### 1. Benefit headline with at least 3 to 4 of the 7 ingredients
- **Rule:** Put the biggest benefit in the headline and build it from at least 3 to 4 of these: curiosity, call out the pain, promise the solution, specificity, simplicity, credibility, time frame. You don't need all 7.
- **Why it works:** One ingredient earns a glance. Four give the reader a reason to keep going. Ogilvy, in Nikita's notes: "Unless your headline sells your product, you've wasted 90% of your money."
- **Weak:** "The Best Topper You'll Ever Buy" (one vague ingredient) *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "Hot Sleepers: Wake Up Dry in 7 Nights With the Topper Physical Therapists Recommend" (call-out, pain, promise, specificity, credibility, time frame) *(illustrative)*
- **Evidence:** GroundingWell: "10 Reasons Why Women Over 50 Are Switching to this 'Special Sheet' to Wake Up Refreshed And Pain-Free in 2026" (call-out, curiosity, promise, time frame). Mellow: "She Tried 9 Pillows Before Discovering This $49 Game-Changer" (curiosity, specificity, simplicity).

### 2. The headline sells the first sentence
- **Rule:** The headline's only job is to get the first sentence read. Make that sentence so short it is read before the reader decides to read it.
- **Why it works:** Readers commit one line at a time. A short first line costs nothing to start, and once started they keep going. (→ Copywriting doc: slippery slide.)
- **Weak:** "Our coffee is crafted from the finest beans sourced across three continents." *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "You don't need more coffee." *(illustrative)*
- **Evidence:** Mellow follows the 9-pillows headline with "I Spent $800+ On Pillows Over The Last Year." Grüns opens its first-order page on "You Have Nutrition Gaps. Grüns Fills Them."

### 3. Say what the rest of the niche isn't saying
- **Rule:** Before you keep a headline, check that it says something new or in a new way. Be truthful, then find the intriguing angle.
- **Why it works:** Starch, in Nikita's notes: "Ads that are different and recognisable, with something new/fresh in headline or picture, score higher (read-most)." A familiar line gets filed with every other ad.
- **Weak:** "Work from home, turn your email into big profits." (Nikita's notes) **Strong:** "19-year-old college kid discovers a clever way to persuade people to send you money via PayPal." (Nikita's notes)
- **Evidence:** Hims: "Be the guy who never skips the encore." Ridge: "Tech's toughest critic approved it." Liquid Death: "PLASTIC RECYCLING IS A MYTH."

### 4. Mechanism of the problem before mechanism of the solution
- **Rule:** First explain why everything the reader tried failed (Unique Mechanism of the Problem). Then explain why this works (Unique Mechanism of the Solution).
- **Why it works:** The UMP takes the blame off the reader ("it wasn't you") and disqualifies every competitor at once. The UMS then has nothing left to fight. (→ Copywriting doc: unique mechanism.)
- **Weak:** "Our formula uses premium, clinically studied ingredients." *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "Your nails aren't damaged, they're dried out… three lipids." (Sassy Saints)
- **Evidence:** UMP: Hims "you might think of the condition as your body's way of telling you to talk with a doctor." Sassy Saints "Normal strengtheners actually make your nails weaker." UMS: Lume "blocks it at the source with Mandelic Acid." Miracle "anti-microbial silver that prevents up to 99.7% of bacteria growth through a natural process called ionization."

### 5. Make the result feel obtainable
- **Rule:** Show someone like the reader, or worse off, who got the result.
- **Why it works:** Most people don't believe in themselves. Desire without belief does nothing. (→ Copywriting doc: "I was just like you, but worse.")
- **Weak:** "Anyone can do it!" *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "The amazing money-making secret of a (desperate nerd from Ohio)." (Nikita's notes)
- **Evidence:** Hers: "This time you have Hers." moves past failures off the reader. Hims tags testimonials by age: "—Scott, 38", "—Daniel, 34". Mellow's narrator tried nine pillows before one worked.

### 6. Make them see the movie
- **Rule:** Be so specific that the reader plays a movie of owning the product before they own it. Name the exact moment and the exact number.
- **Why it works:** Cashvertising, in Nikita's notes: "CRUSH COMP WITH EXTREME SPECIFICITY." A scene is believed and remembered. An adjective is skipped. Nikita's notes add the catch: the "trailer" has to be engaging or they click away.
- **Weak:** "Sleep better and feel less pain." *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "Hadn't reached for ibuprofen all week" (Mellow, week-1 timeline pill)
- **Evidence:** GroundingWell: "Wide awake at 3 AM. Mind racing. Body aching." Mellow's priced graveyard: "Pillow #2: Memory foam from Amazon with 10,000+ reviews. Slept hot… $65 wasted."

### 7. Feature, then benefit, then felt outcome
- **Rule:** Every feature leads to a benefit. Every benefit leads to an outcome the reader can feel. All three appear on the page.
- **Why it works:** People only care what it does for them (Cashvertising). The feature makes the benefit believable. The outcome makes it wanted.
- **Weak:** "Tri-ply stainless core." *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "Tri-ply core, so the eggs slide off and you're done washing up before the coffee's ready." *(illustrative)*
- **Evidence:** MUD\\WTR: "Lion's Mane → focus and clarity, so your brain feels switched on instead of scrambled."

### 8. Proof sits at each claim, and every piece has a job
- **Rule:** Beyond the review section, embed proof next to each claim it backs. No proof "for the sake of it." Casting rules → ① Universal 3.2.
- **Why it works:** The reader doubts at each claim, not at the end of the page. Proof that arrives with the doubt settles it on the spot.
- **Weak:** One "What our customers say" carousel at the bottom of a long page. *(illustrative)* **Strong:** Hiya puts a pediatric dentist quote inside its reason about sugar and cavities.
- **Evidence:** Grüns puts "84% improved digestion… *independent consumer survey of 3,330 participants in Feb 2026" beside the digestion claim. GroundingWell embeds a Facebook-comment screenshot inside reason #5.

### 9. Handle the biggest objections early, the rest in the FAQ
- **Rule:** The top 2 or 3 objections get embedded proof and an embedded mini-FAQ mid-page, right where they come up. The FAQ handles everything else. (→ ① Universal F10.)
- **Why it works:** A reader who hits her main objection at paragraph 4 and sees no answer never reaches the FAQ.
- **Weak:** The price objection first answered in FAQ question 9. *(illustrative)* **Strong:** Mellow reveals "$49. That last part made me pause." mid-story, right after the reader has added up $800 of failed pillows.
- **Evidence:** Hers pre-handles the side-effect fear in the body: "Side effect support from the start. We've got you covered from day one." Lume answers "is it safe?" in one line: "Strong enough for underarms. Gentle enough for everywhere."

### 10. Credibility from the authority this buyer trusts
- **Rule:** Find the authority your ICP actually trusts through research. Don't assume "a doctor." Name them and their title.
- **Why it works:** Trust is borrowed from whoever the reader already believes. A tech buyer trusts a harsh reviewer more than an MD. A parent may trust a pediatric dentist, or another parent, more than a celebrity.
- **Weak:** "Doctor recommended" over a stock photo of a white coat. *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "Tech's toughest critic approved it." (Ridge, quoting MKBHD)
- **Evidence:** Mellow: a chiropractor. Jones Road: Bobbi Brown. Hiya: "Dr. Joel Warsh: Board-Certified Pediatrician." AG1: "Lewis Hamilton ('SEVEN-TIME FORMULA 1 WORLD CHAMPION')".

### 11. Risk reversal aimed at the real fear
- **Rule:** Name the buyer's specific fear, then remove it in their terms. Be more creative than "money back." Repeat it under every button and give it its own section. (→ ① Universal F8, 3.3.)
- **Why it works:** A generic guarantee answers a question nobody asked. A specific one answers the exact "what if" that is stopping this buyer.
- **Weak:** A "30-Day Money-Back Guarantee" badge. *(illustrative)* **Strong:** "SHADE MATCH GUARANTEE: Shade Match Promise / 30-Day Returns / 45-Day Exchange" plus "Still not sure? Send a no make-up selfie to get matched by the experts." (Jones Road)
- **Evidence:** True Classic: "if it doesn't fit right, let us know within 100 days and we'll replace it for free, no questions asked." Grüns: "Less than 1% of customers use our Money-Back Guarantee."

### 12. Urgency and scarcity are true or absent
- **Rule:** Give a real reason to act today and add real scarcity wherever you write copy. Real deadlines and real stock only. (→ ① Universal 3.4.)
- **Why it works:** Scarcity moves people to act now instead of later (Cialdini's sixth weapon, in Nikita's notes). His own CRO note sets the limit: fake urgency "looks desperate, kills credibility." A buyer who catches a timer resetting stops trusting every other claim.
- **Weak:** "OFFER RESERVED 00:06:34" that resets on every reload (Ryze). Miracle's timer read 14:55, then 14:36, then 14:34 across visits. **Strong:** "🎁 Order by October 6th for 61% Off" (Grüns, live on October 6)
- **Evidence:** Ollie shows "Lamb w/ Cranberries" as "OUT OF STOCK" with "Notify Me." Caraway: "Estimated Ship Date: 12/18." Dr. Squatch: "New customers only."

## Techniques

### Zone 1: Pre-headline and headline

- **Pre-headline.** One short line above the headline whose only job is to get eyes onto the headline.
	- Swipe: "40,000+ Reviews" (True Classic). "1,600+ clinicians shared this with their patients without compensation." (Grüns). "SPECIAL REPORT" (Mellow).
	- **Good for →** putting credibility before the claim (→ ① Universal F5), a call-out, or an editorial frame.
	- **When to use →** every sales page. Grade 5 to 8, no thinking required. Never a question unless the answer is a guaranteed yes.
- **Headline scored on the 7 ingredients.** Draft 10 headlines, count the ingredients in each, keep only those with 4 or more.
	- Swipe: see the table below.
	- **Good for →** the H1 of any long-form page.
	- **When to use →** every headline draft. Then run Caples' check from Nikita's notes: "4 qualities of a good headline: self-interest, news, curiosity, quick/easy way." Finally check it against Fundamental 3.

- **Identity headline.** Sell the person the buyer wants to be, not the deficiency.
	- Swipe: "Be the guy who never skips the encore." (Hims)
	- **Good for →** stigmatized or aspirational categories.
	- **When to use →** when naming the problem outright would shame the reader. Template: "Be the \[X\] who never \[pain\]." *(illustrative)*
- **"If I \_\_\_, then I \_\_\_" open loop.** Gary Halbert's frame from Nikita's notes. The condition opens a loop the reader reads on to close.
	- Swipe: "If I lost 20 pounds without giving up Friday pizza, then you can too." *(illustrative)*
	- **Good for →** story-led sales letters and founder openings.
	- **When to use →** headline or first line, when the payoff is personal and the reader shares the "if."
- **Humiliation open.** Start at the lowest point, because the bigger the climb, the bigger the pull. Nikita's notes: "My friends laughed when they heard the idea / said I was a dreamer." (→ Copywriting doc: humiliation open.)
	- Swipe: "'Maybe It's Not The Pillow. Maybe It's Just Me.'" (Mellow, the self-blame low point before the reveal)
	- **Good for →** underdog products, founder stories, categories where the buyer feels judged.
	- **When to use →** headline or the first story beat. The win must come later on the page.

### Zone 3: Mechanism (UMP / UMS)

- **Problem-meaning reframe.** Recast the problem as a neutral signal, which removes the shame and adds urgency in one move.
	- Swipe: "you might think of the condition as your body's way of telling you to talk with a doctor." (Hims). "You were told it's hormones or 'just getting older'" (GroundingWell).
	- **Good for →** the UMP when the reader blames herself.
	- **When to use →** right after the problem agitation, before any product appears.
- **Install a belief, then sell the cure.** Plant a concern the reader never had, then show the product built to answer it.
	- Swipe: Gummies "NOTORIOUSLY UNSTABLE" → "Optimized for Freshness" (Hiya, which then sells the monthly refill as the fix).
	- **Good for →** crowded categories where the obvious benefits are taken.
	- **When to use →** only with a true, sourced concern. The new belief must hold up if a skeptic checks it.
- **Itemized villain.** Quantify the old way until staying with it feels careless.
	- Swipe: "Sugar is the new cigarettes", "5g sugar = 1 Oreo," "80% test inaccurately." (Hiya). "Many bottled water brands are just processed municipal tap water in plastic bottles." (Liquid Death). Gummies "CAN CONTRIBUTE TO CAVITIES" (Hiya).
	- **Good for →** stage 3 to 4 markets where every competitor claims the same benefit.
	- **When to use →** only with a sourced, specific number. Hiya's hedged verbs ("CAN", "UP TO", "NOTORIOUSLY") keep each claim defensible while the nouns carry the weight.
- **"Instead of \[their method\], \[our method\]."** The UMS in one sentence.
	- Swipe: "Instead of masking odor with fragrance, Lume blocks it at the source with Mandelic Acid—a gentle alpha hydroxy acid (AHA) that paralyzes the bacteria that cause odor." (Lume)
	- **Good for →** any product that works differently from the category default.
	- **When to use →** after the UMP is established. Use the same enemy words everywhere (→ ① Universal F13).
- **Name the unnamed feeling.** Give the reader a word for a problem she couldn't name. Once named, only you fix it.
	- Swipe: "the food noise was gone. I never really thought I was hearing food--but I definitely was." (Hers testimonial)
	- **Good for →** problems people feel but don't talk about.
	- **When to use →** mine reviews for a term customers coined, then use it in the mechanism block and the headline.
- **Mechanism in plain voice.** One analogy beats three paragraphs of science.
	- Swipe: "I'm not a scientist… but here's what I understand." (Mellow). "Earthing or grounding is like plugging into the Earth's natural energy… Imagine it like recharging your life's battery." (GroundingWell).
	- **Good for →** any technical claim.
	- **When to use →** right after the claim. Gloss every clinical term in the same breath (→ ② PDP Zone 8: inline gloss).

### Zone 4: Fascinations

- **Fascination block.** Bullets that paint what the buyer will get, learn or feel, so the reader's desire rises before the offer. (→ Copywriting doc: fascinations, for the full shape library.)
	- Swipe: "Look jacked WITHOUT going to the gym" (True Classic). "No Blender. No Shaker Bottle. No Dishwashing Required." (Grüns). "Stays fresh 3x longer. 3x less laundry needed." (Miracle).
	- **Good for →** raising desire after the mechanism and before the offer.
	- **When to use →** a block of 7 to 12, each true, each a different shape. Nikita's pro tip: real diversity, never "How to / How to / How to." Mix at least 5 shapes: the secret, the number, the myth, the warning, the page reference, the odd fact, the question, the "without", the identity, the reveal.

### Zone 5: Proof and credibility stacking

Already owned by ① Universal 3.2: hero stat with method footnote, expert placed at the claim, credibility gate on endorsers, tenure proof, testimonial casting. On a sales page, place them like this: stat footnotes under every number, each expert beside the one claim they back, tenure proof beside durability claims.

- **Credibility ladder.** Stack proof from institutional to expert to elite user to mass crowd.
	- Swipe: "Backed by 4 clinical studies a–d / Tried & tested by 3,9m people / Tested for 250+ banned substances." (AG1)
	- **Good for →** high-skepticism categories and premium prices.
	- **When to use →** as a one-line proof strip under the lead, then expand each rung later on the page.
- **Founder who was told no.** Credential, then a dismissive villain, then vindication by numbers.
	- Swipe: "When big brands said, 'no one needs this,' she built Lume from the ground up to prove them wrong. And with over 300,000 five star reviews, it's clear she did." (Lume)
	- **Good for →** founder-led brands and the "dramatic story" lead.
	- **When to use →** after the UMS, as the origin of the mechanism. The founder must be real and the numbers true.
- **Reviews that defuse the ad itself.**
	- Swipe: "I recently was advertised soap on Instagram. It's the first Instagram advertised product I have purchased." (Dr. Squatch)
	- **Good for →** cold traffic where "I don't buy from ads" is an objection.
	- **When to use →** in the first review block after the offer.
- **Retention data as proof.** Show what happens after the honeymoon.
	- Swipe: "We wanted to understand what happens after the honeymoon… 95% of users take Grüns at least 4-6x per week." (Grüns)
	- **Good for →** subscriptions, to answer "will I actually keep using it?"
	- **When to use →** next to the subscription option in the offer stack.

### Zone 6: Offer stack

Already owned by ① Universal 3.1: subscribe as the default, per-day price, gift stacking with values, choose-your-gift, replacement math, quantity ladder. ① Universal 3.3 owns renewal stated in the hero. The sales page's job is to present it as one stack: what's included, what each piece would cost separately, the per-day price, and what happens after the first order. AG1's stack is the model: Welcome Kit "free ~~€28~~", D3+K2 "free ~~€24~~", 5 Travel Packs "free ~~€17~~", "Total €87 ~~€156~~."

- **Process transparency.** Show the path from click to doorstep.
	- Swipe: "Better sex in 3 steps: Take a free, online assessment → Connect with a provider → Get meds delivered… ships discreetly to your door, for free." (Hims)
	- **Good for →** subscriptions, services, anything with an unfamiliar purchase path.
	- **When to use →** right above the offer stack, so the reader knows exactly what happens after the click.

### Zone 7: Guarantee and risk reversal

Already owned by ① Universal: exposed and explained under the button (F8), named guarantee, refund rate as proof, human fallback (3.3), honest cost of returns (F8). On a sales page the guarantee sits under every CTA, in its own section after the offer stack, and in the P.S. The length is identical everywhere.

- **Specific-fear guarantee.** Name the exact "what if" and promise the exact remedy.
	- Swipe: "SHADE MATCH GUARANTEE: Shade Match Promise / 30-Day Returns / 45-Day Exchange" (Jones Road). Templates: "If it doesn't fit, we replace it free." "If your dog won't eat it, full refund, keep the bag." *(illustrative)*
	- **Good for →** fit, shade, taste, size and "will it work for me" fears.
	- **When to use →** whenever research shows one fear outweighs the rest.
- **Repeated friction-killer.** Say the reassuring phrase twice in one guarantee.
	- Swipe: True Classic uses "no questions asked" twice in one guarantee.
	- **Good for →** buyers who expect a return fight.
	- **When to use →** the guarantee section, not the button line, where space is short.
- **Guarantee as a promise of character.** Turn the policy into a statement about the brand.
	- Swipe: "BUILT FOR LIFE WARRANTY / No matter what comes your way, Ridge has your back." "TRY IT FOR 99 DAYS. LOSE NOTHING." (Ridge)
	- **Good for →** durable goods and premium prices.
	- **When to use →** as the guarantee section headline.

### Zone 8: Urgency and scarcity (true only)

① Universal 3.4 owns the true-only mechanisms: real stock, real dates, real drops, first-order-only offers, live demand signals. On a sales page, state the reason to act now in the offer stack, under the final CTA, and in the P.S.

- **Dated deadline that matches today.** A real end date the reader can check.
	- Swipe: "🎁 Order by October 6th for 61% Off" (Grüns, on October 6). "…created on try.javvycoffee.com/as by 10/06/2026…" (Javvy fine print).
	- **Good for →** launches, seasonal offers, first-order discounts.
	- **When to use →** only when you will end the offer on that date. Update the date the day it changes. Ryze's fine-print date had gone a month stale.
- **Cost of waiting.** Put a number on every month of doing nothing (replacement math → ① Universal 3.1).
	- Swipe: "if your go-to order is $5–7 a cup, that's potentially $1,500–2,500 a year." (Caraway)
	- **Good for →** products that replace a recurring spend or stop a worsening problem.
	- **When to use →** after the offer stack, when you have no deadline to give.
- **Avoid:** session timers that reset on reload, "only 3 left" that never changes, and stacked sales that contradict each other. Grüns ran "End of Summer Sale," "It's Our Birthday! We Lowered Our Prices" and "Order by October 6th" on one page.

### Zone 9: FAQs

① Universal 3.3 owns the FAQ that teaches correct use. ① Universal F8 owns plain subscription and cancel terms. Both belong in the sales-page FAQ. The FAQ is the last major block before the final CTA. It repeats the top objections briefly and handles the rest.

- **Hidden-objection FAQ.** Answer the question the reader is embarrassed or suspicious to ask.
	- Swipe: "If there's no added sugar, how does your vitamin taste so sweet?" (Hiya)
	- **Good for →** any product whose main claim sounds too good.
	- **When to use →** first or second question in the FAQ.
- **Warm voice in a scary category.**
	- Swipe: "What if it doesn't work for me? Don't worry - we got you!" / "Is insurance required? Good news! No insurance is required." (Hims)
	- **Good for →** health, money, and anything the reader is nervous about.
	- **When to use →** throughout the FAQ, in the brand's spoken voice.
- **Expose a competitor's trick, with a fact.**
	- Swipe: "Beware! Many brands selling sheets claiming a 1,000+ thread count are bending the truth…" (Miracle)
	- **Good for →** categories with a misleading spec everyone quotes.
	- **When to use →** once, with a fact the reader can check.

### Zone 10: Close and P.S.

- **Recap close.** Restate the deal in a few lines before the last button.
	- Swipe: "Ready To Try It?" + a 4-bullet recap + "30-night trial | Lifetime warranty" + "Try CloudAlign Risk-Free." (Mellow)
	- **Good for →** long pages, where the reader has forgotten the offer by the end.
	- **When to use →** directly before the final CTA.
- **Honest under-claim.** Admit what the product won't do, right before the ask (volunteered negatives → ① Universal F15).
	- Swipe: "I don't wake up every morning feeling like I'm 25 again." (Mellow, "The Honest Truth")
	- **Good for →** pages full of strong claims.
	- **When to use →** just above the recap close.
- **P.S.** Many readers jump to the bottom. The P.S. restates the whole deal for them. The research captured no live P.S. on a DTC page, so these are templates.
	- Swipe: "P.S. Quick recap: \[outcome\] in \[time\], \[guarantee\] so you risk nothing, and \[true deadline\]." *(illustrative)* "P.S. Still on the fence? Try it for \[N\] days. If \[specific fear happens\], \[specific remedy\]." *(illustrative)* "P.S. The \[first-order discount\] only applies to orders started from this page by \[date\]." *(illustrative)*
	- **Good for →** every TSL and long sales page.
	- **When to use →** after the final CTA, with a button under it.
- **Capture the non-buyers.** → ① Universal 3.6 (capture on the sales page and the order page). Nikita's Chad funnel: "collect everyone's email even non-buyers → convert later via email/SMS."

### Zone 11: After the click (bridge)

The order page removes friction and repeats the guarantee next to the pay button. One-time offers and the downsell come after the main yes.
Their rules and examples live on the next page.

## Blueprint

Build order for a long-form sales page. For a VSL, steps 5 to 10 live inside the video and the rest sits below it.

1. **Point and belief chain.** Write the one sentence "After reading, the buyer believes \_\_\_ and orders \_\_\_." Then the max 6 "I believe that…" statements the reader must hold to buy (→ Copywriting doc). Each becomes a section.
2. **Number sheet.** Price, discount, per-day price, guarantee length, shipping terms, review count, deadline. Every number on the page comes from it (→ ① Universal F3).
3. **Pre-headline:** proof number, call-out or editorial frame.
4. **Headline:** 4+ of the 7 ingredients, uses the ad's or presell's words, says something new.
5. **Subhead and hero media:** the outcome in a spoken, specific line. Then the VSL, or an Ogilvy image with the headline under it. Copy and media get the time. Design comes third.
6. **Lead:** one of the 12 lures, matched to awareness. First sentence very short.
7. **Problem agitation** in the customer's words, as a mental movie. Include the silent problem they haven't named.
8. **UMP:** why everything they tried failed. Take the blame off them.
9. **UMS:** how this works, in one "Instead of X, we Y" sentence, one analogy, one diagram.
10. **Product reveal** with feature → benefit → felt outcome for each key feature.
11. **Proof stack:** credibility ladder strip, then the expert at the claim, the stat with method, a transformation, reviews tagged by situation.
12. **Embedded FAQ** for the top 2 or 3 objections, each with proof.
13. **Fascinations:** 7 to 12, at least 5 shapes.
14. **Timeline:** day 1, week 1, month 1 (→ ① Universal F14).
15. **Us vs them** table or replacement math. Nikita's notes place "Them vs Us" at market sophistication stage 4, where everyone already claims the benefit.
16. **Offer stack:** what's included, separate values, per-day price, gifts, plan choice, renewal terms, shipping terms true for each plan.
17. **Guarantee section:** the named fear, the specific remedy, how to claim it.
18. **True urgency and scarcity**, or the cost of waiting.
19. **CTA**, repeated every 1 to 2 screens once the offer has appeared, with the guarantee line under each.
20. **FAQ:** all remaining objections, correct use, cancel terms.
21. **Final reviews** and the crowd number.
22. **Honest under-claim, recap close, P.S.**
23. **Email and SMS capture** for non-buyers.
24. **Hand-off** to the order page and offers (→ ⑦ Cart, Checkout & Upsell).
25. **Number audit** against the ad, presell, PDP and checkout.

## Chiefing checklist

**Before you write**
- [ ] The page's point and the belief chain (max 6) are written, and each belief has a section
- [ ] The number sheet exists and every number on the page matches it
- [ ] Copy and media got the effort. Design was done last

**Headline and lead (Holy Grail 1)**
- [ ] Pre-headline present, grade 5 to 8, no question unless the answer is a guaranteed yes
- [ ] Headline has at least 3 to 4 of the 7 ingredients and the biggest benefit
- [ ] Headline passes Caples' 4 qualities and says something the niche isn't saying
- [ ] First sentence is very short
- [ ] Lead type chosen for the reader's awareness level
- [ ] Body copy reads at grade 5 to 6 (→ Copywriting doc: clarity)

**Mechanism (Holy Grail 5)**
- [ ] UMP explains why past attempts failed and takes the blame off the reader
- [ ] UMS stated in one "Instead of X, we Y" sentence, with one analogy
- [ ] Any planted belief or villain number is true and sourced

**Desire**
- [ ] The result feels obtainable: someone like the reader, or worse off, got it
- [ ] At least one scene specific enough to play as a mental movie
- [ ] Every feature tied to a benefit and a felt outcome
- [ ] Fascinations use at least 5 different shapes

**Proof and credibility (Holy Grail 6 and 8)**
- [ ] Proof is embedded at each major claim, and every piece has a job
- [ ] The authority was chosen from ICP research, and is real and named with a title
- [ ] Every stat has n, date and method
- [ ] Credibility ladder strip under the lead

**Objections and FAQ (Holy Grail 7)**
- [ ] Top 2 or 3 objections answered mid-page with embedded proof
- [ ] FAQ handles the rest, teaches correct use, and states cancel terms

**Offer, urgency, scarcity (Holy Grail 2 and 3)**
- [ ] Offer stack shows what's included, values, per-day price and renewal terms
- [ ] Shipping and gift claims are true for each plan shown
- [ ] There is a true reason to act today (a real date, a first-order window, or the cost of waiting)
- [ ] Any scarcity shown is real stock or a real limited run. No resetting timers, no frozen "only 3 left"
- [ ] No two sales on the page contradict each other

**Risk reversal (Holy Grail 4)**
- [ ] The guarantee names a specific fear and a specific remedy
- [ ] It sits under every CTA and in its own section, and the length matches everywhere

**Close**
- [ ] Honest under-claim and recap before the final CTA
- [ ] P.S. restates outcome, guarantee and deadline
- [ ] Email and SMS capture for non-buyers
- [ ] Every image has a caption
- [ ] Hand-off to the order page is wired (→ ⑦ Cart, Checkout & Upsell)
- [ ] Proof follows the policy in ① Universal 3.2
