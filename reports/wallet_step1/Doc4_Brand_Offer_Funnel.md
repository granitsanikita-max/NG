## Top 5 Takeaways
*(final — post-§20)*

1. **The leather-labelling lie is our #1 legal trap AND our biggest wedge.** FTC 16 CFR Part 24 names "wallets, card cases, billfolds, purses" as covered products and bans unqualified "leather" on non-leather, in *all* advertising, not just the label [S1]. **Decision:** label every SKU "vegan/PU/faux" or "genuine [type]" in the ad creative itself, and turn material honesty into the brand's proof layer — the thing the $12 no-name structurally cannot say.
2. **The category is occupied, but no incumbent owns our anti-logo "edit" identity + snug-never-stuck fix at an accessible price — a positioning gap, not virgin territory.** Two female-first cardholder brands already sit near our band — Katie Loxton (card holders ~$19–28, fold-out wallets ~$39–55; cute/sentiment/gift, monogram) and The Daily Edited (card holders ~$66, wallets ~$95; monogram/personalization, premium-leaning) — alongside men's-EDC (Ridge/Ekster), luxury (Celine/Coach), and Amazon/dropship commodity [S11]. **Decision:** compete on positioning + mechanism + economics — anchor the hero at $39–49 with the "edited her life down / taste is the receipt" stance + the "snug, never stuck" product fix + radical material honesty + a durability guarantee; out-position named occupants, don't claim an empty lane.
3. **Ridge/Ekster prove a bundle engine lifts AOV well above entry price — but only on a man.** Ridge AOV ~$185 (3rd-party estimate · directional; ~4× its $45 entry wallet); Ekster attaches a $39 tracker-card + AfterSell upsell [S13][S14]. Nobody runs that engine on her. **Decision:** copy the AOV engine (his-&-hers / 2-pack / color-set + strap/AirTag add-on + gift box) to lift AOV to $65–90.
4. **Service is a wide-open differentiator — the closest women's comp sits at 2.5★.** Portland Leather is 2.5★ with documented "costly returns," "poor CS," "deceptive sale countdowns" [S16]; dropship/Amazon offer no guarantee at all [S18]. **Decision:** make a real guarantee + easy/free returns a front-of-funnel promise; it's the trust wedge no no-name can match.
5. **She's Solution/Product-aware and this is an identity object, so no quiz is needed — a Discovery Stack fits.** Awareness split product 68 + solution 58 (126 of 201) [S19]; not a high-variant or eligibility product. **Decision:** build Advertorial/Listicle pre-sell → Offer/Product page → bundle cart, not a quiz funnel.

## §2B — Compliance & Claims (the rules behind the table)

### The three real risks, ranked
1. **Material / leather mislabeling — highest probability, easiest to trip.** FTC 16 CFR 24.2 (VERBATIM): *"It is unfair or deceptive to use the unqualified term 'leather' or other unqualified terms suggestive of leather … unless … composed in all substantial parts of leather."* §24.2(g): the disclosure must appear *"in all advertising … irrespective of the media"* — so you can't say "leather" in a vegan product's ad and hide "PU" on the label [S1]. §24.2(d): no hide-shaped/animal-silhouette hangtag on vegan goods.
2. **RFID over-claiming — the fear copy is the trap, not the feature.** Feature claim is fine *with a basis* the liner blocks 13.56 MHz. Inflating the threat is the violation: AARP calls these scams *"largely unheard of,"* ITRC says *"we do not believe this topic addresses a real risk,"* a 2025 fact-check rated the long-range-skim claim **false** [S8]. Keep RFID a minor reassurance line.
3. **Luxury-design / IP imitation — fastest ban + legal exposure.** Meta Third-Party IP (VERBATIM): no *"products that copy the trademark … and/or distinctive features of another company's products"* [S3]. TikTok Shop Listing Policy (VERBATIM): sellers may not claim products are *"dupes, fakes, knock-offs, or anything similar,"* and bans unauthorized brand marks in images, descriptions, livestreams or videos [S4]. Deliver the *feeling* of Celine/Coach with **your own design language — never their marks, never "dupe."**

### The rest of the pillars (one line each)
- **Meta Personal Attributes** (SNIPPET-VERBATIM): protected list includes *"vulnerable financial status"*; *"We don't allow advertisers to run ads that assert or imply the personal attributes of their audience"* — guaranteed rejection if you run "declutter your messy finances / drowning in debt." Reframe to first/third person [S2].
- **Apple / AirTag:** the "Works with Apple Find My" badge is MFi-only; a holder that merely *holds* an AirTag is not a Find My accessory. Nominative use only: "fits Apple AirTag," AirTag as adjective + generic noun [S5].
- **Endorsement / UGC — 16 CFR 255.5:** a material connection *"must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously"* — difficult to miss, simultaneous visual + audible on video; bio/"more"/closing-credits disclosure is NOT enough; both gifted and paid creators disclose; the platform's paid-partnership tool alone is not enough [S6].
- **Green / vegan — FTC Green Guides 16 CFR 260 + UK ASA:** "vegan leather" is PU (plastic); factual "animal-free" is safe, broad "eco/sustainable/biodegradable" needs qualification + pre-publication substantiation [S7][S9].

### Why ads get rejected + TikTok Shop specifics
**One-liner:** rejections are almost never the product — they're **(a)** a luxury name/logo/"dupe" in the creative, **(b)** an absolute/unsubstantiated claim ("waterproof," "stops thieves," "100% genuine leather" on PU), or **(c)** a second-person "you"/finances line tripping Personal Attributes. Fix the words, not the product [S1][S2][S3].
**TikTok Shop:** reviews visuals + on-screen text + the listing, not just copy; live traps are counterfeit/IP look-alikes and material misdescription; accessories are **not** a restricted category (no special cert needed) — the gate is IP + truthful description; bans "dupe/fake/knock-off" anywhere in the listing and requires accurate listings + your own trademark in the IP portal [S4].

**So What → do this:** build a substantiation file before launch (material spec + origin, RFID bench test, any survey behind a "%", warranty terms you'll honor); keep a creator-brief + disclosure log; qualify every green claim or default to "animal-free."

## The Funnel Map — DISCOVERY STACK

**Funnel choice:** per the **Funnel Selection Board** (reviewed earlier this session), the **Discovery Stack (Advertorial → Offer Page)** is the default for a **single-hero product** — which this is. It also fits the buyer: she's **Solution/Product-aware** (126 of 201) [S19] and this is an **identity object**, not a high-variant or eligibility product. So the **Discovery Stack** applies: **Advertorial/Listicle pre-sell → Offer/Product page → bundle cart.** **A quiz is NOT needed** — there's no eligibility gate, no complex fit/variant to diagnose, and no need to educate her that cardholders exist; a quiz would add friction to a buyer who already knows the category and is choosing on identity + taste [S19][S10].

- **Ad** — the-edit / compliment UGC (creator pulls it from a small bag at the register, "I get so many compliments on it" energy), benefit **+** identity hook. Honest material wording, no luxury marks, #ad up front [S10][S22][S6].
- **Pre-sell (advertorial)** — *"The card case was always hers — we're bringing it back"*: the sourced revival story (ladies' calling-card case, buried by plastic-card bloat + the logo-status era, the 2012–14 slim movement built for men) + the **material-honesty mechanism** ("snug, never stuck — in a material that ages, not peels — made for her") + proof (reviews, color-story, guarantee). Never claim brand provenance, a founder, antique materials, or a "dupe" [S10][S12].
- **Product page** — sells the cardholder: lead with the feeling, then the nameable color story, then radical material honesty (one honestly-named material per SKU) + exact card count + durability guarantee + easy/free returns; the offer inputs above [S10][S11].
