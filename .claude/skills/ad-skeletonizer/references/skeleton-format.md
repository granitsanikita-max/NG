# Copyable Ad Skeleton — the output format

The goal of a skeleton is that Nikita can drop his OWN product into the exact same machine and get a winning ad. So it must capture the *structure and the psychology*, not a summary. A vague "this ad builds curiosity and shows proof" is useless. A skeleton that says "Beat 3 opens a SECOND loop with a crazy stat so the first loop stays unresolved" is copyable.

## Two hard rules

1. **Derive the beats from the actual ad — never force-fit a template.** Watch/read the real ad and map what it actually does, beat by beat. Two winners have different structures; the value is capturing the *real* one. The example below is a model of the FORMAT and depth, not a template to impose.
2. **Every beat names its FUNCTION, not just its content.** Content ("she talks about cocoa") is disposable. Function ("reinforces belief with a picture-able fact") is what Nikita reuses. If a beat's line doesn't say *why it's there / what it does to the viewer*, it's not done.

## Required structure of every skeleton

```
[Niche/Product] — "[Structure Name]" Structure
Source: [store] ([revenue tier]) · [ad link] · running [how long] · [platform]

1. BEAT NAME — what happens + its FUNCTION (loop opened/closed? authority? proof? pivot?)
2. BEAT NAME — ...
   ...continue for every real beat in the ad...

CORE RULES:
• [the 3–6 structural laws this ad lives by]

STEAL THIS (map to our product):
• Beat-by-beat, how Nikita drops his product/offer into this exact structure — grounded in his real
  brand research, never invented specs.
```

## Track these levers explicitly (tag them on the beats where they appear)

- **Loops** — where each curiosity loop OPENS and CLOSES. The governing law of these ads is *never close a loop without opening another* — at least one stays open until the end. Call it out.
- **Authority** — self ("I know something you don't") vs outside (a tribe/expert/group). Note which and where.
- **Proof** — the fact/stat/demo, and whether it's *picture-able* (e.g. "40x more antioxidants than blueberries" — anyone can see it).
- **Contrast / crazy statement** — the bold claim most people don't know that reframes the topic.
- **Pivot words** — "But" (flip attention when interest dips, open fresh curiosity) and "So" (re-hook, reinforce). Mark them.
- **Belief tapped** — an existing belief the ad reinforces so the new idea slides in.
- **Common enemy** — who/what the ad positions against, putting the brand on the customer's side.
- **Blame removal** — "it's not your fault / it's hidden" — builds trust.
- **Agreement stack** — things they already agree with + yes-questions.
- **Community / belonging** — where the viewer is made to feel part of a group.
- **Product placement** — where the product finally enters (should be LAST, never a pitch).

## Model example (this is the DEPTH to hit — not a template to force)

Storytelling Ad — "Open Loop / Authority" Structure

1. HOOK — "Did you know…" curiosity hook, then "I'll give you a hint, it's not what you think." → opens loop #1 (creates a gap).
2. TEASE — more info, but DON'T reveal the main thing. Loop #1 stays open.
3. EDUCATE + CRAZY STATEMENT — teach a little, then a bold contrast statement most don't know → opens loop #2.
4. AUTHORITY #1 (you) — back to educating; "I know something you don't."
5. AUTHORITY #2 (outside) — bring in a tribe/group that makes/uses the thing → more credibility.
6. REINFORCE WITH A FACT — one simple picture-able proof ("cocoa has 40x the antioxidants of blueberries").
7. BENEFIT #1 → BENEFIT #2 — two benefits back to back. Loop still not closed.
8. THE "BUT" PIVOT — right as interest dips, "But…" starts closing loop #1 while opening a NEW loop.
9. TAP THE BELIEF — reinforce a belief they already hold with a crazy fact.
10. SECOND FACT + COMMON ENEMY — a fact that positions a shared enemy. Facts, not opinions.
11. REMOVE THE BLAME — "hidden" → it's not their fault → trust.
12. "SO" REHOOK — "So…" re-hooks and reinforces.
13. AGREEMENT STACK — another thing they agree with, then 3 yes-questions.
14. RICH WORDS — vivid language reinforcing the new thing.
15. COMMUNITY FEEL — the viewer belongs to a group.
16. PRODUCT + SOFT CTA — product finally enters, CTA that doesn't feel salesy.

CORE RULES:
• Never close a loop without opening another — keep ≥1 open until the end.
• State facts, not opinions.
• Position a common enemy; put yourself on the customer's side.
• "But" and "So" are the re-hook words — flip attention when interest dips.
• Product comes LAST, never framed as a pitch.
