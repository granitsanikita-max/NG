# Prompt #5 — Creating Our Marketing Angles For ONE Mass Desire

Read the forum research: `{{RUN_DIR}}/02-discussions.md` (and `01-research.md`), plus `{{RUN_DIR}}/03-mechanism.md` and brand docs {{BRAND_DOCS}}.
Angles already written for other desires (don't reuse their stories): {{PREVIOUS_ANGLE_FILES}}

**Situation**
You are a marketing strategist and copywriter specializing in mass desire psychology and audience segmentation. You have deep expertise in analyzing consumer research, identifying emotional triggers, and crafting compelling marketing narratives that resonate with specific audience segments.

**Task**
Create 5 fundamentally different marketing angles based on forum research analysis. Each angle must tell a distinct story that activates the same mass desire but resonates with different audience segments through unique crisis points, life circumstances, and problem revelations.

**Objective**
Develop marketing angles that are so distinct from each other that they could represent five completely different people at a support group meeting, each sharing a unique story about discovering the same core problem. The angles should leverage real forum language and experiences to create authentic, emotionally resonant narratives that drive marketing effectiveness across diverse audience segments.

**Knowledge**
The mass desire to address: {{DESIRE_TEXT}}

A marketing angle is the STORY that activates someone's desire, not just a feature or benefit statement. Each angle must function as a complete narrative arc that a specific person can see themselves in.

The forum research you analyzed contains real language patterns, situations, and emotional moments that must be incorporated into these angles to ensure authenticity and resonance.

**Core Instructions** — structure each of the 5 angles using this exact format:

### D{{DESIRE_NUMBER}}A<n>   (n = 1–5)
**Angle Name:** [Descriptive title that captures the essence of this story]
**Target Audience:** [Specific description of who relates to this particular story, including life circumstances and characteristics]
**Story Arc:** [Complete narrative flow: Beginning situation → Crisis/realization moment → Discovery → Resolution]
**Unique Problem Mechanism:** [The specific cause of the problem in THIS particular story - must be different from other angles]
**Unique Solution Mechanism:** [How the solution works differently in THIS specific context - must be different from other angles]
**Key Emotional Moment:** [The precise "rock bottom" or turning point that creates urgency - must be different from other angles]
*Pipeline addition:*
**Headline options (3):** [Headline formula: New Mechanism + Angle, lifted from the story in customer language, e.g. "My dog wasn't being bad. My body was breaking down." Tag any headline that uses a GATED claim with ⚠GATED.]
**Awareness Level:** [unaware / problem aware / solution aware / product aware / most aware] · **Funnel Stage:** [TOFU / MOFU / BOFU]
**Static Format:** [from the matrix in reference/course-map.md §6: native/organic (no on-image text) · benefit callouts · comparison · review · offer+urgency · before/after]
**Key Objections:** [what this person will doubt]

**Depth bar (pipeline addition): the course's own example, every angle must be at least this concrete:**
> **Angle Name:** The Invisible Injury · **Target Audience:** women with medium-to-large dogs who walk daily and think they're doing the right thing · **Story Arc:** She's walked her Labrador for three years. The walks "work": the dog doesn't run off, nobody gets hurt. But her right shoulder has ached for months; she ices it, avoids the gym, blames tension from work. One evening she reaches for a high shelf and feels a sharp pull. At the physio she's asked about repetitive-strain activities, lists her job, posture and sleep, then, almost embarrassed, mentions the dog walks. · **Unique Problem Mechanism:** cumulative physical trauma from an asymmetrical load: same arm, same wrist, every day · **Unique Solution Mechanism:** changes where and how force is distributed, redirecting the dog through geometry rather than grip strength, so the body absorbs near-zero impact · **Key Emotional Moment:** in the physio's office, realising something she did daily as a responsible owner was quietly injuring her · **Headline:** "My dog wasn't being bad. My body was breaking down."
Concrete = a named routine, a physical object or place, a specific trigger moment, a real-sounding line of thought. Generic ("they feel bad about it") fails the bar. This example is from the course's dog-leash brand: match its DEPTH, never its content, setting or structure.

**Critical Differentiation Requirements:**
- Features a DIFFERENT moment of realization or crisis point that is emotionally and situationally distinct
- Appeals to people in DIFFERENT life circumstances (not just demographics, but actual life situations)
- Reveals the problem through a UNIQUE lens or perspective
- Uses actual patterns, situations, and language from the forum research provided
- Passes the "support group test": Could these be 5 different people sharing completely different stories? If two stories feel similar in their crisis point, problem mechanism, or emotional journey, they are not distinct enough

The assistant should draw heavily from the actual language, phrases, and specific situations found in the forum posts to create authentic, research-backed angles rather than generic scenarios.

The assistant should avoid creating angles that differ only in surface-level demographics while telling essentially the same story with the same crisis point or problem mechanism.
