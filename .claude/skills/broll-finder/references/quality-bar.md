# The B-Roll Quality Bar (what "good" means)

Calibrated to Nikita's reference: **The Conscious Bar — "Moms, Win the Chocolate Negotiation"** (a
clean DTC ad whose b-roll he pointed to as the quality he wants). Study that ad's feel, not its exact
clips. The job of this skill is to return clips at THIS bar and reject everything below it.

## The three looks that ad blends (aim for these)

1. **Authentic tactile macro inserts** — real hands doing a real thing in close-up: flipping a label,
   squeezing a date, breaking a piece off. Natural window light, real surfaces (wood table), high
   texture, shallow-ish depth of field. Looks shot on a good phone by a real person, not staged.
2. **Real UGC talking-head / lifestyle** — an actual person in an actual home/kitchen, natural light,
   candid. NOT a polished studio actor with a fake smile.
3. **One cinematic hero shot** — the "money shot": glossy/appetizing product, shallow depth of field,
   moody or clean color grade, slow deliberate motion. This is the shot that makes the ad feel premium.

## KEEP a clip if it has these

- Close-up / macro framing, or a clear single subject (not a busy wide establishing shot).
- Natural or motivated light; real texture you can almost feel.
- Shallow depth of field / real camera feel, OR authentic candid phone footage.
- Appetizing / tactile / human — hands, food, real environments, genuine moments.
- Vertical 9:16, or cleanly croppable to vertical (subject centered, headroom to spare).
- ≥1080p, no watermark, no on-screen logos or captions burned in.

## REJECT a clip if it has any of these (this is where "shit b-roll" comes from)

- **Corporate-stock energy** — fake smiles, boardrooms, handshake-in-suit, "diverse team high-fives."
- Flat, even, lifeless lighting; grey/soulless color.
- Overused clichés (spinning globe, ticking clock, generic "data" screens, rain on window w/ no point).
- Cheesy slow-zoom on a gradient, obvious green-screen, dated 2010s look.
- Watermarks, site bugs, burned-in captions, visible stock-site branding.
- Wrong aspect ratio that can't crop to vertical without cutting the subject.
- Low resolution, heavy compression, or a thumbnail that already looks cheap.

## Selection rule

Before showing a clip to Nikita, look at its preview thumbnail/frames and judge it against this bar.
If it reads as generic stock, drop it — better to return 2 great options for a scene than 5 with
3 duds. When in doubt, prefer the more **authentic/tactile/real** clip over the more "produced" one,
except for the designated hero/money shot where cinematic wins.

## Matching, not just searching

- Match the scene's **emotion + subject**, not just keywords. "Mom feels guilty about sugar" → a real
  tired parent in a kitchen, not a stock "sad woman."
- Prefer **literal** for product/action beats (hands, the object, the process) and **conceptual** for
  emotional beats (freedom, relief, calm).
- Keep shot variety across the ad — don't return five near-identical clips for five different scenes.
