**Terminology (don't blur these):**
- **Format** = the vehicle (how it's delivered: UGC testimonial, advertorial listicle, us-vs-them static…)
- **Angle** = the wound/message it hits ("it's not your fault," "eat more lose weight," "doctor's warning")
- **Concept** = Format × Angle (the actual ad idea)

---

## TL;DR — the money insight

The big money in this niche is **NOT** made with pretty product ads. It's made with ads that **don't look like ads** — persona-authored advertorials, raw UGC testimonials, and native "article/review" statics — pointing at a long advertorial landing page. Every top winner does this. The prettier and more "brand" an ad looks, the shorter it runs.

Second insight: almost none of the 8-figure winners are *drops*. They're powders/capsules (Happy Mammoth, PROMIX, Arrae, Provitalize, Gundry). That's your opening — **your form factor (drops) + your honesty angle is white space.** You copy their FORMATS, keep your differentiated angle.

## TOP 3 VIDEO FORMATS (ranked by how hard they're winning)

### #1 — Persona-Authored Advertorial / "Here's exactly what I did" Listicle
**Who wins with it:** Happy Mammoth (the category king — runs it from fake-blog persona pages "Stacy's Blog," "Women's Health & More" so it reads like a real woman, not a brand).
**The concept:** A relatable woman narrates her own turnaround as a numbered protocol.
**Structure that's working:**
1. Hook = counterintuitive result + emoji: *"I ate more and still lost 17 pounds 😅 here's exactly what I did…"*
2. Numbered steps (1-2-3-4) that feel like free advice, not a pitch
3. ❌ / ✅ contrast block ("I didn't cut my favorite foods… I just followed a step-by-step protocol")
4. Mechanism named simply ("eliminate the 5 blocks that stop women over 30 losing fat: Detox & Debloat, Hormone Balance, Gut Repair")
5. Stacked ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ testimonials
6. Risk reversal ("try it 90 days, money back")
**Angle:** *"It's not your fault / it's not willpower — your body has hidden blocks (hormones, gut, bloat)."* Same core angle as your funnel.
**Steal for LucenWell:** A 30–45s UGC woman: *"I'm 41 and puffy by 5pm every day. Here's exactly what I changed — and it wasn't drinking more water."* → 3 steps → your honest mechanism ("it's fluid, not fat") → keep-the-bottle guarantee. **Run it from a plain-looking page, not a branded one.**

### #2 — UGC Testimonial (single spokesperson, talking-head or voiceover-over-Broll)
**Who wins with it:** PROMIX Debloat (971 active ads — they mass-produce this), Zurafit (319 days on testimonial-quote videos).
**The concept:** One believable person tells the before→after in their own words.
**Structure:** Relatable problem confession → "I found the only thing that worked" → simple mechanism + 2–3 benefit bullets → soft offer ($ off / guarantee).
**Angle:** *"I'd tried everything and given up — then this."* (skeptic-converted)
**Steal for LucenWell:** You already wrote the reviews for this — turn your Rachael/Joanne/Steph copy into 3 separate UGC videos, one skeptic angle each ("plant drops won't change my body… I was wrong"). Cheap to mass-produce, which is the whole point — PROMIX wins on *volume* of this format.

### #3 — Founder / Expert "Borrowed Authority"
**Who wins with it:** PROMIX ("Ask The Expert" with Albert Matheny), Gundry MD (doctor VSL), Emma (Dr. Gina Sam).
**The concept:** A credible face (founder, dietitian, doctor) explains the *why* — makes the claims feel earned, not hyped.
**Structure:** Authority intro → "here's what's actually happening in your body" (mechanism) → why other products get it wrong → the honest fix.
**Angle:** *"A real expert is finally telling you the truth about why you're puffy/bloated."*
**Steal for LucenWell:** Perfect fit for your honesty brand. A founder piece-to-camera: *"I started LucenWell because the debloat industry lied to me. Here's what 'lymphatic drainage' actually is — a tide, not a drain — and what these drops can and can't do."* Your "what it CAN'T do" honesty IS the borrowed-authority differentiator.

## TOP 3 IMAGE FORMATS (ranked)

### #1 — Native Advertorial / "Article-Screenshot" Static
**Who wins with it:** Happy Mammoth ("I ate more and lost 17 pounds," 344 days as an image), Emma (*"💩 Where does the poop go? Dr. Gina Sam, world-renowned gut doctor, just made a public announcement…"*), Daily Health.
**The concept:** Looks like a news article, blog, or a woman's own post — NOT an ad. Screenshot aesthetic, real-person photo, headline that reads editorial.
**Angle:** curiosity + authority ("a doctor's warning," "the secret that…").
**Steal for LucenWell:** A static styled like a health-article screenshot: *"An Australian lab just confirmed what most 'debloat' brands hide — the puffiness isn't fat."* Real woman photo, small "article" chrome. Points to your advertorial.

### #2 — UGC Testimonial / Review-Card Static
**Who wins with it:** Arrae (*"I thought bloating was just part of life, that pizza and I would never be friends…"*), Bloat No More, PROMIX.
**The concept:** A ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ review or a first-person quote over a real (un-retouched, phone-quality) lifestyle photo.
**Angle:** relatable confession / skeptic-converted.
**Steal for LucenWell:** Your 8 written reviews → review-card statics over the real-woman avatars you already generated. This is the cheapest format to test at volume and matches your placed photos.

### #3 — Problem-Agitation / "Us-vs-Them" Comparison Static
**Who wins with it:** Tropeaka ("feeling like a balloon after every meal 🎈"), Better Body Co ("Does Provitalize work? Yes, but…" Q&A), and the classic 2-column honest-vs-scam table (which your own site already runs).
**The concept:** Either dramatize the pain (balloon belly, rings not fitting) OR a side-by-side "honest brand vs typical debloat scam."
**Angle:** *"You've been sold the wrong fix"* / us-vs-them honesty — **your brand's core edge.**
**Steal for LucenWell:** Turn your on-site "LucenWell vs typical debloat pills" table into a paid static. Every-herb-named vs hidden blend, no auto-renew vs auto-renew traps, 60-day refund vs no refunds. This is on-brand AND a proven static format.

## The winning ANGLES map (what message is actually converting)

Ranked by how consistently the winners lean on them:
1. **"It's not your fault / not willpower"** — hidden cause is hormones, gut, or fluid, not fat. (Happy Mammoth, Provitalize, your funnel)
2. **"Eat more / do less, still improve"** — anti-effort, anti-diet. (Happy Mammoth's #1 hook)
3. **Skeptic-converted** — "I'd given up / I left a 1-star review on the last one, then this." (PROMIX, Arrae, your Rachael review)
4. **Doctor / expert warning** — borrowed authority. (Emma, Gundry)
5. **Menopause / perimenopause / women 30–55** — the highest-LTV buyer in this niche. Nearly every winner targets her.
6. **Balloon-after-meals / rings-won't-fit / puffy-by-5pm** — concrete visible-symptom hooks (not abstract "wellness").
7. **Honesty / us-vs-them** — "here's what it CAN'T do," named ingredients, no subscription. **Underused by competitors = your moat.**
