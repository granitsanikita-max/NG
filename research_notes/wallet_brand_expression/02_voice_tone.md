## 0) THE MARLBORO TECHNIQUE, TRANSLATED TO VOICE (operator's reference, grounded)

Marlboro is the model for *manufacturing and delivering an identity*. The facts (citable):
- It launched in **1924 as a women's cigarette**, slogan **"Mild as May,"** with ivory/"beauty" filter tips designed to match a woman's lipstick — explicitly feminine, status-coded. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlboro_Man ; https://quartr.com/insights/edge/making-the-marlboro-man
- By 1954 it held **~0.25% of the US market**. **Leo Burnett** reinvented it as the **Marlboro Man / cowboy** = masculine strength, freedom, rugged independence — becoming a global, premium, iconic symbol. — https://quartr.com/insights/edge/making-the-marlboro-man
- Sales went from ~18M cigarettes (1954) to **6 billion by 1955**; #1 in New York **30 days** after launch. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlboro_Man

**The FOUR techniques we steal (each becomes a voice rule):**
1. **Sell the identity, not the product claim.** Burnett *refused* to argue the filter/health benefit ("emphasizing filter technology would only reinforce the anxiety it was meant to ease"). He sold who you *became*. → **We do not argue RFID/specs; we project the put-together woman.** (Quartr, VERBATIM reasoning)
2. **The object is a signal every time it's seen.** Burnett, *The New Yorker*, 1958: *"Every time you expose it, it says something about you."* → Our cardholder is a micro-status object pulled out at the register. The voice always writes to *"who am I when I pull this out,"* never *"what holds my cards."*
3. **Sound like a confident leader; no apology, no hype.** Burnett's brief: *"We want to look and sound like a confident leader."* / *"There was nothing sissy about it."* → Calm, sharp, declarative. We never beg, hype, or over-explain.
4. **Consistency across every surface, held for years, is the moat.** A decade of identical imagery built a visual identity "so complete it no longer depended on any single medium" — it survived the 1971 TV-ad ban. → **Our voice must be byte-identical on the hero, the PDP, the email, the insert, and the DM.** One world, one woman, everywhere.
- Guardrail: Marlboro is a MODEL OF CRAFT only. We sell a leather good; never borrow tobacco's deception, health dodge, or any brand mark.

## 1) VOICE PRINCIPLES (6, each with a one-line rule)

1. **Calm confidence, never shouty.** — State the true thing ONCE, plainly; no exclamation stacks, no ALL-CAPS, no fake urgency. (Marlboro "confident leader.")
2. **Dry wit, not jokes.** — The smile lives in a knowing, true observation ("you won't be going back to the brick"), never in puns, gags, or "lol."
3. **Say the true thing the category won't.** — Name the logo tax and the peel-in-a-year fake leather out loud; radical honesty IS the flex and the trust wedge no no-name offers.
4. **She's in on it, not sold to.** — Write to a peer who already has taste ("if you know, you know"); assume she gets it, never explain the obvious, never pitch. The reader is the insider, not the mark.
5. **Plain words, 5th–8th grade.** — Short sentences, concrete nouns, zero marketing abstraction. If a word needs a thesaurus or sounds like a press release, cut it.
6. **Show the feeling, don't claim it.** — Give her the *scene* (three people asked at the register; the bag suddenly lighter) and let "fancy / complimented / in-control" happen. We never assert "this is luxurious"; Marlboro never captioned "this is masculine." Affirm she already IS the woman — the object matches her, it does not fix her (dodges the "that girl = buy-your-worth" critique).

Every surface runs the same test: *Does it sound like a woman with great taste telling a friend the true thing — calm, a little dry, never shouty, never precious, zero spec-dump, zero hype?* If yes, ship.
