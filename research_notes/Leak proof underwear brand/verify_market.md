# Market Verification — US Women's Stress-Incontinence Underwear

**Adversarial fact-check** · verified 2026-10-10 · goal: refute, not confirm. Verdicts: CONFIRMED / CORRECTED / UNVERIFIABLE. "Solidity" flags vendor/marketing/single-source stats that should NOT go into ad copy as fact.

---

## 1. "Stress UI is the most common female UI subtype — 45.9% of women with UI (US 2005–2018)"

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED (with a real competing estimate — do not state as settled)**

- Best-verified fact: In Abufaraj et al. (AJOG, NHANES 2005–2018, n=19,791 women ≥20), stress UI was the most prevalent subtype at **45.9% (95% CI 42.1–49.7)**, ahead of urgency UI (31.1%) and mixed UI (18.1%). Note the 45.9% is reported for the **2017–2018 cycle**, not an average of the whole 2005–2018 span.
- **Refutation found:** A separate NHANES 2015–2018 analysis ranks subtypes differently — **mixed UI most common (29.2%), then stress UI (23%), then urgency UI (10.8%)**. So "stress is #1" is classification/method dependent, not uncontested.
- URLs: https://www.urotoday.com/recent-abstracts/pelvic-health-reconstruction/urinary-incontinence/129903-prevalence-and-trends-in-urinary-incontinence-among-women-in-the-united-states-2005-2018-beyond-the-abstract.html · https://vivo.weill.cornell.edu/display/pubid33727114
- **Solidity:** Good (peer-reviewed, NHANES). But flag: ranking flips to "mixed UI #1" under a different definition. Safe ad framing: "stress UI is among the most common types," not "the single most common."

## 2. "~1 in 3 women have UI postpartum; stress UI = 54% of postpartum UI; 73% still symptomatic at 6 yrs if symptomatic at 3 months" (PMC8295150)

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED**

- Verified directly in PMC8295150 (systematic review/meta-analysis, 24 studies, 35,064 women): pooled postpartum UI prevalence **31.0% (95% CI 26.0–36.0)** = ~1 in 3. Among subtypes (9 studies), **stress UI = 54%** of cases (urgency 26%, mixed 16%, unexplained 4%). The **73% at 6 years** figure is quoted in the discussion from an external cohort (MacArthur et al.), NOT this review's own longitudinal finding.
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8295150/
- **Solidity:** Strong for 31% and 54%. The 73% is a secondary citation of one UK/NZ cohort — attribute it to that study, not to "postpartum women broadly."

## 3. "Stress incontinence 30% at 5 years post-first-delivery (Viktrup/Lose)"

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED as "about one third" (exact 30% is secondary-source wording)**

- Primary: Viktrup & Lose, "Stress incontinence 5 years after 1st delivery" (ICS 1999 / Neurourol Urodyn 2002). Cleveland Clinic Journal of Medicine review restates it as "about one third of women experience stress incontinence 5 years after first vaginal delivery." The precise "30%" is the review's rounding, not a quoted primary number I could pull.
- URLs: https://www.ics.org/1999/abstract/36 · https://www.ccjm.org/content/72/3/225.full.pdf
- **Solidity:** Weak-to-moderate. Single small Danish cohort (305 women, 242 followed), old, and the same authors published work on recall bias for SUI onset. Use "around one third" with a cohort caveat; do not present as a US population figure.

## 4. "Female CrossFit athletes: UI prevalence 44.5%, of those SUI 81.2%" (PubMed 35635565)

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED (exact match)**

- Verified directly from PubMed 35635565 (Dominguez-Antuña et al., Int Urogynecol J 2023; meta-analysis, 13 studies, 4,823 women aged 18–71): UI prevalence **44.5%**; stress UI **the most common type at 81.2%**.
- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35635565/
- **Solidity:** Numbers are real and exactly as claimed. BUT: level-4 evidence, all cross-sectional, study quality rated poor (n=10) to fair (n=3), international (not US), self-report, self-selected CrossFit samples. A competing CrossFit meta-analysis and other studies report 20–26%. **Flag for ad copy:** acceptable as "studies of CrossFit athletes have found UI rates as high as ~45%," not as a universal/US stat.

## 5. "Mean age of SUI complaint onset ≈ 45.4 years (youngest of UI types)"

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED (single weak source)**

- Fact: a hospital-based retrospective study (ICS 2019 abstract #603, 620 incontinent patients) found mean age at symptom onset lowest for SUI (**45.4 yrs**) vs mixed (51.0) and urgency (54.7), p<0.001; SUI group also youngest at diagnosis.
- URL: https://www.ics.org/2019/abstract/603 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7157848
- **Solidity:** Weak. One clinic-based retrospective sample (not population-representative, not clearly US), self-reported onset, and in that sample SUI was only 13.2% of patients (mixed UI dominated). Directionally fine ("SUI tends to start younger"); do not cite 45.4 as a precise population figure.

## 6. "NHANES: any UI rises from ~17% (20–29) to ~54.5% (50–59)"

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED**

- Fact: US NHANES (2005–06 + 2007–08, women 20+): any UI **17.0% at 20–29** rising each decade to **54.5% at 50–59**. Claim as worded is accurate.
- **Caveat (refutation of implied "keeps rising"):** the curve is NOT monotonic after 50–59 — it plateaus/dips (52.4% at 60–69, 48.4% at 70–79, 51.7% at 80+). So the rise holds only through the 50s.
- URLs: (NHANES 2005–08 age-banded data, cited via) https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3197263/
- **Solidity:** Good. Safe to use the 17%→54.5% arc for the under-60 story.

## 7. "80% of women leak while they exercise and 70% avoid exercise because of leaks" (Knix)

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED as a real cited stat — but it is BRAND MARKETING, not research**

- It is genuinely the headline of Knix's Aug 2022 LeakStrong launch release. Knix attributes it to "Knix's own customer research." No sample size, no method, no population definition, not peer-reviewed, not independent.
- URL: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/knix-announces-the-solution-to-leaks-with-all-new-leakstrong-activewear-301605861.html · https://www.retaildive.com/press-release/20220815-knix-announces-the-solution-to-leaks-with-all-new-leakstrongtm-activewear-1
- **Solidity: DO NOT USE IN AD COPY AS FACT.** It is a competitor's self-serving marketing number. Independent literature supports the *direction* (UI is a real barrier to exercise — e.g. Kaiser Permanente / Bond Univ "too wet to exercise" studies) but with far more modest, variable figures. If leak-while-exercising needs a citable number, use the CrossFit/athlete literature (claim 4), not Knix.

## 8. UNCONTESTED CLAIM — "No MAJOR US brand is positioned specifically for the ACTIVE 30s–40s woman with stress/exercise incontinence framed as athletic/performance (not period, not elderly/clinical)"

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED — but narrowly. The whitespace is real at the MAJOR-brand level, yet the edges are already contested by small players and sub-lines.**

I tried hard to break this. Findings, mapped to the exact position:

- **Knix — LeakStrong / KT by Knix activewear** (Essity-owned, "#1 leakproof brand in N. America"): the only *major* brand with real athletic-leak product. BUT it is a **sub-line**, not the brand's standing position, and it is framed as multi-use (bladder leaks + periods + sweat), not dedicated stress/exercise-incontinence-for-30s–40s. Closest competitor; could pivot into the exact position. https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/knix-announces-the-solution-to-leaks-with-all-new-leakstrong-activewear-301605861.html
- **Moxie Fitness Apparel** (gowithmoxie.com): positioning is almost exactly the claimed slot — "leakproof activewear for the 1 in 3 women who experience bladder leaks while exercising." BUT a tiny startup (not a "major brand"), and also markets to periods. https://gowithmoxie.com/
- **Prickly Pear**: niche leakproof workout shorts/leggings for exercise bladder leaks (up to ~40 ml). Small/niche, low awareness.
- **Sweaty Betty — Leak Protect leggings** and **Adidas Techfit period-proof tights**: major athletic brands, but product lines, and period-anchored.
- **Thinx / Speax, Saalt Wear, Modibodi**: Thinx & Saalt & Modibodi are period-primary; Speax (Thinx's incontinence line) and **attn:grace**, **Lumana (Wearever)** are incontinence-focused but clinical/discreet/older-leaning — NOT athletic/performance for 30s–40s.
- URLs: https://saalt.com/collections/workout-underwear · https://attngrace.com/pages/our-story · https://theoriginway.com/blog/the-best-incontinence-products-according-to-pelvic-floor-physical-therapists
- **Solidity / honest read:** As stated ("no *major* brand *holds this exact standing position*"), CONFIRMED — no large brand is anchored as the athletic/performance stress-incontinence brand for active 30s–40s women; they're either major-but-period/multi-use, or niche-but-correctly-positioned. **Do not overstate as "no one is doing this."** Moxie, Prickly Pear and Knix LeakStrong already occupy the edges, so the accurate claim is "the position is open at the major-brand level, but small players and a Knix sub-line are already circling it" — first-mover-at-scale, not empty category.

## 9. Market sizing — period/leak underwear ~$150–270M (2025); adult incontinence products ~$14–17B

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED as defensible RANGES (vendor-sourced, wide spread)**

- **Period/leak-proof underwear (2025):** vendor estimates cluster but disagree. GMInsights ~$271M (2025), Mordor (reusable) ~$186M (2025); 2024 anchors range $137M (IMARC) / $174M (Credence) / $261M (GMI) / $319M (Stats&Research). The claimed **$150–270M (2025)** is defensible and brackets the mainstream estimates. Growth wildly disputed (4.2% to 35% CAGR).
  - https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/period-panties-market · https://www.imarcgroup.com/periods-panties-market · https://www.credenceresearch.com/report/period-underwear-market
- **Adult incontinence products (global):** GMInsights $16.4B (2024) → $17.2B (2025); Meticulous ~$15.0B (2025) / $15.9B (2026). The claimed **$14–17B** is well-supported at the mainstream end (realistically $15–17B for 2025). One outlier (grandresearchstore ~$47.9B/2024) looks like a scope/currency error — discard.
  - https://www.gminsights.com/pressrelease/adult-incontinence-products-market · https://www.meticulousresearch.com/product/adult-incontinence-products-market-6610
- **Solidity:** Medium. All figures are paid market-research vendor estimates with undisclosed methods and large between-house variance. Ranges are fine for sizing/pitch; **do not cite any single precise number as fact in ad copy.** The incontinence-products TAM ($15–17B) is the sturdier of the two; the period/leak-underwear number is small and volatile.

---

### Bottom line / ad-copy flags
- **Hard no for ad copy as fact:** the Knix "80%/70%" stat (#7 — competitor marketing). Use only as directional, or cite real studies.
- **Use with qualifiers, not as universal/US facts:** CrossFit 44.5%/81.2% (#4, low-quality international meta), SUI onset 45.4 yrs (#5, single clinic sample), "SUI is THE most common subtype" (#1 — flips to mixed UI under other NHANES cuts), 5-yr postpartum 30% (#3, one small old cohort).
- **Solid enough to lean on:** postpartum ~1 in 3 & stress=54% (#2), age gradient 17%→54.5% through the 50s (#6), market-size ranges as ranges (#9).
- **The "uncontested" thesis (#8):** true at the major-brand level but already contested at the edges — position it as "open at scale," not "empty."
