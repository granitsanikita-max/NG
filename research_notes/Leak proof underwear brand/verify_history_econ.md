## 1. 1550 BC Egyptian papyrus — vaginal inserts for compression

- **VERDICT: CONFIRMED** (quote and date are real, in the attributed source).
- **Verified wording (verbatim from PMC10285159):** "Products to manage adult incontinence (AI) go back to at least 1550 BC where an Egyptian text on Papyrus … suggests vaginal inserts to provide compression for women." The "1550 BC" corresponds to the Ebers Papyrus era (independently dated to ~1550 BCE).
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10285159/ (open access; this is "The history of adult incontinence products," the article the claim cites).
- **Solidity / copy-safety:** SOLID. Safe to use. The quote is the secondary source's own framing ("suggests"), so present it as "according to a published history of incontinence products" rather than as an archaeological certainty. Do not over-claim precision of the date beyond "~1550 BC."

## 2. Arnold Kegel 1948 — "over 700 users / complete relief" + perineometer

- **VERDICT: SPLIT — underlying facts CONFIRMED; the "over 700 users / complete relief" wording UNVERIFIABLE.**
- **Confirmed:** Kegel published his pelvic-floor exercise work in **1948** (paper: "Progressive Resistance Exercise in the Functional Restoration of the Perineal Muscles," Am J Obstet Gynecol); he was first to report clinical success treating stress urinary incontinence; he invented the **perineometer** (pneumatic vaginal probe + manometer). All confirmed.
  - URL: https://www.urologynews.uk.com/features/features/post/ics-updates-on-continence-care-what-s-hot-in-physiotherapy-after-80-years (ICS/Urology News) and https://embryo.asu.edu/pages/arnold-henry-kegel-1894-1972
- **Could NOT verify:** The **URMC "menoPAUSE" blog** page could not be located via search (urmc.edu domain search + title search both failed). The specific figure **"over 700 users"** and the phrase **"complete relief"** are not corroborated anywhere I could reach. Other reputable sources give DIFFERENT patient counts for Kegel's results (one cites ~455 patients / 90% improvement; another 64 patients). No independent source says "700" or "complete relief."
- **Solidity / copy-safety:** Use "1948," "pelvic-floor exercises," and "invented the perineometer" freely — SOLID. **DO NOT USE IN COPY** the specific "over 700 users" number or the words "complete relief" until the original URMC menoPAUSE page (or Kegel's 1948 paper) is located and the figure read directly. Results in the literature are reported as "improvement/cure rates," not blanket "complete relief," and the count varies by source — a hard number in an ad is a refutation risk. "Obsolete perineometer" is a defensible characterization (it is now largely superseded by EMG/biofeedback, though still used in some research).

## 3. Margaret Morris described the technique before Kegel

- **VERDICT: CONFIRMED (as a disputed-credit claim), but NOT a single-blog claim.**
- **Verified fact:** Pelvic-floor muscle training appears in modern medicine via St Thomas' Hospital physiotherapists in the 1930s — **Margaret Morris** (and her teacher/collaborator **Minnie Randell**) wrote about conscious control of pelvic-floor muscles for incontinence ~1936, i.e. BEFORE Kegel's 1948 paper. Reported by an ICS-affiliated Urology News feature (not just a brand blog), plus a historical review in a peer journal.
  - URLs: https://www.urologynews.uk.com/features/features/post/ics-updates-on-continence-care-what-s-hot-in-physiotherapy-after-80-years ; https://rhm.sums.ac.ir/article_51037.html
  - Note: a product brand (The Origin Way) pushes the "actual inventor of the Kegel" framing — that stronger phrasing IS largely single-advocacy. https://www.theoriginway.com/blog/meet-margaret-morris-movement-icon-actual-inventor-of-the-kegel
- **Solidity / copy-safety:** MEDIUM-SOLID as "a British physiotherapist described pelvic-floor training before Kegel / credit is contested." Safe if framed as "some historians argue…" **Do NOT state flatly "Margaret Morris invented the Kegel"** — that specific attribution is contested (Randell is often credited as foundational) and traces mainly to advocacy/brand sources. Frame as a credit dispute, not settled fact.

## 4. FRANCE — all women get ~10 govt-paid "rééducation périnéale" sessions; dates to 1985; US has no equivalent

- **VERDICT: CONFIRMED on the substance; the exact "10 sessions" number is SOFT (varies 10–20).**
- **(a) All women, government-paid, regardless of symptoms:** CONFIRMED. Postnatal perineal re-education is routinely prescribed/offered to women after childbirth and covered by French social security/Assurance Maladie; up to ~20 sessions covered on prescription, with ~10–15 commonly sufficient. An international survey states it is "routinely provided by physical therapists or midwives … regardless of symptoms."
  - URLs: https://www.ics.org/Abstracts/Publish/349/000791.pdf (ICS survey abstract — "instituted in 1985 … regardless of symptoms"); https://obgyn.ucsf.edu/node/2961 (UCSF OB/GYN); https://beyondbasicsphysicaltherapy.com/blog/ooo-la-la-la-reeducation-perineale-pelvic-floor-en-france/ ("In France the entire cost of pelvic floor physical therapy is covered by the government"); Calgary Herald "The French Solution" https://www.pressreader.com/canada/calgary-herald/20180504/282089162396560
- **(b) 1985 date:** CONFIRMED by two+ sources — a 1985 decree placed postnatal rehabilitation under France's universal healthcare coverage; the ICS survey abstract also says the program was "instituted in 1985."
- **(c) US has no universal equivalent:** CONFIRMED directionally — US standard is the single 6-week postpartum OB/GYN checkup; there is no universal publicly-funded postpartum pelvic-floor PT referral. (Pelvic-floor PT exists in the US but is not universal/automatic.)
- **Solidity / copy-safety:** SOLID for "France routinely prescribes government-paid postpartum perineal re-education to women after birth; the program dates to 1985; the US has no universal equivalent." **Use "~10 sessions (up to ~20 covered)" or "about 10"** — do NOT state a hard "exactly 10 sessions for all women," because sources range 10–20 and the number is a typical prescription, not a fixed legal entitlement. Strongest citations: ICS survey abstract (academic) + UCSF OB/GYN. Avoid citing only a PT blog in copy; lead with ICS/UCSF.

## 7. Economics sanity-check (DTC / apparel)

- **VERDICT: Ranges are DEFENSIBLE as directional industry figures (vendor-benchmark sourced, not peer-reviewed).**
- **Meta CAC ~$30–60 (new DTC brand):** DEFENSIBLE. Benchmarks put apparel/fashion Meta CAC roughly $20–50 (2025), with blended DTC CAC ~$53; some 2026 fashion figures run higher ($90–120). $30–60 sits within the credible band for a new brand. Source: Eightx CAC benchmarks https://eightx.co/blog/ecommerce-cac-calculator-benchmarks
- **Apparel gross margin ~70–80% target:** Defensible as a DTC *target/blended* margin, but note public apparel-comp gross margins cluster ~50–62% (median DTC ~47%). 70–80% is achievable for DTC basics with direct sourcing but is at the optimistic end — frame as a target, not an industry average. Source: Eightx.
- **Apparel return rate ~20–40%, underwear suppressed by final-sale:** DEFENSIBLE. Best-supported US online-apparel return rate ~23–25% (Loop Returns 23.2% across 22M Shopify returns; Coresight 24.4%; Statista ~23%), with 30%+ cited for some DTC/EU. Underwear/intimates are routinely final-sale or exchange-only for hygiene reasons, legitimately suppressing net returns. Sources: https://eightx.co/blog/average-apparel-return-rate-benchmarks ; https://www.corso.com/post-purchase-resource-center/ecommerce-return-rate-benchmarks-by-industry
- **Solidity / copy-safety:** Fine for internal modeling. These are vendor/aggregator benchmarks (Eightx, Corso, Loop, Coresight, Statista), not primary research — **do not cite specific percentages as hard facts in public ad copy.** 20–40% return range and ~23–25% midpoint are safe to state generally; "70–80% gross margin" should be treated as an internal target, not claimed publicly.
