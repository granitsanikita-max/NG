# Leak-Proof / Absorbent Underwear: Product Truth, Technology, PFAS & Sourcing (for a new brand, 2026)

> Scope note: A large share of the best technical detail online comes from manufacturer/brand blogs (especially ljvogues.com, a Chinese OEM/ODM) that have an incentive to sell. Where a figure is self-reported or unverified, it is flagged. The strongest independent sources found were Which? (consumer testing), Hohenstein and Bureau Veritas (test labs), OEKO-TEX/Hohenstein (certification), academic incontinence research (UCL/Southampton), and Trustpilot reviews of Everdries. Treat vendor numbers as starting points to verify with your own sampling and third-party lab tests.

## 1. The Technology: layer system, realistic capacity, why cheap ones fail

### Takeaway
Leak-proof underwear works via a laminated multi-layer gusset: a wicking top layer, an absorbent core, and a heat-bonded waterproof film (TPU or PUL) — the film, not any chemical finish, is what actually stops leaks. Capacity realistically spans ~30-50 ml (light dribbles) to 100-150 ml (moderate) to 200 ml+ (heavy/overnight); products are marketed as "light / moderate / heavy" but the ml rating on the specific product is what matters, since tier labels are inconsistent. Cheap garments fail at the seams (overlock instead of flatlock), via film delamination (iron-pressed vs heat-bonded), from insufficient core layers, and from odor retention after washing.

### Cited Findings
- Standard gusset is a three-part sandwich: a wicking top layer pulls moisture off skin, an absorbent core holds fluid, and a waterproof barrier blocks pass-through — the same architecture as reusable menstrual pads; some vendors use a four-layer version, so layer count is not fixed — [Wazoodle fabric guide](https://help.wazoodle.com/article/1107-guide-period-underwear-discovery); [ljvogues layer tech](https://www.ljvogues.com/more-than-just-leak-protection-inside-the-four-layer-absorbent-technology-of-modern-period-underwear.html)
- Barrier material is PUL (polyurethane laminate) or TPU (thermoplastic polyurethane) film; sources disagree on which is "typical," both are presented as common and effective — [Wazoodle](https://help.wazoodle.com/article/1107-guide-period-underwear-discovery)
- One OEM specifies the waterproof barrier as TPU film at 18-25 microns, heat-bonded with a constant-temperature/constant-pressure machine (not iron-pressed) — [ljvogues construction guide](https://www.ljvogues.com/period-underwear-construction-layer-by-layer)
- What actually stops leaks is the laminated film, not a chemical finish; a DWR water-repellent finish on the *outer* face fabric (so the outside feels dry to touch) is a separate, optional design choice — [Saalt PFAS explainer](https://saalt.com/blogs/news/everything-you-need-to-know-about-pfas-and-period-underwear)
- Core absorbency is scaled by number of core layers: vendor guide suggests 1-2 layers for light, 3-4+ for heavy/overnight — [Wazoodle](https://help.wazoodle.com/article/1107-guide-period-underwear-discovery)
- Vendor core-material claims (unverified): microfiber absorbs 7-8x its weight; organic cotton ~25-30x its weight — [ljvogues tech](https://www.ljvogues.com/period-underwear-technology-how-absorbent-fabrics-work.html)
- Capacity tiers (ml), consolidated from seller guides: Light up to ~100 ml (some say light bladder leaks only need 30-50 ml, or 30-60 ml for a few dribbles); Moderate 100-150 ml; Heavy 150-200 ml or more — [ljvogues absorbency guide](https://www.ljvogues.com/leak-proof-underwear-absorbency-levels-guide)
- Real product example: CARER washable incontinence brief rated "up to 150 ml," aimed at light-to-moderate — [Walmart listing](https://www.walmart.com/ip/18937062070)
- Tier labels are inconsistent: a Jockey "moderate absorbency" hipster is listed at just 25 mL, and a disposable Abena brief at up to 2,600 mL — so "moderate" is not a fixed ml value; buy by the stated ml, matched to the heaviest typical leak, not the average — [Walmart/Jockey](https://www.walmart.ca/en/ip/Jockey-Women-s-Worry-Free-Cotton-Stretch-Moderate-Absorbency-Hipster/1JKCI7LAHMAH)
- Why cheap ones fail — seam leakage: gusset/leg-opening seam splits are blamed on standard overlock stitching used where repeated stretch + wash stress occurs; the fix is a 4-needle, 6-thread flatlock seam — [ljvogues construction](https://www.ljvogues.com/period-underwear-construction-layer-by-layer)
- Why cheap ones fail — delamination: heat-bonded film reportedly survives 100 washes without delaminating, whereas iron-pressed film commonly starts failing between 3 and 8 washes (vendor's own internal factory test, not third-party certified) — [ljvogues heat bonding](https://www.ljvogues.com/period-underwear-manufacturing-process-heat-bonding)
- Everdries (a heavily-marketed clone) real-world failure modes from customer reviews: leaks not reliably contained even with light incontinence, "leaks go through at the side seams," and "a persistent smell after washing" — [Trustpilot Everdries](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/everdries.com?page=3)

### Inferences
- The leak-proofness is a function of the laminated film + bonding method; absorbency and "all-day" claims are a function of core layer count/material. A new brand should spec both independently.
- "All-day" for incontinence realistically means a product rated ≥100-150 ml; sub-50 ml products are only honest for occasional dribbles. Everdries-type failures suggest many cheap imports are effectively in the low tier despite "all-day" marketing.

### Gaps
- No independent, cross-brand lab dataset of actual ml capacity vs advertised claims was found; capacity figures are seller-reported.
- Top-layer fiber, exact GSM, and core fiber blends are not published in the sources found; these must be obtained from supplier spec sheets.

## 2. Odor Control: what's real vs marketing

### Takeaway
Silver-ion / antimicrobial finishes (silver chloride, silver-copper-zeolite like Agion) are the dominant odor-control marketing claim, but the strongest independent source (Which?) argues they are often unnecessary and may not work; treatment levels vary widely between brands and antimicrobial finishes tend to wash out over repeated laundering. The most durable "odor control" is good moisture management (wicking + full absorption so bacteria have less to feed on) rather than a chemical that masks or kills.

### Cited Findings
- Which? (UK consumer group) tested period pants and said "you don't need silver — or any antimicrobial agent — in your period pants either for odour or safety," calling it "an unnecessary addition," and cited prior AltroConsumo testing showing silver-treated textiles "often do not actually have the promised antimicrobial properties" — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/policy-and-insight/article/some-popular-period-pants-contain-unnecessarily-high-levels-of-silver-which-warns-aFFwv6E0Sb8r)
- Measured silver content varies enormously between brands: Thinx 8.3 mg/kg, Luna by Superdrug 7.4 mg/kg, Modibodi 0.9 mg/kg, with several brands showing none detectable — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/policy-and-insight/article/some-popular-period-pants-contain-unnecessarily-high-levels-of-silver-which-warns-aFFwv6E0Sb8r)
- Essity (owns Modibodi/Bodyform) defends silver-copper-zeolite "to prevent odor when wearing the pants for up to 12 hours" — vendor claim, menstrual not urinary — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/policy-and-insight/article/some-popular-period-pants-contain-unnecessarily-high-levels-of-silver-which-warns-aFFwv6E0Sb8r)
- The Thinx settlement required Thinx to disclose its antimicrobial treatment (Agion) on its website — i.e., silver/antimicrobial use had been undisclosed — [Fox29/AP](https://www.fox29.com/news/thinx-period-underwear-lawsuit-over-pfas-settles-for-4-million)
- Patent literature concedes odor control is hard: "complete elimination of urine odor has not been achieved with activated carbon and/or silver," and antimicrobial treatments "tend to wash out of the material over time as the material is repeatedly laundered" — [USPTO patents / Justia](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20160338884)
- Vendor marketing claims (unsupported by cited studies): silver "reducing odor development by up to 99.9%" — [ljvogues/odor marketing, per search]; Mack Weldon markets silver antimicrobial fabric for men — [Mack Weldon](https://mackweldon.com/blogs/learn/silver-technology-antimicrobial-fabric-mens)

### Inferences
- For a new brand, silver-ion is best treated as a marketing feature with weak independent backing and wash-durability risk, not a core performance claim. If used, specify the agent (e.g., silver-copper-zeolite), disclose it (post-Thinx expectation), and verify it is not PFAS-based.
- The defensible, durable odor story is moisture management + full absorption + washability, plus honest care instructions; urine odor (ammonia) is chemically harder to eliminate than menstrual odor.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed independent test of silver underwear specifically against urine/ammonia-producing bacteria in real wear was found.
- No data on how much silver content degrades after N washes in these garments.

## 3. PFAS: why it was used, the Thinx case, and how to source/verify PFAS-free

### Takeaway
PFAS were used as a durable water/stain-repellent (DWR) finish — not as the leak barrier itself — and sometimes appear via manufacturing cross-contamination rather than intentional addition. Thinx settled a class action (~$4-5M fund, claims closed April 2023) and agreed to never intentionally add PFAS. To source PFAS-free, the key move is a third-party lab test (total fluorine + restricted-PFAS) on finished garments, not just a fabric-supplier declaration; OEKO-TEX Standard 100 bans intentional PFAS and caps it at 100 mg/kg but is explicitly NOT a "PFAS-free" certificate.

### Cited Findings
- PFAS repel water, oil and stains; in textiles they're usually a deliberate durable water repellent (DWR) finish; some period-underwear makers used PFAS as a stain/water-resistant coating — [WUKA](https://wuka.co.uk/blogs/periods-a-z/are-period-pants-safe); [Saalt](https://saalt.com/blogs/news/everything-you-need-to-know-about-pfas-and-period-underwear)
- PFAS in menstrual products "isn't always intentional — but it's widespread"; cross-contact during manufacturing can cause it — [ljvogues PFAS guide](https://www.ljvogues.com/the-pfas-free-promise-a-practical-material-guide-for-the-safest-period-underwear-brands.html)
- Thinx PFAS class action settled (~late 2022); fund reported as $4M by Fox affiliates but up to $5M by KSL/Bloomberg Law (treat $5M as stated cap); covered purchases Nov 12, 2016 – Nov 28, 2022; payouts $7/pair (max 3) with proof, $3.50/pair without, or 35% discount voucher; claims deadline April 12, 2023 (now closed); S.D.N.Y., Judge Jesse M. Furman; Thinx denied wrongdoing, said "PFAS has never been part of our product design" — [Fox29/AP](https://www.fox29.com/news/thinx-period-underwear-lawsuit-over-pfas-settles-for-4-million); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/attorneys-seek-1-5-million-for-period-underwear-pfas-settlement)
- Settlement required Thinx to "never intentionally add PFAS" and to disclose antimicrobial treatments (Agion) on its site — [Fox29/AP](https://www.fox29.com/news/thinx-period-underwear-lawsuit-over-pfas-settles-for-4-million)
- OEKO-TEX bans intentional PFAS use in textiles/apparel/footwear; as of Jan 1, 2024 the STANDARD 100 limit value is 100 mg/kg; testing is two-step (all legally restricted PFAS + total fluorine) — [Hohenstein OEKO-TEX PFAS](https://www.hohenstein.de/en-us/oeko-tex/restrictions-and-testing/pfas)
- Critical caveat: "OEKO-TEX does NOT certify the product as 'PFAS-free'"; brands that call OEKO-TEX fabric "PFAS-free" are going beyond the certificate — [Hohenstein](https://www.hohenstein.de/en-us/oeko-tex/restrictions-and-testing/pfas); [Boody](https://boody.com/blogs/eco/is-boody-oeko-tex-certified)
- Sourcing advice: request a third-party lab test report on the finished garment, not a material declaration from the fabric supplier (a supplier declaration may be insufficient); PFAS/fabric testing budgeted at ~$300-$1,200 — [ljvogues cost guide](https://www.ljvogues.com/private-label-period-underwear-cost)
- Non-PFAS DWR alternatives exist (e.g., Polartec transitioned to PFAS-free DWR claiming no sacrifice in durability/water repellency), but hobbyist reports say PFAS-free DWR is "highly vulnerable to washing machines, sweat and abrasion" — [SGI Europe/Polartec](https://sgieurope.com/polartec-announces-its-transition-to-dwr-finishes-without-pfass/90685.article)

### Inferences
- Because PFAS can enter via cross-contamination, a brand must test finished goods per production batch, not rely on a one-time fabric cert. Specify "no intentionally added PFAS" contractually AND require total-fluorine testing.
- Marketing "PFAS-free" is a legal/compliance risk (Thinx precedent): back it with third-party finished-garment test reports and avoid overclaiming beyond what OEKO-TEX covers.
- The leak barrier (TPU/PUL film) does not require PFAS; dropping any PFAS DWR mainly affects how "dry to the touch" the outer face feels, a cosmetic tradeoff.

### Gaps
- Exact cost of a full PFAS panel (total organic fluorine + targeted) at a named lab (e.g., SGS, Bureau Veritas) for one SKU not pinned down beyond the $300-$1,200 vendor range.
- OEKO-TEX online certificate-database lookup procedure not confirmed from a primary source in this research (general knowledge: certificates are searchable by number on oeko-tex.com).

## 4. Sourcing paths & economics: dropship vs private label vs custom

### Takeaway
Three paths with very different economics: (a) pure dropship (AliExpress/CJ) has near-zero setup but poor/uncontrolled quality, no PFAS control, 1-6 week ship times, and is now hit hard by the 2025 end of US de-minimis (duties on <$800 parcels) — viable only for testing demand, not a credible brand; (b) low-MOQ private label (stock style + your logo, ~50-300 pcs, or ODM 500-1,000 pcs) is the realistic entry for a small new brand at roughly $4.50-$12/unit ex-factory; (c) full custom OEM needs 3,000-50,000+ pcs and only makes sense at scale. Chinese ODMs in Shantou/Guangdong and Fujian dominate; budget ~$8,000-$15,000 to launch one private-label style of 500 pcs.

### Cited Findings
**(a) Pure dropship (AliExpress / CJdropshipping)**
- Quality reality: Everdries-grade clones get mixed-to-negative reviews — unreliable leak containment, side-seam leaks, smell after washing, refund/return friction (e.g., EU buyers told to return to USA) — [Trustpilot Everdries](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/everdries.com?page=3)
- Ship times: CJ Packet ~5-18 days (CJ's own figures); CJ US-warehouse stock 2-5 days; AliExpress standard 15-30 days, can stretch to 60+; one reviewer reported 3 weeks to process + 3 weeks to ship — [CJ blog](https://www.cjdropshipping.com/blogs/cj-news/CJdropshipping-vs-AliExpress); [Branvas](https://branvas.com/blogs/news/aliexpress-vs-cjdropshipping)
- Platform cost: AliExpress $0/mo pay-per-order; CJ has a free tier plus paid tiers (one source lists Growth $29 / Scale $79 / VIP $149 per month; another says no subscription fee — verify current pricing) — [Branvas](https://branvas.com/blogs/news/aliexpress-vs-cjdropshipping)
- Big 2025 cost shock: US eliminated duty-free de-minimis entry for shipments ≤$800 — China/HK effective May 2, 2025, all other origins effective Aug 29, 2025; CJ cites tariffs "up to 245%" on most Chinese goods (CJ blog figure, verify); US/domestic-warehouse fulfillment avoids the per-package tariff hit — [CJ tariffs blog](https://blog.cjdropshipping.cn/detail/How-CJdropshipping-Helps-You-Adapt-to-New-U-S--Tariffs)

**(b) Low-MOQ private label & (c) full custom OEM** (all from ljvogues cost guide, a vendor; verify with quotes)
- Stock style + your logo: MOQ 50-300 pcs, $4.50-$12.00/pc ex-factory — startup testing — [ljvogues cost](https://www.ljvogues.com/private-label-period-underwear-cost)
- Private label ODM: MOQ 500-1,000 pcs, $6.00-$11.50/pc — new brand launch
- Custom OEM: MOQ 3,000-10,000 pcs, $4.50-$8.00/pc — growing DTC
- Full custom OEM: MOQ 30,000-50,000+ pcs, $3.80-$6.00/pc — retail/wholesale
- One OEM claims MOQ as low as 50 pcs for select private-label orders (also quoted as 50 pieces per color per size); another article says "progressive" OEMs now accept 500-1,000 pc custom MOQs — [ljvogues manufacturer page](https://www.ljvogues.com/period-underwear-oem-manufacturer-in-china); [ljvogues construction](https://www.ljvogues.com/period-underwear-construction-layer-by-layer)
- Sampling/setup add-ons: sampling $200-$600; custom printed packaging $0.20-$1.50+/unit (or $200-$800) with its own higher MOQ (1,000-3,000 pcs for printed boxes); PFAS/fabric testing $300-$1,200; third-party inspection $300-$500; freight + duties $1,000-$3,000 — [ljvogues cost](https://www.ljvogues.com/private-label-period-underwear-cost)
- Budget totals: launch one style @500 pcs ≈ $8,000-$15,000; multi-style growth launch $40,000-$80,000; landed cost $5-$15+/pc (serious DTC $7-$12/pc); prototype sampling lead time ~7 days (vendor claim) — [ljvogues cost](https://www.ljvogues.com/private-label-period-underwear-cost)
- Named supplier types/regions (from vendor listicles — verify independently): LJ Underwear (Shantou, Guangdong, vertically integrated OEM/ODM); Shenzhen Grace Underwear / "Period Factory" (Shantou); Xiamen Yishangyi Garments (Xiamen, Fujian, seamless/knitted). Sourcing platforms: Alibaba, and curated EU platform Wonnda lists menstrual-pants manufacturers — [ljvogues top manufacturers](https://www.ljvogues.com/top-period-underwear-manufacturers-china); [Wonnda](https://wonnda.com/product-types/menstrual-pants-manufacturers/)
- Certifications to require: OEKO-TEX Standard 100, GOTS, GRS; plus absorbency, leak-resistance and wash-durability test reports — [ljvogues cost](https://www.ljvogues.com/private-label-period-underwear-cost)

### Inferences
- For a small no-/low-MOQ brand in 2026, the realistic play is (b): a stock or ODM style from a Shantou/Fujian ODM at 50-1,000 pcs, with your own PFAS test + logo/packaging, landing around $7-$12/unit all-in. Pure dropship is now economically worse than it was pre-2025 because de-minimis is gone, and quality/PFAS cannot be controlled — use it only to validate demand, ideally with US-warehoused stock to dodge per-parcel tariffs.
- Full custom OEM (3,000+ pcs) is premature for a new brand; its lower unit cost is offset by $40k-$80k+ outlay.
- All MOQ/price figures trace largely to one vendor (ljvogues); get ≥3 independent quotes to triangulate.

### Gaps
- Independent (non-vendor) confirmation of these exact MOQ/price tiers was not found; strongly vendor-sourced.
- Named suppliers come from self-authored listicles; no independent audit/reputation data gathered.
- Current exact US tariff/duty rate applicable to imported absorbent underwear (HTS code) not confirmed from a primary government source.

## 5. Vetting checklist: good garment vs Everdries-grade clone when sampling

### Takeaway
A concrete, physical + documentary checklist: inspect the seams (flatlock not overlock at crotch/legs), test the film bonding (heat-bonded doesn't peel/bubble; iron-pressed fails in a few washes), run your own repeated-wash + absorbency/leak test, and demand third-party lab reports (PFAS finished-garment test, absorbency via a real method, accredited lab). Everdries-grade failures are side-seam leaks, under-capacity, and post-wash smell.

### Cited Findings
- Seams: feel by hand to tell flatlock (4-needle, 6-thread, strong) from overlock (fails under stretch/wash); overlock at crotch/leg openings is a red flag — [ljvogues construction](https://www.ljvogues.com/period-underwear-construction-layer-by-layer)
- Film bonding: gently flex the gusset and wash-test repeatedly; peeling/bubbling after a few washes indicates iron-pressed (fails ~3-8 washes) vs heat-bonded (~100 washes in vendor test); ask for video of the heat-bonding line running — [ljvogues construction](https://www.ljvogues.com/period-underwear-construction-layer-by-layer); [ljvogues heat bonding](https://www.ljvogues.com/period-underwear-manufacturing-process-heat-bonding)
- Lace/trim edges: pull gently at the trim line — heat-bonded holds; glued edges fray or leak at the seam line — [ljvogues construction](https://www.ljvogues.com/period-underwear-construction-layer-by-layer)
- Other visible defects: shifted cores, skipped stitches, inconsistent bonding; require inspection throughout production (AQL 2.5, 3-stage) not a single end-of-line QC check — [ljvogues construction](https://www.ljvogues.com/period-underwear-construction-layer-by-layer)
- Demand third-party lab data on *production samples* from an accredited lab (check accreditation: CNAS, A2LA or equivalent; check report date) — [ljvogues wash guide](https://www.ljvogues.com/how-to-wash-period-underwear.html)
- Absorbency testing: no dedicated ISO for period underwear; use a real lab method — Hohenstein's FEMTECHMAS-6513 (measures max absorption capacity before leakage, retention, speed of absorption; covers menstrual + urinary; note version-year differences 2022/2023) or Bureau Veritas proprietary absorbency methods; academic work (UCL/Southampton) found acquisition-rate methods robust and rewet methods (UNE 153601-2:2008) poorly repeatable — [Hohenstein period underwear](https://www.hohenstein.de/en-us/trust/hohenstein-quality-labels/from-a-z/reusable-period-underwear); [Bureau Veritas](https://www.cps.bureauveritas.com/needs/absorbency-testing-period-and-leakproof-underwear); [UCL study PDF](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10060189/3/Cottenden_26Macaulay_Manuscript_for%20deposit.pdf)
- Real-world failure signature of a bad clone (Everdries): leaks not contained even at light incontinence, "leaks go through at the side seams," persistent smell after washing — use these as your pass/fail test scenarios — [Trustpilot Everdries](https://au.trustpilot.com/review/everdries.com?page=3)

### Inferences (practical sampling protocol)
- Hands-on: (1) confirm flatlock at crotch/legs; (2) flex/stretch the gusset to check film doesn't crack; (3) tug trim to confirm bonding not glue.
- Lab/own test: (4) pour a measured volume (e.g., 50/100/150 ml saline) to verify the rated ml holds without side-seam leak under light pressure; (5) wash 20-50x on your customers' real cycle, re-test absorbency and check for delamination/smell; (6) require a finished-garment PFAS test + accredited absorbency report per batch.
- Documentary: (7) OEKO-TEX Standard 100 certificate number (verify online) plus a separate PFAS finished-garment report, since OEKO-TEX alone is not "PFAS-free"; (8) AQL 2.5 inline QC.

### Gaps
- No single published, standardized "spec sheet template" with target GSM, core-layer counts, and pass thresholds was found — the brand will need to define its own acceptance criteria with the lab.
- Absorbency test methods are private/proprietary (Hohenstein, Bureau Veritas) rather than a free public ISO, so per-test costs weren't pinned down.
