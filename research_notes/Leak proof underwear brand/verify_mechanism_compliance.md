# Mechanism / Product / Compliance Claims — Adversarial Fact-Check

Verified 2026-10-10. Market: US women's leak-proof / incontinence underwear. Adversarial standard: each claim was pursued to refute. Verdicts: **CONFIRMED** / **CORRECTED** / **UNVERIFIABLE**. "DO NOT SAY" = copy/compliance guardrail.

---

### 1. Urine ≠ menstrual flow; period products underperform for bladder leaks
**VERDICT: CONFIRMED (mechanism) — source attributions partly UNVERIFIABLE**

- Best-verified fact: Urine is thinner/less viscous, released faster and in larger volume than menstrual blood; period pads are built for slow, small, thicker flow and leak/underperform with the sudden, high-volume flow of incontinence. Multiply corroborated across independent and competitor sources.
- URLs:
  - Continence Foundation of Australia (independent): https://www.continence.org.au/living-with-incontinence/living-well-with-incontinence/continence-products/difference-continence-and-period-pads/
  - Poise/Kimberly-Clark: https://www.poise.com/en-us/know-your-flow
  - Rael: https://www.getrael.com/blogs/r-blog/incontinence-pads-vs-period-pads
  - attn:grace blog (the cited source — exists, is a competitor/vendor): https://attngrace.com/blogs/the-edit/why-period-pads-dont-work-for-bladder-leaks
- Solidity: HIGH on the mechanism. The cited "attn:grace" source is a vendor (self-interested) but the claim stands on independent sources. "Rose City Urology" as a specific citation was NOT located/verified — do not attribute to it.
- DO NOT SAY: Don't cite "Rose City Urology" until the specific page is located. Fine to state the fluid-difference mechanism as general/educational; avoid implying the reader personally has incontinence (see #8).

### 2. Leak barrier is heat-bonded TPU/PUL film ~18–25 microns, not a chemical finish; iron-pressed film fails in ~3–8 washes vs ~100 for heat-bonded
**VERDICT: SPLIT — qualitative part CONFIRMED; the specific numbers UNVERIFIABLE (vendor-only / no independent source)**

- Best-verified fact: The waterproof barrier is a physical polyurethane film (TPU heat-/thermally-bonded without chemical glue; PU is a chemical coating/laminate = PUL), not a topical chemical repellent finish. This qualitative contrast is confirmed.
- URLs:
  - Shanhao (OEM manufacturer): https://shanhaoundies.com/tpu-vs-pu-leakproof-layers-whats-the-difference-and-will-they-melt-in-the-dryer/
  - Zephyr Hill (PUL = TPU film bonded to knit): https://www.zephyrhillblog.com/2015/09/diaper-fabrics-what-are-pul-and-tpu/
  - Nature's Fabrics: https://naturesfabrics.com/blogs/all-things-sewing/what-is-pul-fabric
- Solidity: Qualitative = MEDIUM-HIGH. Specific figures = NOT SUBSTANTIATED. No independent source states "18–25 microns" or "3–8 washes (iron) vs ~100 (heat-bonded)." These appear to be vendor/self-test or fabricated numbers; the flag in the brief is correct.
- DO NOT SAY: Do not put "18–25 microns," "3–8 washes," or "~100 washes" in copy as fact — unsubstantiated, and wash-durability numbers are vendor self-test, not independent lab data. "Heat-bonded film barrier, not a chemical finish" is sayable.

### 3. Silver/antimicrobial odor finishes disputed; Which? calls silver "an unnecessary addition," often doesn't deliver antimicrobial properties; washes out over time
**VERDICT: CONFIRMED (with scope notes)**

- Best-verified fact: Which? (UK consumer body), testing period pants with AltroConsumo, says silver is "an unnecessary addition as not all period pants contain silver, and testing has shown it may not be effective anyway," and that "textile items treated with silver often do not actually have the promised antimicrobial properties." Verbatim match.
- URL: https://www.which.co.uk/policy-and-insight/article/some-popular-period-pants-contain-unnecessarily-high-levels-of-silver-which-warns-aFFwv6E0Sb8r (13 Nov 2023)
- Solidity: HIGH for the two quoted points. Caveats: (a) Which? is UK, period-pants-focused, not US/incontinence; (b) the "antimicrobials wash out over time" sub-claim is NOT a Which? finding — Which? didn't say this (a brand, Primark, instead claimed effectiveness "for 50 washes"). Treat "wash out over time" as general/unsourced here.
- DO NOT SAY: If making an anti-silver positioning claim, attribute the two quotes precisely to Which? (2023) and don't extend "washes out over time" to Which?. Don't make blanket safety claims either direction — Which? says evidence is insufficient, not that silver is proven harmful.

### 4. OEKO-TEX Standard 100 bans intentional PFAS, caps 100 mg/kg (since Jan 1 2024), but is NOT a "PFAS-free" certificate
**VERDICT: CONFIRMED**

- Best-verified fact: Effective Jan 1 2024, OEKO-TEX set a general PFAS ban and a 100 mg/kg limit value across STANDARD 100, ECO PASSPORT, LEATHER STANDARD, ORGANIC COTTON. Hohenstein (an OEKO-TEX institute) explicitly states OEKO-TEX does NOT certify a product as "PFAS-free." (Note: limits tightened further in 2025/2026 updates.)
- URLs:
  - https://www.oeko-tex.com/en/news/press-releases/oeko-tex-new-regulations-2024-press-release/
  - https://www.hohenstein.us/en-us/oeko-tex/restrictions-and-testing/pfas
- Solidity: HIGH (primary sources).
- DO NOT SAY: Never label OEKO-TEX Standard 100 fabric as "PFAS-free." That overclaims and contradicts the certifier itself. Say "OEKO-TEX Standard 100 certified (tested to its PFAS limits)" at most.

### 5. Thinx settled PFAS class action ~$4–5M (Dickens v. Thinx, SDNY), purchases Nov 2016–Nov 2022; Thinx denied wrongdoing
**VERDICT: CONFIRMED**

- Best-verified fact: Dickens, et al. v. Thinx Inc. settled for up to $5M; class covers Thinx period underwear bought Nov 12 2016 – Nov 28 2022. Thinx denied wrongdoing/liability (standard class-settlement denial; reported across coverage).
- URLs:
  - Official settlement site: https://www.thinxunderwearsettlement.com/
  - https://www.classaction.org/blog/thinx-settles-forever-chemicals-menstrual-underwear-class-action-for-5-million
  - Docket: https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/63337079/dickens-v-thinx-inc/
- Solidity: HIGH. Note: "$5M" is the stated cap (up to), not necessarily paid out; "~$4–5M" is a fair description.
- DO NOT SAY: In comparative/competitor copy, state it as "a settlement" and that Thinx denied wrongdoing — a settlement is not an admission or a finding that products contained harmful PFAS. Overstating it as proven guilt invites a Lanham Act false-advertising risk.

### 6. CA AB 1817 and NY both ban intentionally-added PFAS in apparel, effective Jan 1 2025 (CA: no intentionally-added PFAS or total organic fluorine ≤100 ppm)
**VERDICT: CONFIRMED**

- Best-verified fact: CA AB 1817 bans manufacture/distribution/sale of new textile articles with "regulated PFAS" from Jan 1 2025 — defined as intentionally-added PFAS OR total organic fluorine ≥100 ppm (stepping down to 50 ppm on Jan 1 2027). New York (ECL §37-0121) bans sale of new apparel with intentionally-added PFAS, also effective Jan 1 2025.
- URLs:
  - Morgan Lewis: https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/11/new-york-and-california-bans-on-pfas-in-textiles-and-apparel-begin-january-1-2025
  - Bill text: https://legiscan.com/CA/text/AB1817/id/2609117
  - Buchalter: https://www.buchalter.com/blogs/navigating-new-pfas-regulations-in-california-and-new-york-critical-updates-for-the-textile-and-apparel-industries/
- Solidity: HIGH. Nuance: CA threshold is "≥100 ppm" (at or above) — the claim's "≤100 ppm" phrasing inverts the operator; compliance means staying BELOW 100 ppm. NY uses "intentionally added" only (no identical ppm trigger in the apparel provision).
- DO NOT SAY: Don't claim "complies with CA/NY PFAS law" without test data; compliance requires ≤100 ppm TOF (CA, below 50 ppm from 2027) AND no intentionally-added PFAS. State the threshold as "below 100 ppm," not "≤" loosely.

### 7. FDA: "protective garment for incontinence" = Class I, 510(k)-exempt, 21 CFR 876.5920, product code EYQ; period/menstrual underwear FDA status comparatively unsettled
**VERDICT: CONFIRMED**

- Best-verified fact: Product code EYQ, "Protective garment for incontinence," Device Class 1, 510(k) Exempt, Regulation 876.5920. Device = absorbent padding + fluid barrier to protect an incontinent patient. (Older examples still show 510(k)s like K820961 from before exemption.)
- URLs:
  - FDA product classification EYQ: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpcd/classification.cfm?id=EYQ
  - eCFR 21 CFR 876.5920: https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-H/part-876/subpart-F/section-876.5920
- Solidity: HIGH (primary FDA/CFR). "Period underwear FDA status comparatively unsettled" is a reasonable characterization — menstrual products fall under different/newer FDA framing and lack a single clean exempt code like EYQ; stated as "comparatively unsettled" it is defensible but is commentary, not a cited FDA position.
- DO NOT SAY: Only call a product a "medical device" / "FDA Class I" if it actually meets the EYQ definition and the maker has done the required registration/listing. 510(k)-exempt ≠ "FDA approved" or "FDA cleared" — never say "FDA approved."

### 8. Meta Personal Attributes policy prohibits ads asserting/implying a viewer's medical condition ("Do you suffer from incontinence?" = reject); applies to image/video text; symptom-management allowed, cure claims for listed conditions banned
**VERDICT: CONFIRMED (core) — "cure vs management" nuance should be checked against live Meta text**

- Best-verified fact: Meta's Personal Attributes policy bars ad content that asserts or implies personal attributes, including a medical/health condition; "Do you suffer from [condition]?" is the canonical rejected phrasing. Policy applies to text in images/video, not just body copy.
- URLs (Meta's official policy pages weren't scraped; these summarize/apply it):
  - https://www.medicalmarketinggroup.com.au/compliance/meta-healthcare-policies/
  - https://searchengineland.com/meta-ads-restrictions-health-wellness-campaigns-453094
  - (Primary to confirm in-house: Meta Transparency Center → Advertising Standards → Personal Attributes / Personal Health.)
- Solidity: MEDIUM-HIGH. The "Do you suffer from…" rule is well-established and consistent across sources. The specific "symptom-management allowed / cure claims banned" split is an accurate paraphrase of Meta's health-claims posture but the exact wording should be confirmed against the current Meta Advertising Standards before it becomes a hard guardrail.
- DO NOT SAY: Never "Do you suffer from incontinence/bladder leaks?" or any second-person "you have/your condition" framing, including as on-image text. Use third-person/neutral ("Leaks happen. Stay dry."). No cure/"eliminates incontinence" claims.

### 9. NAD (BBB National Programs) recommended SCA/TENA discontinue "zero leaks" claims for overnight underwear (test not designed for ad substantiation)
**VERDICT: CONFIRMED (with a reasoning correction)**

- Best-verified fact: Jan 3 2018, NAD recommended SCA Personal Care discontinue "zero leaks" claims for TENA Overnight adult incontinence underwear, after a challenge by Kimberly-Clark. SCA said it would comply (claims already discontinued).
- URL: https://bbbprograms.org/media/newsroom/decisions/nad-recommends-sca-personal-care-discontinue-zero-leaks-claims-for-tena-overnight-adult-underwear-fo
- Solidity: HIGH that NAD recommended discontinuing "zero leaks." CORRECTION to the parenthetical: NAD noted the Medi-Cal test "was not designed for the purpose of advertising claim substantiation" but explicitly did NOT treat that as a fatal flaw. The actual fatal flaws were that the test (fixed synthetic-urine dose to product center) ignored body positions and body types, so it didn't reflect real-world use. Don't frame it as "test not designed for ads = disqualified."
- DO NOT SAY: Avoid absolute "zero leaks" / "no leaks" / "leak-proof guarantee" absorbency claims — NAD has struck these down as unsupportable by standard absorbency lab tests. "Leak-resistant," "designed to help prevent leaks," with real-world-representative substantiation, is safer.

### 10. FTC 2024 Fake Reviews Rule bans fake/AI-generated reviews and fabricated testimonials; fake doctor endorsements also implicate right-of-publicity / Lanham Act §43(a)
**VERDICT: CONFIRMED**

- Best-verified fact: FTC's final Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials (16 CFR Part 465) — announced Aug 14 2024, effective Oct 21 2024, 5–0 vote — prohibits fake or false reviews/testimonials including those from people who don't exist or AI-generated, and buying positive/negative reviews. Fabricated expert/doctor endorsements separately expose right-of-publicity and Lanham Act §43(a) false-advertising/false-endorsement liability (standard, well-settled law).
- URLs:
  - FTC press release: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials
  - FTC Q&A: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumer-reviews-testimonials-rule-questions-answers
- Solidity: HIGH on the FTC rule (primary). The §43(a)/right-of-publicity point is accurate general law, not a specific FTC rule provision.
- DO NOT SAY: No fabricated/AI-written reviews, no invented testimonials, no fake "doctor/gynecologist recommended" endorsements or stock-photo "experts." Disclose any material connection with endorsers; use only real, substantiated testimonials.

## Summary flags for copy/compliance
- **Hard bans in copy:** "PFAS-free" (if only OEKO-TEX) · "FDA approved" · "zero leaks / leak-proof guarantee" · "Do you suffer from [condition]?" · any cure claim · fake/AI reviews or fake doctor endorsements.
- **Unsubstantiated numbers — do not state as fact:** "18–25 micron" film, "3–8 vs ~100 washes" durability (vendor self-test / not independently sourced).
- **Attribution fixes:** "Rose City Urology" (#1) unverified; attribute silver quotes precisely to Which? 2023 (#3); CA PFAS threshold is "below 100 ppm," not "≤" loosely (#6); NAD's objection was test realism, not that the test wasn't built for ads (#9).
