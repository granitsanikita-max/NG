# Competitor Landscape: Women's Leak-Proof / Incontinence / Period Underwear (2026)

Scope note: Research conducted Oct 2026. Traffic/revenue figures are third-party estimates (WinningHunter/SimilarWeb-style modeling) unless otherwise stated; treat as directional. Ad copy is quoted verbatim from the Meta ad library (via WinningHunter) with capture dates. US and UK/EU English markets prioritized. Two distinct category framings run through this market and should not be conflated: **incontinence/bladder-leak-positioned** (Everdries, Jude, attn:grace, Orykas, the DR clones, and increasingly Thinx) vs **period-positioned** (Thinx core, Knix, Modibodi, Saalt). The DR/dropship tier almost universally leads with the incontinence/bladder angle because it targets an older, higher-urgency, less brand-loyal buyer.

## Q1: Positioning, target customer, pricing, offer, AOV, subscription, and tech stack per competitor

### Takeaway
There are three structurally different business models competing under one SEO keyword. (1) DR dropship (Everdries + clones): no subscription, steep fake "5-pack 50% off" bundles, cheap advertorial funnels, incontinence-framed, older women. (2) Credible femtech incontinence brands (Jude, attn:grace): subscription-led, clinical/sustainable framing, 50+ women, retail + DTC. (3) Period-first retail brands (Knix, Thinx, Modibodi, Saalt): one-time apparel purchases, millennial/Gen-Z, wide SKU range, mostly ceding Meta DR to focus on brand/retail. Knix is by far the largest (~$3-6M/mo), the DR tier runs the most aggressive Meta volume.

### Cited Findings

**Tier A — Everdries (collapsing DR dropship king)**
- Incontinence-positioned ("if you struggle with bladder leaks"); product framed as "Leakproof underwear that looks & feels just like regular underwear." Target: older women with light-to-moderate bladder leakage, plus nighttime leaks. — [Meta ad library via WinningHunter, ads captured Sep 2026]
- No subscription. Offer is deep quantity bundles at a fake discount: "5 pairs of Everdries for 50% off"; high-rise "5-pack for only $79.95, while they're usually $31.95 a pair"; boyshort "5 pairs of Everdries Boyshorts for only $69.95." — [Meta ad library via WinningHunter, Aug-Sep 2026]
- Stack is minimal DR: Shopify, PayPal, Google Ads pixel, Klaviyo, Convertful, and Taboola (native advertorial traffic) on several ads (technologies: SH, PP, GM, KV, CO, TA). Sells across two domains (everdries.com and shop.everdries.com) with advertorial-style landing pages (e.g. /pages/leakproof-underwear-by-everdries-cottonhw). — [WinningHunter store/ad data, Sep 2026]

**Tier B — Credible incontinence brands**
- **Jude (wearejude.com):** London-based bladder-care brand, founder/CEO Peony Li (ex-Daye, ex-Founders Factory). Supplement-LED, not underwear-led. Lineup: Bladder Health Supplement from £26.63/mo, Night Bladder Supplement from £25/mo, Pelvic Floor Supplement from £25/mo; Leakproof Underwear from £16.60 (£24.95 for high-waist at Boots/Holland & Barrett); Bamboo Everyday Liners from £13.77; Bamboo Pads ~£4.99/24 at Chemist Direct. Bundles: Day & Night Duo £99 / Strength & Sleep Duo £99 (both "SAVE 30%"). Subscription core: "Subscribe & save up to 33%"; 20% off first order via quiz/newsletter; £10 referral both sides. Target: women 40+ (skews older/menopausal), "1 in 3 women," symptom-segmented (daytime frequency / night waking / leaks on laughing/exercise). Clinical-authority framing: "Developed by experts," gynaecologists/pharmacologists/dietitians, "Backed by 3000+ studies," "Trusted by 150,000+ women," Made in UK. — [wearejude.com, Oct 2026; womenofwearables.com; tech.eu]
- Jude stack is the most sophisticated in the set: custom Next.js + Storyblok CMS (not Shopify), Amplitude, Segment, Triple Whale, Okendo reviews, Trustpilot, Gorgias, LyveCom (shoppable video), Taboola (native advertorials), Bing Ads, Pinterest Pixel, AWIN affiliate. — [WinningHunter tech scan]
- Jude funding: £2M pre-seed (2022, led by June Angelides/Samos VC, Reckitt's innovation fund participating); $4.24M (£3.36M) seed 2023 (co-led Eka Ventures + Joyance Partners); £400k Innovate UK grant. Omnichannel: DTC + UK retail (Boots, Holland & Barrett, Chemist Direct). — [tech.eu; femtechinsider.com; uktech.news]
- **attn:grace (attngrace.com):** US (Cambridge MA), incontinence-positioned: "Skin-Safe Protection for Bladder Leaks. Tough on leaks. Gentle on skin. Sustainably designed for women, by women." Certified B Corp; Good Housekeeping Best Sustainable Innovation Award 2024. Disposable + washable range: Heavy/Moderate/Light Pads for Bladder Leaks (~£/$17-17.50), washable underwear, disposable odor-proof bags (50-ct $17.50 / 150-ct $45), plus body-care (deodorant $14, body oil $28). Shopify + Recharge Subscriptions (subscription core), Klaviyo (email/SMS), Okendo, Gorgias, RevenueHunt Shop Quiz, Intelligems A/B testing. Target: eco-conscious women with bladder leaks, likely 45-65. — [WinningHunter store data, Sep 2026]

**Tier C — Period-first retail brands**
- **Knix (knix.com):** The giant. "Wireless bras, period proof underwear, sports bras, loungewear." Broad intimates range (828 products). AOV ~$109.50. Target: millennial women, comfort/body-positive, period + everyday intimates (not incontinence-led). One-time purchase model (no subscription core); growth via Rebuy upsell engine, AIOD automatic discounts, Klaviyo email/SMS, Okendo + Trustpilot reviews, Northbeam attribution. Owned by Essity (80% for $320M, 2022). — [WinningHunter store data; retailtouchpoints.com]
- **Thinx (thinx.com / shethinx.com):** Period underwear pioneer, now Kimberly-Clark-majority-owned (since Feb 2022; minority since 2019). Notably, its WinningHunter bestsellers are now heavily bladder/incontinence SKUs (Bladder Hiphugger $41, Bladder Thong $31, Bladder Basic Hi-Waist/Brief $27) alongside period (Everyday Comfort Hi-Waist/Brief $20, Lace Hi-Waist $43, Sleep Shorts $61). Four sub-brands: Thinx, Thinx BTWN (teens), Speax by Thinx (incontinence), Thinx for All. Stack: Shopify, Attentive (SMS), Yotpo, SwellRewards loyalty, Loop Returns, Tatari (TV advertising — signals brand/TV over Meta DR), Optimizely, Richpanel. — [WinningHunter store data; fashionunited]
- **Modibodi (modibodi.com):** "The original period underwear and swimwear." AU (Sydney), Essity-owned (A$140M, 2022). Wide range (400 products): period underwear, swimwear, activewear, leggings, camis, anti-chafing, maternity, incontinence (tagged). Pricing: underwear ~AUD $18.50-28 (on 50% promo from ~$37-56), activewear $35-53. Heavy, permanent promotional posture (every SKU tagged "50 Off" and "PROMO" across AU/EU/UK/US). Stack: Shopify, Yotpo + Okendo + Reviews.io, Criteo, Emarsys, Nosto, SwellRewards, smsbump (SMS), Triple Whale, Loop Returns, Upfluence (influencer). — [WinningHunter store data]
- **Saalt (saalt.com):** US (Boise), B Corp. "Sustainable period underwear and reusable period cups." Dual hero category: menstrual cups/discs ($32-35) + period underwear (Leakproof Cotton Brief $29, Comfort Brief $39, Comfort CloudShort $47). AOV ~$49.28. Stack: Shopify, Yotpo, Klaviyo (email/SMS) + Postscript (SMS), Triple Whale. Target: eco-conscious Gen-Z/millennial menstruators. — [WinningHunter store data]

**Tier D — Dropship clones (the recycled template)**
- **Vera's Undies / Vera Cares (shopvera.com, vera-cares.com, vera-undies.com):** US/Canada, incontinence-positioned. "Signature Leakproof Underwear 5-Pack." Offer: "Buy 5 pairs, get 1 FREE / Buy 10 get 2 FREE / Buy 15 get 4 FREE" + free shipping; "Up to 60% OFF + Free Gifts." Advertorial landing page (/pages/leakproof-underwear-guide). Stack: Shopify, PayPal, Klaviyo, Convertful, bundle app (SH, PP, KV, CO, KB, BU). — [Meta ad library via WinningHunter, Mar-Sep 2026]
- **Miriano (miriano.com):** US/AU, same "Signature Leakproof Underwear 5-Pack," $64.54, "BLACK FRIDAY OFFER - 40% OFF." Shares the identical Shopify product ID (7082150330417) with the Vera template — literally the same dropship product/catalog. Stack: Shopify, Convertful, bundle app, Koala, TrackingPackage (SH, CO, TP, KB, BU). — [Meta ad library via WinningHunter]
- **Orykas (orykas.com):** MEN'S incontinence brand — bamboo (Oeko-Tex) boxers absorbing "up to 300 ml," light-to-moderate urinary leakage, used over diapers by prostate-cancer patients. Trustpilot 4.0/5 (256 reviews). Distinct audience (men). — [trustpilot.com/review/orykas.com]
- Other clone/low-trust domains surfaced: Duares (duares.com), Everleakproof (everleakproof.com), underwear-stores.com. — [Trustpilot; malwaretips.com]

### Inferences
- The clones aren't competitors so much as a recurring Chinese-template "winning product" (same 5-pack, same gusset spec, same bundle ladder) relaunched under disposable brand names. A new DTC brand will be perpetually surrounded by these; they poison keyword trust but have no retention/brand defensibility.
- Jude is the most strategically complete incontinence play (supplement subscription + consumable pads/liners + underwear + retail), giving it recurring revenue the apparel-only brands lack. Its supplement-first model means underwear is a low-price entry/attach item, not the hero.
- Knix and Thinx have effectively ceded Meta direct-response: Thinx runs Tatari (TV) and Knix runs a retention/upsell stack (Rebuy/Northbeam) rather than aggressive prospecting creative — leaving the Meta incontinence-DR lane wide open to Everdries and the clones.

### Gaps
- Exact AOV for Everdries, Jude, attn:grace, Thinx, Modibodi not available (WinningHunter returned null AOV for most; Knix $109.50 and Saalt $49.28 are modeled estimates).
- Could not confirm the legal entity / operator behind Everdries or the clones (no About/registry data retrieved).

## Q2: Advertising angles and hooks (verbatim ad copy)

### Takeaway
The DR tier wins on emotional, problem-agitation advertorial copy (shame/relief, fake first-person testimonials, "100% leakproof," urgency + fake discounts). The credible brands sell authority (clinical, B-Corp, awards) and the period brands sell lifestyle/comfort. The single most-copied hook in the category is the fake personal testimonial ("I've worn these for X and they changed my life").

### Cited Findings

If you LOVE Everdries but struggle with skin irritation, these will be a game-changer for you... the same award-winning leakproof protection Everdries are known for... A new brand can out-position this by being verifiably real (named founder, real reviews, real testing) — the exact trust gap the clones leave open.
- "Better than pads / stop wearing pads / no more stained sheets" is the universal category promise; ironically it is also the DR tier's biggest liability (see Q3 — Everdries' own CS tells buyers to keep wearing a pad).

## Q3: Reputation and documented product/service failures

### Takeaway
Trust cleanly separates the tiers: Everdries (Trustpilot 2.5) and the clones are documented underperformers with "still need a pad," absorbency, sizing and refund complaints; Thinx carries the PFAS-settlement reputational scar; Jude (4.5) and Knix (4.5) are genuinely well-reviewed; Modibodi (3.5) is sliding on service/quality post-acquisition.

### Cited Findings
- **Everdries — Trustpilot 2.5/5 (646 reviews).** Confirmed "still need a pad" failure: a Feb 2026 reviewer says the company told them to use a pad with the underwear — "What's the point of having 'leak proof' underwear if I still need to wear a pad?" Other documented complaints: absorbency fails on small volumes ("a teaspoon went straight through," "pad area is tiny and completely non absorbent"); sizing off ("the back of the panties never reached up to my waist"); overpriced for performance; refund/return difficulty ("not returnable if you wash them or use them"). Some positives on light leakage and CS exchanges. — [Trustpilot multiple country sites via WebSearch; WinningHunter lists Everdries Trustpilot 2.5/646]
- **Thinx — PFAS class action.** Settled for $4 million (denying wrongdoing). Suit alleged PFAS "forever chemicals" in products marketed as "safe, organic, and chemical-free." Terms: never intentionally add PFAS; disclose antimicrobial treatments (e.g., Agion). Payouts: $7/pair (max 3) with proof, $3.50/pair without, or 35% voucher. Claims deadline April 12, 2023 (closed). — [fox29.com and syndicated Fox affiliates; settlement site thinxunderwearsettlement.com]
- **Modibodi — Trustpilot 3.5/5 (1,191 reviews), low 21.7% reply rate to negatives.** Recurring themes (WinningHunter gap analysis of reviews): "Poor customer service responsiveness and reliance on AI bots" (27 reviews), "Leakage and absorbency problems especially on heavy flow" (9), "Product quality decline and durability issues," "Difficult and costly returns/exchanges" (9), sizing inconsistency, slow/failed delivery (12), and alarmingly "Received used or unsealed hygiene products" (3). — [WinningHunter review-gap data, Sep 2026]
- **Knix — Trustpilot 4.5/5 (674 reviews), strong 92.6% reply rate to negatives.** Minor recurring issues: inaccurate/inconsistent sizing and AI size-rec inaccuracy, final-sale/return policy friction, occasional fabric-thickness and durability complaints. Overall healthy. — [WinningHunter review-gap data]
- **Jude — Trustpilot ~4.5/5 (≈5,218 reviews).** "Reviewers overwhelmingly had a great experience... consistently praise the products for their effectiveness." Main criticism is price ("Good but very pricey"). 90-day first-order refund. — [Trustpilot via WebSearch, Oct 2026]
- **Saalt — Trustpilot ~3.0/5 but tiny sample (18 reviews).** Issues: durability, slow/unpredictable delivery, high customs fees (EU), painful cup removal. Low volume makes this unreliable. — [WinningHunter review-gap data]
- **Orykas (men's) — Trustpilot 4.0/5 (256 reviews):** works for many; main complaints sizing-runs-tight and no returns on opened/washed hygiene items. — [Trustpilot]
- **Clones (Duares, Everleakproof, underwear-stores.com):** reviews report ineffective product, non-delivery, missing guarantees, restocking fees; MalwareTips describes a network playbook of rotating domains/brand names on the same templates once complaints accumulate. — [Trustpilot; malwaretips.com]

### Inferences
- The category's defining vulnerability is the gap between the "no more pads / 100% leakproof" promise and real absorbency, especially on heavy/incontinence flow. Everdries and clones monetize the promise and absorb the refund/complaint cost as a churn-funnel; credible brands (Jude, attn:grace) hedge by selling pads/liners alongside underwear and never over-claiming "replace your pad entirely."
- Thinx's PFAS settlement permanently damaged the "clean/non-toxic" claim for period underwear generally — a new brand can turn third-party PFAS-free testing into a trust wedge, but should expect heightened scrutiny of any "chemical-free/organic" language.

### Gaps
- No current (2026) Trustpilot score captured for Thinx or attn:grace (WinningHunter returned empty Trustpilot arrays for both); Thinx reputation inference rests on the PFAS case, not a current review score.

## Q4: Who is scaling vs declining (traffic/revenue evidence)

### Takeaway
Knix is the clear scaled leader and growing; Everdries runs enormous and still-growing Meta ad volume despite falling site traffic (classic DR churn); Thinx is clearly declining; Modibodi and Saalt are large-but-flat-to-softening; attn:grace is small but recovering. All figures are third-party estimates.

### Cited Findings
- **Knix — scaling / dominant.** Est. monthly visits rose from ~650k (Mar 2026) to a peak ~942k (Jul 2026), ~733k (Aug). Est. 30-day revenue $3.1M-$6.2M; est. 1-day revenue $111k-$221k; AOV ~$109.50; 83% US. 2M+ customers; 2021 net sales $133.6M (+97% YoY). Founder Joanna Griffiths stepped down July 2026 (Nicole Tapscott now leads) — first time run without founder. — [WinningHunter SimilarWeb-style data; thelogic.co; retail-insider.com]
- **Everdries — ad volume scaling, traffic declining.** Active ads on its Meta page grew from ~1,386 (3 months prior) to ~2,366 (Sep 2026), i.e., still ramping spend. But SimilarWeb (via Semrush) shows site traffic falling: ~221.8k visits Sep 2026, down ~21.6% MoM; an earlier period showed June ~317.8k, down 46% from May. Traffic ~41% direct, ~22% from l.facebook.com (heavy paid-social dependence). — [WinningHunter ad-count data; Semrush/nl.semrush.com]
- **Thinx — declining.** Est. monthly visits fell from ~215.8k (Jun 2026) to ~156.8k (Sep 2026). Est. 30-day revenue $821k-$1.37M; ~59% US. — [WinningHunter data, Sep 2026]
- **Modibodi — large but softening.** Est. monthly visits peaked ~344.8k (Jul 2026), down to ~254k (Sep 2026). Est. 30-day revenue ~$1.0M-$1.7M (AUD). Split AU 36.5% / US 31.6%. Permanent 50%-off promo posture. — [WinningHunter data]
- **Saalt — large, flat-to-soft.** Est. monthly visits ~350.5k (Jan 2026) down to ~232k (Sep 2026). Est. 30-day revenue $900k-$1.6M; AOV ~$49.28; 64% US. — [WinningHunter data]
- **attn:grace — small but recovering.** Est. monthly visits dipped to ~17.3k (Apr 2026) then recovered to ~34.9k (Sep 2026). Est. 30-day revenue $180k-$360k; ~79% US. — [WinningHunter data]
- **Clones — ephemeral.** Vera/Miriano pages each show 0 persistent active ads now (burst-and-die pattern); individual ad sets ran heavily Nov 2025-Apr 2026. — [WinningHunter ad data]

### Inferences
- Everdries fits the "collapsing DR king" thesis on the demand side (traffic down 20-46% across recent months, pure paid-social dependence) even while it floods Meta with more creative — a sign of rising CAC / creative fatigue forcing volume to hold revenue. This is the textbook late-stage DR curve and the opening for a retention-built brand.
- The two Essity-owned brands (Knix, Modibodi) diverge: Knix growing, Modibodi softening and leaning on permanent discounting + sliding service scores — suggesting post-acquisition under-investment in Modibodi specifically.
- Thinx's decline + Kimberly-Clark ownership implies it is being run for margin/retail distribution, not DTC growth — it is not defending the Meta DR lane.

### Gaps
- Revenue figures are modeled ranges, not reported financials (except Knix's 2021 $133.6M and Modibodi's 2021 $56.7M sales / $5.7M EBITDA). No 2025-2026 reported revenue for any brand.
- No reliable standalone Everdries revenue estimate (WinningHunter had no store record; only Semrush traffic).

## Q5: Strengths, structural vulnerabilities, and ignored audiences

### Takeaway
Every incumbent leaves a clear gap. The DR tier owns attention but has zero trust/retention; the period brands own trust but have ceded Meta DR and under-serve the incontinence buyer; the credible incontinence brands (Jude, attn:grace) are small and either supplement-led or sustainability-niche. The largest un-owned space is a trustworthy, real-brand, incontinence-positioned leak-proof underwear sold with retention economics (subscription/replenishment) directly on Meta — exactly where Everdries/clones play but with none of their liabilities.

### Cited Findings (strength / vulnerability / ignored audience per brand)
- **Everdries:** Strength — massive creative volume, proven advertorial funnel, first-mover on the incontinence-DR angle. Vulnerability — 2.5 Trustpilot, "still wear a pad" product failure, refund friction, falling traffic, no retention/subscription, disposable brand equity. Ignores — anyone who researches before buying; heavy-flow/true incontinence; younger buyers; retail. — [Trustpilot; WinningHunter; Semrush]
- **Jude:** Strength — clinical authority, supplement subscription recurring revenue, retail distribution (Boots/H&B), VC + Reckitt backing, 4.5 Trustpilot, sophisticated stack. Vulnerability — premium price ("very pricey"), supplement efficacy is self-reported not clinically proven, underwear is a secondary attach item (not a focused apparel play), UK-centric (US still nascent). Ignores — budget buyers; younger/period customers; US scale (early). — [wearejude.com; Trustpilot; tech.eu]
- **attn:grace:** Strength — B Corp + skin-safety + sustainability differentiation, Recharge subscription, awards, disposable + washable range. Vulnerability — small scale (~35k visits), niche eco-positioning limits TAM, no strong reputation signal captured. Ignores — price-sensitive mainstream; period customers; non-eco buyers. — [WinningHunter]
- **Knix:** Strength — scale, 2M+ customers, trusted (4.5), broad intimates range, Essity backing, strong retention/upsell stack. Vulnerability — period/everyday-intimates focus means it is NOT incontinence-positioned; founder just departed (transition risk); premium AOV. Ignores — the dedicated incontinence/older-leakage buyer; Meta DR prospecting (runs retention/TV-style instead). — [WinningHunter; retail-insider.com]
- **Thinx:** Strength — category pioneer, brand recognition, Kimberly-Clark distribution, TV (Tatari), four sub-brands incl. Speax (incontinence) and BTWN (teens). Vulnerability — PFAS reputational scar on "clean" claims, declining traffic, corporate ownership = margin/retail focus not DTC aggression. Ignores — Meta DR; has effectively conceded performance-marketing share. — [fashionunited; WinningHunter; Fox PFAS coverage]
- **Modibodi:** Strength — "original" period underwear, widest SKU range (swim/active/maternity), Essity backing, AU leader. Vulnerability — 3.5 Trustpilot, service decline ("AI bots"), hygiene-handling complaints, permanent discounting erodes margin/brand, softening traffic. Ignores — premium positioning (now a discounter); US-first focus (AU-led). — [WinningHunter]
- **Saalt:** Strength — B Corp, dual hero (cups + underwear), decent AOV, sustainability. Vulnerability — cups are a different (shrinking-growth) category, thin review base, soft traffic. Ignores — incontinence/older buyer; non-eco mainstream. — [WinningHunter]
- **Clones (Vera/Miriano/Duares etc.):** Strength — cheap to spin up, ride proven creative. Vulnerability — no trust, no retention, disposable domains, same product-failure risk as Everdries. Ignores — everything except cold Meta impulse buyers. — [WinningHunter; Trustpilot]

### Inferences — the white space for a 2026 DTC entrant
- **Incontinence-positioned but credible:** The incontinence buyer (40-70, often post-partum/perimenopausal/menopausal) is served either by untrustworthy DR (Everdries/clones) or by small/niche credible brands (Jude supplement-led, attn:grace eco-niche). A real, well-reviewed, focused leak-proof underwear brand aimed squarely at this buyer — with honest absorbency tiers (light/moderate/heavy + overnight) and PFAS-free third-party testing — attacks Everdries' exact weakness.
- **Retention economics in a lane that has none:** Everdries and clones have no subscription; Jude proves bladder-care subscription/replenishment works. A replenishment/subscription model on underwear + consumable liners would out-LTV the entire DR tier.
- **Audiences broadly ignored across the whole set:** men with incontinence (only Orykas serves this); plus-size/true-heavy-flow (recurring absorbency failures everywhere); post-surgical/post-partum medical buyers; and the "buys on Meta but checks reviews first" shopper the DR tier repels and the period brands don't target.

### Gaps
- No hard CAC/ROAS or margin data for any competitor (not publicly available); white-space conclusions are inferential from positioning + traffic + reputation signals, not unit economics.
- US market share split between incontinence vs period framing not quantified from a single authoritative source.
