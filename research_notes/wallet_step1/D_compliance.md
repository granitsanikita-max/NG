# Women's Slim Cardholder — §2B Compliance & Claims (SAY / DON'T SAY)
**Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Region:** US (primary), UK, CA, AU, NZ · **Language:** English
**Product (actual category):** women's fashion ACCESSORY / small leather good — a slim cardholder in **vegan "leather" (PU), genuine leather, or metal/aluminum**, accessible tier $25–55, advertised on **Meta (FB/IG) + TikTok (ads + Shop)**.
**Analyst slice:** D (Compliance & Claims). **Risk class:** LOW vs health/supplements — but NOT zero. The real traps are **material/leather labelling, RFID over-claiming, and luxury-design/IP imitation**, plus endorsement disclosure and (because of the vegan angle) green-claim substantiation.
**Source tags:** **VERBATIM** = policy/reg page opened & quoted · **SNIPPET** = search-result excerpt (verbatim policy text inside it) · **INFERENCE/GK** = my reasoning or established general-knowledge framework to verify live before launch.
**Tools:** Firecrawl (gov_search + scrape + search). USD.

> **DO NOT paste supplement/health rules onto this product.** The health playbook (`compliance_playbook.md`: Meta Health & Wellness, DSHEA, disease claims, fake-doctor advertorials) is **irrelevant** here. The rulebooks that actually govern a cardholder are: **FTC Leather Guides (16 CFR Part 24)**, **FTC Endorsement Guides (16 CFR Part 255)**, **FTC Green Guides (16 CFR Part 260)** for the vegan/eco angle, **Meta Personal Attributes + Third-Party IP ad standards**, **TikTok Shop Prohibited/IP/Listing policies**, **Apple trademark + "Works with Apple Find My" rules**, and the overseas mirrors (UK ASA/CAP + CMA + Textile labelling + CPUT 2008; AU ACCC/ACL; CA Competition Act). ## 1) MASTER TABLE — SAFE TO SAY ✅ / NEVER SAY ❌

### A. MATERIAL / LEATHER (the #1 trap for wallets)
| ✅ SAFE TO SAY | ❌ NEVER SAY |
|---|---|
| "**Vegan leather** / **faux leather** / **PU leather**" (imitation disclosed) | "Genuine leather" / "real leather" / bare "**leather**" on a PU product |
| "Leather-look finish," "buttery vegan-leather feel" | Unqualified "leather" or terms *suggestive* of leather on non-leather (24.2) |
| "**Full-grain cowhide**" (only if it is, and disclose non-leather parts) | "Leather" on a product made of **bonded/reconstituted/shredded leather** (24.2(f)) |
| "Italian **vegan** leather" only if the PU is actually made in Italy | "Italian leather" when it isn't Italian, or isn't leather (origin = 24.1) |
| "Designed in [place]" / factual "Made in [country]" | False "Made in USA/Italy," or a hide-shaped/animal-silhouette hangtag on vegan goods (24.2(d)) |
| "**Handcrafted**" only if genuinely largely made by hand | "Handmade / handcrafted" on a machine-mass-produced unit |

### B. RFID / SECURITY
| ✅ SAFE TO SAY | ❌ NEVER SAY |
|---|---|
| "**RFID-blocking layer**" (if the material/liner actually blocks 13.56 MHz) | "Stops hackers stealing your money/identity from your pocket" |
| "Adds a layer of privacy / peace of mind" | "Thieves are scanning your cards right now — you're exposed" (fear-inflation + implies attributes) |
| "Blocks unauthorized contactless reads" (with test basis on file) | "Guaranteed 100% protection from fraud" / "bank-grade security" (absolute, unsubstantiated) |

### C. FINANCIAL / "YOU" FRAMING (Meta Personal Attributes)
| ✅ SAFE TO SAY | ❌ NEVER SAY |
|---|---|
| "I finally ditched my bulky wallet" (first-person, self-identifying) | "**You** carry too much debt / your finances are a mess" |
| "For the woman editing down to what matters" (third-person) | "Struggling with money? Simplify." (implies vulnerable financial status) |
| "Too many cards? Here's the small-bag edit." (about possessions, self-ID) | "We know your wallet's overstuffed and your budget's tight" (implies knowledge of the viewer) |

### D. LUXURY / IP / COMPARISON
| ✅ SAFE TO SAY | ❌ NEVER SAY |
|---|---|
| "Quiet-luxury look," "elevated minimalist cardholder" | "**Celine/Chanel/Coach dupe**," "looks just like [brand]" |
| Your own brand name, your own product photography | Any luxury house's logo, monogram, trade-dress, or product photos in creative |
| "The cardholder for your capsule edit" | Copying a protected design (monogram canvas, signature silhouette) = trade-dress risk |

### E. APPLE / AIRTAG
| ✅ SAFE TO SAY | ❌ NEVER SAY |
|---|---|
| "**Fits Apple AirTag**" / "AirTag slot" (AirTag as adjective + generic noun) | "Works with Apple Find My" (badge) unless **MFi-certified & approved** |
| "Holds the tracker you already own" | Implying Apple partnership/endorsement; using the Apple logo or "Apple" in your brand name |
| "AirTag sold separately" (clarity) | "AirTags" (plural/possessive) or leading with Apple's marks over your own |

### F. GREEN / VEGAN (only if you run the sustainability layer)
| ✅ SAFE TO SAY | ❌ NEVER SAY |
|---|---|
| "**Animal-free** / no animal leather" (factual) | "Eco-friendly / sustainable / green" unqualified (PU is plastic — Green Guides) |
| "Made with [X]% recycled PU" (if substantiated) | "Biodegradable / compostable / plastic-free" without proof |
| "A lower-impact alternative to animal leather" (qualified) | "Saves the planet," "zero-impact," "100% sustainable" (absolute env. claim) |

### G. DURABILITY / "PROOF" WORDS (16 CFR 24.3)
| ✅ SAFE TO SAY | ❌ NEVER SAY |
|---|---|
| "Water-**resistant**," "scratch-**resistant**" (with a real basis) | "**Waterproof**" unless it truly keeps water off contents for the product's life |
| "Holds up to daily wear" | "Scratchproof / scuffproof / lasts forever" (absolute, 24.3) |
| "Backed by our [X]-yr warranty" (if real) | "Lifetime guarantee" you won't honor |

### H. ENDORSEMENT / UGC
| ✅ SAFE TO SAY | ❌ NEVER SAY |
|---|---|
| Creator post with **#ad / Paid partnership** up front, clear & conspicuous | Gifted/paid creator with no disclosure, or disclosure buried in bio/after "more" |
| Real, verified customer reviews | Fabricated reviews, fake star counts, "#1 rated" with no basis |
| "In a survey of N customers, X% said…*" (with the survey on file) | Unqualified "everyone loves it," invented testimonials |

## 2) THE 3 BIGGEST REAL RISKS (ranked) + one-line reframes

**RISK 1 — Material / leather mislabeling (highest probability, easiest to trip).**
FTC Leather Guides (16 CFR 24.0) *explicitly list "wallets," "card cases," "billfolds," "French purses," "purses"* as covered "industry products." §24.2 (VERBATIM): *"It is unfair or deceptive to use the unqualified term 'leather' or other unqualified terms suggestive of leather to describe industry products unless the industry product so described is composed in all substantial parts of leather."* Imitation must be disclosed ("Not leather; Imitation leather; Simulated leather; Vinyl; … Plastic"). §24.2(g): the disclosure must appear **"in all advertising … irrespective of the media"** — so a vegan product's ad can't show it and say "leather" with the PU disclosure only on the hidden label.
→ **Reframe:** say "**vegan leather / PU / faux leather**" everywhere; reserve "leather / full-grain" for genuine-leather SKUs and disclose any non-leather parts.

**RISK 2 — RFID over-claiming (fear-based copy is the trap, not the feature).**
Feature claim ("RFID-blocking layer") is fine *with a basis* that the material blocks. The violation is inflating a threat experts call near-nonexistent: AARP — *"these scams … are largely unheard of … 'We do not believe this topic addresses a real risk'"* (ITRC COO James E. Lee); *"very theoretical fraud"* (Point Predictive). A 2025 fact-check rated the long-range-skim claim **false**. Fear copy ("thieves are scanning you right now") is both unsubstantiated **and** can imply the viewer's vulnerability.
→ **Reframe:** sell it as "**a quiet layer of privacy / peace of mind**," never as protection from a rampant, specific threat.

**RISK 3 — Luxury-design / IP imitation (fastest ban + legal exposure).**
Meta Third-Party IP ad standard (VERBATIM): ads may not promote *"counterfeit goods, such as products that copy the trademark … and/or distinctive features of another company's products to imitate a genuine product."* TikTok Shop Listing Policy (VERBATIM): *"Sellers are prohibited from making claims anywhere in their product listing that the products are dupes, fakes, knock-offs, or anything similar,"* and bans unauthorized brand names/logos in images, descriptions, livestreams or videos. The brand's whole thesis is to deliver the *feeling* of Celine/Coach at $25–55 — do it with **your own design language, never their marks, monograms, trade dress or imagery, and never the word "dupe."**
→ **Reframe:** "quiet-luxury minimalist cardholder" — own the aesthetic, name no house.

## 3) RULE DETAIL PER PILLAR (for the write-up / legal file)

**3.1 FTC Leather Guides — 16 CFR Part 24 (VERBATIM, ecfr.gov).**
- §24.0 scope: covers wallets, card cases, billfolds, purses, handbags, etc. — directly on-point.
- §24.1: deceptive to misrepresent *"kind, grade, quality, … material content, … durability, … origin …"* — this is the hook for false "Italian leather," inflated durability, and origin claims.
- §24.2: unqualified "leather" only if *composed in all substantial parts of leather*; (a) disclose imitation/simulated; (d) **no hide-shaped tag / animal silhouette** on non-leather goods; (f) bonded/reconstituted leather is **not** "leather"; (g) disclosures must be conspicuous **and appear in all advertising**.
- §24.3: "Waterproof / scratchproof / scuffproof" are near-absolute — only truthful if the product genuinely is; "water/scratch **resistant**" needs *"a basis for the representation"* and must be *"meaningfully and significantly resistant."*

**3.2 Meta Personal Attributes (SNIPPET-VERBATIM, transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/objectionable-content/privacy-violations-personal-attributes/ + facebook.com/business/help/2557868957763449).**
Protected list includes *"vulnerable financial status."* *"We don't allow advertisers to run ads that assert or imply the personal attributes of their audience"* / *"Imply knowledge of personal or organizational financial information of a user."* Meta's own violating example uses the word *"you."* For a cardholder this only bites if copy leans into finances/debt or second-person "we know you're…". **Low probability for pure aesthetic copy, but a guaranteed rejection if the "declutter your finances / drowning in debt" angle is used.** Reframe to first-person ("I pared down to 4 cards") or third-person ("for the woman who travels light").

**3.3 RFID efficacy (SNIPPET, AARP + McAfee + Roy Howard fact-check).** Feature claim needs a basis (bench test that the liner blocks 13.56 MHz). Threat-level claims are where brands get into "unsubstantiated/misleading" territory; most experts say contactless skimming is "low risk / very theoretical," modern cards use encrypted dynamic tokens. Keep RFID a *minor* reassurance line, not the hero claim.

**3.4 IP / counterfeit (SNIPPET-VERBATIM).**
- Meta Third-Party IP ad standard + Community Standard: no counterfeits, no copying trademark/distinctive features.
- TikTok Shop **Prohibited Products** (VERBATIM): bans *"counterfeit products, and any products that infringe on a third party's intellectual property."* **IP Policy**: counterfeits include *"trade dress (the overall look and feel of a product or its packaging)"* and *"unauthorized display of brand names, logos, or trademarks in product images, product descriptions, livestreams, or videos."* **Listing Policy**: no "dupe/fake/knock-off" claims; *"Do not use another person's name, image, or likeness for commercial purposes … without their permission."* (→ don't use a celeb's name/photo either.)

**3.5 Apple / AirTag (SNIPPET-VERBATIM, apple.com newsroom + Find My Identity Guidelines).** The **"Works with Apple Find My" badge is part of MFi**; only *approved* products that connect to the Find My network may use it. A cardholder that merely **holds** an AirTag is NOT a Find My accessory — so **don't use the badge or say "Works with Apple Find My."** Descriptive/nominative use is allowed: "fits Apple AirTag," "AirTag slot." Follow Apple trademark rules: AirTag as an adjective + generic noun, no plural/possessive, acknowledge Apple ownership, never imply partnership/endorsement, never put Apple marks in your logo or brand name.

**3.6 Endorsement / UGC — 16 CFR 255 (VERBATIM/SNIPPET).** §255.5(a): a material connection not reasonably expected by the audience *"must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously."* §255.0(f): *"'clear and conspicuous' means … difficult to miss (i.e., easily noticeable) and easily understandable."* 2023 update: video disclosures should be **simultaneous visual + audible**; a disclosure only on the profile page / in closing credits / buried under "more" is **not** sufficient; relying on the platform's built-in "paid partnership" tool alone is not enough — responsibility rests on **the brand and the influencer.** Both gifted and paid creators must disclose.

**3.7 Green / vegan claims — FTC Green Guides 16 CFR 260 (GK) + UK ASA.** "Vegan leather" is almost always PU (plastic). Factual "animal-free / no animal products" is safe; broad "eco-friendly / sustainable / green / biodegradable" needs clear qualification and substantiation (ASA has been aggressively ruling against absolute/unqualified environmental claims; CAP Code §3 requires documentary substantiation *before* publication). Don't let the sustainability wedge become a greenwashing liability.

## 4) PLATFORM SPECIFICS + "why your last ads probably got rejected"

**Meta (FB/IG):** reviews the **landing page + checkout**, not just the ad; flags ad↔page claim/price mismatch and non-functional/low-quality pages. Top rejection triggers *for this category*: (1) **IP** — luxury logo/monogram/product imagery or "dupe" wording; (2) **unrealistic/absolute claims** ("waterproof," "100% fraud-proof," "lifetime"); (3) **Personal Attributes** — second-person "you"/financial framing; (4) landing-page/engagement-bait quality issues.

**TikTok (Ads + Shop):** stricter and reviews **visuals + on-screen text + the listing**, not just copy. For an accessory the live traps are **counterfeit/IP** (luxury look-alikes, "dupe" claims, unauthorized marks in video/livestream) and **material misdescription**. TikTok Shop also requires accurate listings, functioning IP-portal trademark registration for your own brand protection, and bans "dupe/fake/knock-off" language anywhere in the listing. Accessories are **not** a restricted category (unlike beauty/electronics-with-batteries), so no special certification is needed — the gate is IP + truthful description.

> **One-liner — why your last ads probably got rejected:** almost never the product; it's **(a) a luxury brand's name/logo/"dupe" in the creative, (b) an absolute/unsubstantiated claim ("waterproof," "stops thieves," "100% genuine leather" on PU), or (c) a second-person "you"/finances line tripping Personal Attributes** — fix the words, not the product.

## 5) REGIONAL NOTES (US primary; UK/CA/AU/NZ)
- **US:** FTC Leather Guides (24), Endorsement Guides (255), Green Guides (260), §5 deception. State law: CA Automatic Renewal Act if you run subscriptions.
- **UK:** ASA/**CAP Code §3** (misleading — most-breached section; objective claims need documentary substantiation *pre-publication*), **CMA** (new powers/fines up to 10% global turnover for consumer-law breaches), Textile Products (Labelling) Regs, **CPUT 2008** (misleading commercial practices incl. false "leather"/origin). ASA actively names-and-shames undisclosed influencer ads — require **#ad/Paid partnership**.
- **Canada:** Competition Act (false/misleading representations), Textile Labelling Act, Competition Bureau influencer-disclosure guidance.
- **Australia:** **ACCC / Australian Consumer Law** (misleading or deceptive conduct, s18/s29) — covers false "leather," origin ("Made in"), and endorsement/"fake review" crackdown; ACCC has specifically pursued undisclosed influencer and fake-review conduct.
- **New Zealand:** Fair Trading Act 1986 (misleading conduct, country-of-origin, endorsement disclosure) — mirrors AU.

## 6) PRE-LAUNCH COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST
1. Confirm exact material → label every SKU "vegan/PU/faux leather" or "genuine [type] leather"; put the disclosure **in ad creative too**, not just the product label.
2. Kill every absolute word: no "waterproof," "scratchproof," "lifetime," "100%/guaranteed," "stops thieves."
3. RFID = optional reassurance line with a bench-test basis on file; never the fear-based hero.
4. Zero luxury marks/monograms/product photos/"dupe" language in any creative, listing, livestream or UGC.
5. "Fits Apple AirTag" only; no "Works with Apple Find My" badge unless MFi-certified; AirTag as adjective.
6. All paid/gifted creators use clear, up-front **#ad / Paid partnership** (visual + audible on video); brand keeps a creator-brief + disclosure log.
7. Keep a substantiation file: material spec/origin docs, RFID test, any survey behind a "%", warranty terms you'll honor.
8. If running the green wedge: qualify every environmental claim; default to factual "animal-free."
9. Ad↔landing-page↔price consistency; functional, non-bait pages.
10. Subscriptions (if any) → clear terms + easy cancel (CA ARL / UK / AU).
