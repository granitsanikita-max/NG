# Paid-Acquisition Compliance Playbook — Restricted-Category DTC

**Date:** 2026-10-17
**Purpose:** Document the ACTUAL in-market playbook restricted-category DTC/dropship brands use to run paid ads at scale without getting banned — so we can decide what a NEW, low-budget brand can realistically replicate. Operational/competitive research. Focus is **ad-policy navigation**, not law-breaking.
**Categories covered:** (1) women's sexual-wellness / "arousal" creams, (2) men's prostate / urinary supplements, (3) "detox" / parasite-cleanse supplements, (4) weight-loss — plus the general pattern.
**Tools:** WinningHunter (`search_facebook_ads`, store/ad detail), Firecrawl (live advertorial + policy scrapes), WebSearch. Every brand/number is tagged **(observed)** via a tool on 2026-10-17, **(est)** for estimates, or **(gen)** for general-knowledge/industry sources. USD unless noted.

> **How to read this.** "How they stay live" is a mix of (a) genuinely compliant positioning, (b) **greyhat** review-evasion that works on the platform but still carries FTC/FDA and ban exposure, and (c) **blackhat** deception (cloaking, fake doctors). Tactics are documented so we understand competitors and the landscape; the **recommended** build for a new low-budget brand is the whitehat lane in §7. Risk levels are stated as fact, not judgment.

## 1. THE 6 CORE BAN-AVOIDANCE MECHANISMS (the general pattern)

Every restricted-category operator, across all four niches, is running some combination of these six:

1. **Angle laundering (reframing).** Relabel the product from the *prohibited claim* to a *compliant adjacent benefit*. Arousal cream → "intimate skin / menopause comfort serum." Parasite cleanse → "gut reset / bloating / debloat." Prostate shrinker → "urinary comfort / healthy-aging / bladder support." Weight-loss → "cortisol / testosterone / metabolism / liver support." The product barely changes; the *words* change to clear the policy that bans the original claim.

2. **Funnel splitting: benign ad → advertorial "bridge page" → money page.** The ad creative Meta reviews is deliberately mild (a relatable story, an ingredient fact, a question). It links to an **advertorial / listicle bridge page** ("safe page") that pre-sells with the strong claims, then clicks through to the **money page** (checkout/PDP). The heavy claims live one or two clicks *away* from the reviewed ad. Observed bridge-page URL tells: `/adv`, `/list1`, `/lp`, `.../advertorial-1/...`.

3. **Claims are put in someone else's mouth.** First-person UGC ("I added this jelly to my routine, I feel lighter"), customer testimonials, and **fake expert personas** (a "board-certified urologist," a "parasitologist") carry the claims the brand supposedly "can't say itself." **FTC reality (gen):** this is a myth as a *legal* matter — the FTC treats endorsements/testimonials as the advertiser's own claims and holds the advertiser liable; it defeats the *platform reviewer*, not the regulator.

4. **Account & identity infrastructure.** Aged/verified Business Managers, multiple ad accounts, rented agency/whitelisted accounts, rotated FB pages, rotated domains, backup pixels. Purpose: survive the inevitable rejections and bans without losing the whole operation, and buy higher trust/spend limits than a cold account gets.

5. **Volume + rotation + burn-and-reburn.** Winners run **hundreds** of creative variants simultaneously (so a few rejections don't stop delivery) and **constantly spin up new pages/domains** as old ones die. The churn *is* the strategy, not a failure of it.

6. **Channel arbitrage.** Move the hardest claims off Meta entirely — to native (Taboola/Outbrain), Google/YouTube, TikTok *organic*/Shop, affiliate/ClickBank, and owned email/SMS — and keep Meta for the softest, most compliant top-of-funnel angle (or skip Meta for the worst categories).

**Plus creative micro-tactics (§6):** benign thumbnails, emoji/icon substitution, deliberate misspellings ("arous@l"), avoiding before/after imagery and second-person "you have [condition]" framing.

## 2. META POLICY GROUND TRUTH (what is actually restricted)

Scraped verbatim from Meta's **Health and Wellness** advertising standard, `transparency.meta.com/policies/ad-standards/restricted-goods-services/health-wellness` (observed 2026-10-17; page change-logged **Jul 22, 2026**):

- **Sexual-wellness "health exception" (verbatim):** *"Ads must not promote the sale or use of adult sexual arousal products or services, provided that the ads are focused on health and wellness, not sexual pleasure or enhancements, and are targeted to people 18 years or older."* Allowed when the focus is **health/medical efficacy**: prevention of erectile dysfunction, premature ejaculation, **Hypoactive Sexual Desire Disorder (HSDD)**, **pain relief during sex**, **the effects of menopause**, lube and pheromones. **Prohibited:** arousal products focused on **pleasure or enhancement**, sex toys, "non-surgical genital enhancement," genital procedures "focused on sexual pleasure."
- **Weight-loss / supplements / cosmetics:** must target **18+**. Ads **can't** contain "statements of inferiority about physical appearance," depict **"close up on specific body area by pinching fat,"** or claim results "solely by using wearable products." Ads **can** (18+) promote "dietary weight loss… supplements or pills," "illustrate people using the product… and clearly indicate the time taken to achieve noticeable results."
- **Incurable-disease claim ban (exhaustive list, verbatim):** no claims (incl. from health pros) to **cure, heal, or eliminate**: Diabetes, Herpes, Thyroid, Psoriasis, Ebola, **Cancer**, Autism, Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, ALS, HIV. *"This does not apply to claims about treating or managing symptoms."* → why prostate copy says "support healthy prostate" not "shrink/cure," and parasite copy stacks symptoms rather than naming a disease.
- **Clickbait ban:** no "sensational language with exaggerated or extreme claims, or promises of specific outcomes within a set timeframe without disclaimers or qualifiers" in a health/weight context.
- **Personal Attributes policy (gen):** ads may not **assert or imply knowledge of a user's personal attributes**, including medical conditions. → why compliant copy avoids *"Do YOU have parasites / an enlarged prostate / belly fat?"* and uses third-person ("millions of men…") or first-person ("I was peeing 5x a night") instead.
- **Landing-page review (gen):** Meta reviews the **destination page**, not just the ad, and flags **ad↔page mismatch** in claims or price. This is the exact tension the advertorial funnel is built to exploit (benign ad, hot page) — and the exact thing that gets funnels caught.
- **Recent change caveat (gen):** a policy tracker reports Meta folded "Weight Loss & Cosmetic" into a broader "Health and Wellbeing" category on 2026-07-23 and removed some body-image language; practical effect unclear. Treat the live page as truth and re-check before launch.

**Net:** Meta bans the *claim and the imagery/targeting*, not the product category. The lane is: reframe to the allowed health benefit, drop disease/cure words, drop before/after and "you," target 18+. Everything in §3 is operators threading (or evading) exactly these lines.

## 3. PER-CATEGORY PLAYBOOKS

### 3.1 — Women's sexual-wellness / "arousal" creams

**Policy problem:** "Arousal / orgasm / stop faking it / pleasure" = prohibited (pleasure/enhancement). Allowed lane = **sexual/reproductive health**: vaginal dryness, pain during sex, menopause effects, moisturization, HSDD.

**Reframing — compliant vs non-compliant wording:**
| Non-compliant (banned fast) | Compliant reframe (observed in winners) |
|---|---|
| "Arousal cream," "intensify orgasms," "stop faking it, start feeling it" | **"Intimate skin / comfort serum," "pleasure plumper for your lower lips," "menopause intimacy care"** |
| "Increase sexual desire/pleasure" | "Hydration, elasticity, skin-barrier, **dryness relief**, comfort during intimacy" |
| Direct "sex" imagery | Clinical/"no hormones, no parabens, no petroleum" + personified-body advertorials |

**Observed brands & scale:**
- **Hygiene Hero / "Vipstick Serum"** (hygiene-hero.com) — **$400–650k/mo (est)**, AOV $59, **387 active ads (observed)**, running since 2020. Best-in-class reframe: literally sells a *skin* product ("pleasure plumper for your lower lips"), menopause/dryness lead, pleasure kept "educational" ("the C-Spot"). Advertorial personas ("Hi, I am Pearl" — personifies the body to avoid direct claims). One live ad spelled it **"awaken arous@l"** to dodge text classifiers. Trustpilot only 2.5★ (shipping/legitimacy complaints) — the reframe sells but trust is low.
- **Promescent** (promescent.com) — **$1.77M/mo (est)**, AOV $65, the biggest *legit* sexual-wellness DTC; wins by also being clinical/retail/Amazon, not Meta-dependent.
- **Tier context:** MyNumy, Hello Nancy (Trustpilot 3,156), Trybloomin, Lubracil (dropship/performance); Dame, Maude, Foria, Rosebud, plusOne (premium/retail, pleasure-positive but younger/urban); Evernow, Midi, Kindra (Rx/clinical menopause).

**Funnel:** advertorial (menopause/dryness story, gyno/"derm-backed" persona) → GemPages/Beae PDP → BOGO → ~$60 bundle AOV → subscribe-&-save (consumable replenishment funds CAC).

**Channel:** Meta = health-framed only. **TikTok paid** largely restricted/prohibited for sexual products → organic/affiliate/TikTok Shop only. Email/SMS + Amazon/retail are the durable backbone.

**How they stay live (one-liner):** *Sell it as menopause/dryness "intimate skincare," never an "arousal" product — keep pleasure secondary and "educational," and rotate creative hard because even the reframe gets flagged.*

### 3.2 — Men's prostate / urinary supplements

**Policy problem:** can't claim to "shrink/cure prostate" or imply the user's condition; can't claim to cure cancer. Allowed lane = **structure/function** "support healthy prostate / urinary flow / bladder comfort / healthy aging."

**Reframing — compliant vs non-compliant wording:**
| Non-compliant | Compliant reframe (observed) |
|---|---|
| "Shrink your enlarged prostate," "cure BPH," "avoid surgery" | **"Support healthy prostate function," "urinary comfort," "bladder support," "healthy aging for men 50+"** |
| "Do YOU have an enlarged prostate?" (personal attribute) | Third-person ("for millions of men…") or first-person story ("I was peeing 4–5x a night – Andy R") |
| "Lowers PSA / reverses cancer risk" | Ingredient-education, hedged: "saw palmetto **traditionally used to support** prostate size"; "polyphenols **studied for** anti-inflammatory effects" |

**Observed brands & scale (keyword "prostate" = 15,183 ads; "bladder" = 34,949 ads):**
- **Ben's Natural Health** (bensnaturalhealth.com, 112 active ads, US/GB) — VSL funnel to `pages.bensnaturalhealth.com/aatp-uk` ("All About The Prostate"), "doctor-recommended," longest-standing / most legit-leaning; still pushes "naturally shrinking prostate" in copy.
- **Fling "Pee Easy"** (hellofling.com/pages/pee-easy, 84 ads) — clean reframe to a **"daily gummy that supports prostate and urinary health,"** testimonial-led, structure/function ingredient list. A good whitehat-ish template.
- **"Dr. Elizabeth Monroe, MD"** (zorlaofficial.com, 112 ads) — doctor persona, "Doctor-approved method for naturally shrinking prostate," symptom stack incl. "intimacy issues." **Ran only Jul 18→23 (5 days) = banned fast (observed)** — the aggressive version burns.
- **"Holistic Health with Dr. Oliver" → Prostova** (caliraglow.com/prostova/adv, 42 ads) — see §3.5; the canonical fake-doctor advertorial.
- **Natures Pure Blend** (naturespureblend.com/pages/prostate-lp-urge, 70 ads) — ingredient-education hedging ("research points to… studied for").
- **Dr. Simpson Haruka / Tallevida** (93 ads) — blends prostate with ED/stamina ("BUILT FOR MEN WHO WANT TO GET IT UP AGAIN 🍆"); **Mars GHC "Dr. Shannon Anderson"** reframes nitric-oxide/ED gummies as "prostate support."
- **Bladder-specific:** **Lovy / UroControl** (secure.trylovy.com/uc-mt-list1, **877 active ads (observed)** — note `-list1` = listicle bridge) reframes female bladder leaks as "supporting the four systems of bladder control"; **Fyne "Arctic/Scandinavian Bladder Control,"** **Strauss Bladder Support Drops** (10-herb structure/function).

**Funnel:** benign first-person FB ad ("Last night I got up four times…") → **long advertorial / VSL bridge page** (fake-doctor, pseudo-mechanism, invented patients) → bundle checkout (3–5 bottles) → subscription.

**Channel:** Meta (health-framed) + heavy on **advertorial/VSL** style; native (Taboola/Outbrain) and YouTube are natural homes for the long VSL; Amazon for the legit SKUs (Ben's).

**How they stay live (one-liner):** *Say "support healthy urinary flow / prostate function" (structure-function), tell it in first person or through a "doctor," never "shrink/cure," and keep the escalation on the advertorial page, not the ad.*

### 3.3 — "Detox" / parasite-cleanse supplements

**Policy problem:** "detox / parasite / cleanse" + miracle-cure + "do you have parasites?" + fake doctors = multiple violations at once (unverified claims, personal attributes, impersonating medical pros, clickbait). The *hardest* of the four on Meta.

**Reframing — compliant vs non-compliant wording:**
| Non-compliant | Compliant reframe (observed/ recommended) |
|---|---|
| "Kill the parasites living in your gut," "parasite cleanse," "flush toxins" | **"Gut reset," "bloating / debloat," "digestive comfort," "feel lighter after meals," "gut-health support"** |
| "Do YOU have parasites? (symptom quiz: fatigue, brain fog, 3am waking)" | "Occasional bloating and sluggish digestion are common — support your gut's natural balance" |
| Fake "parasitologist / Dr." persona | Named formulator or dietitian with real credentials, or founder story |

**Observed brands & scale (keyword "parasite cleanse" = 7,653 ads):**
- **Resilia** (resilia.shop) — **4,171 active ads (observed)**, $39.99, US-led, AOV $65, huge traffic ramp; *adjacent* (immune/antimicrobial: oregano oil, garlic, black seed) rather than literal "parasite," heavy subscription back-end (Trustpilot complaints = "can't cancel subscription").
- **Puracienne / Orvessa™ ZA** (puracienne.com + DE sister **sovarello.com**) — classic greyhat: two-step "kill AND purge," fake **"parasitologist"** advertorial, ads run on **burner doctor pages created days before launch** ("Dr. Aaron Vidal Health," 48 ads, **page created 2026-10-13 — ~4 days before scrape (observed)**; "Dr. Maya Santos Health," 76 ads), **domain + persona rotation per geo** (UK/ZA English + DE).
- **High churn (observed):** 5+ parasite brands launched on **2026-10-17 alone** (getpurava.com, vermune.com, Noraveli, Madison Clarke…). Operators **burn and re-spin pages/domains** to survive bans — the churn is the model.
- **Legit retail/Amazon:** Zahler ParaGuard, Microbe Formulas, Global Healing/Paratrex, Organifi — the compliant version lives on Amazon/retail, not Meta.
- **Gap worth noting:** **pet** gut/parasite cleanse (Green Paw Wellness, Furbos) is far less saturated and claims are *defensible* (pets genuinely get worms) → lower ban risk.

**Channel:** Meta = only survivable with aggressive greyhat infra OR a fully de-clawed "gut-reset/bloating" reframe. **TikTok Shop rejects** supplement + disease/detox claims; organic only with heavily softened claims. Amazon/retail for the compliant SKU.

**How they stay live (one-liner):** *Either go full greyhat (fake-doctor advertorial on burner pages, domain/persona rotation, expect weekly bans) — or drop the word "parasite" entirely and sell a compliant "gut-reset / bloating" brand; there is little middle ground on Meta.*

### 3.4 — Weight-loss

**Policy problem:** before/after, "pinching fat," "statements of inferiority," negative self-perception, "lose X lbs in Y days" = all prohibited. Allowed lane (18+): dietary supplement that "illustrates people using it and clearly indicates time to results" — but the whole category is a minefield, so winners **reframe away from "weight loss" entirely.**

**Reframing — the dominant move is to NOT be a weight-loss product:**
| Non-compliant | Compliant reframe (observed) |
|---|---|
| "Lose weight / melt belly fat," before/after, pinching fat | **Hormone angle:** "cortisol belly," "natural testosterone support," "crush cortisol" (Mars Men) |
| "Burn fat fast, −4kg in 4 weeks" | **Digestion angle:** "bloating → feel lighter," "debloat," "after-meal routine" (Foodology jelly) |
| "Fat burner / appetite suppressant" | **Organ/metabolism angle:** "liver support, bile flow, healthy fat metabolism" (biocol) |

**Observed brands & scale (keyword "belly fat" = 12,344 ads):**
- **Mars Men** (mengotomars.com) — **3,390 active ads (observed)** — the standout. Reframes a belly-fat product as **men's hormone optimization**: FB ad = "a stress hormone (cortisol) turns everything you eat into belly fat… Tongkat Ali… Shilajit… More T. Less cortisol." On the landing page (observed) the word **"weight loss" appears once, "lose weight" once — but "testosterone" 139× and "cortisol" 36×.** Survey-based claims with asterisk disclaimers ("90% rated Mars Men effective at improving testosterone levels*"), LOCKTOBER countdown, $70 free-gift stack, 90-day guarantee. This is the masterclass: *a weight-loss product that is legally and linguistically a testosterone supplement.*
- **Foodology Global "Cutting Jelly"** (foodology-global.com, Avery Collins 620 ads + 232) — **UGC/first-person** "I added this red jelly to my after-meal routine… I feel way lighter," "Korea's viral belly-fat melting jelly," benign "snack" framing, bloating→fat bridge.
- **biocol labs "Something" liver protocol** (biocollabs.com/pages/sm-tof-offer) — reframes to **"liver / bile flow / fat metabolism"**; runs the **same offer across 3 persona pages** (abbydiane.cooper, trulyaverageash, biocol labs — 160 ads each) = page rotation in the open.
- **SHEKO** (DE/AT) meal-replacement, $89.92, "avg −4kg" — runs the riskier direct angle in a laxer EU market.

**Channel:** Meta (reframed, never "weight loss"); **TikTok bans slimming/fat-loss outright** (visuals + hashtags reviewed) — organic lifestyle only; native (Taboola/Outbrain) + YouTube for VSL fat-loss advertorials; ClickBank affiliate for aggressive offers.

**How they stay live (one-liner):** *Stop being a weight-loss product — become a testosterone/cortisol, bloating, or liver/metabolism product — and never show before/after or pinched fat.*

### 3.5 — Anatomy of the advertorial "money page" (verbatim specimen)

Scraped live: **caliraglow.com/prostova/adv** (observed 2026-10-17; built on **Funnelish**; template fingerprint `info.shopbrizo.com/advertorial-1/…`). This single page contains the whole greyhat toolkit and is worth studying as the archetype:

- **Fake news skin:** dressed as a health-news article ("Health Times / Health / Nutrition / Fitness").
- **Fake expert + fake institution:** *"Dr. James Whitfield, board-certified urologist, 26 years… founder of The Whitfield Institute for Prostate Research."*
- **Conspiracy/authority hook:** "I'm about to make every urologist angry… this could end my career… the entire $16 billion prostate industry is built on a lie."
- **Invented patients carrying the claims:** "Robert," "Graham" — detailed stories ("slept through the night for the first time in 4 years," "drove 8 hours without stopping").
- **Pseudo-mechanism:** invented "Prostatic Mineral Starvation" + "Sublingual Mineral Bypass."
- **Disease-adjacent escalation the ad would never pass:** "naturally shrinking prostate," "selenium reduced prostate cancer incidence by 63%," "makes tamsulosin and surgery obsolete."
- **Fabricated social proof:** "14,914 verified ratings, 4.9/5," "0.2% refund rate," "over 22,000 men" (note the **internal number inconsistency** 14,914 vs 22,000 — a tell).
- **Fake Facebook-style comments** (styled with like/reply/timestamps — "Sophie Wilson: my husband started these drops…").
- **Manufactured urgency:** "discount expires tonight at midnight… if you close this page the price will be different."
- **Legal cover:** a **medical disclaimer** at the foot ("not intended as medical advice") sitting under a page that is nothing but medical advice.

**Contrast with the reviewed FB ad** (benign, observed): *"Last night, I had to get up four times to use the bathroom… my wife started sleeping in the guest room."* — a relatable story, **no product, no claims, no doctor** → "Learn more." This is the "safe page vs money page" split in one example: the **ad** is clean; the **advertorial** carries everything.

**FTC reality (gen):** the fake-doctor persona, invented testimonials, fabricated review counts and "63% less cancer" claim are exactly what the FTC pursues as **deceptive advertising + unsubstantiated/disease claims**, and the advertiser is liable for claims made via personas/testimonials. The disclaimer does not cure it. This is blackhat — documented here as the competitor archetype, **not** a recommended build.

## 4. ACCOUNT & FUNNEL INFRASTRUCTURE (costs + risk)

### 4.1 — The whitehat / greyhat / blackhat ladder

| Layer | Whitehat (new brand can do) | Greyhat (works, real risk) | Blackhat (deception, high/legal risk) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Positioning** | True compliant reframe; claims you can substantiate | Euphemism for a claim you still imply | Outright false claims |
| **Funnel** | Advertorial clearly labeled, claims match page, honest reviews | Benign ad → hot advertorial (claim escalation), legal disclaimer as cover | **Cloaking**: show reviewer a different page than the user |
| **Accounts** | Your own BM, your own verified account, warmed slowly | **Aged/verified BM bought**, rented **agency account** | Stolen/hacked profiles, identity farms |
| **Personas** | Real founder/clinician, disclosed | Unnamed "wellness" personas | **Fake "Dr. ___"** + fake institute |
| **Reviews/proof** | Real, verified (Loox/Okendo) | "Verified" badges on seeded reviews | Fabricated review counts, fake FB comments |

### 4.2 — What the infrastructure actually costs (observed forum listings + gen; treat as est/unverified)

- **Ad accounts** (with $50→"unlimited" spend limits): **~$15–70/account (observed listings)**.
- **Business Managers:** **~$15–499/BM** by age/level (observed). Example listings: *newly-verified BM with 3 ad accounts, $50 limit ≈ $60*; *1–4-year-old verified BM with a $250-limit account ≈ $210*. Older/higher-limit/"blue-tick" page = more.
- **Personal profiles** (to admin the BMs): **~$5–20/profile (observed)**.
- **Agency / whitelisted ad accounts (rented):** access to an agency-owned BM with higher trust + spend limits, typically **~3% of ad spend (observed seller)**; vendor pieces cite **4–6%** tiers; sometimes + setup fee. You run through *their* account/pixel so your bans don't follow you.
- **Cloaking services:** **$399–$1,999/mo (gen — from Meta's lawsuit vs LeadCloak)**; tools like "Cloaking House" sold similarly.
- **Page/domain rotation:** domains are cheap ($10–15 ea); operators pre-register several and pre-build the advertorial template (e.g., puracienne.com + sovarello.com for the same product).
- **Backup pixels:** multiple pixels/datasets across accounts so a disabled account doesn't kill tracking/learning (gen).

### 4.3 — Critical risk facts (gen, important for the go/no-go)

- **Buying/renting/transferring ad accounts & BMs violates Meta's Terms.** Purchased accounts are frequently restricted or disabled, and the money (and any ad spend on them) can vanish. This is a *rented-at-risk* asset, not an owned one.
- **Reinstatement is unreliable.** The real route is the **Account Quality** appeal; practitioners report **≈ one meaningful appeal**, no guarantee. Crucially, **Meta treats new accounts opened to get around a restriction as "circumvention,"** which *deepens* the ban. That is precisely *why* scaled operators keep *separate* aged BMs on different identities/payment rails rather than re-appealing — not because reinstatement works, but because it usually doesn't.
- **Cloaking** = showing Meta's reviewer/bots a compliant "white/safe page" while real users get the "black/money page." It is a **direct ToS violation**, Meta litigates it (the LeadCloak suit cited diet pills, crypto scams, misinformation), uses AI + human review to detect it, and depending on the offer it edges into **deceptive-advertising/fraud** territory. Highest risk tier; used by throwaway operators who expect to be banned anyway. **Not viable or advisable for a brand that wants to build an asset.**

## 6. CREATIVE TACTICS (observed)

- **Benign thumbnails / hooks:** the reviewed ad is a story, a question, or an ingredient fact — never the claim. ("Last night I got up four times…"; "Cranberry isn't just for UTIs 🍒"; "I added this red jelly to my routine.")
- **First-person / third-person, never second-person "you have [condition]":** dodges the Personal Attributes policy. ("Millions of men…"; "I was peeing 5x a night" — not "Do YOU pee at night?").
- **Structure/function hedging:** "traditionally used to support," "studied for," "supports healthy ___ function" — never "treats/cures/shrinks."
- **Emoji / icon substitution** to carry suggestive meaning the text can't: 🍆 (prostate/ED), 🍒, 💧, 🔥.
- **Deliberate misspelling / character swaps** to beat automated text classifiers: **"arous@l,"** spacing, homoglyphs, putting the hot word only in the image. (Short-lived; flags the account once human review catches it — a burn tactic, not a durable one.)
- **No before/after, no pinching fat, no "ideal body"** imagery (explicit Meta weight-loss prohibitions).
- **Claim displacement to the page:** keep the ad clean; let the advertorial, the testimonials, and the "doctor" carry the claims.
- **Asterisk disclaimers** on survey/claim lines ("*based on a survey of customers"; "results vary") as thin legal cover.
- **Volume as insurance:** 100s of active variants so individual rejections don't stop delivery (Hygiene Hero 387, Lovy 877, Mars Men 3,390).

## 7. WHAT A NEW LOW-BUDGET BRAND CAN REALISTICALLY DO — tiering

Ranked by (cost, skill, ban-risk). **The honest headline:** a new low-budget brand cannot out-infrastructure or out-volume incumbents, and the greyhat/blackhat layer is both unaffordable *and* an asset-destroying liability for someone trying to build a real brand. The winnable game is **whitehat positioning + channel selection + owned-audience back-end.**

### START-HERE — cheap, whitehat, do now (low cost / low-moderate skill / low ban-risk)
- **Pick the compliant reframe and actually build the product/brand around it** (not a euphemism for a banned claim): "intimate/menopause comfort serum," "prostate/urinary support," "gut-reset/debloat," "testosterone/cortisol or metabolism support." Make the claim one you can substantiate.
- **Run your OWN warmed BM + one verified ad account.** Spend small, ramp slowly, keep claim↔landing-page↔price consistent. Low ban-risk because you're not fighting policy.
- **Advertorial/listicle bridge page done honestly:** real story, real reviews (Loox/Okendo), clear "advertorial/sponsored" label, structure-function language, disclaimers. You get the pre-sell lift without the deception.
- **Lead with owned channels:** email/SMS + subscribe-&-save on a consumable. This is where margin and LTV live and there's no reviewer.
- **TikTok organic + UGC creators**, and **Amazon/retail listing**, for the categories Meta won't take.
- **18+ targeting, third/first-person copy, no before/after, no "you have ___."**

### INTERMEDIATE — some cost / more skill / moderate ban-risk
- **2–3 FB pages + a small stable of creatives (20–50+)** so rejections don't stop delivery; disciplined page/domain hygiene (not evasion — redundancy).
- **Native (Taboola/Outbrain) advertorials** (properly labeled) and **YouTube VSLs** for prostate/weight-loss long-form; **Google Search** for high-intent prostate/urinary.
- **Influencer whitelisting** so creators carry the testimonial (with #ad disclosure).
- **ClickBank/affiliate** to let others run the aggressive angle at their risk (understand FTC still reaches you).
- **A second owned ad account/backup pixel for continuity** (your own, not purchased).
- Skill required: real compliance literacy, creative volume, funnel/CRO, substantiation file.

### ADVANCED — expensive / specialist / high ban-risk (scale only, mostly NOT for a new low-budget brand)
- **Bought aged/verified BMs ($60–500+ ea), rented agency accounts (~3% of spend)** — buys trust/limits but violates ToS and is rented-at-risk.
- **Heavy page/domain/persona rotation, burner "Dr." pages, backup-pixel webs, reinstatement workflows** — the infrastructure of operators who *expect* weekly bans.
- **Cloaking ($400–2,000/mo), fake-doctor advertorials, fabricated proof** — blackhat; direct ToS violation, FTC/FDA exposure, litigated by Meta. Documented as the competitor reality; **do not build a brand on it.**

### Honest per-category verdict for a NEW low-budget brand

| Category | Whitehat-only viable on a small budget? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Weight-loss** | ⚠️ **Only via full reframe.** As "weight loss," no. As a **testosterone/cortisol, bloating, or metabolism** product with honest claims — **yes, this is the most replicable** (Mars Men proves the reframe is the moat, and it's a legitimate supplement angle). Hardest-blocked on TikTok. | **Enter via reframe; never as "weight loss."** |
| **Prostate / urinary** | ✅ **Yes — the friendliest of the four to start whitehat.** Strong real demand (15k+ ads), structure-function "support healthy urinary flow" is a genuinely allowed lane, high-intent on Google/YouTube/native. Avoid the fake-doctor/"shrink" escalation. | **Best whitehat entry.** (Fling "Pee Easy" is the clean template.) |
| **Arousal / intimate** | ⚠️ **Conditional.** The *menopause/dryness "intimate skin/comfort serum"* lane is allowed and there's a real **trust gap** (incumbents are 2.5★), but incumbents out-volume you and TikTok paid is closed. Winnable on **trust + CX + owned channels**, not ad volume. | **Enter only with a trust-led, health-framed angle + email/Amazon backbone.** |
| **Parasite / detox** | ❌ **Not as "parasite/detox."** Saturated, $100M-scale incumbents, brutal Meta/TikTok enforcement, needs greyhat infra you can't afford. **Only** viable whitehat as a **de-clawed "gut-reset/bloating" brand** or a **pet** cleanse (defensible claims, less saturation). | **Skip the clone; enter only via gut-reset/bloating or pet angle.** |

**Bottom line:** With a small budget and whitehat-only, **prostate/urinary is the cleanest entry; weight-loss works only if you reframe it into a hormone/metabolism supplement; arousal is a trust/execution play in the menopause-skincare lane; parasite/detox is effectively closed unless you abandon the "parasite" framing.** None of the four should be attempted as a Meta-only, ad-volume game — the owned-audience/subscription back-end and multi-channel (native/Google/YouTube/Amazon/email) spread are what make a low-budget entry survivable, because they remove the single point of failure (one ad account) that the whole greyhat infrastructure exists to work around.
