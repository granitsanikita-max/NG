Start here

The 20-second versionIf you only read one page, read this.

A standard blood panel checks hemoglobin, the iron in her blood today. It usually does not check her storage iron unless you ask for it by name. So you ask for it by name. Here's the whole ask:

You say
"I'd like her to have a serum ferritin test, please. I know her blood count can look normal while her iron stores are low, and I want to see the actual number."

That's it. You're not demanding, you're not arguing, you're asking for one specific, reasonable test. Most of the time, that sentence is all it takes.

The one word that does the work

ferritin

Not "an iron test" (too vague, they'll run a blood count). Not "is she anemic" (that's a different question). The storage number is serum ferritin. Say that word and you've already done the hard part.

Bonus line if she has heavy periods or plays a sport: "She has heavy periods and trains most days, so I'd especially like to know her ferritin, not just her blood count."

The details

Exactly what to ask forGet the right test, not just "a blood test."

If you want to be precise (some parents like to be), here's what to request and why each part matters.

  • Serum ferritin — the main event. This is her iron storage number. Without it, you're flying blind.
  • A full blood count (CBC) — fine to include. It shows hemoglobin and whether she's anemic. Just know it is not the ferritin number on its own.
  • Optional: CRP or inflammation marker — ferritin can read falsely high if she's fighting an infection. If she's unwell that week, it's reasonable to recheck when she's better, or test this alongside.

Why "iron test" isn't enough

Ask vaguely for "an iron test" and you'll often get a blood count that measures the cash in her pocket, not the savings account. That report comes back "normal," everyone relaxes, and the storage number, the one that was actually low, was never looked at. The word ferritin is what closes that gap.

You can even write it on a sticky note and hand it over: "Please check: serum ferritin." No one will think less of you. Clinics see this every day.

3
If you get pushback

The shrug, handledCalm words for the three things you might hear.

You are not anti-doctor. You're anti-shrug. These lines keep it respectful and still get you the number. Say them warmly, you're on the same team.

If they say: "Her bloodwork is normal."

You say
"I understand her blood count is normal. That checks hemoglobin, not her iron stores. Can we add a ferritin so we can see the storage number too?"

If they say: "She's just a teenager / it's her age."

You say
"Maybe. But she bleeds every month and she's still growing, and that's exactly when iron runs low. I'd feel better knowing her number before we call it her age."

If they say: "We don't usually test that."

"I know it's not routine yet, but the newer guidance leans toward checking a girl's iron by around 14. I'd like to do that one check now. If it's fine, we'll both be reassured."

If a clinic still won't order it, you can usually request a direct serum ferritin test through a lab yourself for a modest fee. You don't have to escalate or argue. You have options.

The results

Reading it togetherYou got the number. Now what?

Write her ferritin value down (put it on Her Page). Then use this as a plain-English map to talk through with her doctor. These are guides for a conversation, never a diagnosis you make alone.

under
~15

Very low, or anemic

This is a doctor's call, and usually a doctor's dose, which is much higher than a daily top-up. Do not try to fix this with a gentle supplement. Ask her doctor about treatment and a recheck plan. This is the honest "see them, not us" zone.

~15
to 30

Low-normal, "fine on paper"

The classic gap. A gentle daily top-up and a retest around 8 to 12 weeks fits here. Write this number down as her "before." This is where a daily strip does its best work.

~30
to 50+

Okay (but athletes aim higher)

Comfortable for many girls. If she trains hard or has heavy periods, many clinicians like to see her nearer 50. Keep an eye on symptoms and recheck if they return.

One question to ask the doctor out loud

"What's her actual ferritin number, and what would you want it to be for a girl her age who has periods and plays sport?" That one question turns a vague "she's fine" into a real target you can work toward.
