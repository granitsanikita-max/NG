## ⚑ THE DISPLAY RULE (this is what was broken — get it exactly right)
**The only per-tin / per-day numbers on the page are the SUBSCRIPTION STEADY RATE.** First-order prices are shown as a flat **"today you pay $X (save $Y)"** with **NO per-day attached.**
- Why: a cheap single-tin intro ($29 for 1 tin) will always *look* cheaper per-day than a 3-tin box — if you show per-day on intro prices, the 30-Day visually beats the 90-Day and the funnel inverts (the bug in v5).
- Fix: show per-day ONLY on the steady rate, where the ladder is clean and the 90-Day always wins. The intro discount is a separate sweetener, not a per-unit number.

## THE ARCHITECTURE — why this converts (deep analysis)
**Two different buyers, two different jobs:**
- The **value buyer** compares per-tin/per-day → the 90-Day wins every line → she takes the hero.
- The **hesitant buyer** looks at "least money today" → the 30-Day ($29) catches her instead of losing her → and she's now on a sub we can upgrade to 90-Day post-purchase.
- Nobody rational picks the one-time (worst value + pays shipping) — it exists only to make the subs look smart.

**The three levers that push the 90-Day without weakening the 30-Day:**
1. **Per-unit framing** (90-Day cheapest per tin/day) — the value argument.
2. **The mechanism** ("3 tins gets her to the retest") — makes 90 days the *correct* amount, not just the cheaper one. The 30-Day can't own this (1 tin doesn't reach a retest).
3. **Decoy + default** — one-time anchors high, 90-Day is pre-selected + "MOST POPULAR."

**What we deliberately did NOT do:** discount the 30-Day's *per-day* below the 90-Day (the v5 bug). The 30-Day's discount is a flat dollar sweetener ($29 today), shown without a per-day, so it never visually out-values the hero.

**Margin ladder (first order → recurring):** 90-Day $40 → $50/qtr · 30-Day $10 → $17/mo · one-time $25. Break-even CPA on the hero ≈ **$40**. Lever: 90-Day first box $89→$99 (no intro discount) lifts gross to ~$50 / CPA ~$50 if cold CPA runs high.
