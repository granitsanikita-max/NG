# IRYN - Step 4: THE 9 STATIC SCRIPTS (image ads)

**Framework used for all 9:** Notion `Reference - Copywriting Fundamentals`, every step in order (③ analyse the goal, ④ fundamentals active in every copy, ⑤ techniques picked per goal, ⑥ chief it). Statics use the Copywriting doc (videos use Ad Fundamentals, separate file).

## The one belief chain every static marches to (North Star + the 6 necessary beliefs)
Before a word was written I locked the 6 "I believe that..." statements Jen must accept to buy (Copywriting ⑦ Necessary Beliefs). Every line below moves her down this chain and no further:
1. I believe my daughter's exhaustion might not just be "being a teenager."
2. I believe a normal blood test can miss the real problem, because ferritin is a separate number most panels never run.
3. I believe low iron stores (the ferritin gap) is a common, specific, fixable cause.
4. I believe a gentle daily strip will actually get into her, unlike the pills she quits.
5. I believe IRYN specifically is the right one (right dose, made for teen girls, guaranteed).
6. I believe trying it now is low-risk (70-day money-back, cancel anytime).
**North Star (the ONE belief that unlocks the buy):** *"Her blood test was normal, but it never checked the number that was actually low, and there's a gentle way to fix it."*

## Sophistication read (④/③C) applied to every static
Market stage for teen-girl iron aimed at the mum = **Stage 3 to 4 (claim/mechanism war)** in the broad iron category, but **Stage 1 to 2 for the exact teen-ferritin-gap angle** (almost nobody runs it). So the play across all 9: **lead with the NEW MECHANISM (the ferritin gap), stated plainly**, because the mechanism itself is still fresh for this avatar. We do not out-claim ("more iron"); we re-frame ("the test checked the wrong number").

## The swiped Native Advertorial structure (rebuilt from the 3 competitor winners)
I pulled the full copy of three long-running mum-health advertorials and reverse-engineered the shared skeleton. Lengths ran 800 to 1,500 words. Common DNA, in order:
1. **Confession / curiosity hook** (first line = a 3 to 6 word vacuum): a dramatic admission or a result gap ("My son potty trained in 3 days, his sister took a year").
2. **Origin + symptom escalation with a timeline** ("it started two years ago... at first... then every month... now all the time").
3. **Identity loss** ("I look in the mirror and don't see myself").
4. **The failed-solution stack** ("I tried everything... spent hundreds... none of it worked. Because the problem isn't on the surface").
5. **The authority moment** (a doctor, an OT, a nutritionist, usually found by "doom-scrolling at 2am"), and rejecting the easy medical answer with a real reason.
6. **UMP, the mechanism of the PROBLEM** ("here's what nobody tells you: it isn't about X, it's about Y") + a "that's why..." line that anchors it to something she already sees.
7. **The villain** ("the system just suppresses it" / "brands sell half-solutions at full price").
8. **UMS, the mechanism of the SOLUTION** (the 2 to 3 specific things the fix does, why it is different).
9. **Brand + specs + proof** (dose, made-for, guarantee, real reviews).
10. **Results timeline with specific weeks** ("week 2 nothing, week 3 I noticed, by week 10...").
11. **Direct address to the reader** ("if you're reading this because...") + identity restored + **soft, almost reluctant CTA** ("I just wanted you to know it exists").
Rules observed: first-person mum narrator (a persona is standard and allowed), no fabricated named doctor presented as real, no invented clinical numbers, real published guidance only, honest flaws raised and flipped, 5th to 8th grade, stop-slop voice, no em-dashes.

# FORMAT 1 - NATIVE ADVERTORIAL (3 variants)
Surface = Pre-Sales / native ad copy (③A). Reader = cold, guard up. Lean hardest on story, curiosity and contrast, belief change, native editorial voice. Image = candid, un-branded phone snap (not a product shot). Primary text = the article below.

## STATIC 1 - Native Advertorial - Variant A (Angle C: the normal bloodwork)
**Per-ad plan:** Awareness = Unaware to Problem-aware · enters on a relatable confession · Technique stack: "I was just like you," Necessary Beliefs march, UMP then UMS, that's-why evidence, anticipate-and-resolve, let-them-conclude, honesty, soft urgency.
**Headline / hook (3 to 6 word vacuum):** **Her blood test said she was fine.**
**Body:**
Her blood test said she was fine. Twice. And I believed it, right up until I stopped.

For about a year I told my daughter the same things every mum tells her. Go to bed earlier. Eat something green. You're a teenager, this is normal. She was wiped out by the afternoon. Falling asleep on the couch before dinner. Pale in every photo. Snapping at everyone, then crying that she didn't know why.

So I did the responsible thing. I took her to the GP. They ran her bloods. The results came back "normal." I felt relief and confusion at the same time, because nothing about her was normal.

A few months later we went back. Same tiredness. Same "her bloodwork is fine." They talked about her age, her screen time, maybe her mood. We walked out with nothing.

Here is the part nobody told me, and it took a nurse friend to say it out loud. A standard blood count checks her haemoglobin. That is the iron moving in her blood right now. It does not check her ferritin. Ferritin is the iron she has in storage. Think of it like this: the standard test checks her spending money, and it looked fine. Nobody checked her savings account, which was nearly empty.

That is why she could be "normal" on paper and still running on fumes. The fuel in the tank reads okay. The reserve tank is dry.

And this is not rare. Around 40% of teenage girls are low in iron, and most are not anaemic, which is exactly why the usual test waves them through. In 2026 the guidance actually changed: paediatric experts now say check a girl's ferritin by age 14, because so many were being missed.

I was not missing effort from my daughter. I was missing a number nobody ran.

When we finally had her ferritin checked, it was low. Not dangerous, just empty. The doctor said topping up iron stores is simple, the hard part is getting a teenager to actually take iron for months. Tablets upset her stomach and she quit them in a week. Gummies were sugar. Nothing stuck.

What stuck was a small dissolvable strip called IRYN. One a day, raspberry, it melts on her tongue, no pills to fight over. It is made for teen girls at a sensible daily amount, not a horse-pill dose. She actually takes it, which is the whole game, because the best iron in the world does nothing in the drawer.

I will be honest about the one thing. If your daughter's iron is very low or she is already anaemic, this is a doctor's-dose conversation first, not a strip. For the huge group in the middle, the ones who keep coming back "normal," a daily top-up is the thing we were never offered.

I am not going to tell you her life changed in a week. Week two I noticed she wasn't asleep by five. By about week six she was back at the dinner table talking, not melting into the couch. We will retest her ferritin at the 90 day mark, which is the honest way to know it is working, a number, not a vibe.

If you are reading this because your girl keeps coming back "fine" and you know she isn't, get her ferritin checked by name. Not just a blood count. The actual storage number. It is one line to add to the form.

IRYN gives you a 70 day money-back guarantee and you can cancel anytime, which is the only reason I was willing to try one more thing after a year of being told she was normal. I just wish someone had told me which number to ask for a lot sooner.
