## MY PERSONAL QA PASS (read this first)

I went through all 15 myself, line by line. Verdict: **all 15 are usable and genuinely deep on the ICP.** They don't read like supplement ads. They read like one worried mum talking to another ("I spent a year grieving a daughter who was still in the house," "nodding along in that parking lot thinking maybe I was the crazy one," "I had to leave the room so she wouldn't see me cry"). Every one runs the same belief chain to the same North Star, so whichever angle wins, the landing page and the rest of the funnel still line up.

**What every one of the 15 does (checked, not assumed):**
- **ICP depth:** opens on a moment Jen (or the sport mum) has actually lived, uses her words, and hits the 4 feelings — guilt ("I got frustrated with her"), relief/absolution ("it was never her"), vindication ("you weren't imagining it"), and control ("one line to add to the form"). Not generic.
- **Mechanism before solution:** the Ferritin Gap (spending money = blood count / savings = ferritin, savings empty first in teen girls) lands BEFORE the strip, every time. UMP → UMS, no exceptions.
- **Proof policy held:** real published guidance only (AAP 2026, ASH 2026, ~40% low, 83.6% not anaemic, Cleveland Clinic's teen-athlete 50+). No fabricated reviews, no invented customer ferritin numbers shown as real labs, no fake doctors, no infusion footage. Persona mum stories say "it came back low, not dangerous, just empty" with no number.
- **Honesty caveat in all 15:** very low or already anaemic = a doctor's dose first, not us.
- **Offer exact + price off the creative:** 70-day money-back, cancel any time, raspberry strip, one a day, made for her years. No price, no currency — sidesteps the open AUD-vs-USD buy-box decision. No free-shipping claim (subs-only).
- **Write-like-a-human:** body copy swept for em/en-dashes, "not just X it's Y," rhythm triads, semicolons. Clean.
- **Message match:** each ad names the exact landing-page hero it continues (modular hero per homepage-architecture).

**The one fix I made:** the sport video's internal notes named a specific Cleveland Clinic doctor. I verified the *claim* is real and published ([Cleveland Clinic Consult QD, "Recognizing Iron Deficiency in Teen Athletes"](https://consultqd.clevelandclinic.org/recognizing-iron-deficiency-in-teen-athletes): "many clinicians aim for ferritin levels of at least 50 ng/mL," normal range 15–200), but I could not verify the personal name, so I stripped it. On-screen and in VO it's attributed to **Cleveland Clinic** only. Rule going forward: no unverified doctor name ever goes on a kids'-health creative.

**One open flag (not a blocker):** brief targets US, store is AUD-only / buy box not multi-currency. Resolved by keeping all prices off every creative. If any future version shows a price, settle the currency decision first.
