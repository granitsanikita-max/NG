# PART 0 - THE ONE-PARAGRAPH TAKEAWAY

The whole iron/ferritin category is being won by **one story**: *"Her/my bloodwork came back normal, but something was still wrong, and the one number nobody checked was ferritin."* It runs for 300 to 420 days on persona/advertorial pages (Sophia Byrd, Flora, Jessica Harrison), on founder videos (Bio Blends), on kid-iron education (Love Ya Guts, 417 days), and on the exact same dissolvable strip we sell (Cavaé, 163 days). **Every single winner is aimed one avatar away from us**: the adult woman herself, or a little kid. **Not one brand runs it as "the mum of a teen girl, about her daughter's heavy periods or her sport season."** That is our entire opening. We do not need to invent a new ad. We take the proven "normal bloodwork, ferritin gap, gentle iron she'll actually take" machine and we are the first to point it at Jen about Maddie. Lead with heavy periods, sport, and the 2026 guidance. Never lead with "tired."

## 1B. The winning FORMATS (what to actually make)

**Images / statics win on longevity. This is the cheapest, most durable vehicle and it is where the longest runners live.**

1. **Long-copy advertorial static (persona/"blog" style).** THE longevity king: 300 to 420 days (Sophia Byrd, Flora, Jessica Harrison, Emily Jackson). A first-person story told as text over one plain image or a lab-slip. This is the proven cold vehicle in iron, not UGC.
2. **Before/after NUMBER static.** A ferritin number going up is the recurring hero creative (Cavaé "22 to 58 in 13 weeks," "12 to 61"). The number IS the creative.
3. **Lab-slip-as-hero static.** "Hemoglobin: normal. Ferritin: 9." (Nobody runs this cleanly yet. See Part 3.)
4. **Benefit-stack / emoji static.** Needed's longest runners (180 to 228d): a headline + 3 clean benefits. Cheap to test, months of life.
5. **Us-vs-them static with an honest footnote.** Needed "Not all prenatals are created equal... 8x more*" with an asterisk methodology (Meta-safe, persuasive).
6. **Authority / stat carousel.** Organic Burst (WHO stat), Cavaé ("iron and ferritin are two different tests, ask for ferritin by name"). Great for the doctor-script format.

**Videos (secondary for cold iron, but the scalers):**

7. **First-person story VSL from an "insider who sees many girls."** Chewy's front-office-lady (the single best teen-to-mum template). The POV (school nurse, XC coach) gives authority with no fake doctor and opens a curiosity loop.
8. **Clinician / expert talking-head reveal.** AdvancedTrichology (159d, TikTok): "Has anyone tested your ferritin? Without fail, the answer is no." The fastest path to Angle C.
9. **Founder origin video.** Bio Blends: "Five years, dozens of trials... iron was the hardest." Builds trust via the build story.
10. **Relatable-mum UGC + mechanism.** First Day "I'm 4'11, I wish someone told me at 13." Regret hook + hard mechanism + deadline. This is First Day's hardest-scaling format.
11. **Raw compliance demo.** NovaFerrum's 53d clip of a kid happily taking the drop ("More? Good job!"). Kills the "will she take it?" objection on camera.
12. **Before/after testimonial with a concrete timeframe.** PurePurc (478d), "in 3 weeks." Highest raw engagement. (We use seeded REAL mums only, never fabricated numbers.)

## 1D. How they actually RUN the ads (mechanics worth copying / avoiding)

- **Persona / "blog" pages** host the advertorials (fake first-name FB pages). They decouple the ad from the brand and read as editorial. Powerful, but for a kids-health brand we keep a real brand voice and real-mum stories (no fabricated personas presented as real customers - research + our policy).
- **Affiliate / whitelisted creator networks:** Hiya barely runs its own page; it lives on "mom" creator pages (Scary Mommy = 497 active ads). A scale lever for later, not the $1,500 test.
- **DCO + duplicate testing:** winners get duplicated and run as DCO to find audiences. Normal.
- **Spanish localization** (First Day) once a concept wins. A later lever.
- **Discount codes + "BACK IN STOCK" scarcity** (Ferosom WELCOME15, Bio Blends) = middle-of-funnel. **We model their hooks, not their discount mechanics, for a cold launch.** Our cold ads sell the belief, not the deal.

# PART 4 - THE IRYN TOP-OF-FUNNEL AD PLAN

**Audience:** broad US women 35 to 55 on Advantage+; the creative does the targeting (research §2, §7). Mum is the buyer; we talk ABOUT the daughter, never TO her.
**Job of every cold ad:** move her through belief-chain links 1 to 3 only (her periods/sport/growth could be draining her iron → a normal blood count misses it, ferritin is the number → the 2026 guidance says know her number). The offer is NOT the cold ad's job; the advertorial and PDP carry 4 to 6.
**Three angles** (research-locked): **A = heavy periods**, **B = sport**, **C = "normal" bloodwork + 2026 news**.
**Mechanism in every ad:** the Ferritin Gap (blood count = her spending money, ferritin = her savings). Test → Top Up → Retest.
**Proof we're allowed:** real published experts (AAP 2026 "check her ferritin by 14," ASH 2026 "30 or under is low, 50 with heavy periods/sport," Dr Weyand's 38.6% / "most have no idea," Dr Ferri on athletes aiming for 50), real seeded mum lab-number UGC. **Never** a fabricated named doctor, fake review count, or invented ferritin number.
**Voice:** the mum who figured it out. US English. No em-dashes. Say the hard part first ("under 15 or anemic is a doctor's dose").

# PART 5 - GUARDRAILS (so we never freeze an ad account)

- **No fabricated named doctors** presented as real people for this kids-health product. Use real published experts (AAP/ASH 2026, Weyand, Powers, Ferri) quoted from their real outlets, and real seeded mum UGC. AI spokespeople as brand educators are fine; a fake "Dr. Smith" is not.
- **No invented ferritin numbers, fake review counts, or fake clinical results.** Before/after numbers run only when a real seeded result exists. (Cavaé's mismatched proof counts are a liability we avoid.)
- **Say the ceiling out loud:** "under 15 or anemic is a doctor's dose." It wins the researcher-mum and pre-empts the "did nothing" complaint.
- **Never** lead with "tired/energy" (no ferritin-fatigue link in the 2026 study; lead with periods, sport, the number). Never use "moody/drama/hormonal/lazy" except to reject them. Never "bypass the gut," "absorbs better," "gives her energy back."
- **No em-dashes** and no AI tells in any ad, script, or page. US English only.
- **Discounts/scarcity are middle-of-funnel.** Cold ads sell the belief and the number, not a code.

# APPENDIX - the numbers an ad can cite (all from research.md, sourced)
- 38.6% of US girls 12 to 21 are iron deficient; 83.6% of them are NOT anemic (so a normal blood count misses them). PMID 37367984.
- A standard blood count caught only 41.4% of low iron in heavy-period teens. PMID 27262832.
- Heavy periods roughly triple the odds of deficiency; 50.9% of girls referred for heavy periods had ferritin under 20. PMID 41335582 / 27262832.
- 2026: AAP recommends a ferritin check about a year after her first period and by 14; ASH calls 30 or under low (50 with heavy periods/sport).
- Gentle dose vs big dose: far fewer stomach complaints (7% vs 87% in trial). PMID 42124000.
- Her iron need nearly doubles at 14 (8 to 15 mg). NIH ODS.
