## MECHANISM SECTION (A FIRE DRILL, NOT A SMOKE ALARM BLOCK)

Headline
It's not a hearing problem. It's a practice problem.

Subhead
They're not lazy. Their brain has never practised waking up to their bladder. The alarm trains it, one wet night at a time.

Step 1
Their bladder sends the signal. They don't wake.
Their arousal threshold is too high. They sleep too deep to feel it. So they wet.
(In a sleep study, kids who wet the bed woke to sounds 9.3% of the time. Non-wetters woke 39.7%. Their brain isn't broken. Just untrained.)

Step 2
The alarm pairs "bladder full" with "wake up."
When they leak, the alarm fires. They get up. Their brain starts linking the two. Same way your own brain wakes you to pee at night. Theirs will do it too.

Step 3
Their brain does it alone.
After enough reps, the pairing locks in. They wake before the alarm even fires. They're dry on their own.

## FAQ SECTION (QUESTIONS)

Section title
Questions

1. What if my kid sleeps through everything?
Probably on night 1. By design. The reason your kid wets isn't that they can't hear. Their brain hasn't practised waking to their bladder. First 3 nights, you walk them up. By week 2, they're waking before the alarm.

2. Will it go off when they're just sweaty?
No. The sensor fires on urine, not sweat. Skin moisture doesn't trigger it.

3. Does it miss the pee like our last alarm did?
No. The clip sits on the outside front of their underwear where the first drops hit. It fires within seconds. If your last alarm was a bed mat or a cord-based chest clip, that's usually what caused the miss.

4. What if the clip falls off at night?
It locks to the waistband of regular underwear. If it ever does come loose, the alarm goes silent, which tells you to check. Hasn't been an issue for our customers.

5. Will it wake their siblings?
Switch to vibration mode. Only they feel it. Add the parent receiver and the kids' room stays quiet all night.

6. Does it use Wi-Fi? Is it safe?
No Wi-Fi. No Bluetooth. Just a short-range signal that fires when moisture is detected. Lower power than your phone. Safe for any age.

7. Does it work with pullups?
No. Pullups block the sensor. Your kid needs regular underwear for the plan to work. That's part of the training, not a workaround.

8. How do I clean it?
Wipe the clip with a disinfecting wipe. The housing is waterproof, so no soaking or disassembly needed.

9. Do I have to charge it every night?
No. Full charge in under an hour, lasts 3 to 5 nights.

10. How long before we see anything change?
Smaller wet patches by week 2. First dry streak by week 4 to 6 for most kids. Full dry nights by week 6 to 8.

11. What age does it work for?
Ages 5 and up. Works for any kid with a waistband to clip to. Doctors' guidelines list alarms as the first-line option from age 5.

12. Can they wear it when they're away from home?
No. It's a home training tool for the weeks before. Once they're dry, they don't need the alarm anywhere.

13. Can't we just use the pill or wait?
The pill masks the symptom. 7 out of 10 kids relapse when they stop taking it. Waiting only resolves 15% of kids a year. The alarm trains them so they don't need either.

14. What if it comes back?
Up to half of kids relapse once. If that happens, clip the alarm back on. It works faster the second time.

15. What if it breaks or doesn't help us?
Full refund for 100 nights. Keep the kit. Keep the plan. No return needed.
