## 0. BOTTOM LINE (decision lines)
1. **UK — it's a medical device the moment we make a medical claim (and very likely by its mode of action anyway).** A device that repositions the jaw = "modification of the anatomy or of a physiological process" under UK MDR 2002 [R3]. To say ANY health/medical claim (snoring relief, "opens your airway", sleep apnoea) in a UK ad, CAP 12.1 requires "a medical device with the applicable conformity marking" [R5] — i.e. CE or UKCA marked AND registered with MHRA, with a UK Responsible Person if the manufacturer is outside the UK [R2]. The ASA wrote a snoring-specific note saying exactly this [R4]. **Blocker: confirm the supplier unit has a valid CE/UKCA Class I declaration + technical file and get it MHRA-registered via a UKRP before UK launch.** Without it, our ads can't make the claims the mechanism depends on.
2. **AU — needs ARTG inclusion via an Australian sponsor before supply.** Mandible-repositioning antisnoring orthoses are on the ARTG as **Class 1** medical devices [R8]; "medical devices must be included on the ARTG before they can be supplied in Australia" [R9]; advertising for an indication not in the ARTG entry is an offence [R10]. Overseas drop-ship to consumers relying on the Personal Importation Scheme does not cover a business advertising to Australians (INFERENCE — scheme conditions are about the individual importer [R9]). **Blocker for AU.**
3. **US — Class II, 510(k) required (21 CFR 872.5570; product codes LRK "Device, Anti-Snoring" and LQZ "Device, Jaw Repositioning")** [R11][R12]. OTC clearance is achievable and common: VitalSleep (K201719), ZQuiet (K180124), SnoreRx (K170825) and several Chinese boil-and-bite makers (e.g. K250028, K251628, K262104) are cleared OTC "as an aid in the reduction of snoring for adults at least 18 years old" [R13]. **Path: source from a manufacturer that already holds a 510(k) for that exact device; never sell an uncleared unit in the US.**
4. **Claims we can make (once conformity-marked/registered):** "aid in the reduction of snoring", "holds the lower jaw forward to help keep the airway open" (mirrors FDA indications/device descriptions [R13]). **Never:** "cure/stop snoring forever", "treats sleep apnoea" (UK CAP 12.2 — condition needing medical supervision [R6]; US — OTC clearances are snoring-only), "FDA approved", "MHRA approved/certified" [R6], "NHS/NICE recommended" (NICE ranks boil-and-bite last [science.md S46]).
5. **Meta:** no ad can "assert or imply" she has a health condition — ❌ "Do you snore?" / "Your snoring…" style hooks; ✅ "1 in 4 women snore" / "Snoring mouthpiece for women" [R15]. Health & Wellness policy: 18+ targeting and no clickbait time-bound promises without qualifiers [R14].

## 1. UNITED KINGDOM (launch market)

### 1a. Is it a medical device? (UK MDR 2002 definition)
- CAP quoting the Regulations: medical device = "an instrument, apparatus, appliance, material or other article … which is intended by the manufacturer to be used for human beings for the purposes of: diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, treatment or alleviation of disease; diagnosis, monitoring, treatment, alleviation of or compensation for an injury or handicap; investigation, replacement or modification of the anatomy or of a physiological process; or control or conception; and which does not achieve its principal intended action in or on the human body by pharmacological, immunological or metabolic means" [R5] VERBATIM
- MHRA borderlines guidance (updated 29 June 2026): "Whether or not a product is considered to have a 'medical purpose' will be defined by the manufacturer's intention for the product as defined in their labelling, instructions for use and promotional material and its mode of action in conjunction with the definition of a medical device as stated in the UK MDR 2002." [R3] VERBATIM. The guidance does NOT list snoring devices either way (searched full text — no "snor" hit) → status turns on intended purpose + claims. INFERENCE.
- ASA/CAP snoring-specific advice ("Not-so-silent night? – Snoring aids and the Ad Codes", 26 Apr 2019): "Depending on how your anti-snoring product works, or the claims you make, it could be a medical device as defined by The Medical Devices Regulations 2002 or a medicine. Both require certification. Medicines need to be licensed by the MHRA and medical devices need to be CE Marked. If your product is a medical device, failure to achieve a CE device certification will mean any claims you make about it could be in breach of the CAP Code." [R4] VERBATIM
- Market practice: UK MAD sellers present them as Class I medical devices (e.g. Tomed SomnoGuard listed by NowPatient as registered with MHRA as Class I [SNIPPET, R7]; NICE NG202 treats mandibular advancement splints as clinical treatments [science.md S45]). BSSAA sells MADs openly [science.md S31].
- **Decision:** treat the product as a **Class I general medical device** in GB. (Class I non-sterile, non-measuring = self-declaration: "manufacturers of non-sterile and non-measuring Class I devices and general IVDs can self-certify against the UKCA marking" [R2] VERBATIM.)

### 1b. What's needed to place it on the GB market
- MHRA "Regulating medical devices in the UK" (last updated 20 Feb 2026) [R2] VERBATIM:
  - "All medical devices, including IVDs, custom-made devices and systems or procedure packs must be registered with the MHRA before being placed on the Great Britain market."
  - "if you are a medical device manufacturer based outside the UK and wish to place a device on the Great Britain market, you need to appoint a single UK responsible person for all of your devices, who will act on your behalf to carry out specified tasks, such as registration"
  - "The MHRA will only accept registration of devices from manufacturers where the manufacturer is based in the UK. If the manufacturer is based outside the UK, they must appoint a UK [Responsible Person]…"
  - CE acceptance: "general medical devices … compliant with the EU medical devices regulation (EU MDR) … can be placed on the Great Britain market up until 30 June 2030"; MDD-compliant devices with valid declaration "until the sooner of expiry of certificate or 30 June 2028"; "The government has announced that it will consult on indefinite recognition of CE marked medical devices."
  - Fee note: "The standard fee per application has changed to £240" (8 Feb 2024 update)
- **Implication for an AliExpress/CJ unit (INFERENCE):** a generic factory unit typically has no EU MDR declaration/technical file, no UKRP, no MHRA registration. Options: (a) find a supplier that is already CE (EU MDR) or UKCA Class I and MHRA-registered via a UKRP (ask supplier for Declaration of Conformity + MHRA PARD/registration number); (b) become the "manufacturer" under our own brand (private label) → we own the technical file, risk assessment (ISO 14971), biocompatibility (ISO 10993 for intraoral EVA), labelling/IFU, PMS, and appoint a UKRP if Nikita's entity is outside the UK. Either way: **no medical claims in UK ads until this exists.**
- Labelling: "manufacturers should not state or imply that their device has been 'approved' or 'certified' by the MHRA." [R6] VERBATIM

### 1c. UK advertising rules (CAP Code section 12, ASA)
- CAP 12.1: "Objective claims must be backed by evidence, if relevant consisting of trials conducted on people. … Medicinal or medical claims and indications may be made for a medicinal product that is licensed by the MHRA … or for a medical device with the applicable conformity marking." [R5] VERBATIM
- CAP 12.2: "Marketers must not discourage essential treatment for conditions for which medical supervision should be sought. For example, they must not offer specific advice on, diagnosis of or treatment for such conditions unless that advice, diagnosis or treatment is conducted under the supervision of a suitably qualified health professional." [R5] VERBATIM → **sleep apnoea = do not claim to treat it; add "see your GP if…" (NHS red flags).**
- CAP 12.4: "Marketers must not confuse consumers by using unfamiliar scientific words for common conditions." [R5] VERBATIM → kills "mandibular advancement" / "genioglossus" / "oropharyngeal" as ad headlines (aligns with §13 plain-words test).
- CAP 12.5: "Marketers inviting consumers to diagnose their minor ailments must not make claims that might lead to a mistaken diagnosis." [R5] VERBATIM → relevant to a "which kind of snorer are you? tongue test" quiz/demo: frame as "the BSSAA tongue test can hint whether your tongue is involved" + "if you gasp, choke or feel exhausted, see your GP" — never "you don't have apnoea".
- CAP 12.6: "Marketers should not falsely claim that a product is able to prevent or treat disease or a malformation; restore, correct or modify a physiological function; or modify a person's appearance." (reflects DMCCA Schedule 20 prohibited practice) [R5] VERBATIM
- CAP 12.7: "References to the relief of symptoms … are acceptable if they can be substantiated. Unqualified claims such as 'cure' … are not generally acceptable" [R5] VERBATIM
- ASA snoring note — evidence bar: "Typically, appropriate evidence could include at least one adequately controlled experimental human study. Before and after studies with little or no control, studies without human subjects, self-assessment studies and anecdotal evidence are unlikely to be considered acceptable." · "the ASA found that, in spite of being marked as a class 1 medical device, this advertiser couldn't show their product alleviated a number of health conditions which included snoring." · testimonials: "if a testimonial includes a direct or indirect claim to relieve a condition or symptom then this is subject to the same rules as any claims." · "Claims that products can 'cure', 'restore', 'prevent', 'avoid', 'fight' or 'heal' are likely to be considered 'medicinal'." [R4] VERBATIM
- CAP medical devices advice (29 Jan 2026): "Whilst CE/UKCA certification may demonstrate that the device is safe and fit for its intended purpose, the ASA has ruled that the certification does not in itself constitute evidence for advertising claims" + rulings against medical claims where "the appropriate conformity marking was not held" or device "had not been registered with the MHRA" (e.g. Prime Star Shop 'Hear Pods' 13 Nov 2024; Person(s) unknown calming vest 18 Dec 2024) [R6] VERBATIM
- **Substantiation available for a generic MAD (INFERENCE):** controlled human studies exist for the device CLASS (O'Sullivan 1995 randomised half-night; Aarts 2011 MRA vs placebo 38% loudness reduction; TOMADO incl. self-moulded device) [science.md S43,S49,S52]. ASA may require evidence on THIS device or one shown to be equivalent → keep claims modest ("helps reduce snoring", "for many people") and attributable to the class + mechanism; avoid % or "in X nights" claims we can't back.

[R16] VERBATIM
- TGA: "Unless a specific exemption applies, medical devices must be included on the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG) before they can be supplied in Australia." · Penalties "up to five years imprisonment and/or 5000 penalty units. One penalty unit is currently valued at $330" · Offences include "supplying therapeutic goods not included on the ARTG … misrepresenting medical devices" [R9] VERBATIM
- Personal Importation Scheme: "any individual may import a three-month supply of unapproved therapeutic goods into Australia in any one importation without any approval required by the TGA provided: the devices are for the individual's own treatment or the treatment of immediate family · the individual does not supply (sell or give) the devices to any other person" [R9] VERBATIM — this is a consumer exemption, not a licence for a business to advertise unapproved devices to Australians (INFERENCE).
- TGA: "Overseas manufacturers: If you are an overseas manufacturer, you will need an Australian sponsor." [R17 SNIPPET]
- Advertising: "it is an offence to advertise therapeutic goods for an indication or purpose that is not included in the ARTG entry for the goods" · Therapeutic Goods Advertising Code 2021: "There are criminal offences and civil penalties for advertising to the public in a manner that does not comply with the Code." [R10] VERBATIM
- TGA social media guidance exists (incl. product reviews / ACCC fake-review warning) [R18 SNIPPET].
- **Decision:** AU launch needs (a) an Australian sponsor, (b) ARTG Class 1 inclusion for this device (or buy from a sponsor whose ARTG entry covers it), (c) ads limited to the ARTG intended purpose. Brief says "pharmacies stock mouthpieces" — consistent with ARTG-listed products in AU retail. NZ: GAP (Medsafe WAND notification not researched).

## 4. META ADVERTISING POLICY (all regions)
- Personal Attributes (change log Jun 27, 2024) [R15] VERBATIM: "ads must not contain content that asserts or implies personal attributes. This includes direct or indirect assertions or implications about a person's … age … physical or mental health (including medical conditions) …" · Ads can't: "Imply knowledge of Medical information of a user or user's family" · Ads can: "Contain passing reference to a personal attribute. This includes gender, age groups or age ranges." · "Use 'you/your' language without a personal attribute." · "Make Public Service Announcements that inform the public about issues related to personal health attributes, as long as they don't assert someone or their family has a health condition." · examples: ✅ "New diabetes treatment available" ❌ "Do you have diabetes?" ❌ "Depression getting you down? Get help now." · AGE ❌ "Meet other seniors" ❌ "Ready to upgrade your skin to look younger?"
  - **Applied to the brief's hooks (INFERENCE — Meta review is discretionary):**
    - "1 in 4 women snore. Nobody talks about it." → ✅ statistic/PSA style, no assertion she snores.
    - "He jokes about it at breakfast. You don't laugh." → ⚠️ implies she's the snorer ("you") → rewrite third-person/UGC ("I was the one he joked about…").
    - "After 45, a lot of us start snoring. Here's the quiet fix." → ⚠️ "us" + age + condition; borderline; safer "Many women start snoring after 45."
    - "Mouth tape didn't stop it? It might be your jaw, not your lips." → ❌ risk: asserts she tried tape for her snoring (implied condition). Safer: "Why mouth tape doesn't fix every kind of snoring."
    - "Girls' trip in 3 weeks and you're the one who snores?" → ❌ textbook "Do you have X?" pattern. Safer: "The girls' trip packing list nobody talks about."
    - "He moved to the spare room. Bring him back." → ⚠️ implies personal relationship + condition; test as testimonial voice.
- Health & Wellness (change log Jul 22, 2026) [R14] VERBATIM: "Ads promoting or marketing dietary, health, or weight loss or weight gain products and services must be targeted to people at least 18 years or older." · Ads can't "Employ clickbait tactics in a health, weight loss, or weight gain context, such as sensational language with exaggerated or extreme claims, or promises of specific outcomes within a set timeframe without disclaimers or qualifiers." → avoid "Silent in 1 night".
- "Ads must not request health information, including physical health, mental health, medical treatments, medical conditions or disabilities without our prior permission." (Ad Standards intro) [R15b SNIPPET] → lead-form/quiz ads asking "How loud do you snore?" need care.
- Before/after: H&W policy now permits cosmetic before/after for 18+ (Jul 2026 rewrite) — not directly about snoring audio "before/after"; a snore-decibel before/after is a health claim → governed by CAP/TGA substantiation, not Meta's cosmetic clause (INFERENCE).
