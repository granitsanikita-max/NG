## AUSTRALIA — detail
[R1] Therapeutic Goods (Excluded Goods) Determination 2018, compilation dated 8 Aug 2024 ("Latest version") — https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2018L01350/latest/text (text at .../2024-08-08/.../document_1.html) · Federal Register of Legislation · VERBATIM

[R2] TGA, "Consultation on Draft Guidance — Assistive Technologies and the Therapeutic Goods (Excluded Goods) Determination 2021", V1.0 Aug 2021 — https://consultations.tga.gov.au/tga/assistive-technologies-amendment-exposure-draft/supporting_documents/CONSULTATION%20%20Guidance%20Assistive%20technologies.pdf · VERBATIM (PDF downloaded)

[R3] TGA, "Consultation: Future regulation of assistive technologies", V1.0 July 2024 — https://consultations.tga.gov.au/tga/consultation-future-regulation-of-assistive-techno/supporting_documents/TGA%20Public%20Consultation%20%20Future%20regulation%20of%20assistive%20technologies%2022%20July%202024.pdf · VERBATIM

[R4] TGA, "Future regulation of assistive technologies information session" (published 2024-09-17, modified 2026-03-30) — https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/industry-guidance-and-resources/tga-learn/self-paced-online-learning/presentations/future-regulation-assistive-technologies-information-session · VERBATIM (transcript)

## META AD POLICY (all markets)

- "ads must not contain content that asserts or implies personal attributes. This includes direct or indirect assertions or implications about a person's race, ethnicity, religion, beliefs, age, sexual orientation or practices, gender identity, disability, physical or mental health (including medical conditions)…"
- Ads can't: "Imply knowledge of Medical information of a user or user's family"
- Ads can: "Contain passing reference to a personal attribute. This includes gender, age groups or age ranges." ; "Use 'you/your' language without a personal attribute."
- AGE examples: ✅ "Meet seniors" ❌ "Meet other seniors"; HEALTH examples: ✅ "New diabetes treatment available" ❌ "Do you have diabetes?" ; ❌ "Using the word 'you/your/other' to reference a personal attribute".
→ So: ❌ "Struggling to get up after your knee replacement?" / "Is your mum falling?" (implies medical info of user/family). ✅ "The cane made for getting up — not just walking." / "Built for the first weeks after knee surgery" (no 'you' + condition) — INFERENCE, test against reviewer.

## WHAT THE PRODUCT MUST NOT CLAIM (sourced reasoning)
1. "Prevents falls" / "fall prevention device" — therapeutic "prevention" wording is the medical-device trigger in TGA s41BD ("prevention … of disease"; "alleviation of or compensation for an injury or disability") [R3 VERBATIM extract of s41BD] and would need evidence; science shows cane users still fall (75% weren't using it when they fell; walking aids associated with fall injuries) — see science.md [S9][S10].
2. "Medical-grade", "TGA-approved/FDA-approved/registered" — not true unless we hold the entry; ARTG inclusion ≠ approval for Class I (self-declared) [R8].
3. "Speeds recovery after knee/hip replacement" / "physio-recommended" without a named, consenting physio — therapeutic + endorsement claim.
4. "SOS alarm calls for help" — it's a local buzzer, not a monitored personal alarm (INFERENCE; confirm spec). Don't imply monitoring.
5. Weight capacity: state only the supplier's tested figure, with test evidence (CJ listing gives none — GAP).
6. Any Meta ad copy that says/implies the viewer or their parent has a condition [R19].
