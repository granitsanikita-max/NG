## The call
- **Strongest avatar (Nikita's question):**
  - **The family:** adult children 45–65, mostly daughters, plus spouses, of a parent (median age 90, usually Mum) who can no longer get up on her own and won't accept "old-person" help. 156 of 664 bank rows; 111 of 294 Reddit/forum rows. Top thread: "My 85-Year-Old Mom Refuses to Use a Cane Because She Doesn't Want to Look Old" (190 comments).
  - **Runner-up: knee/hip post-op** (105 rows; sharpest getting-up pain). Own test cell.
  - **Seniors themselves:** Naxir's angle in every ad, so not the wedge.
  - **Living alone / night:** weak. Bunnings/Kmart LED sticks; the alarm calls nobody.
- **Gate: CONDITIONAL.** Painkiller ✅, market ✅. Naxir TESTED the family angle as a Christmas push (~14 ads, 5–12 Nov 2026, AU/CA/NZ/US + GB) and paused it. Its gift listicle page is still live. A clone wave is entering AU: 8 other stand-up canes in the last 30 days, 6 started in November, none family-led. Re-check weekly; move fast.
- **Region:** Australia first (Naxir 75 AU ads, user angle; AU mobility shops sell A$45–50 basic dual-handle sticks without base/LED/alarm/fold; Amazon AU has no incumbent). Then NZ/CA/GB; US last.
- **Mechanism, "the missing armrest":**
  - Couches, loos and car seats have no armrests. Rising from a low seat takes up to 97% of available knee strength, and pushing down on armrests cuts the load.
  - UMS: a lower push-down handle on a cane that stands beside her, "a portable armrest".
  - The picture is partly used by Naxir AU ("No armrest, and nobody lifting you"). The proof (physio "push, don't pull"; armrest studies) and the name are ours.
- **One Belief (core = autonomy, 85):** a portable armrest is the key to Mum getting up off any couch, loo or car seat on her own.
- **Positioning sentence:** "The portable-armrest cane for the parent who won't ask for a hand — at the moment they have to get up off a low couch, loo or car seat."
- **Brand line (LOCKED):** "We are the dry, warm, straight-talking family brand that stands FOR Mum getting up on her own — because it's the seat, not her age — and AGAINST hospital-beige aids that end up in the cupboard and too-good-to-be-true Facebook canes that wobble the moment she leans on them — the stand-up cane for people who'd rather back their parent's independence than haul them up off the couch."
