## House rules for every line of copy
1. Sell the outcome, never the foam. The outcome = waking up not already hurting before the 6am feed, feeling like herself in her own body again, the certainty this is not just her life now, and permission to have one thing that is hers.
2. Core desires, in order of pull: CONTROL/CERTAINTY (a real, lasting, passive end to the ache) · IDENTITY (feel like me again) · PERMISSION/SELF-WORTH (allowed one thing, costs the baby nothing) · LOVE/CONNECTION (present for the baby, not in pain through feeds) · FREEDOM/TIME (nothing to do, no appointment).
3. Emotional arc down the page: meet her in grief and fear → permission ("you don't have to live with it") → belief (mechanism, comparison) → safety (guarantee) → relief and belonging.
4. Her words only: mom neck, nursing neck, the nursing hunch, that deep ache between your shoulder blades, the 3am feed, wake up stiff, dead arm, so sore, let your neck rest.
5. Banned: cervical (in headlines), orthopedic, therapeutic, realign, alignment, traction, correct, treat, cure, fix, heal, repair, relieve pain, pain relief, therapy, undo the damage, upper cross syndrome, the "72%" stat, the Mumsnet "memory foam worked" quote (it is about a man), revolutionary, game-changing, miracle, #1.
6. No em dashes or en dashes in customer-facing copy (they read machine-written). Short sentences, 5th to 8th grade.
7. Every claim is true or sourced. Every number identical across ad, advertorial, PDP, cart.
8. Reviews never reuse store language ("Night Shift", "clock out", "cradles", "slow-rebound", "3am kit"). Real people describe what happened to them in their own plain words.
