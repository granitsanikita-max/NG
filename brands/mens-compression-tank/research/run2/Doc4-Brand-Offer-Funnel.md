# 2B · Compliance & Claims — SAY / DON'T SAY

## The product's actual category
- **Apparel — a men's shapewear undergarment.** ⚠ *§20 correction:* Meta's "undergarments" exemption sits under *Adult Products & Reproductive Health* only [S2]. It does **not** exempt the tank from the *Health & Wellbeing* rules: any ad that reads as slimming / weight-loss / cosmetic must target 18+ and obey the inferiority, pinch and wearables rules (VERBATIM, re-checked 2026-11-02).
- **Not a medical device — unless our claims make it one.** FDA judges intended use by "the objective intent… shown by labeling claims, advertising matter, or oral or written statements" (21 CFR 801.4) [S8] VERBATIM; intent can also be shown by "the design or composition of the article, or by the circumstances surrounding the distribution" — **our claims everywhere (ads, PDP, product title, emails, support replies) and the product's design decide the category. Rename the supplier title ("Tummy Control… Flatten Chest") on our PDP.** (§20 correction)
- Closest device rules: 21 CFR 880.5780 *medical support stocking* (elastic + controlled pressure + medical purpose — leg only) [S6]; **21 CFR 890.3490 *truncal orthosis*** — "a device intended for medical purposes to support or to immobilize fractures, strains, or sprains of the neck or trunk… Abdominal… lumbar… thoracic orthoses" (Class I) [S7] VERBATIM. → "Corrects posture / back support / relieves back pain / hernia / post-op" pull a tank toward a device intended use. **309 ads in the run-2 ad log already make posture claims** [S40] — taken *and* risky.
- Model disclaimer already in the category (copy the idea, not the words): Selure — "intended to provide a temporary visual improvement in body shape and silhouette through compression when worn… not medical devices" [S41] VERBATIM. A disclaimer never cures a deceptive claim (FTC).

## FTC / federal / state
- **FTC Gut Check #7:** "Nothing you wear or apply to the skin can cause substantial weight loss. So weight loss claims for… wraps, body belts… are false." [S10] VERBATIM.
- **Shapewear precedent (2014):** Norm Thompson / Wacoal paid $230k / $1.3M over caffeine-infused shapewear; the order bars claiming "any garment that contains any drug or cosmetic causes substantial weight or fat loss or a substantial reduction in body size", and the complaint called the slimming garments "devices" under the FTC Act [S11] VERBATIM. An analogy for a plain tank, not a rule that binds it (§20 correction).
- Testimonials are not substantiation (16 CFR 255.2); no fake/invented reviewers (16 CFR 465.2); textile label = fiber %, "Imported", care [S12–S15] (run-1 sources, not re-checked).
- State: Connecticut DCP bars weight-loss representations without "competent and reliable scientific evidence" [S16] SNIPPET; Florida's Commercial Weight-Loss Practices Act covers weight-loss *programs* (not an appearance-only garment) [S17] SNIPPET.
- **Competitors already breaking these:** Selure "strengthened abdominal, back, and chest muscles all while wearing" + "Ionic compression" [S41]; Aida "Targeted compression across the core stimulates that stagnant lymph" [S42]; North Alpine "Accelerates Weight Loss" [S43].

## Why your last ads probably got rejected
- **G1:** a question or "your" + gut ("Over 40 and can't get rid of that beer belly?"), or "accelerates weight loss" on a wearable [S1][S2][S10].
- **G2:** "man boobs" + "your" / a question, shirtless nipple close-ups, or "eliminates gynecomastia" [S1][S2][S5].
- **G3:** naming the drug or surgery to the viewer ("On Ozempic?"), or a shirtless loose-skin before/after with a pinch [S1][S2][S3].
- **G5:** age as a question / "your" ("Over 50?"), "look younger", or posture / back-pain claims [S1][S7].

## How to reach them WITHOUT naming the condition
- **Targeting (all):** there is no condition targeting; use broad men + age bands (age *targeting* is allowed, age *assertion in copy* is not) and let the creative self-select. 18+ is mandatory once an ad reads as weight-loss/cosmetic [S1][S2].
- **G1:** name the *shirt situation*, not the body — "the polo that clings at the belt", "tucking in a dress shirt", "the side view in the wedding photo" (ad-log situation tags: dress shirt/office 45 · wedding/event 15 · photos 10 [S40]).
- **G2:** name the *symptom on the shirt* — "nipple outline through a white tee", "the arms-up photo", "a hoodie in July"; first-person fellow-sufferer voice carries the condition word safely.
- **G3:** *achievement* words (down 90 lbs, four sizes down) — never medical words (GLP-1, sleeve); visuals = old belt notches, an XXL tag next to an L; keep drug / surgery words out of URLs, page titles and pixel parameters.
- **G5:** age as a *passing label* ("for men 50+") or older talent in age-coded moments (grandkid photos, a daughter's wedding, a retirement party) — "the same shirt fits different now", never "your aging body".

**Re-check of the §2 draft angle:** "Lost the weight — but it still shows" passes the policy (no "you", no drug, no weight-loss claim for the product) but is kept as an **internal segment label** — §20 found InShape's live hook is nearly the same. Ad copy uses achievement words and the run-1 line "The scale isn't the finish line. Your shirt is.", with a shirt-on demo, never a body before/after.

**So What → do this:** sell **appearance while worn** — "look flatter in a shirt" — never weight, skin, posture or medical change. Speak in third or first person, name shirt situations not bodies, keep drug and condition words off the ad and URL, and put the honest education on the landing page.

# 4B · Brand Positioning System — DRAFT (locks after §10A + §11)

- **The Enemy — the miracle shirt.** The category's pattern: promise a body change a garment can't deliver, then hide behind the fine print. Evidence from its own 1★ reviews and pages: claims ("Accelerates Weight Loss", "Ionic", "drains your beer gut") [S41][S42][S43]; buyers punish it ("Buyer Beware It's just a very lightweight wife-beater. Overpriced wife-beater." — [Trustpilot 1★](https://www.trustpilot.com/reviews/6a18fe6b7d9901e3627e1013) · VERBATIM); scam/trust is the #3 objection bank-wide (137) and #1 in 1–2★ rows (87 of 332) [S1]; the biggest store shows 2,383 reviews and 0 one-star on its own page vs 197 one-star on Trustpilot [Doc 2 §10B].
- **What we stand FOR — the truth the category won't tell:** **"A shirt can't change your body. It can change how you look in your shirt."** It costs us the "lose inches" sale — and it's the only claim that survives Meta and the FTC (§2B).
- **The Attitude:** **blunt, dry-funny, man-to-man** — the guy who lost 85 lb and calls his chest "the deflated balloon". No incumbent occupies it: they're all hype + shame + emoji (§4 #2). Men in the bank already talk this way (§3, §14).
- **The Uncopyable Test:** if North Alpine adopted "a shirt can't change your body" tomorrow, it would indict its own "Accelerates Weight Loss / Crushes Dad Bod / BYE DAD BOD" page, its "ionic" product and its 2,295 live ads. If Aida adopted it, it would kill "drains your beer gut". → **Real moat against the big players.** ⚠️ **But two small players already say it (§20):** InShape's GLP-1 page — "InShape does not tighten skin or change your body" — and Alphacore's GLP-1 ads — "It won't tighten the skin" [S53]. So the honest line alone is *not* the moat; the moat is the honest line **+ the mechanism (§13) + the size-down swap for a changing body (nobody offers it) + the attitude on every surface + speed**.
- **Whole-store thread:** the name (a man's word, not "shaper" — see the name shortlist), the About-page manifesto ("We make one thing: the undershirt that makes your shirt fit. It won't change your body. We'll never say it will."), the PDP proof (shirt-on demo + measured size chart + sit/bend film), the offer (fit swap, not "$10 last sizes"), the reviews shown (men describing the shirt moment, per group), the voice (§14).
- **Weakness-as-weapon:** small and new means **one product made for one job** — the shirt moment — instead of a 109-product catalogue of tanks, socks, shorts and programs. David makes one thing and shows it on real men; Goliath runs 2,295 ads for a blue "ionic" vest. (Never sell "trust us, we're small/honest" — Nikita's rule; let the product proof carry it.)
- **Guardrails:** (1) we must BE it — no inches, no weight loss, no "ionic", no curated-only reviews, a policy that matches the promise; (2) attack the pattern ("miracle shirts"), never name a competitor.

**DRAFT line:** *"We are the blunt, dry-funny brand that stands FOR 'a shirt can't change your body — it can change how you look in your shirt' and AGAINST the miracle shirt — the compression undershirt for men who are done hiding in their clothes."*
Holds every men's group: G3 (the leftover the scale can't move), G2 (the chest on the shirt), G1 (the gut at the tuck), G5 (the shirt that fits different now). Re-checked against §10A's core desire and §11's objections before locking.

**So What → do this:** build the brand to be the one men's undershirt that tells the truth about what a shirt can do — and then proves the part it can do on camera.

# The Funnel Map

**Funnel picked (from the Notion Funnel Selection Board):** the **Discovery Stack — Advertorial → Offer Page** (the board's default for a single-hero product). **Why:** the launch man (G3) is problem → solution-aware (45% + 30%, §5) and burned by the category (Stage 4→5, §8). Never cold traffic to a home page (board rule).
