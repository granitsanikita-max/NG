# BRAND — RecoverMama

> Identity file. Filled when the brand is created; read by every station.

- **Slug:** recover-mama
- **Product (one line):** "The Night Shift" — a contour / butterfly slow-rebound memory-foam cervical pillow built for new moms: a centre dip that keeps the chin level, slow-rebound foam that doesn't flatten by morning, and a side arm/shoulder cutout for sleeping curled toward the baby.
- **Niche / category:** Postpartum / new-mom wellness — cervical neck pillow ("mom neck" / "nursing neck" / "the nursing hunch"). US, English. Meta-interrupt play (the pain is not searched).
- **Target customer:** The new / postpartum US mom, ~25–40, first baby 0–18 months, breastfeeds + does the overnight feeds; carries, rocks and looks down at the baby all day. Daily neck + upper-back + shoulder ache she calls "mom neck." Emotional register = grief (85/401) + fear it's permanent (67/401). Mostly problem-aware (58%), burned by fixes that only "temporarily dull" it. ICP persona: "Maya, 29, first baby ~4mo."
- **Positioning (one line):** The tender-but-defiant, mom-FIRST brand that stands FOR a new mom's body being worth recovering too — the pillow that lets her neck finally rest while the baby sleeps ("the night their neck actually recovers"), against a generic sleep industry, the "just mom life" dismissal, and quick fixes that only dull it. Core she buys on = CONTROL / CERTAINTY (a real, lasting, passive end to the ache). NOT a medical device; NOT a generic slab.

## Notes — READ BEFORE ANY CREATIVE (hard guardrails)
- **Comfort / support / relief-feel language ONLY.** A cervical pillow is a comfort textile, never a treatment. NEVER: treat, cure, fix, heal, repair, realign, decompress, "traction," "cervical traction," "upper cross syndrome," "undo the damage." Say instead: "cradles / holds your neck," "lets your neck rest," "wake up less stiff."
- **No "your neck pain" personal-attribute hook** (Meta rule). No "do you have neck pain?" style call-outs at the viewer.
- **No unverified stats in copy** — especially NOT "72% of breastfeeding mothers" (no primary source).
- **The on-camera "expert" is the highest-risk element.** The research is explicit: a fabricated named doctor is the riskiest thing we can run. Never imply a named/real clinician or brand endorses the product. (See CREATIVES.md open questions.)
