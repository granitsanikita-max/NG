## The Enemy (who we're against)
Not a competitor by name — the **category's pattern**: the doctor who glances for one second and says *"lose weight"*; the **faceless DTC funnels** with fake "Dr. Rebecca Mills" nurses, hidden subscriptions that charge after you cancel, "50% off" that isn't, and "US-based" creams that ship from Dongguan. They **prey on desperate people over something this simple.** Every line pulled from their own 1★ reviews. **A brand with an enemy is a brand people rally to.**

## What we stand FOR (manifesto seed)
> Your body was never the problem. Nobody ever made this *for the fold* — on purpose. You've been handed diaper cream made for babies, foot powder made for feet, and a doctor who looked for one second and told you to be smaller. It's not a hygiene problem and it's not a moral failing — it's **friction**, and friction has a fix. We made a barrier that sits where your skin meets itself, so skin glides instead of grinds. We'll always tell you what it **is and isn't**. No fake nurses. No subscription traps. No shame. **Made for folds. On purpose.**

## The Feeling
**Seen → relieved → unashamed → part of a crew.** The incumbents make her feel like a gross problem to be fixed; we make her feel like a **person someone finally built for.** Emotional job: *"I'm not disgusting, I'm not failing, and this was made for a body like mine."*

## The Attitude & Voice
**Blunt · warm · insider · zero-pity · a little funny.** 5th–8th grade, her words (§14 swipe), never clinical, never shaming.
- **DO:** *"Fold rash isn't a hygiene thing. It's friction. Here's the barrier that stops the rub."* · *"Made for folds, not borrowed from a baby."* · *"Big boob crew — this one's for you."* · *"We'll tell you what it is AND what it isn't."*
- **DON'T:** clinical ("intertrigo," "candidiasis," "antifungal"), shaming ("embarrassing," "hygiene," "lose weight"), hypey miracle-cure ("kills the yeast, cures it overnight"). Those are the enemy's words.
- **Sample lines (voice, not final copy):** *"Your skin folds aren't gross. They're just folds. And they finally have a cream."* · *"The powder turns to paste. The creams stay wet. We did the opposite."* · *"One real person. No fake doctors. A barrier that actually stays put."*

## How we make her JOIN (not just buy)
- **A crew, not a customer base:** lean into the existing identity ("BIG BOOB CREW," plus-size solidarity). The brand is the membership card.
- **Real voices, not personas:** UGC + reviews from actual plus-size women; the founder's real face + real "why I made this."
- **An honest pledge (anti-enemy):** *no fake experts, no subscription traps, no shame* — stated plainly on the site, and kept.
- **The whole store carries it:** name → About manifesto → "what it is/isn't" PDP → 365-day keep-it guarantee → the reviews we choose to show → the voice in every email.

## Weakness-as-weapon
Small, new, one person = **David vs the faceless $10M Goliath funnels.** *"I'm one real person who got tired of watching people get scammed over something this simple — so I made the honest version."* People root for David; the incumbents literally can't tell that story.

## Guardrails
1. We must actually **BE** it — genuinely honest, genuinely US-ship, genuinely no-trap. A hypocritical position detonates harder than none.
2. Attack the category **pattern**, **never** name-and-shame a specific competitor (defamation).
3. The identity lives on **every surface** — if it only lives in the ad, it's an angle, not a brand.
