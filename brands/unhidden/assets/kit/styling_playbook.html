The one idea

Start flat, then build on top

Here is the whole trick in one sentence. When your stomach is held flat, your shirt stops fighting your body and finally hangs the way it was designed to.

Think about why your shirts never sat right before. The fabric was stretching over your middle, pulling at the buttons, riding up, bunching. You were asking the shirt to do a job it was never built for.

The tank gives you a smooth, flat base. Now the shirt just drapes. That is it. Everything else in this book is simply making sure the shirt on top works with that flat base instead of hiding it.

Remember this

A flat base plus a well fitted shirt beats an expensive shirt every time. You already sorted the base. Now let's sort the shirt.

The most important page

The colour rule

The tank comes in more than one colour for a reason. Which one you wear depends on the shirt going over the top. Get this wrong and everyone sees the tank. Get it right and nobody ever knows it's there.

  • Under white or light shirts, wear a light tank. White or light grey. A dark tank under a thin white shirt shows straight through, and that is the one mistake people actually notice.
  • Under dark shirts, wear black. Navy, charcoal, black on top means black underneath. It disappears completely.
  • Not sure? Grey is your safe pick. It hides under almost everything, light or dark.

The 5 second window test

Before you leave, hold your shirt up to a window with the tank behind it. If you can see the tank through the shirt, that shirt is too thin for that colour. Switch to a lighter tank or a thicker shirt. Done.

Shirt by shirt

Under a plain t-shirt

Fit is everything here. This is the one most men get wrong, and it is the easiest to fix.

A tee that is too baggy hides the flat shape you just created and makes you look bigger than you are. A tee that is too thin and clingy shows every outline. You want the middle ground. A mid weight tee that skims your body without gripping it.

  • Go for a slightly structured cotton, not the cheap thin stuff that clings.
  • A crew neck sits cleaner on most builds than a deep v neck.
  • If you tuck it, tuck it fully. A half tuck on a bigger frame usually just looks messy.
  • If it pulls anywhere across the chest or stomach, go up one size. Comfort reads as confidence.

Shirt by shirt

Under a polo

The polo is one of the easiest wins you own. The collar pulls the eye up to your face, and the structure holds its own shape instead of clinging to yours.

  • Buttons. Leave the top one undone and do the rest up. All buttoned looks tight. All undone looks sloppy. One undone looks right.
  • Fit. It should sit close but never pull. The fit across your chest and stomach matters far more than the sleeve. If it tugs, size up.
  • Colour. A solid mid to dark colour is the most flattering. Save the busy patterns for when you're feeling good and want to show it.

Shirt by shirt

Under an open or casual shirt

This layer does a lot of quiet work. An open shirt creates two long vertical lines running straight down your body, and vertical lines make you look taller and leaner without doing anything else.

  • Wear it open over a fitted tee, or with just the tank collar showing. Both work.
  • Keep it from turning into a tent. It should hang straight down, not balloon out at the sides.
  • Roll the sleeves up to the forearm. It looks intentional and easy, and it shows a bit of skin which breaks up the bulk.

Shirt by shirt

Under a dress shirt

This is the one people notice. A dress shirt over a flat base, with nothing pushing it out and no lines showing through, is the whole reason you bought the tank.

  • Get the fit right in the body first. You can tailor a collar and shorten a sleeve. You cannot fake the fit through the chest and stomach, so buy for that.
  • Tuck it in. A flat front, tucked, is the leanest you will look all day. Untucked over a flat stomach is a wasted opportunity.
  • Wear your light tank underneath so nothing shows through, and check it in daylight before you walk out the door.
  • For events, pick a shirt with a bit of body to it. The flimsy cheap kind clings. A shirt with structure holds the clean line all night.

Don't forget the bottom half

The trousers matter too

Half of looking leaner up top is making sure the bottom half agrees with it. You can nail the shirt and still throw it away with the wrong trousers.

  • Wear them at your natural waist, not slung under your stomach. Sitting under the belly makes it look bigger and shorter. Up and over is leaner every time.
  • Go straight or slightly tapered in the leg. Skinny fits fight a fuller middle and baggy fits drown you. Straight is the friend of almost every body.
  • Keep your shoe colour close to your trouser colour. It keeps one long unbroken line running from your waist all the way to the floor, and long lines read as tall and lean.

Keep it simple

Quick do's and don'ts

Do

  • Buy shirts to fit your chest and stomach, size up if they pull.
  • Keep your colours close together for one long line.
  • Tuck in when the front is flat.
  • Check thin pale shirts in daylight before you leave.

Don't

  • Hide under baggy clothes. It makes you look bigger, not smaller.
  • Wear a dark tank under a thin pale shirt.
  • Put horizontal stripes across your middle.
  • Let a shirt balloon out. It should hang straight down.
