## 📏 Standards for every section
- **Every line on this page is a requirement.** Work it top to bottom, in order. Don't drop, merge, or skip anything — every heading, bullet, sub-bullet, and instruction gets done.
- **Painkiller in an uncontested market.** The wedge must be BOTH a specific, painful, unsolved PROBLEM for a large, desperate group AND white space no major competitor already runs in the target region (§2). Miss either half and it's weak.
- **Sourced or it doesn't ship.** Behind every pain, objection, competitor, belief, and mechanism: a **verbatim quote + the source URL**. Never invent a quote, stat, competitor, or run length. Can't verify it? Say so — don't pad.
- **Source tags:** every quote, number, and claim carries a tag pointing to its source — `[S1]`, `[S2]`… — matching the numbered Sources list at the end of that doc (URL · platform · date). Also mark how solid it is: **VERBATIM** (page opened, wording confirmed) · **SNIPPET** (search-result text — confirm before live creative) · **DATA** (a tool's estimate, e.g. Winning Hunter revenue) · **INFERENCE** (your reasoning, no source — keep these rare and always labelled).
- **Count it, don't guess.** Every pain, desire, objection, failed solution, trigger, misconception, and belief comes from the §2C customer data bank and is **ranked by how many real snippets mention it** (e.g. "31 of 142"). No count = no ranking. (Exception: the §9B chain keeps persuasion order — show each belief's count next to it.)
- **Minimums are targets, not quotas.** Fewer real ones exist? Give the real number + why — never pad with invented or weak items.
- **Search snippets:** Reddit / Amazon / Facebook often block full-page fetch. Search-result snippets are real text — quote them, but flag them so the exact wording gets confirmed at the URL before it goes into live creative.
- **Tight, and every section ends in a decision:** the answer + the evidence + a **"So What → do this"** line. No essays.
- **List sections give the FULL set, not one example** — real customers have a dozen pains, objections, failed solutions. List them all (with quotes), tightly.
- **Be decisive.** Commit to the market / wedge yourself, with evidence — lead with the pick + why, runner-up in one line. Never hand back a "which one?" menu. If Nikita hands you a market, pressure-test it honestly. If no uncontested big painkiller market exists, say so plainly instead of forcing a weak wedge.
- **Run it as a workflow:** decide the uncontested painkiller MARKET first (§1–§2 draft). Then fan out one deep agent per area to COLLECT research in parallel (competitors + offers + white-space sweep · customer data bank / voice / beliefs / worldview / failed solutions · authority + science + compliance + mechanism · features / triggers / history). Every agent researches the CHOSEN market, not the generic product, gets the exact text of the sections it collects for (never a summary), and returns findings with quotes + URLs. Then YOU write every section in page order from what they return — no section skipped, none written out of order — then run §20 last.
- **"She" = the ICP,** whatever the buyer's real gender. Never assume female — follow the data.
- **Report corrections honestly.** If verification kills an earlier conclusion, say it was wrong and what replaced it — never quietly drop it.
- **Research + write only.** Never touch live ad accounts or spend. Never place an order or enter payment details on any site.

## Fill these out, in order
**Pre-0 · Economics & KPIs (ask Nikita FIRST, before any research)** — Get: **target country / region + language** (every "target region" check below uses this — never guess it) · **your current product page / store URL** (if any) · **supplier product link** (real price + full spec / feature list — §17 needs it) · **landed cost per unit** (product + shipping + fulfilment) · **planned selling price** + offer shape (single / BOGO / bundle) · **processing %** (default 3%). ⚠️ Use the REAL supplier price — never the fake AliExpress "welcome deal" first-order teaser. Then show a **KPI table** (real math, show the formulas) for each offer variant — metrics as rows, offer variants as columns (max 4), formulas under the table:
	- **Nikita = the person running this.** Wait for his answers. Can't ask in this run? Use what the request already gives. Missing costs are fine (see below) — a missing target region is NOT: stop and ask.
	- **Contribution margin \$** = Price − landed − processing − other (the most you can pay per order and break even).
		- Spelled out: CM \$ = Price − (landed per unit × units in the order) − (processing % × Price) − other per-order costs (packaging etc.; 0 if none).
	- **Contribution margin %** = CM \$ ÷ Price.
	- **Break-even ROAS** = 1 ÷ CM% (= Price ÷ CM \$). **Break-even CPA** = CM \$.
	- **Target ROAS / CPA at \~20% and \~30% net** — the "scale" line and the "kill" line.
		- Formulas: **Target CPA at n% net** = CM \$ − (n × Price). **Target ROAS** = Price ÷ Target CPA. "Price" = the full order value of that offer variant (a bundle = the whole order). If CM% ≤ n, that target is impossible — say so.
		- **Scale line** = the \~30% net CPA (at or under it → scale). **Kill line** = the \~20% net CPA (consistently above it → fix or kill). Show break-even next to both.
	- No costs yet? Flag it, keep going, and add a reminder just above Doc 1's Sources list to send COGS to finish the table.
**0 · Product & core problem** — One line: what it is, the ONE problem it solves, who for. Everything hangs off this. Make sure the market that you are going to sell to is really needing a solution to there problem not just a want this will make the market actually passionate since they have a real big problem that they are needing help for. not just bringing it to a market that might want it.
	- **Mass desire test (Schwartz):** you can't create desire — only channel one that already exists. Score the desire behind this problem 1–5 on **urgency** (how badly she wants it fixed now) · **staying power** (does it keep nagging her, or fade?) · **scope** (how many people feel it). Give real evidence for each score. Low scores = a weak market — say so.
**0B · Demand & Seasonality (real numbers, not vibes)** — Prove there's money here, and WHEN.
	- **Search trend:** Google Trends for the problem + the product (5 years, target country) via `playwright-skill` / `browser-use` (Firecrawl gets blocked) — rising / flat / dying + the peak months. Blocked? Use Winning Hunter `search_exploding_topics` (volume + monthly history + growth), label it "not country-filtered", and log it in §20.
	- **Marketplace demand:** Amazon — top listings' review counts + "bought in past month" badges (`firecrawl_scrape` the Amazon search-results page with `formats: ["query"]`). TikTok Shop — units / sales via Winning Hunter (`search_tiktok_products` with `country: \[target\]` → `get_tiktok_product` slices "metrics" + "history"; target country not covered? say so).
	- **Ad demand:** how many advertisers are running this product / problem right now (Winning Hunter `search_facebook_ads`: 3–5 keywords — product, problem, 2 synonyms — with `countries: \[target\]`; page with the `scroll` cursor until no new advertisers appear, max 5 pages per keyword; de-dupe advertisers with `jq`; report the count + the queries used — plus the Facebook Ad Library) and how long the longest have run.
	- **Verdict:** growing / stable / declining · peak months · launch-timing call. Every number gets its source + date.
**1 · Competitor scan (do this first)** — Reverse-engineer what's already working; never copy it. Confirm real demand exists (winning ads / scaled stores are out there).
	- **Long-list first:** before picking, list EVERY advertiser / store selling this found across all sources (name · URL · # active ads · longest-running ad · region). Expand it with Winning Hunter `find_similar_shops` (each competitor's domain) + `brief_competitor`. Aim for 10+, or give the real number and why. The 3 direct picks below = the 3 with the most proven spend (longest-running + most active ads).
	- **Pick 3 direct competitors** (same/similar product, same market, actively running paid ads). For each, capture:
		- **Shop signals:** monthly traffic · how long in business · # active ads · **longest-running ad** (= proven) · **most-duplicated ad** (= their real winner) · other pages running ads to the same domain (= whitelisting / creator angle).
			- Most-duplicated: Winning Hunter `search_facebook_ads` with `keyword: \[domain\]`, `searchkeyword: "landingurl"`, `min_duplicates: 2`, `sort_by: longestrunning` — the creative with the most duplicates = their winner.
			- Traffic: `get_store_details` covers Shopify stores only — not Shopify / not found → the SimilarWeb range, labelled.
		- **Ads:** the pain (or desire) they lead with · their mechanism · the avatar · the creative format (UGC / native / static / animation) · the hooks/ideas that repeat across ads.

		- **Funnel:** where the click goes (PDP / advertorial / listicle / quiz) and the one job that page does.

**2 · Your gap (Purple Ocean)** — The one angle nobody else is hitting, built on Section 1 (change the angle on demand that's already proven — don't invent a market no one buys). Find your edge from three gaps, in this order:
	- **1. Creative-format gap** — a format competitors are barely using (native when all are polished; UGC / animation when all are static). Take a proven message, express it in a fresher format.
	- **2. Mechanism gap** — a faster / easier / better "how" (e.g. "clears it in 30 minutes"). Speed or ease becomes the differentiator.
	- **3. Avatar gap** — a buyer no one targets (mouth spray → cigar smokers, not students). Rebuild the creative + page around that avatar.
	Pick the first gap you can genuinely win.
	- A format or mechanism gap is HOW you attack — the wedge you commit must still name the MARKET (who + their painful problem) that passes the gate below.
	- **Check all three first:** one line per gap with the §1 evidence for or against it (which competitor ads prove it's open or closed) — then pick.
	- **The Purple Ocean Gate — the gap must pass ALL of this before you commit:**
		- **PAINKILLER** — a specific, desperate, under-served PROBLEM (not a "nicer \[product\] for \[broad audience\]" want). The market must genuinely NEED a fix (§0).
		- **UNCONTESTED** — no major competitor already runs this market / angle in the target region. An angle the biggest competitor already runs = automatic FAIL (red ocean). A proven-but-abandoned angle, or one only run in another region / language = de-risked white space — take it.
		- **A MARKET, not an ANGLE** — a market = a large group bound by one massive painful problem they'll pay to solve (a whole brand + 20 ads). An angle = one entry scenario (one ad). If you wrote a single occasion, zoom out to the market behind it.
		- **3 automatic fails:** (1) vitamin ("a nicer \[product\]") · (2) red ocean (your biggest competitor's own angle) · (3) angle-as-market (a single scenario).
		- **Before you commit the draft, answer in writing:** "Which competitor is already running this exact angle, and where?" If one runs it in your target region, it's NOT purple — find the gap they miss.

**2B · Compliance & Claims — SAY / DON'T SAY (do this before writing any copy — and re-check the §2 draft angle against it)** — Identify the product's ACTUAL category and pull the rules that apply to IT (don't paste supplement rules onto a mobility aid). Output a two-column **✅ SAFE TO SAY / ❌ NEVER SAY** table.
	- **Ad-platform policy (Meta especially):**
		- **Personal Attributes rule — the #1 silent killer.** Never imply you know the viewer's personal attributes — health condition, disability, age, religion, race / ethnicity, sexual orientation, gender identity, financial status, criminal record. "Do you have \[condition\]?" / "Your \[condition\]…" = instant reject; the word "other" ("meet other \[X\] parents") also triggers it. Reframe to third person / self-identifying: "Some people who…", "For anyone who…". Naming a condition is allowed if it doesn't imply the viewer has it ("New bunion relief now available" ✅ vs "Your bunion pain…" ❌). Keep deep condition education on the LANDING PAGE — Meta reviews landing pages too, so the same rules apply there.
		- **No unsubstantiated health / condition / cure claims** — no "cures / treats / heals / prevents / reduces \[condition\]". Sell feeling / comfort / routine / function + outcome.
		- Watch before/after, idealized-body, and medical-device implications.
		- **Category-specific — look up the real rules:** supplements (health-claim substantiation, no disease claims) · kids / baby (CPSIA / ASTM F963, choking warning, no SIDS / "safe for crib") · beauty (cosmetic-vs-drug line) · mobility / daily-living aids (usually Class I / freely advertisable — the risk is copy language, not the product class) · sensory / "calming" (treat as health claims needing substantiation).
	- **Legal / claims substantiation (FTC / FDA-style):** structure/function + comfort claims OK-ish; disease-treatment / efficacy claims not. Flag shaky science and product/mechanism mismatches honestly — never claim a mechanism the product doesn't own. Testimonials ≠ substantiation for a health claim.
	- Add a one-line **"why your last ads probably got rejected"** if relevant, and for restricted audiences a short **"how to reach them WITHOUT naming the condition"** note.

**3 · Ideal customer (ICP)** — Exactly who she is: demographics + mindset + situation. One real person, not "everyone."
	- **Worldview (write as one of them):** capture her **religious · political · social · economic** attitudes + **prejudices / stereotypes** (what she rolls her eyes at, who she thinks "gets it"). Mirror these so the copy reads like a familiar voice, not an outsider. Sum her core view of **life, love & family** in 1–3 sentences.
	- **Required fields:** age range · life stage · household · income band · job type · country / urban-rural · a typical day when the problem hits. Next to each field: the §2C snippet count or SimilarWeb figure behind it. Any field with no data = INFERENCE. Every worldview line quotes at least one §2C snippet or is marked INFERENCE.
**3A · Sub-avatars (the scale targets)** — §3 is who you launch to. Now list the **3–5 OTHER real buyer groups** the §2C data bank shows (different ages, jobs, situations, gift-buyers…) — the ones you'll target once the store gets its first buyers and you start scaling. For each: who they are · their #1 pain in their own words (quoted + sourced) · how big the group looks (how many §2C snippets come from them) · where they gather (subreddits, groups, interests) · the angle that would open them. Rank them. Every sub-avatar must come from real data — no invented personas.
**4 · Contrast** — Do the opposite of the market. Stand out like black letters on white paper. Name the exact contrast.
	- **How:** take the 5 things nearly all §1 competitors do the same way (visual style · tone · claim type · offer shape · page format). For each: the evidence + your exact opposite. §4 = execution contrast; §4B = identity — don't repeat §4B here.
**4B · Brand Positioning System (how to actually stand out — run this for every product)** — A different *ad angle* is not enough in a saturated market. When you can't outspend the incumbents, you win by owning a whole-brand IDENTITY they structurally can't copy. Don't sell the product's benefit — build a brand people JOIN. Model: Liquid Death — a zero-differentiation commodity (canned water) that beat billion-dollar giants on identity + attitude alone, not on product. Research and LOCK these for the product before you build anything:
	- **The Enemy** — what/who is the brand against? Usually the dishonest / lazy / corrupt way the whole category currently operates (fake experts, hidden fees, overclaiming, the thing that quietly screws the customer). A brand with an enemy is a brand people rally to. Find the enemy inside the category's own 1★ reviews and repeated complaint patterns.
	- **What we stand FOR** — the belief / truth we'll tell that the category won't (even when it costs us a sale). This is the identity the customer *adopts* by buying from us.
	- **The Attitude** — the personality + tone NO incumbent occupies (if they're all warm / soft / polished → be blunt / funny / raw; if all clinical → be human). Attitude is the moat: a big corporate operation can't authentically switch into it.
	- **The Uncopyable Test** — ask: "if the #1 competitor tried to adopt this positioning tomorrow, would it INDICT them or make them look insane?" If yes → real moat. If they could just bolt it on (a discount, a feature, a nicer photo) → it's not positioning, keep digging.
	- **Whole-store thread** — the position must live on EVERY surface, not one ad: the name, the About-page manifesto, the product-page proof, the offer / guarantee, which reviews you show, the voice. If it only lives in the ad, it's an angle, not a brand.
	- **Weakness-as-weapon** — being small / new / broke is an ASSET here: underdog vs. rich incumbents = David vs. Goliath, and people root for David. Name how the brand's limitation becomes its story.
	- *Guardrails:* (1) you must actually BE what you claim — a hypocritical position detonates harder than none; (2) attack the category PATTERN, never name-and-shame a specific competitor (defamation risk).
	- **Output (lock one line):** "We are the \[attitude\] brand that stands FOR \[truth\] and AGAINST \[enemy\] — the \[category\] for people who \[identity\]." Everything downstream (§13B story, §14 voice, the offer, the store) executes this one line.
**5 · Awareness level** — Where is she: Unaware → Problem → Solution → Product → Most aware? Each level is spoken to differently. Then pick the funnel that moves her to buy.
	- Here, name the funnel type her stage needs; the final pick is made once, in the Funnel Map.
	- **Measure it, don't guess (two ways):** (1) **Discourse-volume proxy** — search "\[problem\] + Reddit" (and forums). Pages and pages of threads = she clearly knows the problem (at least problem-aware); barely any threads = low awareness (unaware/problem-aware) → you'll have to build the problem before you sell. *How burned / skeptical she is gets measured in §8, not here.* (2) **Real-data split** — sort the §2C snippets by stage and count them: "what is this / why does this happen" = problem-aware · "does \[solution type\] work? / what actually helps?" = solution-aware · "\[brand\] review / is \[brand\] legit?" = product-aware · "best deal / discount code for \[brand\]" = most aware. Report the counts as a **% split across the 5 stages**, so you enter where the MAJORITY actually sits (most-aware is almost always the smallest slice). Unaware people don't post — that slice can only be estimated, so label it INFERENCE.
	- **Low awareness? Bridge a symptom → the problem.** When the market doesn't know the problem exists, don't sell the problem cold. Find a **symptom she already notices and already wants gone** (or one that becomes urgent once she learns what it means), then walk her: symptom → hidden problem → your product. Hunt symptoms in the research (Google AI-overview for "signs of \[problem\]", then "\[symptom\]" + Reddit) and pick the ones that (a) she'd act on anyway, or (b) turn scary once tied to the real cause.
		- No AI-overview tool: `firecrawl_search` "signs of \[problem\]" → read the top medical / expert pages (e.g. Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, NHS), then search each symptom + Reddit.
**6 · Consciousness level** — Her **emotional state**: where she sits on the [Map](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fbucketeer-e05bbc84-baa3-437e-9518-adb32be77984.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6869b8ab-b480-4897-854f-6525d7b26cc9_3573x3195.jpeg) ladder right now (Shame · Guilt · Apathy · Grief · Fear · Desire · Anger · Pride) and where the copy moves her (Courage → Neutrality → Willingness → Acceptance). Use the emotion names only — **ignore the numbers on the map** (they aren't real science and never go in copy). Back her current state with real quotes + the §2C emotional-state tag counts (e.g. Fear 38 · Shame 21 · Apathy 9). (Awareness = how much she knows. Consciousness = how she feels about it.)
**7 · Funnel level** — Top, middle, or bottom — where this traffic sits.
	- "This traffic" = the cold traffic your launch ads will reach (the §3 ICP at her §5 stage). State the level and what it means for the ad's job and the first page (pre-sell vs. straight to the product page).
**8 · Sophistication stage** — How tired the MARKET is ([map](https://swipefile.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/stages-of-market-sophistication.png)). Skepticism rises with every level (more time + more buying experiences = more burned). Match your marketing to the level:
	- **Level 1 — Birth of the market:** just make the claim.
	- **Level 2:** make the claim BIGGER than the competition's.
	- **Level 3:** introduce a **mechanism** — a logical reason WHY your claim is achievable (Section 13).
	- **Level 4:** create a BETTER mechanism, or an upgraded version of the previous one.
	- **Level 5:** skepticism is at the max — she no longer believes in ANY mechanism. New advanced strategies must be activated: sell **identification** (who she is + what the brand stands for, §4B), not another mechanism.
	- **Prove the level, don't pick it by feel:** classify every §1 competitor ad you captured by what it leads with (plain claim · bigger claim · mechanism · upgraded mechanism · identity) and count them (e.g. "14 of 22 lead with a mechanism"). Add the §2C count of burned snippets ("tried everything" · "scam" · "nothing works"). State the level + the evidence.
**9 · Beliefs: now → needed** — What she believes now vs. what she must believe to buy. Map how to change it. Pull from real reviews/forums/Reddit.
	- **The One Belief (Evaldo Albuquerque):** sum the whole sale in one line before you write §9B — "This new opportunity — \[name the opportunity\] — is the key to \[her #1 desire by §2C count\], and it's only attainable through \[your mechanism — working name now\]." Every belief in §9B leads to this one. It's a DRAFT here: rewrite it at the end of §13 with the final mechanism.
**9B · The Belief Chain (North Star)** — the **≤6 beliefs** she MUST hold before she'll buy, written as **"I believe that…"** statements, in order. This is the destination you lead her to *before* you mention the offer — every line of copy walks her one step closer. Marketing = changing what she believes now into these. Build it from the research (believe now → the gap → the belief that unlocks the buy).
	- Each belief: what she believes now (quote + §2C count) → the belief she needs → the proof that moves her (§11 / §13 proof list).
**10 · Everything about the ICP** — Pains · objections · needs · core desires / dream outcome · misconceptions · mindset (psychographics) · key experiences & moments · and what she's already tried and failed (failed solutions). All from real reviews / forums / Reddit / Amazon — no assumptions.
	- **Pulled from the §2C data bank and ranked by count** — every pain, desire, objection, need, and misconception shows how many snippets mention it, highest first, with its 1–2 best quotes + sources. §10B (failed solutions) and §11 (objections) go deeper and refer back to these items by name — never re-paste the same quote.
	- **Past victories + failures:** where she's WON before (what worked, even briefly — shows what she already believes works + gives you aspirational proof) AND where she's FAILED (the let-downs that made her cynical).
		- Product failures → refer to §10B items by name. Only add here the failures that weren't a product (a diet, a doctor visit, a habit change).
	- **Goals — near + far:** her short-term wins (what she wants this week/month) AND her long-term dream outcome. Hit both — the near-term win earns the click, the long-term dream sells the identity.
	- **The 4 forces (Jobs-to-be-Done — she only switches when Push + Pull beat Anxiety + Habit):** **Push** — the pain pushing her away from what she does now · **Pull** — the dream pulling her toward something new · **Anxiety** — her fears about the NEW thing (will it work, is it a scam, will it fit, is it a hassle) · **Habit** — what keeps her stuck with the old way (comfort, sunk cost, "it's fine for now"). Anxiety + habit are why people DON'T buy — each one gets answered in §11.
**10B · Existing Solutions Teardown** — tear apart every alternative she already uses:
	- **What she uses now** — all of it (competitors, DIY, home remedies, doing nothing).
	- **What she LIKES about it** — keep this in your offer too.
	- **What she DISLIKES about it** — this is your differentiation.
	- **Horror stories** — the worst experiences people had with those solutions (grab the real quotes — they go straight into copy).
	- **Do they even believe these solutions work?** — if YES, you've got more convincing to do; if NO, capture exactly why they gave up.
	- *Source: Amazon 1★ + 5★, forums, Reddit.*
	- Rank the alternatives by §2C failed-solution count; at least 1 sourced horror-story quote + 1 like / dislike quote for each.
**11 · Objections → answers (2 columns)** — Every worry you don't answer, she answers for herself, then leaves. For each: say the worry in her own words → kill it with proof (guarantee, a number/study, a testimonial from someone like her, a demo, or an honest trade-off). Run this checklist so none slip through: price · "will it work" · "for someone like me" · trust/scam · ease of use · upkeep/cost · time to results · returns/guarantee · looks. **Power move:** raise the product's most obvious flaw first, then flip it — it reads as honesty. *(Amazon 1–2★ mining fills this fast.)*
	- **Rank + reality-check:** list objections highest §2C count first. Tag every proof **HAVE NOW** · **CAN CREATE before launch** (a demo to film, a guarantee, a spec photo) · **DON'T HAVE** (e.g. reviews for a new store). An objection whose only answer is DON'T HAVE is flagged OPEN, not answered. The count + proof tag go inside the answer cell, so it stays 2 columns.
	- **Now LOCK §2, §4, and §4B:** go back and update them with what §10–§11 found (the enemy and the real gap live in the 1★ reviews and objections). Write what changed from the draft — and if the MARKET itself changed (not just the angle), redo §0–§11 for the new market (re-run the §2 gate) and say so.
**12 · Authority figure** — Who SHE actually trusts in this niche (don't assume a doctor). Borrow that trust in your store + copy.
	- List the top 3–5 by §2C count. For each: name · type (doctor / creator / community figure / fellow sufferer) · reach · a quote showing she trusts them + URL · how the store can borrow that trust without implying they endorse you.
**13 · Unique mechanism** — The hidden CAUSE of her problem + your named FIX (a 2–3 step system). This is what makes a plain product un-copyable, and it's the spine of your advertorial + ad. The more crowded the market (Section 8), the more this matters.
	- **Core Metaphor:** decide the one metaphor the whole pitch hangs on ("discs like a dried-out sponge") so the mechanism is instantly graspable.
	- 🐱 **Split it: UMP vs UMS (research both — you can't write either until the research surfaces them).** **UMP = mechanism of the PROBLEM** — the hidden root cause / villain behind her symptom (dig it out of research: why is this actually happening?). **UMS = mechanism of the SOLUTION** — why YOUR product specifically fixes that cause (the spec/ingredient/dose/process/design that lets you claim it, and that separates you from the category + cheaper lookalikes). Uncover the problem-mechanism first (creates the need), then the solution-mechanism (owns the fix).
	- **Honesty check:** never claim a mechanism the product doesn't actually own, or an efficacy / health outcome you can't substantiate (§2B). If the product and the mechanism don't match, flag the gap plainly.
	- **Pick from candidates:** draft 3 UMP → UMS pairs. Score each: proof available now (Y/N) · already used by any §1 competitor (check the TAKEN map — quote the ad if yes) · sayable under §2B (Y/N). Pick the winner and show all 3 scores.
	- **Proof list:** match every mechanism claim to its proof — a study (with link), a demo you can film, a spec / material fact, or a real customer result (quoted + sourced). No proof → cut the claim or flag it as a gap.
	- **Now rewrite the §9 One Belief** with this final mechanism, and re-check §9B against it. Write what changed.
**13B · Discovery Story** — the origin story of the mechanism/product: the struggle before it, how it was "discovered," the turning point. This is the spine of the advertorial. Even for a sourced product, build it from **real, verifiable history** — the true history of the product, its ingredients / materials / design, or the mechanism (who first found or used it, when, how, what happened to it). Every fact gets a source (§20 B fact-checks it); never invent a founder, an expert, or an event. Then pull real emotional beats from customer stories in the research.
**14 · Voice** — Exactly how she talks. Save a swipe of her real phrases to drop into hooks and copy. 5th–8th grade reading level, never higher.
	- **Emotional Journey:** map the arc — **Awareness → Frustration → Desperation / seeking a fix → Relief / commitment** — and know where your traffic enters it.
	- **Swipe size:** at least 50 short, vivid phrases (her metaphors, exaggerations, her own labels — e.g. "walking on marbles"), not full pain sentences. Mark ★ the ones that appear in 3+ snippets.
	- **Quote buckets:** as you scrape, sort real quotes into pain/frustration · mindset · deep fears · motivation & urgency · emotional response to struggle. These drop into hooks and body copy verbatim.
		- "As you scrape" = sort the §2C quotes; use quotes not already shown in §10.
	- **Words to USE vs. words to AVOID:** **USE** = her exact words from the §2C data bank (the swipe) — these go into hooks, headlines, and body copy. **AVOID** = words she never uses: clinical / technical terms, marketer-speak, and the hype words the category's ads overuse. List each avoid-word with the plain word she'd say instead (e.g. "hallux valgus" → "bunion"). An avoid-word may appear ONLY on the product page or FAQ when it's needed for accuracy or education (e.g. naming the condition) — never in ads, hooks, or headlines.
**15 · Competitor Offers + Price Ladder** — Go through **EVERY competitor named earlier on this page that sells online** (the whole §1 long-list incl. direct + indirect, plus any others found in §0B and §10B — skip DIY / doing nothing) — not a sample. To keep it fast: for Shopify stores, first `firecrawl_scrape` `\[domain\]/products/\[handle\].js` (every variant, tier price, compare-at price in one call); then ONE `playwright-skill` script loops the whole URL list (product page → add to cart → cart → checkout, stop) and saves each step's text + screenshot to files. A store the script can't get through → write "cart not walked — why" in its block. For each one, open the actual product page and walk the cart / checkout (`playwright-skill` / `Firecrawl`) and capture **everything** they sell with. Go as far as the checkout page and stop at the payment step — never enter payment details, never place an order, never use real personal data.
	- **One-time purchase:** the single-unit price · the compare-at / "was" price.
	- **Bundles + tiers:** every quantity tier (1 / 2 / 3-pack…) + price per unit · BOGO · subscription price + terms.
	- **Free gifts + bonuses:** what's thrown in, at which tier, and the "value" they claim.
	- **Upsells + cross-sells:** order bumps · cart add-ons · shipping protection · warranties — each with its price. Post-purchase one-click upsells only show after paying — so infer them from the store's installed apps (Winning Hunter `get_store_details`, e.g. Zipify OCU / ReConvert / Rebuy) and label INFERENCE.
	- **Guarantee + returns:** length · conditions · who pays return shipping · where returns go.
	- **Shipping:** free-shipping threshold · stated delivery times.
	- **Discount mechanics:** pop-up offers (what they give for email / SMS) · countdowns · coupon codes · the "reason for the discount."
	- **Where it's weak / missing** — the gap you can beat.
	- **Output:** one short block per competitor (bold name, one line per item above, the page URL as the source for every number) + a 4-column summary table: competitor · price · best offer · gap. Under it, 3 lines: **Table stakes** (what at least half of them do — you must match it) · **Best-in-class** (the strongest offer + who runs it) · **Nobody offers** (the open slots → Offer inputs).
	- **Price ladder:** the same (or closest) product's REAL price at every level — AliExpress / CJ (not the welcome-deal teaser) → Amazon → TikTok Shop → each competitor above. Shows the price floor, the price ceiling, and how much brand + offer you need to justify your price.
	- **Offer inputs for Step 2:** the problems your offer stack must solve (top of §10 + §11) · the guarantee gap · the natural bundles (e.g. both feet, day + night) · the price point to test (then re-run the Pre-0 KPI table at this price in Doc 1). Open and match the input list of the offer reference; Step 2 builds the offer (<mention-page url="https://app.notion.com/p/3d9d53123cd681dabc18f09e90184c44">Reference — Offer Build (Grand Slam Offer)</mention-page>); this section hands it the raw material.
