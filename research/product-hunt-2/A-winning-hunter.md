Source: Winning Hunter MCP (`search_facebook_ads`, `find_winning_products`, `get_store_details`, `get_store_top_ads`). Filters were English, last seen 2026-09-22 to 2026-10-06, mostly Shopify. The base probes used min price $35, ≥30 days running and ≥15–20 active page ads. Some probes were capped at ≤800 page ads to drop mega-brands. Generic scans also used min copy 1,000 chars with the scaling or winning ad score, plus active-ads growth ≥100% over 1 month. The keyword probes covered ~35 tribes: hunters, anglers, golfers, pickleball, cyclists, runners, overland, gardeners, chickens, homestead, woodworking, mechanics, nurses, truckers, veterans, new dad, lifters, pitmaster, frenchie, German shepherd, aquarium, cold plunge, riders, espresso, snoring, equestrian, guitarists, crochet, knitters, bowhunting, bourbon, sourdough, beekeeping, golf swing, book lovers, gamers, jiu jitsu, smoker, kayak and camper.

Notes:
- **Revenue** is Winning Hunter's 30-day estimate band, which is a model and not audited.
- **Days running** counts the named ad's start date through 2026-10-06.
- **Page ads** is the total active ads on the advertiser page; the 1-month figure is in brackets.
- **Cost** is an estimate for 1688/AliExpress unit cost plus shipping, landed in the US. It was reasoned from comparable listings and was not looked up live. Every cost figure is **INFERENCE**.
- **Verbatim hooks** are quoted from the ad copy Winning Hunter returned.
- What the generic "1k-copy + scaling/winning" scans surfaced was overwhelmingly supplement and pet-health advertorials (taurine, collagen, berberine). These are excluded by the criteria. The tribe keyword probes were far more productive.

## Top 8, with culture sketches

### 1. Long-haul motorcycle rider comfort system (gel rider and passenger cushion, lumbar belt, earplugs, visor kit)
- **Proof:** Holie earns $340k–650k/mo after six months as a generic HK gel-cushion store, with riders as one of its audiences. BikerVision adds $92k–138k/mo selling intercoms to the same people.
- **Tribe:** touring and long-distance riders: Iron Butt Association members, ABR / ADV riders, Harley touring guys and Gold Wing couples.
- **Belief / enemy:** "Pain isn't a badge. The ride should end when the road does, not when your back does." The enemy is the OEM seat and the "toughen up" macho culture that cuts rides short and leaves the passenger miserable.
- **How the product carries it:** cushions graded by miles ("300-mile", "1,000-mile"), a passenger-specific pad and a Saddle-Sore spec sheet. The kit is sold as "range extender" gear, not as comfort.
- **Rituals and assets:** logged "pain-free miles" UGC; Iron Butt certificate shout-outs; a "first 1,000-mile day" patch; route-of-the-month content; rally presence (ABR, Sturgis).
- **Margin and sourcing:** $74–149 bundle against $10–16 landed per cushion (INFERENCE). Honeycomb TPE gel is a commodity on 1688.

### 2. AFOL "display culture" brand (LEGO light kits plus a dust/clean kit)
- **Proof:** BrickBling $765k–1.45M/mo; Light My Bricks UK alone $190k–320k/mo; CleanMyBricks $60k–95k/mo.
- **Tribe:** adult fans of LEGO, people who came out of their "dark ages", Icons/Ideas set collectors and display-shelf owners.
- **Belief / enemy:** "Adult building is a craft, not a toy habit. A $400 set deserves to be shown, not dusted." The enemy is the "aren't you too old for LEGO?" judgement and builds left dead and dusty on a shelf.
- **How the product carries it:** set-specific "scene" lighting designed as stagecraft (night mode, story lighting), sold with a dust kit as a "display care" ritual. The packaging reads like a gallery kit.
- **Rituals and assets:** "Lights-off reveal" videos (TikTok-native); a monthly "shelf tour" UGC contest; "dark ages" origin stories; a new-set launch-day kit drop calendar.
- **Margin and sourcing:** $50–115 against $8–20 (INFERENCE). Many 1688 factories make these, and BrickBling itself is one.
- **Risks:** LEGO trademark use (keep it nominative, "for LEGO® sets"); a SKU per set; BrickBling's price floor.

### 3. Driveway mechanic tools brand (offset extension wrench as hero, with brake press, inspection light and magnetic beam)
- **Proof:** WildBear $858k–1.5M/mo, 437 page ads, top ad running 288 days. The adjacent brake-tool seller runs 1,414 ads.
- **Tribe:** DIY / "shade-tree" mechanics, truck and old-car owners, the "I'll do it myself" guys.
- **Belief / enemy:** "Never pay the dealer $920 for a $40 job." The enemy is dealership shop rates and planned un-repairability, which aligns with right-to-repair.
- **How the product carries it:** tools named after the jobs they save (for example "The EGR Job"), each with a "dealer quote vs your cost" card in the box.
- **Rituals and assets:** a "Dealer Quote Wall" UGC of people posting the quote they beat; "Saturday Job" video tutorials; a busted-knuckle hall of fame.
- **Margin and sourcing:** $79–99 against $8–15 (INFERENCE). Generic AliExpress tooling.
- **Competition:** WildBear already uses the anti-dealer story in its advertorials, but as copy only; there is no brand behind it.

### 4. US sourdough tribe kit
- **Proof:** You Knead Sourdough $155k–295k/mo, with a kit ad running 518 days, mostly from Australia; an AU competitor is only now entering the US.
- **Tribe:** home sourdough bakers, who name their starters and post crumb shots.
- **Belief / enemy:** "Real bread takes time; supermarket 'sourdough' is a lie." The enemy is ultra-processed bread and fake-sourdough labels.
- **How the product carries it:** a heritage starter with a name and a lineage certificate; a kit built around a 72-hour ritual (feed, fold, bake); a temperature-strip jar.
- **Rituals and assets:** "Starter birthdays"; a crumb-shot leaderboard; "first loaf" UGC; weekly bake-along.
- **Margin and sourcing:** $89–110 against $14–20 hardware (INFERENCE).
- **Risk:** the dry starter is a food item. Either source it from a US producer or ship the kit hardware-only with a starter partner.

### 5. "Garage golfer" home-practice system (training aids plus a swing-filming pod)
- **Proof:** WhyGolf $203k–390k/mo, AOV ~$97. Strivon pod and PlaneSwing are also running.
- **Tribe:** mid- and high-handicap golfers who practise at home, the 15-handicap dad with 20 minutes a night.
- **Belief / enemy:** "You don't need lessons at $120 an hour or tour gear; you need reps." The enemy is the gear-buying and lesson industry that sells improvement as a purchase.
- **How the product carries it:** a "20-minute garage plan" built into the kit (discs, wrist trainer, pod) with a progress card.
- **Rituals and assets:** handicap-drop challenges; nightly "rep streaks"; before/after swing video UGC.
- **Margin and sourcing:** $50–130 against $3–15 (INFERENCE).
- **Competition:** functional incumbents ("Is your golf swing inconsistent?") own no identity.

### 6. Bookish "Ex Libris" brand (custom embosser as hero)
- **Proof:** My Book Embosser $84k–128k/mo after 13 months.
- **Tribe:** BookTok / "bookish" readers, romantasy and "dark academia" readers, home-library builders.
- **Belief / enemy:** "Books are owned, marked, lent and returned — not streamed." The enemy is ephemeral e-reading, and the friend who never returns books.
- **How the product carries it:** genre-themed plates (Spooky, Mythology, Romantasy) and a "lending card" system.
- **Rituals and assets:** an "embossing session" ASMR video format; shelf tours; a "lent and returned" tracker; seasonal plate drops.
- **Margin and sourcing:** $42 plus plates and clips gives AOV of $60+, against $8–14 (INFERENCE).
- **Watch:** the AOV, and the incumbent's non-delivery reputation, which is an opening.

### 7. BJJ "open-mat" gear brand (rash guards and shorts first, gis later)
- **Proof:** Hooks $51k–95k/mo with 243–391 page ads and a bundle ad running 337 days. It passes the ≥20 ads ≥30d gate, not the $100k gate.
- **Tribe:** Brazilian jiu-jitsu practitioners, white to purple belts, open-mat regulars.
- **Belief / enemy:** "Tap, learn, come back." The enemy is ego and McDojo marketing.
- **How the product carries it:** belt-rank colourways that are unlocked or earned (a stripe-promo discount), and a gi built for the hobbyist's sweat rather than the competitor's.
- **Rituals and assets:** promotion-day photos; "roll count" streaks; academy affiliate programme.
- **Margin and sourcing:** rash guards $45–75 against $8–12; gis $150–200 against $20–30 (INFERENCE). Pakistan/China OEM with small MOQs, so this is not pure dropship.

### 8. Hunter "trophy wall" brand (custom steel European-mount plaques, beard and fan mounts, antler stands)
- **Proof:** **weak.** Prized Possessions $4k–8k/mo with 70 ads running 242 days; Zanvos $11k–18k/mo store-wide.
- **Tribe:** whitetail and turkey hunters. It is the deepest culture in the set.
- **Belief / enemy:** "Every animal is a story; it deserves more than a nail in the garage." The enemy is $600 taxidermy bills and trophies left in a bucket.
- **How the product carries it:** state-outline plaques with the harvest date, GPS coordinates and weapon. The plaque is the ritual object.
- **Rituals and assets:** opening-day countdowns; "first buck" kids' plaques; a harvest-wall photo series; seasonality (Sep–Jan turkey and deer).
- **Margin and sourcing:** $76–100 against $15–25 (INFERENCE). Heavy shipping, so a US laser-cut partner is likely better.
- **Why #8 and not higher:** demand proof is thin. Test only with low CPMs in Sep–Dec.

## Ruthless notes
- **Biggest raw money seen:** handhearth.com ($880k–1.6M) is excluded on the medical gate; iToolMax is a general store and not a tribe brand. The handhearth playbook — one generic SKU re-niched into "the Guitarist's / the Knitter's" version with a tribe-named Facebook page ("Guitar circle") — is **the most transferable tactic found**. Apply it to non-medical products such as #1 and #5.
- **Rejected or excluded:** supplements and taurine advertorials (gate 6); the EMS sleeves (device); the holster (Meta policy and fit risk); the mini RC trucks (no data); the trucker watch (the buyer is a gift-giver, not the tribe); the sauna (logistics).
- **Data not pulled:** store details for norbbshop, thegiftnorth, anglercofishing, megelin, senviacare and crochetpark. Their proof in the table is ad count and days running only.
