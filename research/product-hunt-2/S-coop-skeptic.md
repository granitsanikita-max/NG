# S — Skeptic review: auto coop door / "Night Watch" brand (2026-10-06)

Target: `V-coop-door.md` (verdict TEST). Gates: `CRITERIA.md`. Owner rule: no "honest/trusted/quality" positioning; a real tribe culture is required.
Tools: Winning Hunter (WH) Meta ads, store details and top ads, TikTok Shop; WebSearch/WebFetch; Firecrawl. **INFERENCE** marks my estimates.

## Verdict: **KILL the door-led brand.**

The tribe is real, and Meta can reach it profitably. But the money there is in consumables sold through advertorials, and doors are not where it goes. The door is a small, seasonal, Amazon-anchored hardware market. At realistic CPA its unit economics go negative, and the "deadbolt" differentiator already exists everywhere.

A narrow rescue path exists; see the end of this file.

## Attack 2 — Demand reality: how big is the door market, and can a DTC newcomer take share?

**Evidence:**
- **Analyst reports:** the North American automatic coop door market is **~$32.5M (2025)** and global is **~$81.7M** ([intelmarketresearch](https://www.intelmarketresearch.com/automatic-chicken-coop-door-market-9652), [globalgrowthinsights](https://www.globalgrowthinsights.com/market-reports/automatic-chicken-coop-door-market-112306)). These are low quality, but the order of magnitude agrees with Amazon.
- **Amazon, via [asinsight](https://www.asinsight.com/report/US/automatic-chicken-coop-door):**
  - 131 tracked listings. The top 10 average **670 units/mo** each, and the top 3 hold 34% of click share.
  - Weekly search volume is about **9.2k**. Average top-seller price is **$71.59**, and the densest band is $50–100.
- **Bottom-up (INFERENCE):** V's page-1 count is 7–9k units/mo in October. At a peak-season multiplier of ~2–2.5× in Mar–May and a trough in Nov–Jan, that is roughly 100–130k Amazon units/yr × ~$65 ≈ **$7–9M/yr on Amazon**. Adding Tractor Supply, Omlet, Run-Chicken direct and farm stores gives **≈$25–40M total US**.
- **Run-Chicken's real trajectory:**
  - "$10M in the first three years" (from Sept 2019, so this is mostly the 2020–22 COVID chick boom).
  - Then "**~200 units/month generating $30K+**" now ([jingsourcing](https://jingsourcing.com/fb-chicken-coop-door/)).
  - The article is internally inconsistent: 2,600 units × $129 ≈ $335k, not $482k.
  - The category leader built its business on **Amazon + Google search with 18.8% branded traffic**, not paid social. It also attracted "numerous knockoffs" despite patents.
- **Price anchor:** the best-sellers are $38–60 Chinese solar doors (NyPots 900–1k/mo at $59.99; $38.39 door 800+/mo). In V's Amazon data, the $120–150 tier with a camera or lock sells about 200 units/mo per listing.

**Ruling: WOUNDED (severe).**
- The demand is real but small: a ~$30M/yr US category, ~60% of it in Feb–May, owned by Amazon search and anchored at $40–70.
- **INFERENCE:** a DTC newcomer at $129–169 that reaches 1–2% share books **$300–700k/yr** of door revenue, mostly in one quarter. That is not a brand.
- Winning share means competing on Amazon at $60. That kills the 25% landed-cost gate and makes any brand culture invisible.

## Attack 3 — Product risk: Chinese door failure modes, and can we get a reliable deadbolt?

**Failure modes (evidence):**
- **Cold kills batteries and sensors:**
  - "when temperatures dropped… both units stopped working on the same day, even after replacing batteries".
  - The "light detector was fickle".
  - Alkaline batteries fail at −30°F (MN).
  - Sources: [permies](https://permies.com/t/248922), [Omlet reviews](https://www.omlet.us/reviews/automatic-chicken-coop-door/?page=30), and the search summary of those pages.
- **Longevity and mortality:**
  - "automatic door opener failed and one of their chickens was killed".
  - "first door opener failed after about 9 months".
  - "replacement unit also failed in just a couple months" ([desertcart listing reviews](https://www.desertcart.nl/products/12047033), [bitchinchickens](https://bitchinchickens.com/2024/01/18/chickcozy-automatic-coop-door-my-experience/)).
- **Predator behaviour:** a fox learned the opening time and "the door sliding up… seemed like a dinner bell" ([Shooting UK](https://www.shootinguk.co.uk/news/sharpshooter-14/)). A door with a timer or light sensor does not stop a predator that waits for dawn. The "night watch" promise only covers the night.
- **Even the premium brands freeze:** Run-Chicken "failed to open maybe 5 times due to ice", and Omlet has ice and frozen-droppings complaints (from V).

**Deadbolt sourcing:**
- **It is easy, and that is the problem.**
- Alibaba listings already advertise "**Self-Lock Door**" ([listing](https://www.alibaba.com/product-detail/Automatic-Chicken-Coop-Door-Timing-Light_1601199856770.html)), "Sliding Door **Lock** – Automatic Mechanism" ([listing](https://www.alibaba.com/product-detail/Design-Modern-Chicken-Coop-Sliding-Door_11000022827206.html)), and self-locking linear actuators ([listing](https://www.alibaba.com/product-detail/12v-24v-Mini-Linear-Actuator-for_60631541768.html)).
- The H0Qex door is **$12.78 at 100–499 pcs**.
- ChickenGuard sells a self-locking door kit ([hatchingtime](https://hatchingtime.com/products/chickenguard-pro-with-self-locking-door-kit)). Kerbl EasyProtect has "a bolt in the housing [that] blocks the slide if the door is lifted by hand" ([Kerbl](https://katalog.kerbl.com/en/hobbyfarming/61/)). Amazon has a "Dual-motor lock solar" door at $149.99 (from V).
- **The spec V calls the product embodying the belief (gate 4c) is a commodity feature.**

**Warranty / returns (INFERENCE):** cheap solar/battery electronics in harsh outdoor use realistically run **10–15% warranty plus returns in year 1**, not V's 7%. Each failure here is a potential "my flock died" review.

**Ruling: WOUNDED.**
- Reliability is solvable only with real engineering: a gear-motor with torque headroom, lithium cells rated for −20°C, a drip hood, a fail-closed state, and freezer and ice testing. A sample-and-RFQ approach does not deliver that.
- The lock gives no differentiation.
- The brand promise ("guardian") is the single most dangerous one to make on a $24 FOB electronic part. It also edges toward the "trust/quality" positioning the owner bans: a guardian brand only works if it is the reliable one.

## Attack 4 — Economics stress test

**Duty, corrected:**
- The likely classification is **HTS 8436.29.00 (poultry-keeping machinery)**, MFN free, not 8479.
- The China overlay is the Section 301 forced-labor tariff (9903.05.31, +12.5%). The **Section 122 surcharge lapsed on 2026-07-08**.
- [Wove](https://tariffs.wove.com/us/tariff/8436.29.00.00) shows a **total effective 37.5%** for China origin.
- V's ~40% was about right. Get a broker ruling, because 8501/8479 treatment could differ.

**Worst-case unit economics (INFERENCE):**

| Line | Hero door, $129 | Bundle, $169 |
|---|---|---|
| FOB (lock spec, 500 MOQ, not the $18 bait SKU) | $26 | $34 |
| Duty 37.5% | $9.75 | $12.75 |
| Freight in + packaging (≈2.2 kg, ≈0.012 m³) | $4.50 | $5.50 |
| **Landed** | **$40.25 (31% ✗)** | **$52.25 (31% ✗)** |
| 3PL pick/pack + ship (2.2–3 kg, zones 5–8) | $13 | $15 |
| Payment fees 3% | $3.90 | $5.10 |
| Warranty + returns reserve 12% | $15.50 | $20.30 |
| **CM before ads** | **$56.35 (44%)** | **$76.35 (45%)** |
| **Break-even ROAS** | **2.29** | **2.21** |

**Realistic CPA (INFERENCE):**
- Benchmarks for cold Meta prospecting on $120–170 outdoor or pet hardware run about **$55–95 per first purchase**. Generic "$18–20 average CPA" figures ([influee](https://influee.co/blog/average-cpa-facebook-ads)) are inflated by cheap-AOV verticals and leads, so they don't apply here.
- At a $70 CPA the hero door loses **$14/order** and the bundle makes **$6/order**. That is before creative production, seeded doors, and off-season inventory carrying.
- **The only peer with a matching first-order structure (Roosty's) works because AOV is $104 on a ~$5 COGS consumable with a repeat loop.** A door has no repeat.

**Competitor cost gap:** Run-Chicken is made in Slovenia and avoids the 37.5% China stack, at the same $119–129 price.

**Ruling: KILLED** at the stated gate. Landed cost is ≤25% only with the $18 bait FOB on a non-lock door, which is the exact product that freezes and fails. Contribution after realistic CPA is ≈0 or negative in the peak and impossible off-peak.

## Attack 5 — Brand ceiling and culture monetization

**Next five products, with demand evidence:**
1. **Gravity waterer cups.** DallasWind runs 135 Meta ads; TikTok gravity feeder sold 80 units in 30 days at $16.79 (from V).
2. **Hardware cloth / predator mesh.** VEVOR is running 1,283-ad campaigns. It is commodity, heavy and low-margin.
3. **Coop solar lights.** On TikTok: a solar light at $15.59 sold **12,598 units in 30 days** (lifetime 297k); a solar shed pendant at $49.99 sold 1,196 in 30 days ([WH](https://app.winninghunter.com/tiktok-shop/product/1729461174272103357?period=30)). Demand is real, but these are generic and not chicken-specific.
4. **Poop scooper.** $29.99, 366 units in 30 days on TikTok; itoolmax sells one at $79.99 (from V).
5. **Coop camera.** A $123 Amazon camera door sells 200+/mo, but camera units bring FCC, app and support burden.

**Consumables, where the money actually is:**
- Roosty's: supplements, $577k–1.09M/mo.
- Reliant: coop spray, $48–90k/mo. Pesticidal claims bring EPA FIFRA exposure.
- First Saturday Lime: $26.99, 4,089 units in 30 days on TikTok.
- BSF / mealworm treats (Joyprotyn).
- Gate 6 bars supplements, and pest sprays carry regulatory risk. That leaves **treats and bedding/herbs without claims**, which are commodity and low-differentiation, so subscription upside is real but thin.

**Positioning strength:**
- "Night watch / predator" is a sharper story than Omlet (design, multi-pet) or Run-Chicken (engineering).
- But the predator fear is already used on Meta by **repellent sellers** (Sofyre, 6,339 ads), and the tribe's strongest spending emotion on Meta is "hen health / eggs" (Roosty's).
- The culture assets in V (Predator Diaries, First Egg Wall, chicken math) are genuine, but **they belong to the tribe, not the product**. Influencers (Fresh Eggs Daily, Chicken Chick) and BYC already own them.
- Nothing about a $129 door gives a recurring reason to engage between Feb and May.

**Ruling: WOUNDED.**
- The tribe has a high ceiling, as Roosty's growth in 18 months shows.
- A door-first brand has a low ceiling. Its hero is a once-per-household, seasonal and failure-prone purchase, and its natural attach products are generic hardware anyone can list.

## Final verdict: **KILL** (door-led "Night Watch" DTC brand)

**Deciding reasons:**
1. The tribe's proven Meta money machine (Roosty's, ~$0.6–1.1M/mo, ~900 ads) sells consumables and never touches doors. Meta's silence on doors is evidence against them, not a gap.
2. The category is small (~$30M US) and Amazon-anchored at about $65. A $129+ DTC door needs a differentiator, and the proposed one (deadbolt) already ships from Alibaba and from ChickenGuard and Kerbl.
3. The worst case lands at 31% landed, BE ROAS of about 2.2–2.3 and a ~$70 CPA, which is negative or flat on the first order with no repeat loop.
4. The brand promise is "guardian", while the hardware is a $26 part that fails in ice. One cold snap turns the culture into dead-hen reviews.

## Rescue fixes (only if the owner insists on this tribe)

- **A. Flip the hero (best).**
  - Build the tribe brand around a **non-supplement consumable with repeat purchase**: BSF/mealworm treats sold as "the evening lock-up treat". This is a lock-up ritual product, so the culture carries it.
  - Use **no health claims**, and check **state commercial feed registration**: poultry treats count as commercial feed in many states.
  - Copy Roosty's advertorial and persona structure, but under a **tribe identity** (e.g. "the night-shift keepers").
  - Add the door later as a seasonal Google/Amazon SKU. Don't use it as the Meta acquisition hook.
- **B. If the door stays the hero:** sell on **Amazon plus Google Shopping** in Feb–May only, at $89–99 with a lock and lithium cold-rated cells.
  - Do Meta only as retargeting and UGC whitelisting.
  - Source from a **non-China (Vietnam/Thailand) assembler** to drop the 37.5% stack.
  - Require −20°C ice-chamber testing and a fail-closed design before any guardian claim.
  - Expect a $0.5–1M/yr side business, not a brand.
- **C. Cheap kill-test before any inventory:**
  - In Feb 2027, spend $1.5k on Meta driving to a pre-order page for the lock-door bundle at $149.
  - Kill if CPA (pre-order) is above $60, or if add-to-cart rate is below 4%.
  - This replaces V's $3–5k plus 500-unit RFQ plan.
