**About pull-ups:**

- Pull-Ups / disposables are the #1 failed solution (67).

**About training underwear:**
- The category doesn't hold: "won't hold pee / leaks" (47 objection), "holds only one small accident" (14), "leak proof training underwear exists" misconception (1).
- "ads overpromise" (5) and "all training pants are kind of a scam" (1).

**About feeling wet:** "feeling wet teaches the child" (25) is the bank's #1 belief. This one **helps** us, but it's commoditised (§8).

## What she must believe to buy (the gap)
1. Her kid is within the normal range of "still getting there", and she isn't failing.
2. Pull-ups are the wrong tool for a big kid, and plain underwear isn't safe at school.
3. Training pants leaked because they're built for a toddler-sized accident, not because the idea is a scam.
4. A pant that publishes and *shows* what it holds, sized for her kid, will hold her kid's accident.
5. This brand is honest and on her kid's side: no tricks, a real guarantee.

(Full ordered chain with proofs: §9B.)

## How to change it
**Proof first, then story:**
- Sourced normalising stats: daytime wetting at 7–10% of 5–13-year-olds [M64]; "usually 5 years old" as the potty-training age [W9].
- The pour test on camera.
- The ml-per-size chart.
- Real parents of 6–8-year-olds.
- A worn-product guarantee.

**Never:**
- argue methods
- say "your child"
- promise a timeline

## The One Belief (DRAFT, rewritten at the end of §13)
> **"This new opportunity — training underwear built and pour-tested for a big kid's accident, not a toddler's — is the key to getting through the school day without a soaked outfit and a call home, and it's only attainable through the *Big-Kid Hold* (working name: size-matched, ml-rated capacity in underwear that still looks like underwear)."**

**Why this desire:**
- Her #1 desire by count in the segment is **"daycare/school-ready" (19 of 225)**, and it is also #1 in the locked school-age cut (**8 of 79**). Bank-wide it's 39 of 601, #2 after "dry nights" (43) (tally #8; 2C-tally; tallies-3-11-601.md).
- "Dry nights" is #1 in the whole bank, but it belongs to the night lane, which our daytime product can't honestly serve (§3A #3). So we anchor on the school day.

**Mechanism status:**
- The *Big-Kid Hold* is a **working name** for §13.
- **UMP (draft):** pants are built for a toddler bladder.
  - Expected bladder capacity ≈ (age + 2) × 30 ml (Koff), so ~120 ml at 2 and ~270 ml at 7 [P55].
  - Honest brands state 60–90 ml [P49].
  - Reviews: "doesn't hold more that 10-20ml" [C:N205] (VERBATIM 1★); "It might hold like a tablespoon of liquids" [C:N031] (SNIPPET).
  - "because of his size and the amount of liquid output, these leaked very easily" [C:N226] (SNIPPET).
- **UMS (draft):** capacity scaled per size and pour-tested, printed on every pack.
- **Honesty gap to close in §13:** we have **no measured sample yet**. Whether a real-underwear-looking big-kid pant can hold ~150–250 ml is **unproven**. If the sample can't, the claim becomes "holds X ml, which buys time to reach the bathroom", not "holds an accident".
