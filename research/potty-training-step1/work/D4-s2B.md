# §2B · Compliance & Claims — SAY / DON'T SAY

**The product's actual category:** a **US children's product (wearing apparel) for children ≤12**: reusable cotton training underwear, usually with a TPU waterproof layer, printed fabric, and sometimes snaps. It is **not** a medical device, a diaper drug, or a supplement.

The rules that apply are:
- CPSC / CPSIA (children's product + apparel)
- FTC (advertising substantiation, endorsements / reviews, textile labelling, subscriptions)
- State PFAS law
- Meta ad policy (personal attributes, health, imagery of minors)

The supplement and cosmetic rules **do not** apply. Health-claim *substantiation* law still applies to any claim that the product teaches or changes a child's body function.

## Category rules that apply to THIS product (US)
| Rule | What it requires | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Children's Product Certificate (CPC)** | Domestic manufacturers and **importers** must certify in writing, in English, based on tests by a **CPSC-accepted third-party lab**, that the product meets every applicable children's product safety rule. 7 required elements. **New: from July 8, 2026 importers must electronically file (eFile) certificates with U.S. CBP.** | VERBATIM [M48] |
| Rules a training pant must be tested / certified to | **Wearing-apparel flammability (16 CFR 1610)** and **total lead content (15 U.S.C. §1278a)**: ≤100 ppm across the whole garment *including screen-printed inks*. Lead in surface coatings applies to painted / coated snaps. The CPSC textile deck: "Children's Apparel – Summary: 16 CFR part 1610… CPC required… Lead Content, Lead Surface Coating, Tracking Labels" | VERBATIM [M49][M51][M52] |
| Phthalates | Apply to "child care articles" (sleep / feeding, age ≤3). Training underwear is likely **not** in scope, but the TPU layer should be screened anyway (INFERENCE; confirm with the lab) | [M51] |
| **Tracking label** | Permanent, visible, legible marks on the **product and its packaging**: manufacturer, location and date of production, batch / cohort. A "tagless" garment still needs a **printed** tracking label | VERBATIM [M50] |
| Children's-product status can't be labelled away | "Can I simply label my product to ensure that it is not considered a children's product? **No.**" | VERBATIM [M53] |
| Snaps / side-open fasteners | Not a toy (ASTM F963 is not cited for apparel), but snaps that detach on a toddler garment are a common recall pattern. **Pull / torque-test snaps** and add them to the CPC test plan (INFERENCE; verify with the lab) | — |
| **Textile labelling** (FTC) | Every garment: **fiber content** (generic names, % by weight), **country of origin** (front of label), **manufacturer / RN**; plus **care instructions** (Care Labeling Rule) | VERBATIM [M60][M61] |
| De minimis | Since 2025-08-29 every parcel is a formal or informal import paying duty. The importer of record issues and eFiles the CPC (see Pre-0) | SNIPPET [M12] |

## Ad-platform policy (Meta first)
- **Personal attributes (the #1 silent killer)** [M57][M58]. Meta prohibits ads that "assert or imply" a viewer's "medical or genetic condition", "disability", "physical or mental health", "age" and "family status" (VERBATIM [M58]). Its own examples:
  - ❌ "Are you disabled? We can help!"
  - ❌ "Have you been diagnosed with cancer?"
  - ❌ "people like you who just turned 62" (age implication) [M57]
- **What that means here:**
  - "Your toddler…" with a *neutral* fact is tolerated in the category (UpAiry runs it).
  - "Your 6-year-old still wetting?" / "Autistic kid still in Pull-Ups?" implies a condition or age-delay in the viewer's family = reject risk.
  - BrightKidCo's caption ran on ads that mostly lived **1–9 days** [ad log, M30]. INFERENCE: probable rejections or failed tests. "Meet other [X] parents" also triggers the rule.
- **Health & wellness / data restrictions:** domains whose URLs, product names or pages read as health ("autism", "incontinence", "enuresis") risk Meta classifying the data source as health-and-wellness. That can block Purchase optimisation (D §3 [M47-D]; Meta help [M65]; not re-verified this run). **Keep store handles neutral.**
- **No unsubstantiated outcome claims:** sell function (contains small accidents, lets wetness be noticed), comfort, dignity, routine and cost. Not "trains your child".
- **Imagery:** see the table. No minors-in-underwear-only shots.

## Legal / claims substantiation (FTC)
- **Efficacy / health-type claims** ("helps toddlers recognize when it's time", "builds body awareness", "trained in weeks") need **"competent and reliable scientific evidence… conducted and evaluated in an objective manner by experts"** (VERBATIM [M55]).
- **Anecdotal evidence from customers is not substantiation** (FTC substantiation training lists "Anecdotal evidence from customers" separately from tests and studies [M56]). Testimonials ≠ proof.
- **"Feel wet" claim — what's honest:**
  - "The inner layer lets wetness reach the skin" is a **construction fact**. Substantiate it with the spec / lab sheet.
  - "So your child learns faster" is an **efficacy claim**. Even Pull-Ups only claims "Learning Layer Feels Wet, Stays Dry" [M63].
  - We may say "lets them *notice* accidents". We may **not** say "teaches the brain" or "operant conditioning" (UpAiry [M5]) without evidence.
- **Mechanism / product mismatch flags:**
  - UpAiry says "100% cotton" on a TPU-lined pant [M5].
  - It says "3-layer" / "six layers" / "2-layer" on the same page [M1].
  - **Our** copy must match our spec sheet exactly.
- **Comparatives** ("4x more absorbent than leading brands", Tiny Tots [M30]) need head-to-head testing on file.
- **"Pediatrician approved" / "Parents' Choice"** need a real basis or an award certificate.

## Re-check of the §2 DRAFT angle against these rules
**Pick B ("big kids still learning", 5–9, ND / sensory core) — workable with constraints:**
1. **Ad copy never names a diagnosis or implies the viewer's child has one.**
   - Use "big kids still learning", "sizes up to 10", "tagless, flat seams", "looks like real underwear at school".
2. **Neurodivergent identity lives only where the parent speaks for themselves:**
   - First-person creator video: "My autistic son wore these…" (creator about own child, disclosed as paid / gifted).
   - Community, email, SEO pages. Never in a "you / your child" hook.
3. **Domain, handles and product names stay neutral.** No "autism", "incontinence", "special-needs" in URLs or SKUs.
4. **No timeline, no medical or behavioural outcome.** Sell fit, dignity, measured capacity and comfort.
5. **CPC + tracking labels + fiber / origin / care labels before any US sale.** A dropship parcel without them is the biggest hidden risk for a "safe for sensitive kids" brand (D §3 [M47-D]).

**Why your last ads probably got rejected:** N/A. There's no account yet. The category's cautionary tale is BrightKidCo: "Autistic kid still in Pull-Ups?" + "Autism Constipation SOLVED!" ads ran only 1–42 days [ad log].

**How to reach this audience WITHOUT naming the condition:**
- Broad targeting (US parents, 25–54), with the creative doing the selection:
  - visible big-kid sizes
  - a school backpack / classroom context
  - "for kids who need more time"
  - macro shots of seams and tags
- Creator UGC from ND families, speaking first person.
- Search / SEO for "training underwear size 8 / 10", "sensory friendly training pants".
- Communities via organic and partnerships, not targeting. (Meta removed health-related detailed targeting; D §3 [M47-D].)

## So What → do this
1. Write all copy from the ✅ column.
2. Lead with **measured capacity + fit + dignity**, never a timeline or a diagnosis.
3. Budget for a **CPSC-accepted lab test** (16 CFR 1610 + lead incl. inks / snaps) and a **CPC eFile** before importing.
4. Print the tracking label, fiber, origin and care info on every pair.
5. **No personas, no fake counts, no hidden subscription.** This is both compliance and our differentiation versus UpAiry (§Audit, §1).
