      Why choose these over Pull-Ups?

      Pull-Ups are designed to keep toddlers feeling dry and comfortable, which often makes them feel too much like diapers. Training underwear works differently.

      These are made to help your toddler notice when they’ve had a small accident, so they can start connecting that feeling with the potty. At the same time, they still provide enough backup to help reduce the stress of early accidents.

      It’s the middle ground many parents are looking for: more helpful than Pull-Ups, less overwhelming than regular underwear.

      How do they help my toddler actually learn?

      Potty training works best when toddlers can start connecting body signals with what happens next.

      That’s why training underwear matters. Instead of making accidents feel completely dry, they allow your child to notice the wet feeling, which helps build awareness over time. That awareness is often the missing link between “still having accidents” and “starting to ask for the potty.”

      In simple terms: they help turn accidents into learning moments.

      Will my child still feel the wetness?

      Yes that’s the point.

      These are designed so your toddler can feel enough wetness to recognize that something happened, without the full mess you’d expect from regular underwear alone. That feeling helps them understand the difference between dry and wet, which is a big part of potty training progress.

      If they never notice the accident, there’s much less reason for them to change the behavior.

      Will they help with leaks?

      They’re made to help with small accidents and reduce the kind of mess that makes potty training feel so exhausting.

      They are not meant to work like a full diaper, and they’re not designed to hold a large accident forever. But they do offer more protection than regular underwear, which can make early training feel much more manageable at home or on the go.

      The goal is simple: less panic, less cleanup, and more confidence during training.

      When do parents usually see progress?

      Every toddler is different, so there’s no one timeline that fits every family.

      That said, many parents start noticing small changes first like their child reacting to being wet, asking for the potty more often, or having fewer accidents as the days go on.
      For some toddlers, those changes happen quickly. For others, it takes more consistency and repetition.

      The biggest difference is that training underwear helps create the kind of awareness potty training depends on.

      Are they worth it over time?

      For many families, yes.

      Instead of continuing to spend money month after month on disposable training products, reusable training underwear gives you something you can wash, reuse, and keep using throughout the training phase.

      But the value isn’t just about cost. It’s also about progress. A lot of parents are not just tired of buying Pull-Ups they’re tired of paying for something that doesn’t seem to move training forward.

      That’s why the real comparison isn’t only “What costs less today?”

      It’s “What actually helps us get out of diapers sooner?”

      Can they handle poop accidents too?

      Yes they can help with poop accidents too.

      Like with pee accidents, the goal is to make cleanup easier than regular underwear while still supporting the potty training process. Most parents simply shake, rinse if needed, and wash as usual.

      Good fit matters here. When training underwear fits snugly around the waist and legs, it does a much better job during those early accidents.

      What Exactly Is Brilliant.kids Training Underwear?

      Brilliant.kids is not a diaper, and it’s not just regular underwear either.

      It’s training underwear designed for the stage between Pull-Ups and fully independent potty use.

      Our 3-layer design helps toddlers feel enough wetness to notice an accident, while still helping contain the small messes that make potty training stressful for parents. That’s what makes it different: it supports learning, not just absorbency.

      In simple terms, it helps your child start connecting the feeling of being wet with the need to use the potty.

      How do I wash them?

      Brilliant.kids is made for real life, which means easy washing matters.

      After an accident, rinse if needed, then machine wash with the rest of your child’s laundry. Most parents keep a simple routine: rinse, wash, dry, repeat.

      Because potty training usually involves multiple wears and washes every week, easy care is a big part of what makes training underwear realistic for everyday use.
