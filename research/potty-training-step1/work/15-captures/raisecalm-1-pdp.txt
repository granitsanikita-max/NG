Real Science Vs. Myth

What parents get told about potty training, and what actually works.

✕ Myth: You cannot speed up potty training, kids go when they are ready.
✓ The Science:

Readiness matters, but the right tool speeds it up. When a child can feel the moment they go, they connect the cue far faster than in pull-ups that hide it.

✕ Myth: Pull-ups and training pants are the same thing.
✓ The Science:

They are opposites. Pull-ups absorb everything so your child stays dry and never learns. Training pants let them feel wet, which is what drives the learning.

✕ Myth: Accidents mean it is not working.
✓ The Science:

Accidents are part of the process. Each one is a chance for your child to feel the difference and learn. Steady progress over a few weeks is the real sign it is working.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know before you order.

How are these different from regular pull-ups?
+

Pull-ups are made to keep your child dry, so they never feel the moment they go. These hold small accidents while still letting your toddler feel wet, which is the cue that teaches real bladder awareness.

How many pairs do I need?
+

Most families do best with 5 to 10 pairs so there is always a clean pair ready during the day. The 5 and 10 pair bundles also bring the price per pair right down.

Will they hold a full accident?
+

They are built to catch small accidents during daytime training, not a full release. That is on purpose, because feeling wet is what helps your toddler learn. Just change soon after an accident.

What size should I order?
+

Size by your child's weight, not age. Small fits up to about 9 kg (20 lb), Medium about 9 to 12 kg (20 to 26 lb), and Large about 12 to 18 kg (26 to 40 lb), which covers most kids about 1 to 6 years. If your child is between two sizes, size up.

How do I wash them?
+

Rinse in cold water right after an accident, then machine wash cold with like colors and air dry. Skip fabric softener, since it reduces absorbency over time.

Can my child wear them overnight?
+

These are made for daytime training, not heavy overnight wetting. They hold small accidents so your toddler feels the cue and learns. For nighttime, keep using your usual night protection until daytime training is solid.
