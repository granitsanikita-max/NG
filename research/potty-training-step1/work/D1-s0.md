# §0 · Product & core problem

**One line:** Reusable, washable potty-training underwear. The ONE problem it solves is a child who is out of diapers but not yet reliable. Diapers and pull-ups hide every accident, so nothing is learned. Plain underwear turns every accident into a floor, couch or car-seat cleanup, so parents give up and go back to pull-ups. **Who it's for:** US parents of children who are in, or stuck in, toilet learning. That's mostly ages 2–4, with a long tail to 9.

**Is this a NEED (painkiller) or a WANT?** It's a need, and the market already treats it as an urgent, money-on-the-table problem:
- **Daycare and preschool eligibility hangs on it.**
  - "must be potty trained in one month or be dismissed from care" (S093)
  - "Help before we get kicked out of preschool" (S091)
  - 38 of 252 parent snippets are deadline-driven (SNIPPET [M47] C §3).
  - The leader's best-running hooks are the same pains: "My daughter's daycare is kicking her out in 30 days." (ad 1529550321663011, running 274 days) and "We lost my daughter's preschool spot." (1416810516666498, 264 days) (VERBATIM, ad log [M23]).
- **It damages the parent's self-worth and the relationship.**
  - "Potty training makes me hate myself, dislike my child" (S070)
  - "making me resent my toddler" (S086)
  - "I feel like a failure" (S080–S085)
  - 77 of 252 snippets carry an emotion tag. The largest are BURNOUT 27, JUDGE 24 and FAIL 16 (SNIPPET [M47] C).
- **Parents already pay, repeatedly, for things that fail.**
  - Failed-solution tags: 3-day / Oh Crap / naked 20, Pull-Ups 18, training pants or cloth 13, rewards 7 (SNIPPET [M47] C).
  - Disposable training pants sell **10K+ per month per listing** on Amazon: Pampers Easy Ups 10K+ and 7K+; Pull-Ups 8K+ and 7K+ (DATA, "bought in past month" badges [M17]).

**What the product can and cannot honestly fix (claim boundary, see §2B):**
- **It can:** make each accident *noticeable* to the child and *contained* for the parent. That covers small accidents, depending on the spec. It's also reusable, so it costs less than ongoing disposables.
- **It cannot (unsubstantiated):**
  - Guarantee a training timeline ("trained in X days").
  - Hold a full void or a night's urine on a generic cotton spec. Amazon's AI summaries say "absorbency… mixed" on every major reusable [M47] B.
  - Fix withholding, constipation or a medical cause.

**Caveat (honest):**
- The *problem* is universal, but the *product's* credibility is weak. Leaks are the #1 complaint against the leader: 12 + 10 Trustpilot reviews, plus 2 "leak-proof claim false" (DATA [M24]). Reddit calls trainers "completely useless… pointless imo" (SNIPPET [M47] B §4).
- Desire for the *outcome* is high. Belief in *this category* is damaged. That is an awareness/sophistication problem to solve in copy (§6–§9), not a demand problem.

## So What → do this
- Treat this as a **high-desire painkiller market** (13/15). Desire is not the risk.
- The real risks are product credibility (leaks) and saturation (§1).
- Build every claim around what the product can *prove*: noticeable but contained accidents, measured capacity, cost vs disposables. **Never** promise a timeline.
- Time launches to the demand peaks: **Jan** and **May–Jul**. Sep–Oct is the trough.
