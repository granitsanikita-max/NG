### So What → do this
- **#1 objection: "it won't hold a pee / leaks".** It shows up as the #2 pain, the #1 objection (47) and the #1 need ("absorbency that holds a pee", 101). Amazon's own aspect data agrees: on MooMoo, 385 of 525 Leakage mentions are negative. **Do this:** state a measured capacity (how much it holds, tested on camera) and the "feel-wet, not flood" trade-off, instead of "leakproof". Shoppers punish "leakproof" claims as overpromising.
- **Feeling wet is the mechanism parents already believe in.** "Feeling wet teaches the child" is the #1 belief (25), and "pull-ups hide the wet" or "are just diapers" adds 21 more. Pull-Ups are the #1 failed solution (61). **Do this:** lead with "absorbs the flood, keeps the feel". That is the bridge between a diaper and plain underwear.
- **Category trust is low.** 71 snippets are burned or skeptical: "waste of money" 34, scam 19. 185 of 557 UpAiry Trustpilot reviews are 1★. **Do this:**
  - Use plain checkout: no add-on subscriptions.
  - Offer a refund that is actually honoured.
  - Give sizing help, because sizing/fit is a top-5 objection.
  - Make these visible trust levers on the page.
- **The daycare/school deadline is the biggest concrete trigger.** It accounts for 31 + 11 + 11 trigger tags, and "working parent / daycare" appears in 61 snippets. **Do this:** test "daycare-ready" hooks and handle the daycare pull-up rule head-on.
- **Two segments the big brands under-serve:** ND/sensory families (117) and late or school-age trainers (64 + 19). They need bigger sizes (23) and a sensory-friendly fit (15). **Do this:** carry both into §3A as scale targets. Do not commit to one positioning before the §2 gate and the §11 lock.
